# Winter in San Andreas

Every year, snow settles across San Andreas from **November 1 through March 31**. That means white roads in Los Santos, snowy desert routes around the Alamo Sea, and deep winter scenery along the northern coast and Mount Chiliad. It's a good time to see familiar places differently, but winter travel deserves a little extra planning.

Snow cover remains through the entire season. Storms, wind, and visibility may change from day to day.

## Police Advisory

**A message to motorists and residents**

Winter brings slippery intersections, reduced visibility, and longer stopping distances. Slow down, leave more room between vehicles, and take particular care after dark. Please don't park across travel lanes or block access for emergency vehicles.

If your vehicle breaks down, move out of traffic when it is safe to do so and seek assistance. Avoid standing in the roadway, especially on bends and hills where approaching drivers may struggle to see you.

Officers ask everyone to allow extra travel time and look out for neighbors who may need a hand during periods of heavy snow.

## Department of Transportation

**Winter road travel notice**

DOT crews work to keep the state's roads accessible throughout the winter season. Conditions can change quickly on steep grades, bridges, and rural routes, even when main roads appear clear.

Before setting out, check your tires, lights, and fuel. Keep the windshield clear of snow and make sure you can see properly from every window. If conditions worsen, take a safer route or delay nonessential travel.

Give maintenance crews room to work. Do not crowd road-service vehicles, drive around temporary barriers, or assume an untreated stretch of road has the same grip as a cleared one.

## Emergency Medical Services

**Cold-weather health advisory**

Cold exposure can become dangerous, particularly when clothing is wet or a person has been outside for a long time. Dress in warm layers, keep your hands and feet covered, and change out of wet clothing as soon as you can.

Shivering, unusual tiredness, confusion, and difficulty speaking can be signs that someone is becoming dangerously cold. Move the person to shelter, warm them gradually, and request medical assistance. If they are confused, drowsy, or getting worse, treat it as an emergency.

Check on older neighbors and anyone spending long periods outdoors. A warm, dry place and an early call for help can make all the difference.

## Winter Destinations

Snow changes the whole state, not just the mountains. Drive through Los Santos to see its familiar streets in winter, take a careful trip toward the Alamo Sea, or visit Paleto Bay for northern coastal views.

The forests and roads around Mount Chiliad are especially scenic, though mountain conditions call for extra preparation. See the [[explore|regional travel guide]] before planning a longer outing.

## Winter Holidays

The season begins in November with Thanksgiving Month, followed by Christmas celebrations through December 25. New Year's celebrations run from **December 26 through January 9**, and February is Valentine's Month.

See the [[city-guide/celebrations|annual celebrations calendar]] for the full year of traditions and seasonal activities.
