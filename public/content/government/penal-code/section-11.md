# ROSEFIRE PENAL CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.6 — Sections 11–12**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing criminal penalties for financial fraud, unlawful banking activity, mortgage and real estate fraud, unauthorized computer access, financial record manipulation, organized crime, racketeering, criminal enterprises, unlawful commercial operations, and associated offenses against the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–10 remain enacted and in force. Sections 11–12 constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Penal Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code, including its provisions governing banking, property ownership, commercial rights, government contracts, judicial authority, and criminal procedure.

---

# SECTION 11 — FINANCIAL CRIMES, BANKING FRAUD & CYBERCRIME

## §11.1 — Financial Fraud

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits financial fraud by knowingly obtaining money, financial property, or another monetary benefit through material deception with intent to unlawfully deprive another person or entity.

2. Financial fraud may involve knowingly false representations concerning ownership, payment authorization, financial obligations, or other material financial facts.

3. The State shall establish the deception, required criminal intent, and resulting unlawful benefit or loss.

4. Ordinary financial loss, unsuccessful investment, or breach of contract shall not independently establish fraud.

5. Where the same conduct satisfies Penal Code §4.30, applicable merger rules shall prevent duplicative punishment.

## §11.2 — Aggravated Financial Fraud

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. Aggravated financial fraud occurs when qualifying financial fraud involves a substantial established loss, repeated fraudulent transactions, or another aggravating circumstance expressly prescribed by law.

2. The State shall establish the qualifying aggravating facts.

3. A person shall not be convicted of aggravated financial fraud solely because the victim is a financial institution.

4. Restitution may be ordered for established losses.

5. Financial records shall remain subject to lawful investigative procedures.

## §11.3 — Major Financial Fraud Scheme

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in an organized scheme involving repeated material financial deception and substantial unlawful acquisition of money or assets.

2. The State shall establish the coordinated conduct, qualifying scale, and defendant's individual participation.

3. A single ordinary disputed payment shall not constitute a major financial fraud scheme.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. A sentence exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. Where the underlying conduct constitutes the same punishable act as another financial offense, applicable merger rules shall govern.

## §11.4 — Banking Account Fraud

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits banking account fraud by knowingly using materially false information, forged authorization, or another fraudulent representation to obtain access to, control of, or a financial benefit from a protected bank account.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent act and required criminal intent.

3. Lawful account recovery, authorized account management, and ordinary banking transactions shall not constitute fraud.

4. An inaccurate account balance alone shall not establish criminal activity.

5. Unauthorized theft of account funds may be prosecuted under §4.33 where its elements are established.

## §11.5 — Unauthorized Financial Transfers

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly initiating, directing, or causing a transfer of money or financial assets without lawful authority and with intent to deprive the rightful owner.

2. The State shall establish the unauthorized transfer and criminal intent.

3. An accidental transfer, bank processing error, or disputed authorized transaction shall not automatically constitute this offense.

4. Legitimate financial institutions shall not be presumed criminally responsible solely because their systems processed an unlawful transfer initiated by another person.

5. Where the same taking is punishable under §4.33, duplicative convictions or punishment shall be prohibited.

## §11.6 — Payment Instrument Fraud

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using a stolen, counterfeit, altered, or fraudulently obtained payment instrument to obtain money, goods, or services without lawful authorization.

2. Payment instruments may include bank cards, electronic payment credentials, and other officially recognized payment methods.

3. The State shall establish the fraudulent use and required knowledge.

4. Possession of another person's payment instrument with lawful permission shall not constitute fraud.

5. Ordinary payment rejection or insufficient funds shall not automatically establish criminal deception.

## §11.7 — Financial Identity Theft

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits financial identity theft by knowingly using another person's identifying or financial credentials without authorization to obtain money, credit, property, or another unlawful financial benefit.

2. The State shall establish unauthorized use and criminal intent.

3. Mere possession of identifying information shall not independently establish financial identity theft.

4. Lawful professional, governmental, and financial record access shall remain subject to applicable authorization.

5. Victims may seek appropriate correction of fraudulent records and restitution.

## §11.8 — Credit Application Fraud

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly submitting materially false information in an application for credit with intent to unlawfully obtain financing or more favorable terms.

2. Material falsehoods may concern identity, income, collateral, existing obligations, or other legally relevant financial facts.

3. The State shall establish the material deception and required intent.

4. A lender's rejection of an application shall not independently establish attempted fraud.

5. An honest estimate, good-faith mistake, or subsequent deterioration in financial circumstances shall not automatically constitute this offense.

## §11.9 — Mortgage Fraud

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits mortgage fraud by knowingly making or using a material false representation to obtain, alter, transfer, or enforce mortgage financing unlawfully.

2. Fraud may concern borrower qualifications, property ownership, collateral, existing liens, or material financing terms.

3. Ordinary mortgage default shall not constitute fraud.

4. A lender's lawful use of creditworthiness criteria shall not independently constitute mortgage fraud.

5. The State shall establish the material deception and criminal purpose.

6. Mortgage enforcement and foreclosure shall remain subject to Civil Rights & Justice Code Section 16.

## §11.10 — Fraudulent Mortgage Lending

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A lender or authorized representative commits this offense by knowingly using material deception concerning interest rates, loan obligations, collateral, or other financing terms to obtain an unlawful financial benefit.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent representation and required criminal intent.

3. A disclosed and lawfully agreed interest rate shall not constitute fraudulent lending solely because it is less favorable than another lender's rate.

4. Lawful credit-based mortgage pricing shall remain permitted.

5. Borrowers may pursue civil remedies for independently established financial harm.

## §11.11 — Fraudulent Foreclosure

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits fraudulent foreclosure by knowingly using fabricated default records, forged judicial authority, fraudulent ownership claims, or other material deception to seize or transfer real property unlawfully.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent conduct and intended or completed unlawful deprivation.

3. A valid foreclosure conducted under applicable law shall not constitute this offense.

4. An ordinary dispute concerning payment calculation shall not automatically establish criminal fraud.

5. Courts may order appropriate restoration of property rights and restitution.

6. Real estate agents, lenders, and other participants shall be individually responsible only for their proven criminal conduct.

## §11.12 — Real Estate Transaction Fraud

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using material deception to obtain money or property through a purported real estate transaction.

2. Qualifying conduct may involve fictitious listings, forged ownership documentation, knowingly concealed lack of authority to sell, or fraudulent property transfers.

3. The State shall establish the criminal intent and material false representation.

4. An ordinary property valuation disagreement shall not constitute fraud.

5. Licensed real estate representatives shall remain subject to applicable civil and professional obligations.

## §11.13 — Fraudulent Real Estate Representation

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly falsely claiming authority to act as an owner's real estate representative for the purpose of obtaining money, property, or a legally significant benefit.

2. The State shall establish the lack of authority and fraudulent purpose.

3. An honest mistake concerning a listing's status shall not automatically establish criminal conduct.

4. Authorized D8 representatives and other lawful real estate professionals shall remain protected while acting within their authority.

5. Civil remedies may be pursued independently.

## §11.14 — Fraudulent Lease Agreements

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly entering or offering a lease through material deception concerning authority to lease, occupancy rights, or the existence of the property interest being offered.

2. The State shall establish the material fraud and unlawful purpose.

3. Ordinary unpaid rent, contract cancellation, or a good-faith dispute shall not constitute fraudulent leasing.

4. Tenant rights and lawful eviction procedures shall remain governed by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §11.15 — Financial Record Falsification

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, altering, destroying, or submitting materially false financial records for an unlawful purpose.

2. Protected records may include banking transactions, credit records, loan documents, and legally required commercial financial records.

3. The State shall establish the material falsification and criminal intent.

4. Authorized corrections, accounting adjustments, and ordinary processing errors shall not constitute criminal falsification.

5. Government records shall remain subject to the applicable official-record offenses.

## §11.16 — Unauthorized Computer Access

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits unauthorized computer access by knowingly accessing a protected computer, electronic account, or information system without authorization for an unlawful purpose.

2. The State shall establish the protected nature of the system, lack of authorization, and required unlawful purpose.

3. Ordinary use of publicly available websites, terminals, and services shall not constitute unauthorized access.

4. Access permitted by the lawful system owner or authorized administrator shall remain lawful within its granted scope.

5. A technical error, mistaken login, or incidental system response shall not independently establish criminal intent.

6. This offense shall apply regardless of whether access is performed through a conventional computer, mobile device, or another lawful technological interface.

## §11.17 — Unauthorized Access to Financial Systems

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully accessing a protected financial system with intent to obtain confidential information, unlawfully manipulate financial records, or obtain an unauthorized financial benefit.

2. The State shall establish the unauthorized access and required criminal purpose.

3. Lawful banking operations and authorized system maintenance shall not constitute this offense.

4. Ordinary customer access to the customer's own account shall remain protected.

5. Unauthorized transfer of funds may constitute a separate theft offense where independently established.

## §11.18 — Theft of Confidential Digital Information

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully acquiring protected confidential electronic information with intent to deprive its lawful holder of exclusive control or to exploit the information unlawfully.

2. Information may include protected business records, financial credentials, and other designated confidential data.

3. The State shall establish the applicable confidentiality interest, unauthorized acquisition, and criminal purpose.

4. Lawful journalism, authorized disclosure, and protected reporting of official misconduct shall not independently constitute this offense.

5. Protected information shall not become criminal contraband merely because it concerns a government institution or powerful private company.

## §11.19 — Digital Identity Impersonation

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly impersonating another individual or legally recognized entity through an electronic system for the purpose of obtaining an unlawful material benefit or exercising authority they do not possess.

2. The State shall establish the deceptive representation and criminal intent.

3. Ordinary pseudonymous communication shall not independently constitute criminal impersonation.

4. Satire, commentary, and other protected expression shall remain subject to applicable civil rights.

5. Financial identity theft may be prosecuted under the applicable specific provision.

## §11.20 — Digital Payment Manipulation

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits digital payment manipulation by knowingly and unlawfully altering electronic payment instructions, payment confirmations, transaction destinations, or financial processing records with intent to obtain an unauthorized financial benefit.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent alteration and required intent.

3. A failed or delayed legitimate transfer shall not automatically constitute criminal manipulation.

4. Authorized system corrections shall remain lawful.

5. Where the same conduct constitutes a completed unauthorized financial transfer, applicable merger rules shall govern.

## §11.21 — Malicious Interference With a Computer System

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully interfering with a protected computer system with intent to materially impair its availability, integrity, or lawful operation.

2. The State shall establish the intentional interference and material impairment.

3. Ordinary equipment failure, accidental malfunction, or authorized maintenance shall not constitute this offense.

4. Legitimate security testing conducted within granted authorization shall remain lawful.

5. More serious attacks against critical public infrastructure may be prosecuted under applicable sabotage provisions.

## §11.22 — Deployment of Malicious Software

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly introducing or deploying malicious software into a protected system without authorization for the purpose of unlawfully damaging, disabling, accessing, or manipulating that system or its information.

2. The State shall establish the unauthorized deployment and criminal purpose.

3. Ordinary possession of software capable of lawful security testing shall not independently constitute this offense.

4. Authorized research, maintenance, and testing shall remain protected within the scope of authorization.

5. Additional financial or government-record offenses may apply where independently established.

## §11.23 — Digital Extortion

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits digital extortion by knowingly demanding money, property, services, or another benefit through unlawful threats to disclose protected information, damage electronic systems, or interfere with lawful digital operations.

2. The State shall establish the unlawful demand and coercive threat.

3. Legitimate commercial negotiations, lawful debt collection, and protected disclosure of misconduct shall not independently constitute extortion.

4. Unauthorized computer access is not required where the prohibited threat and demand are otherwise established.

## §11.24 — Major Cybercrime Operation

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated unlawful digital operation involving repeated unauthorized system access, fraudulent financial manipulation, or serious interference with protected commercial systems.

2. The State shall establish the coordinated activity, qualifying scale, and individual criminal participation.

3. Mere membership in an online community or possession of technical knowledge shall not establish criminal responsibility.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. A sentence exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §11.25 — Extraordinary Financial System Attack

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally conducting a coordinated unlawful attack against designated financial infrastructure that causes substantial disruption to multiple financial institutions or a critical statewide financial service.

2. The State shall establish the intentional attack, qualifying scope, and material disruption.

3. An isolated disputed transaction or minor unauthorized access incident shall not constitute an extraordinary financial system attack.

4. Ordinary service outages shall not establish criminal sabotage.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.

6. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §11.26 — Financial Crimes by a Bank Employee

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly abusing entrusted banking access to unlawfully obtain funds, manipulate protected financial records, or disclose customer information for a prohibited financial purpose.

2. The State shall establish the entrusted access and intentional unlawful conduct.

3. Ordinary authorized account servicing shall not constitute this offense.

4. Financial institution employees shall not be presumed guilty solely because a customer account was compromised.

5. A more serious applicable financial offense may govern where its elements are established.

## §11.27 — Banking Customer Record Disclosure

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person entrusted with protected banking records commits this offense by knowingly disclosing nonpublic customer financial information without lawful authorization for an unlawful purpose.

2. Disclosure under a valid judicial order or another recognized legal basis shall not constitute this offense.

3. The State shall establish the protected information, unauthorized disclosure, and required intent.

4. Ordinary communication necessary to perform authorized financial services shall remain lawful.

5. Affected customers may seek appropriate civil relief.

## §11.28 — Financial Asset Seizure Through Fraudulent Authority

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using forged judicial authority, fraudulent government credentials, or another materially false official claim to obtain control of financial assets unlawfully.

2. The State shall establish the false authority and unlawful financial acquisition or attempted acquisition.

3. Compliance by a bank with an apparently valid order shall not automatically establish that the bank participated in the fraud.

4. Disputed records and orders shall remain subject to judicial verification.

5. Appropriate restitution and asset recovery may be ordered.

## §11.29 — Unauthorized Manipulation of Credit Records

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully altering another individual's credit records or creditworthiness information to obtain an unauthorized financial benefit or unlawfully harm that individual's financial interests.

2. The State shall establish the unauthorized alteration and criminal purpose.

3. Legitimate corrections to inaccurate credit records shall remain lawful.

4. A change in creditworthiness resulting from actual financial activity shall not constitute criminal manipulation.

5. Individuals may seek correction of fraudulent records.

## §11.30 — Financial Crime Investigative and Property Protections

1. Banking records, financial accounts, credit information, and private commercial records shall remain protected under the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

2. Investigations shall comply with lawful search, seizure, and judicial order requirements.

3. An allegation of financial crime shall not independently authorize freezing every account owned by an individual.

4. Account restrictions and asset seizures shall require appropriate legal authority.

5. Permanent forfeiture shall require applicable legal procedures.

6. Innocent owners and third parties shall retain their lawful property interests.

7. A financial institution's compliance with a valid judicial order shall not independently constitute criminal wrongdoing.

8. Officials shall not personally appropriate seized funds or financial property.

9. Ordinary financial disputes shall remain civil matters unless criminal elements are independently established.
