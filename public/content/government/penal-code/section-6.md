# SECTION 6 — PUBLIC ORDER & GOVERNMENT OFFENSES

## §6.1 — Disorderly Conduct

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits disorderly conduct by intentionally engaging in unlawful disruptive behavior that materially interferes with public order or creates a substantial public disturbance.
2. Mere criticism, offensive language, or lawful public disagreement shall not independently constitute disorderly conduct.
3. The State shall establish the specific prohibited conduct.
4. Lawful assemblies and peaceful demonstrations shall remain protected.

## §6.2 — Disturbing the Peace

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $100–$750  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating a substantial and unlawful disturbance contrary to an applicable public order restriction.
2. Ordinary conversation, lawful business activity, and protected expression shall not independently constitute the offense.
3. Applicable time, place, and conduct requirements shall be established by law.
4. An officer shall not impose a citation solely because a person expresses disagreement with government action.

## §6.3 — Public Intoxication Creating a Safety Danger

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by being unlawfully intoxicated in a public place while engaging in conduct creating a substantial and identifiable danger to themselves or others, where prohibited by law.
2. Mere intoxication without the prohibited conduct shall not independently establish this offense.
3. Officers shall consider apparent medical emergencies and seek appropriate assistance where necessary.
4. Lawful consumption shall not automatically constitute criminal conduct.

## §6.4 — Unlawful Assembly

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits unlawful assembly by knowingly participating in a gathering formed with a shared intention to engage in imminent unlawful violence or other expressly prohibited collective criminal conduct.
2. Mere attendance at a peaceful gathering shall not constitute unlawful assembly.
3. Political protest and lawful demonstrations shall remain protected.
4. The State shall establish the required unlawful collective purpose and individual participation.
5. An officer shall not designate a gathering unlawful solely because its participants criticize public officials.

## §6.5 — Riot

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits riot by knowingly participating in collective unlawful violence that creates substantial danger to persons or property.
2. The State shall prove the accused's participation in the prohibited violent conduct.
3. Mere presence near a riot shall not establish criminal participation.
4. Peaceful demonstrators shall not automatically be held responsible for independent criminal acts committed by others.

## §6.6 — Inciting a Riot

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally urging or directing others to engage in imminent unlawful collective violence under circumstances where such violence is likely to occur.
2. Advocacy, criticism, or general political expression shall not independently establish incitement.
3. The State shall establish the specific intent, qualifying communication, and relevant likelihood of imminent violence.
4. Mere association with demonstrators shall not establish guilt.

## §6.7 — Obstruction of Justice

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits obstruction of justice by knowingly and unlawfully interfering with a specific authorized criminal investigation, prosecution, or judicial proceeding.
2. Obstruction may involve material interference with evidence, witnesses, or lawful investigative activity.
3. Mere silence, refusal to consent to a search, or request for counsel shall not constitute obstruction.
4. Verbal disagreement with an officer shall not independently constitute obstruction.
5. The State shall prove intentional unlawful interference.

## §6.8 — Failure to Comply With a Lawful Order

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly refusing a lawful, reasonably clear, and practicable order issued by an authorized officer.
2. The State shall establish the issuing officer's authority and the order's legality.
3. An order shall not be lawful merely because it was issued by a police officer.
4. An individual shall not be criminally punished for failure to perform an impossible or materially unclear command.
5. Protected silence and lawful refusal to consent shall not constitute this offense.

## §6.9 — Resisting Arrest

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits resisting arrest by knowingly and physically interfering with an officer's lawful attempt to take that person into custody.
2. The State shall establish the lawfulness of the arrest and intentional physical resistance.
3. Mere verbal disagreement or refusal to answer questions shall not constitute resisting arrest.
4. Additional assault charges may apply where independently established.
5. Officers shall remain subject to applicable use-of-force restrictions.

## §6.10 — Violent Resistance to Arrest

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits violent resistance by intentionally using unlawful physical violence against an officer during a lawful arrest.
2. The State shall establish the lawful arrest and the qualifying violent conduct.
3. Mere flight without violence shall not constitute violent resistance.
4. An officer's use of unlawful force shall remain independently reviewable.
5. Duplicative punishment for identical conduct shall remain subject to the applicable merger rules.

## §6.11 — Interference With a Law Enforcement Operation

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and materially interfering with an authorized law enforcement operation through unlawful conduct.
2. Lawful observation, recording, criticism, and requests for identifying information shall not independently constitute interference.
3. The State shall establish the unlawful act and its material effect.
4. Authorized emergency or law enforcement activity shall not exempt officers from civil rights protections.

## §6.12 — Impersonating a Law Enforcement Officer

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and falsely representing themselves as a sworn law enforcement officer for the purpose of exercising apparent police authority or obtaining an unlawful benefit.
2. Relevant conduct may include use of counterfeit identification, false official commands, or fraudulent claims of arrest authority.
3. Mere ownership of clothing resembling a uniform shall not independently establish criminal impersonation.
4. The State shall prove intentional misrepresentation and the required unlawful purpose.

## §6.13 — Impersonating a Government Official

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and falsely claiming to hold a government office or official authority for the purpose of obtaining an unlawful benefit or exercising unauthorized power.
2. The offense may involve false representations of judicial, prosecutorial, administrative, or other governmental status.
3. Satire, performance, and statements lacking the required fraudulent purpose shall not independently constitute this offense.
4. The State shall establish the intentional unlawful misrepresentation.

## §6.14 — Bribery of a Public Official

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits bribery by knowingly offering, promising, or providing an improper benefit to a public official with intent to influence an official act unlawfully.
2. An improper benefit may include money, property, services, or other material consideration.
3. Lawful administrative fees or legitimate contractual payments shall not constitute bribery.
4. The State shall establish the corrupt purpose and qualifying offer or transfer.
5. Acceptance of the benefit shall not be necessary where the prohibited offer is otherwise established.

## §6.15 — Acceptance of a Bribe

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A public official commits this offense by knowingly requesting, accepting, or agreeing to receive an improper benefit in exchange for unlawful influence over an official act.
2. The State shall prove the corrupt agreement or understanding.
3. Ordinary authorized compensation shall not constitute bribery.
4. Public employment shall not exempt an individual from criminal liability.
5. Separate official misconduct charges may apply where independently established.

## §6.16 — Official Misconduct

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A public official commits official misconduct by knowingly exercising official authority in a manner expressly prohibited by law for an unlawful personal, financial, retaliatory, or other corrupt purpose.
2. The State shall establish the relevant duty, unlawful act, and required criminal intent.
3. An ordinary good-faith mistake shall not automatically constitute criminal misconduct.
4. Administrative discipline may occur separately under applicable professional procedures.
5. Criminal charges shall remain subject to ordinary evidentiary standards.

## §6.17 — Abuse of Official Authority

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A public official commits this offense by knowingly using government authority to unlawfully deprive another individual of liberty, property, or protected rights for a prohibited purpose.
2. Conduct may include knowingly unlawful detention, extortion through office, or intentional misuse of judicial or administrative powers.
3. The State shall establish the official's authority, unlawful conduct, and required intent.
4. An official shall not be immune from prosecution solely because the act occurred during employment.
5. Separate offenses may be charged where independently established.

## §6.18 — Extortion Under Color of Law

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A public official commits this offense by knowingly demanding or obtaining money, property, services, or another benefit through misuse or threatened misuse of official authority.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful demand and coercive use of apparent government power.
3. Lawful collection of authorized fees shall not constitute extortion.
4. The official's departmental rank shall not limit criminal accountability.

## §6.19 — Evidence Tampering

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits evidence tampering by knowingly destroying, concealing, altering, substituting, or falsifying material evidence with intent to impair an actual or reasonably anticipated investigation or judicial proceeding.
2. The State shall establish the relevant proceeding or investigation and required corrupt intent.
3. Ordinary lawful disposal of property without the prohibited intent shall not constitute evidence tampering.
4. Public officials and private individuals may be subject to this offense.
5. Alteration of judicial records may qualify for additional charges where independently established.

## §6.20 — Witness Intimidation

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits witness intimidation by knowingly threatening unlawful harm or retaliation against an individual with intent to influence, prevent, or alter their participation in a criminal investigation or judicial proceeding.
2. The State shall establish the intended interference.
3. Lawful communication with a witness shall not independently constitute intimidation.
4. Mere criticism of testimony shall not establish the offense without prohibited threatening or coercive conduct.

## §6.21 — Perjury

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits perjury by knowingly making a materially false statement under oath or another legally recognized sworn declaration in an official proceeding.
2. The State shall establish the statement's materiality, falsity, and required knowledge.
3. An honest mistake or immaterial inaccuracy shall not constitute perjury.
4. A statement made outside a proceeding requiring sworn truthfulness shall not automatically constitute perjury.
5. A defendant's lawful silence shall not constitute perjury.

## §6.22 — Filing a False Police Report

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly submitting materially false information to law enforcement concerning an alleged criminal offense with intent to mislead the investigation.
2. A good-faith report later found to be mistaken shall not constitute a false report.
3. The State shall prove knowing material falsity and the required intent.
4. A person shall not be penalized merely because an investigation fails to substantiate their report.

## §6.23 — False Emergency Report

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly making a materially false report of an emergency with intent to cause an unnecessary emergency response.
2. The State shall establish the falsity and required intent.
3. A mistaken emergency report submitted in good faith shall not constitute this offense.
4. An aggravated offense may apply where the false report intentionally creates a substantial danger to life.

## §6.24 — Misuse of Emergency Communications

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 0–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and repeatedly using emergency communication services for a prohibited purpose that materially interferes with legitimate emergency operations.
2. Mere frustration, accidental dialing, or an unsuccessful request for assistance shall not independently establish the offense.
3. The State shall establish intentional prohibited misuse.
4. Emergency personnel shall not deny assistance as punishment for a prior lawful complaint.

## §6.25 — Escape From Lawful Custody

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits escape from lawful custody by knowingly and unlawfully departing or attempting to depart from valid custodial restraint imposed under legal authority.
2. The State shall establish the lawful custody and intentional escape.
3. An individual shall not be convicted under this provision solely for leaving a voluntary police encounter.
4. Escape shall not invalidate an underlying lawful sentence.
5. Any additional fixed-term punishment shall remain subject to the applicable statutory maximum.

## §6.26 — Escape From a Correctional Facility

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly escaping from a correctional facility while under a lawful commitment or remand order.
2. The State shall establish the existence of a valid custody authorization.
3. Escape shall not automatically extinguish the original sentence.
4. Escape shall not independently authorize life imprisonment or capital punishment.
5. A disputed or altered commitment record shall remain subject to judicial review.
6. Any new sentence shall comply with the seven-day fixed-term maximum where applicable.

## §6.27 — Assisting an Escape

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and intentionally assisting another individual to escape from lawful correctional custody.
2. Assistance may involve unauthorized transport, equipment, access, or other material aid.
3. The State shall establish knowledge of the lawful custody and intentional assistance.
4. Mere personal association with an escaped prisoner shall not constitute assistance.
5. Providing ordinary lawful legal representation shall not constitute this offense.

## §6.28 — Armed Correctional Escape

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits armed correctional escape when, during a knowing escape from lawful correctional custody, they intentionally use or threaten immediate use of a deadly weapon.
2. The State shall establish the underlying escape and qualifying weapon-related conduct.
3. Mere possession of an object without the required threatening or violent use shall not automatically establish this offense.
4. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
6. The offense shall not automatically qualify an individual for capital punishment.

## §6.29 — Correctional Riot

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits correctional riot by knowingly participating in coordinated unlawful violence within a correctional facility that creates substantial danger to inmates, staff, or other persons.
2. The State shall establish the defendant's individual participation.
3. Peaceful complaints, authorized petitions, or ordinary refusal to participate in optional institutional activities shall not constitute correctional riot.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require human judicial authorization.

## §6.30 — Introduction of Contraband Into a Correctional Facility

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly introducing or attempting to introduce an item expressly prohibited within a correctional facility without lawful authorization.
2. The State shall establish the facility restriction and the accused's relevant knowledge.
3. Ordinary lawful deliveries, medical supplies, and authorized professional equipment shall not constitute contraband.
4. More serious conduct involving prohibited weapons may support separate charges where independently established.

## §6.31 — Falsification of Government Records

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, altering, deleting, or submitting materially false official government records for an unlawful purpose.
2. Records may include licenses, judicial documents, government identification, property records, and correctional records.
3. The State shall establish the material falsification and required intent.
4. An authorized administrative correction shall not constitute falsification.
5. The mere existence of an inaccurate record shall not establish that its subject committed the offense.

## §6.32 — Judicial Record Manipulation

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits judicial record manipulation by knowingly and unlawfully altering, deleting, forging, or falsifying criminal convictions, warrants, custody commitments, release orders, or other recognized judicial records with intent to change their official legal effect.
2. The State shall establish unauthorized manipulation and the required criminal purpose.
3. The offense may apply to public officials, private contractors, and unauthorized individuals.
4. A person shall not be presumed responsible solely because their own criminal record was altered.
5. A successful unauthorized alteration may cause an official record to appear legally resolved until the irregularity is established through appropriate review.
6. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
7. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

## §6.33 — Unauthorized Interference With the Judicial Intelligence Service

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly gaining unauthorized access to or unlawfully interfering with the State's Judicial Intelligence Service for the purpose of corrupting judicial operations, modifying protected judicial records, or unlawfully influencing an official determination.
2. The State shall establish unauthorized access or interference and the required unlawful purpose.
3. Legitimate authorized maintenance, approved testing, and ordinary use of public judicial services shall not constitute this offense.
4. The offense shall not be established solely because an individual challenges an automated judicial decision.
5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.
6. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §6.34 — Judicial System Sabotage

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits judicial system sabotage by intentionally conducting a substantial unlawful attack against the State's judicial infrastructure that materially disables court operations, corrupts significant official judicial records, or otherwise creates widespread interference with the administration of justice.
2. The State shall prove the intentional conduct and qualifying substantial disruption.
3. A minor unauthorized access attempt shall not automatically establish this offense.
4. Ordinary system malfunction shall not establish criminal sabotage.
5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.
6. Sentencing shall require an authorized human judicial determination.

## §6.35 — Corrupt Interference With Judicial Proceedings

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully attempting to influence a judicial proceeding through prohibited threats, bribery, evidence manipulation, or other corrupt conduct.
2. The State shall establish the intentional prohibited interference.
3. Lawful advocacy, public criticism, and ordinary legal representation shall not constitute this offense.
4. Public officials and private individuals may be prosecuted where the elements are established.

## §6.36 — Forgery of a Judicial Order

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, presenting, or using a forged judicial order with intent to obtain an unlawful official or legal result.
2. Orders may include arrest warrants, search warrants, remand orders, release orders, and other recognized judicial directives.
3. The State shall establish the forgery and required criminal intent.
4. An authorized correction of a genuine order shall not constitute forgery.
5. Any custody action based on a disputed order shall remain subject to applicable judicial review.

## §6.37 — Contempt of Court

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 0–40 minutes

1. A person commits criminal contempt by knowingly and willfully disobeying a valid judicial order or intentionally engaging in substantial unlawful disruption of judicial proceedings.
2. Contempt shall be determined through an appropriate judicial process.
3. Lawful criticism of a judge or judicial decision shall not independently constitute contempt.
4. An individual shall receive an appropriate opportunity to respond to the allegation, subject to lawful immediate courtroom safety procedures.
5. Contempt imprisonment shall remain subject to the statutory sentencing limits.

## §6.38 — Violation of a Judicial Order

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly violating a valid and enforceable judicial order imposing a legal obligation upon them.
2. The State shall establish the order's validity, applicable notice, and prohibited conduct.
3. An expired or invalid order shall not support conviction.
4. Specific violations governed by a separate offense may be prosecuted under that provision where appropriate.

## §6.39 — Retaliation Against a Public Witness or Complainant

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly threatening or committing unlawful harm against an individual because that person reported a suspected offense, provided testimony, or lawfully participated in an official proceeding.
2. The State shall establish retaliatory intent and qualifying prohibited conduct.
3. Lawful criticism, disagreement, or independent civil action shall not automatically constitute retaliation.
4. An official who abuses their office to retaliate may be charged under the applicable official misconduct provisions.

## §6.40 — Government Contract Corruption

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits government contract corruption by knowingly engaging in a materially fraudulent or corrupt arrangement intended to unlawfully influence the award, performance, or administration of a government contract.
2. Conduct may involve bribery, material fraud, intentional falsification of procurement records, or other prohibited corruption.
3. A private company shall not be criminally responsible solely because it received a lawful government contract.
4. The State shall establish the required fraudulent or corrupt conduct.
5. Relevant corporate and individual responsibility shall be determined according to applicable law.

## §6.41 — Misappropriation of Government Property

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully converting government property or funds entrusted to their custody for unauthorized personal or other unlawful purposes.
2. The State shall establish the entrusted interest, unauthorized conversion, and required intent.
3. Ordinary administrative mistakes shall not independently establish criminal misappropriation.
4. Restitution and appropriate employment sanctions may apply separately.

## §6.42 — Obstruction of Emergency Services

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and materially obstructing authorized police, fire, medical, or rescue personnel during an active emergency in a manner creating a substantial risk of harm or significant interference with emergency operations.
2. The State shall establish intentional prohibited obstruction.
3. Mere observation, filming, or noninterfering presence shall not automatically constitute this offense.
4. Separate acts of violence may support additional charges where independently established.
