# SECTION 16 — CORRECTIONAL INSTITUTIONS, PRISONER CONDUCT & CUSTODIAL OFFENSES

## §16.1 — Correctional Authority and Lawful Custody

1. The Department of Corrections shall administer lawful custodial sentences and remand orders within its delegated authority.
2. A correctional institution shall not independently create new criminal penalties.
3. A valid judicial or otherwise lawfully authorized custody record shall establish the basis for confinement.
4. Detention and imprisonment shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.
5. A correctional officer shall not extend a sentence beyond its lawful expiration.
6. Prisoners shall retain applicable legal rights during confinement.

## §16.2 — Unlawful Detention by a Correctional Officer

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A correctional officer commits this offense by knowingly continuing to confine a person without valid legal authority and with awareness that no lawful custody basis remains.
2. The State shall establish the officer's relevant knowledge and intentional unlawful detention.
3. A good-faith temporary verification of a materially disputed custody record shall not automatically constitute criminal detention, provided applicable review requirements are followed.
4. A mere unsupported suspicion that a release record was unlawfully altered shall not authorize indefinite confinement.
5. Additional abuse-of-authority offenses may apply where independently established.

## §16.3 — Aggravated Unlawful Detention

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. Aggravated unlawful detention occurs when a correctional official knowingly confines a person without legal authority while intentionally employing serious unlawful coercion, retaliatory confinement, or another expressly designated aggravating circumstance.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful confinement and applicable aggravating facts.
3. Ordinary lawful administrative segregation shall not independently constitute aggravated detention.
4. A court may order appropriate release and other remedies.
5. The same conduct shall not receive duplicative punishment under another unlawful-custody offense.

## §16.4 — Correctional Abuse of Authority

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A correctional official commits this offense by knowingly misusing their custodial authority to unlawfully punish, threaten, extort, or deprive a prisoner of a protected legal right.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful conduct and criminal intent.
3. Legitimate discipline imposed under valid correctional procedures shall not constitute abuse of authority.
4. An officer's employment shall not exempt them from applicable criminal law.
5. Administrative discipline may proceed separately.

## §16.5 — Assault Within a Correctional Facility

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally committing unlawful assault against another person within a recognized correctional facility under circumstances involving an independently established threat of immediate harm.
2. The State shall establish the underlying assault and relevant custodial setting.
3. Mere verbal disagreement shall not constitute assault.
4. Lawful defense against imminent unlawful violence shall remain subject to applicable protections.
5. Where the same act is punishable under Section 2, merger rules shall apply.

## §16.6 — Battery Within a Correctional Facility

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally inflicting unlawful physical contact upon another person within a correctional facility.
2. The State shall establish the underlying battery and custodial setting.
3. Ordinary accidental physical contact shall not constitute criminal battery.
4. Serious injury may support aggravated battery where independently established.
5. Lawful and proportionate defensive conduct shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.

## §16.7 — Aggravated Assault on a Correctional Officer

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly committing aggravated assault against an identifiable correctional officer performing lawful custodial duties.
2. The State shall establish the underlying aggravated assault and relevant official status.
3. Mere refusal to answer questions or peaceful criticism shall not constitute assault.
4. A correctional officer's use of unlawful force shall remain independently reviewable.
5. Applicable merger rules shall prevent duplicative punishment of identical assault conduct.

## §16.8 — Assault by a Correctional Officer

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A correctional officer commits this offense by knowingly using unlawful physical violence against a prisoner while acting through or abusing official custodial authority.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful violence and required intent.
3. Lawful and proportionate force necessary for immediate safety or authorized custody control shall remain subject to existing use-of-force law.
4. Prisoner misconduct shall not automatically justify excessive force.
5. Serious injury or other independently established offenses may support additional charges.

## §16.9 — Unlawful Prisoner Confinement by Another Prisoner

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A prisoner commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully restraining or confining another person within a correctional facility through force or credible threats.
2. The State shall establish the unauthorized restraint and required criminal intent.
3. Ordinary lawful correctional restraints administered by authorized staff shall not constitute this offense.
4. Kidnapping or other serious offenses may apply where independently established.
5. Community conduct restrictions shall remain controlling.

## §16.10 — Correctional Hostage-Taking

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits correctional hostage-taking by knowingly and unlawfully confining an identifiable person within or in connection with a correctional facility through force or an immediate threat of force for the purpose of compelling another person or institution to act or refrain from acting.
2. The State shall establish the coercive confinement and required purpose.
3. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
4. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
5. Independently established serious injury or death may support additional qualifying charges.
6. This offense shall not authorize community-prohibited roleplay.

## §16.11 — Possession of Correctional Contraband

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing an item expressly designated as prohibited within a correctional institution without lawful authorization.
2. The State shall establish the legally effective contraband designation.
3. Ordinary personal property or authorized professional equipment shall not be classified as contraband solely through an officer's preference.
4. Authorized medical supplies and professional materials shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.
5. Administrative confiscation shall remain subject to lawful institutional procedures.

## §16.12 — Possession of a Weapon Within a Correctional Facility

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a weapon expressly prohibited within a correctional facility without lawful authorization.
2. The State shall establish the item's prohibited status and knowing possession.
3. Authorized correctional and other law enforcement personnel shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.
4. Ordinary authorized work tools shall not automatically be treated as prohibited weapons.
5. Weapons may be seized and preserved as evidence according to lawful procedures.

## §16.13 — Manufacture of a Correctional Weapon

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly manufacturing or materially modifying an object into a prohibited weapon within a correctional facility for an unlawful purpose.
2. The State shall establish the weapon's prohibited classification and intentional manufacturing conduct.
3. Ordinary approved craft or maintenance work shall not constitute this offense.
4. Mere possession of authorized work materials shall not independently establish criminal intent.
5. Serious weapon-related violence may support other applicable charges.

## §16.14 — Correctional Contraband Distribution

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly distributing prohibited correctional contraband within or into a correctional facility without lawful authorization.
2. The State shall establish the prohibited item and intentional distribution.
3. Authorized medical, legal, administrative, and maintenance deliveries shall remain protected.
4. The same conduct shall not receive duplicative punishment under Penal Code §6.30.
5. Additional offenses may apply to separately established weapons or controlled-substance trafficking.

## §16.15 — Correctional Smuggling Operation

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated unlawful operation introducing or distributing restricted weapons, controlled substances, or other designated serious contraband within a correctional facility.
2. The State shall establish the coordinated operation and defendant's individual participation.
3. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
4. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
5. Ordinary authorized supply deliveries shall not constitute criminal smuggling.

## §16.16 — Damage to Correctional Property

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and unlawfully causing substantial damage to a recognized correctional facility or its protected equipment.
2. The State shall establish the damage and required criminal intent.
3. Ordinary wear, accidental breakage, or minor authorized modification shall not constitute the offense.
4. Critical infrastructure sabotage may qualify under another provision where independently established.
5. Restitution may be ordered for established loss.

## §16.17 — Tampering With Correctional Security Systems

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully interfering with a correctional access, surveillance, communication, or other designated security system for the purpose of impairing its lawful operation.
2. The State shall establish unauthorized interference and criminal purpose.
3. Authorized repairs, testing, and system maintenance shall remain lawful.
4. Ordinary system failure shall not establish sabotage.
5. Digital record manipulation may qualify for a separate offense where independently established.

## §16.18 — Unauthorized Correctional Facility Access

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly entering or remaining within a legally designated secure correctional area without authorization.
2. The State shall establish the applicable restricted designation and knowing violation.
3. Publicly accessible reception areas shall remain distinguishable from secure operational areas.
4. An individual who inadvertently enters a restricted area and promptly complies with lawful instructions shall not automatically be criminally liable.
5. Fraudulent credentials may support separate charges where independently established.

## §16.19 — Correctional Escape Planning

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense when, intending to escape lawful correctional custody, they take a substantial step toward executing an unlawful escape.
2. Mere discussion, private thoughts, or unacted-upon intentions shall not constitute this offense.
3. The State shall establish the required intent and substantial step.
4. A completed escape shall remain subject to Penal Code §6.26.
5. Where the conduct constitutes the same punishable act as an attempted escape under §1.5, merger rules shall apply.

## §16.20 — Correctional Escape Assistance

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly providing material assistance to another individual's escape from lawful correctional custody.
2. The State shall establish the valid custody basis, knowledge, and intentional assistance.
3. Ordinary legal representation shall not constitute escape assistance.
4. Mere association with an incarcerated or escaped individual shall not establish guilt.
5. The same conduct shall not receive duplicate punishment under Penal Code §6.27.

## §16.21 — Organized Correctional Escape

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated unlawful escape operation involving multiple persons or substantial intentional interference with correctional security.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying coordination and individual participation.
3. Mere simultaneous escape by several individuals shall not automatically establish organized planning.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
6. Escape shall not independently create capital punishment eligibility.

## §16.22 — Armed Correctional Escape Operation

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated correctional escape involving the intentional use or threatened immediate use of deadly weapons and a substantial danger to multiple persons.
2. The State shall establish the coordinated escape and qualifying weapon-related danger.
3. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.
4. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.
5. The offense shall not independently authorize life imprisonment or capital punishment.
6. Any separately committed qualifying homicide shall remain subject to its own statutory provisions.

## §16.23 — Correctional Riot Leadership

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or coordinating unlawful collective violence within a correctional institution under circumstances satisfying the elements of a correctional riot.
2. The State shall establish the defendant's actual leadership of the violent conduct.
3. Peaceful petitions, complaints, and nonviolent protest shall not constitute riot leadership.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
6. Identical participation charged under Penal Code §6.29 shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §16.24 — Correctional Extortion

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly demanding money, property, services, or another benefit through unlawful threats or coercion involving a correctional institution or its occupants.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful demand and coercive conduct.
3. Legitimate administrative fees and lawful commercial arrangements shall not constitute extortion.
4. Correctional officers and prisoners may be charged according to their individual proven conduct.
5. Additional official misconduct charges may apply where independently established.

## §16.25 — Correctional Bribery

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits correctional bribery by knowingly offering, requesting, accepting, or agreeing to provide an improper benefit to influence an official correctional act unlawfully.
2. The State shall establish the corrupt purpose and relevant transaction or agreement.
3. Lawful authorized compensation shall not constitute bribery.
4. Correctional employees shall remain subject to official misconduct provisions.
5. The same corrupt transaction shall not receive duplicative punishment under §§6.14–6.15.

## §16.26 — Unauthorized Release of a Prisoner

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully causing or directing the release of an individual from valid correctional custody without proper authority.
2. The State shall establish the valid custody basis, unauthorized release, and required criminal knowledge.
3. Release pursuant to an apparently valid and authenticated official order shall not automatically establish criminal misconduct by the executing employee.
4. An employee who knowingly forges or corrupts a release order may be charged under the applicable judicial record provisions.
5. A prisoner shall not be presumed complicit solely because officials released them.

## §16.27 — Falsification of Correctional Sentence Records

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully altering or falsifying correctional commitment, sentence completion, parole, custody, or release records with intent to change their official legal effect.
2. The State shall establish unauthorized manipulation and criminal intent.
3. Authorized record corrections shall remain lawful.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
6. A record's subject shall not be presumed responsible merely because the record was unlawfully altered.
7. Identical conduct under Penal Code §6.32 or §13.26 shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §16.28 — Correctional Financial Misappropriation

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A correctional employee commits this offense by knowingly converting funds or property entrusted to the institution for unauthorized personal or other unlawful use.
2. The State shall establish the entrusted property interest and intentional conversion.
3. Ordinary accounting errors shall not constitute criminal misappropriation.
4. Restitution and authorized professional discipline may apply separately.
5. Applicable merger rules shall prevent duplicate punishment under §6.41 for the same conversion.

## §16.29 — Interference With Prisoner Legal Access

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully obstructing an incarcerated individual's access to legally authorized counsel or required judicial proceedings for a prohibited purpose.
2. The State shall establish the legal access right and intentional unlawful interference.
3. Reasonable security procedures shall remain lawful when properly authorized and not used as a pretext for denial of rights.
4. Correctional personnel shall not punish prisoners solely for requesting legal representation or filing lawful complaints.
5. Material violations may support additional official misconduct proceedings.

## §16.30 — Retaliation Against a Prisoner for Legal Complaints

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A correctional official commits this offense by knowingly taking unlawful punitive action against a prisoner because that prisoner exercised a protected right to legal counsel, judicial review, or lawful complaint.
2. The State shall establish retaliatory intent and prohibited official conduct.
3. Legitimate discipline for an independently established institutional violation shall not constitute retaliation.
4. Administrative review and judicial remedies may be available.
5. Correctional authority shall not exempt officials from criminal accountability.

## §16.31 — Falsification of Prisoner Disciplinary Records

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A correctional employee commits this offense by knowingly creating or materially altering a prison disciplinary record containing false information for an unlawful purpose.
2. The State shall establish material falsity and criminal intent.
3. Ordinary administrative corrections shall remain lawful.
4. A prisoner may challenge an inaccurate disciplinary record through authorized procedures.
5. A fraudulent disciplinary record shall not independently authorize extension of a judicial sentence.

## §16.32 — Unlawful Extension of a Prison Sentence

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully extending or causing the extension of a prisoner's fixed-term sentence beyond its legally authorized expiration.
2. The State shall establish the lawful sentence, unauthorized extension, and required knowledge.
3. A legitimate separate judicial sentence shall remain subject to applicable sentencing and maximum-duration requirements.
4. Administrative delays shall not independently authorize continued detention.
5. The court may order immediate correction and release where appropriate.
6. Separate unlawful detention charges may apply where independent conduct is established.

## §16.33 — Unlawful Interference With Sentence Completion

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully manipulating or disabling a legally required sentence-completion process for the purpose of preventing an otherwise authorized release.
2. The State shall establish the intended unlawful interference.
3. Ordinary system outages or good-faith administrative errors shall not establish criminal intent.
4. Fixed-term sentence expiration shall not depend on the availability of correctional employees.
5. An individual shall retain access to appropriate judicial review of disputed sentence completion.

## §16.34 — Correctional Life Sentence Administration

1. A valid life sentence shall remain distinct from fixed-term imprisonment.
2. Life imprisonment shall not expire automatically after seven real-world days.
3. Life-sentence prisoners shall remain subject to applicable parole, clemency, judicial review, and lawful correctional programs.
4. The Department of Corrections shall not independently convert a fixed-term sentence into life imprisonment.
5. A life sentence may be imposed only through authorized human judicial proceedings.
6. Escape from life imprisonment shall not independently authorize capital punishment.
7. Subsequent qualifying life-eligible offenses shall remain subject to the separate capital sentencing requirements established by law.

## §16.35 — Death Row Custody and Capital Sentence Administration

1. Death row confinement shall require a valid capital sentence imposed through authorized human judicial proceedings.
2. No correctional official shall independently designate a prisoner for execution.
3. The Judicial Intelligence Service shall not independently impose capital punishment.
4. Executions shall require a valid human judicial warrant and satisfaction of applicable appeals, stays, and clemency requirements.
5. A valid stay shall suspend the affected execution according to its terms.
6. Correctional staff shall not proceed with an execution contrary to a controlling judicial order.
7. Execution shall constitute permanent character death under the applicable legal and community procedures.
8. Community conduct restrictions governing permanent character death shall remain independently binding.

## §16.36 — Unauthorized Execution or Deliberate Custodial Killing

**Classification:** Capital Felony  
**Fine:** Up to $75,000  
**Sentence:** 6 hours to 7 real-world days, or life imprisonment following authorized human judicial determination

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and unlawfully killing a person held in state custody through a purported execution lacking valid judicial authority or through another deliberate custodial killing satisfying the elements of intentional homicide.
2. The State shall establish the intentional unlawful killing, causation, and absence of lawful justification.
3. An ordinary custodial accident shall not constitute this offense.
4. Lawful force employed in a genuinely necessary defensive situation shall be evaluated according to applicable use-of-force law.
5. Life imprisonment shall require authorized human judicial proceedings.
6. Capital punishment shall remain subject to the exceptional eligibility and procedural safeguards established by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.
7. Nothing in this provision authorizes community-prohibited conduct or roleplay.

## §16.37 — Judicial Review of Correctional Custody

1. A prisoner may seek lawful review of a disputed commitment, sentence, custody classification, or release record.
2. The Department of Corrections shall comply with valid judicial orders.
3. Material record-integrity disputes may be submitted to an authorized court.
4. An unsupported allegation of database manipulation shall not independently justify indefinite detention.
5. Officially authenticated records may be relied upon within their lawful scope until an appropriate authority determines otherwise.
6. Unauthorized alteration of records shall remain subject to criminal investigation.
7. Prisoners shall retain applicable access to legal representation.

## §16.38 — Fixed-Term Sentencing Maximum and Release

1. No fixed-term imprisonment imposed at one sentencing event shall exceed seven real-world days, equivalent to 168 elapsed hours.
2. This maximum shall apply to the combined fixed-term imprisonment imposed for convictions adjudicated in that sentencing event.
3. Separate charges shall not be artificially divided into proceedings to evade the statutory maximum.
4. Fixed-term sentence time shall advance according to actual elapsed time, including periods when the prisoner is not present within the correctional facility or otherwise connected to state services, subject to any expressly applicable lawful interruption.
5. Sentence completion shall not depend upon the availability of correctional staff.
6. Once lawful fixed-term custody has expired, the individual shall be released unless an independent valid custody order authorizes continued detention.
7. The Department of Corrections shall not impose additional imprisonment merely through institutional discipline.
8. Life imprisonment shall remain subject to its separate statutory rules.

## §16.39 — Correctional Rehabilitation and Programs

1. The Department of Corrections may establish lawful educational, vocational, rehabilitative, and other correctional programs.
2. Participation requirements shall remain subject to applicable law.
3. A prisoner shall not be deprived of fundamental civil rights solely for declining an optional program.
4. Lawfully authorized sentence credits or parole considerations may recognize program participation.
5. Programs shall not arbitrarily extend a fixed-term sentence.
6. Correctional programs may support reintegration and lawful employment following release.
7. Program administration shall remain subject to appropriate correctional oversight.

## §16.40 — Correctional Enforcement Accountability

1. Correctional officials shall remain accountable for actions undertaken through their authority.
2. No officer shall knowingly fabricate prisoner misconduct or sentence records.
3. No officer shall unlawfully confiscate prisoner property for personal benefit.
4. No officer shall intentionally deny required legal access or continue confinement without lawful authority.
5. Allegations of misconduct may be referred for administrative or criminal investigation.
6. Prisoners shall retain applicable complaint and judicial review rights.
7. The Department of Justice may review unlawful custodial actions.
8. Nothing in this section shall authorize conduct prohibited by Rosefire's community conduct rules.
