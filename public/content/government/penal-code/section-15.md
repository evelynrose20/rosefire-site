# ROSEFIRE PENAL CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.8 — Sections 15–16**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing criminal offenses concerning emergency services, public safety, hazardous materials, environmental protection, correctional institutions, prisoner conduct, unlawful custody, correctional corruption, and the administration of lawful sentences throughout the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–14 remain enacted and in force. Sections 15–16 constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Penal Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

---

# SECTION 15 — PUBLIC SAFETY, EMERGENCY SERVICES & ENVIRONMENTAL OFFENSES

## §15.1 — Interference With Firefighting Operations

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully materially interfering with authorized firefighters performing an active firefighting operation.
2. Interference may include obstructing access to an active fire scene, unlawfully disabling equipment, or intentionally preventing necessary firefighting activity.
3. Mere observation, lawful recording, or noninterfering presence shall not constitute this offense.
4. The State shall establish the prohibited interference and required criminal intent.
5. Serious independently established violence or sabotage may be prosecuted under the applicable greater offense.
6. Duplicative punishment for the same conduct shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §15.2 — Interference With Emergency Medical Response

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and unlawfully obstructing authorized emergency medical personnel performing necessary emergency treatment or rescue duties.
2. The interference must materially impede the lawful emergency activity.
3. Protected speech, reasonable questioning, and lawful refusal of treatment shall not independently constitute interference.
4. The State shall establish the applicable emergency duty and intentional obstruction.
5. Where the same act is punishable under Penal Code §2.22, the provisions shall merge for sentencing purposes.
6. Separate violence may be charged where independently established.

## §15.3 — Interference With Rescue Operations

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully materially interfering with an authorized emergency rescue operation.
2. Rescue operations may include vehicle extrication, hazardous-area evacuation, wilderness rescue, and other designated public safety operations.
3. The State shall establish the actual interference and relevant criminal intent.
4. Mere presence near an emergency scene shall not independently establish criminal conduct.
5. Conduct separately punishable as emergency-service obstruction shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §15.4 — Obstruction of Emergency Vehicle Access

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully obstructing a designated emergency access route or the movement of an authorized emergency vehicle during an active response.
2. The obstruction must materially interfere with emergency access.
3. An accidental breakdown or unavoidable traffic delay shall not independently establish the offense.
4. Authorized roadwork, towing, and emergency traffic control shall remain lawful within their authority.
5. A vehicle may be lawfully relocated where immediate access is necessary.
6. Permanent forfeiture shall not result automatically.

## §15.5 — Misuse of Emergency Equipment

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using designated emergency equipment without authorization for an unlawful purpose.
2. Equipment may include emergency communication devices, rescue apparatus, and other specifically restricted public safety equipment.
3. Ordinary lawful possession of common safety equipment shall not constitute this offense.
4. A member of the public rendering reasonable emergency assistance shall not automatically violate this provision.
5. The State shall establish the applicable restriction and intentional misuse.

## §15.6 — Tampering With Emergency Equipment

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully altering, disabling, removing, or damaging emergency equipment with intent to impair its lawful operation.
2. The State shall establish the relevant protected function and prohibited interference.
3. Authorized maintenance, repairs, and equipment replacement shall not constitute tampering.
4. Where the same act satisfies Penal Code §3.16, duplicative punishment shall be prohibited.
5. Additional liability may arise where independently established injury results.

## §15.7 — Theft of Emergency Medical Supplies

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully taking emergency medical supplies entrusted to an authorized medical or rescue service.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful taking and intent to deprive.
3. Authorized medical distribution and emergency use shall not constitute theft.
4. Where the same taking is punishable under §4.27 or §8.28, applicable merger rules shall govern.
5. Restitution may be ordered for established loss.

## §15.8 — Assault on Emergency Personnel

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally committing unlawful assault against an identifiable emergency responder performing authorized duties, with knowledge or reasonable awareness of that status.
2. The State shall establish the underlying assault and qualifying protected duty.
3. A responder's employment shall not independently justify punishment for lawful criticism or disagreement.
4. Serious bodily injury may support aggravated battery or another applicable offense.
5. The same assault shall not receive duplicative punishment under §2.1 and this provision.

## §15.9 — False Report of a Major Emergency

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly making a materially false report of a major emergency with intent to trigger a significant or dangerous emergency response.
2. The State shall establish knowing falsity, intent, and the qualifying response or substantial risk.
3. A good-faith report later found to be inaccurate shall not constitute this offense.
4. Ordinary false emergency reports shall remain subject to §6.23.
5. Separate harm caused by the false report may be considered under other applicable offenses.

## §15.10 — Unauthorized Emergency Scene Entry

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly entering or remaining within a lawfully restricted active emergency scene without authorization.
2. The restriction shall be reasonably identifiable or lawfully communicated.
3. A person shall receive a reasonable opportunity to depart when practicable.
4. Emergency scene restrictions shall remain limited to legitimate safety or operational purposes.
5. Lawful observation outside the restricted area shall remain protected.

## §15.11 — Reckless Creation of a Public Safety Hazard

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk and thereby creating a serious hazardous condition threatening multiple persons.
2. The State shall establish the criminal recklessness and substantial danger.
3. Ordinary accidents or minor unsafe conditions shall not automatically constitute this offense.
4. A more specific applicable offense shall govern identical conduct where appropriate.
5. Lawful emergency work performed with reasonable care shall remain protected.

## §15.12 — Hazardous Materials Handling Violation

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly violating an applicable hazardous-material handling requirement.
2. The State shall identify the relevant material and legally established requirement.
3. Ordinary possession of lawful industrial chemicals or commercial products shall not constitute an offense.
4. Authorized industrial, medical, transportation, and emergency activities shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.
5. The State shall establish the knowing violation.

## §15.13 — Unlawful Transportation of Hazardous Materials

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly transporting designated hazardous materials contrary to an express legal transportation restriction under circumstances creating a substantial safety risk.
2. The State shall establish the material classification, restriction, and qualifying conduct.
3. Lawful commercial transportation shall remain protected.
4. CDL and commercial documentation requirements shall remain governed by Section 5.
5. Cargo and vehicle searches shall require lawful authority.

## §15.14 — Hazardous Materials Release

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly or recklessly causing an unlawful release of designated hazardous material that creates a substantial danger to persons or property.
2. The State shall establish causation and the relevant mental state.
3. An unavoidable accident despite reasonable lawful precautions shall not automatically constitute a criminal release.
4. Qualified emergency personnel may take necessary protective measures.
5. Restitution and lawful cleanup obligations may be imposed separately.

## §15.15 — Aggravated Hazardous Materials Release

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. Aggravated hazardous release occurs when a person knowingly causes an unlawful release of designated hazardous material that creates an imminent and substantial danger of death or serious bodily injury to multiple persons.
2. The State shall establish the prohibited conduct and heightened danger.
3. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
4. A sentence exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
5. Actual deaths may support independent qualifying homicide charges.

## §15.16 — Illegal Disposal of Commercial Waste

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly disposing of regulated commercial waste in a location or manner expressly prohibited by law.
2. The State shall establish the applicable disposal restriction.
3. Authorized waste collection, recycling, and disposal operations shall remain lawful.
4. Ordinary household littering shall not automatically constitute this offense.
5. Where the same act is punishable under §3.19, merger rules shall apply.

## §15.17 — Hazardous Waste Dumping

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly disposing of designated hazardous waste contrary to law and creating a substantial environmental or public safety danger.
2. The State shall establish the relevant hazardous classification and prohibited disposal.
3. Authorized industrial disposal shall not constitute this offense.
4. The same act shall not receive duplicate punishment under §3.20.
5. The court may order lawful remediation or restitution.

## §15.18 — Industrial Environmental Contamination

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly or recklessly causing substantial unlawful contamination of designated land, water, or other protected environmental resources through industrial or commercial activity.
2. The State shall establish the contamination, criminal fault, and applicable legal restriction.
3. Mere operation of an industrial facility shall not establish environmental crime.
4. Lawful permitted operations shall remain protected within their authorization.
5. Significant cleanup costs may be addressed through lawful restitution or civil remedies.

## §15.19 — Major Environmental Contamination

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly conducting or directing an unlawful activity causing extensive environmental contamination and creating substantial danger to public health, major water supplies, or protected habitats.
2. The State shall establish the extraordinary scope, criminal intent, and qualifying harm or danger.
3. Ordinary minor environmental violations shall not constitute major contamination.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.
5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.
6. Appropriate remediation and restitution may be ordered separately.

## §15.20 — Contamination of a Public Water Supply

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly contaminating a designated public drinking-water source or distribution system with unlawful material, creating a substantial danger of serious bodily injury or death.
2. The State shall establish intentional contamination and the resulting substantial danger.
3. Ordinary equipment failure or accidental contamination without the required criminal intent shall not constitute this offense.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.
5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.
6. Resulting intentional homicide may be prosecuted under a separate qualifying offense.

## §15.21 — Unlawful Interference With Public Utilities

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully interfering with a recognized public utility system for the purpose of interrupting or impairing its lawful operation.
2. Protected systems may include power, public water, communications, and other officially designated essential services.
3. Authorized maintenance or lawful service disconnection shall not constitute this offense.
4. The State shall establish the unlawful interference and required intent.
5. Extraordinary attacks shall remain subject to the applicable infrastructure sabotage provisions.

## §15.22 — Tampering With Fire Protection Systems

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly disabling, obstructing, or materially altering a legally required fire protection system without authorization for an unlawful purpose.
2. Such systems may include designated alarms, suppression equipment, and emergency fire safety devices.
3. Authorized inspection, maintenance, repair, and replacement shall remain lawful.
4. The State shall establish the protected function and intentional unlawful interference.
5. Resulting serious injury or death may support additional offenses.

## §15.23 — False Activation of a Fire Alarm

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly activating a designated fire alarm without a reasonable belief in an emergency and with intent to cause an unnecessary response or disruption.
2. A good-faith activation shall not constitute this offense merely because no fire is later found.
3. The State shall establish the required knowledge and intent.
4. A false report creating substantial danger may qualify for a more serious offense where independently established.

## §15.24 — Obstruction of a Lawful Evacuation

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully preventing or materially obstructing persons from complying with a lawful emergency evacuation under circumstances creating a substantial safety danger.
2. The State shall establish the applicable emergency authority and prohibited interference.
3. Ordinary confusion or inability to evacuate shall not establish criminal conduct.
4. Lawful assistance to persons requiring support shall remain protected.
5. Separate violence or unlawful restraint may be prosecuted where independently established.

## §15.25 — False Representation as Emergency Personnel

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and falsely representing themselves as an authorized emergency responder for the purpose of exercising apparent official authority or obtaining an unlawful benefit.
2. The State shall establish the false representation and criminal purpose.
3. Ordinary civilian emergency assistance shall not constitute impersonation.
4. Clothing or equipment resembling an emergency uniform shall not independently establish fraudulent representation.
5. Duplicative punishment under applicable government impersonation offenses shall be prohibited.

## §15.26 — Unlawful Closure of a Public Road

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly establishing or maintaining an unauthorized road closure that materially restricts public movement.
2. The State shall establish the absence of applicable authority.
3. Authorized DOT work, emergency operations, lawful public works, and permitted events shall remain exempt within their authorization.
4. An individual temporarily warning motorists of an immediate danger shall not automatically commit this offense.
5. Lawful removal of the obstruction may be authorized.
6. The same act shall not receive duplicate punishment under §5.9.

## §15.27 — Endangerment of Emergency Responders

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly engaging in unlawful conduct that creates an imminent and substantial danger of serious bodily injury to identifiable emergency personnel performing authorized duties.
2. The State shall establish the criminal conduct and heightened danger.
3. Ordinary accidental interference shall not automatically constitute the offense.
4. The offense shall not be established solely because a person refuses voluntary medical treatment.
5. Independently established attempted murder or aggravated assault may be charged where applicable.

## §15.28 — Public Safety Enforcement and Property Protections

1. Emergency and environmental enforcement shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.
2. Officials may take necessary protective action during a genuine emergency within their lawful authority.
3. Emergency circumstances shall not authorize unrelated investigations or unrestricted searches.
4. Commercial equipment and vehicles shall not be permanently confiscated without appropriate legal authority.
5. Lawful businesses shall retain their licensing, property, and judicial review rights.
6. Restitution and remediation shall remain distinct from criminal fines.
7. No public safety offense shall authorize conduct prohibited by Rosefire's community conduct rules.
