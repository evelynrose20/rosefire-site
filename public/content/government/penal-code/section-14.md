# SECTION 14 — CRIMINAL PROCEDURE, WARRANTS & SENTENCING ENHANCEMENTS

## §14.1 — Purpose and Application

1. This section shall establish supplementary criminal procedures governing the application of the Rosefire Penal Code.

2. These provisions shall be interpreted consistently with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

3. The procedural rules established herein shall not create independent authority to violate fundamental civil rights.

4. Criminal offenses shall remain subject to the burden of proof established by law.

5. Judicial authority shall be exercised only by legally authorized persons or systems acting within their delegated powers.

## §14.2 — Lawful Criminal Charges

1. Every criminal charge shall identify an applicable offense established by law.

2. Charges shall identify the relevant statutory provision.

3. The State shall maintain sufficient supporting evidence for each alleged offense.

4. A person shall not be charged with an offense solely because an officer finds their behavior suspicious or objectionable.

5. Charging decisions shall remain subject to authorized prosecutorial and judicial review.

6. A charge shall not establish criminal guilt before lawful disposition.

## §14.3 — Arrest Without a Warrant

1. An authorized officer may conduct a warrantless arrest only where applicable law permits.

2. Probable cause shall be required where established by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

3. An officer shall not arrest an individual merely for exercising the right to remain silent or declining voluntary consent to a search.

4. Arrests shall be documented according to applicable procedures.

5. An arrested individual shall be informed of the general basis for the arrest when reasonably practicable.

6. Custodial interrogation shall remain subject to applicable legal representation and silence protections.

## §14.4 — Arrest Warrants

1. An arrest warrant shall be issued only by an authorized judicial authority acting upon a legally sufficient application.

2. The application shall establish the required factual and legal basis.

3. A warrant shall identify the person or persons subject to arrest with reasonable particularity.

4. A warrant shall not authorize unrelated searches or seizures beyond applicable law.

5. Officers shall verify the current enforceability of a warrant where reasonably practicable.

6. A warrant that has been lawfully recalled, quashed, or discharged shall not provide continuing arrest authority.

7. Fraudulent or disputed warrants shall remain subject to judicial review.

## §14.5 — Search Warrants

1. A search warrant shall require the legal grounds established by Section 4 of the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

2. A warrant shall describe the place, property, records, or information subject to the authorized search with reasonable particularity.

3. Officers shall not exceed the warrant's lawful scope.

4. Digital records, private business files, financial information, and personal communications shall remain subject to applicable protections.

5. A warrant shall not provide unrestricted authority to search unrelated locations or persons.

6. Evidence obtained outside lawful authority may be challenged before the court.

7. A search warrant shall not independently authorize permanent property forfeiture.

## §14.6 — Warrant Applications and Supporting Evidence

1. Warrant applications shall be supported by truthful and relevant evidence.

2. Knowingly false statements material to establishing warrant authority may constitute a criminal offense.

3. An officer shall not omit material information with intent to mislead the issuing authority.

4. A warrant issued through material fraud may be challenged.

5. The Department of Justice may review the validity of disputed applications.

6. Authorized judicial systems may maintain records of applications and resulting determinations.

## §14.7 — Judicial Intelligence Service and Warrants

1. The State's Judicial Intelligence Service may process, record, or determine warrant applications only within powers expressly delegated by law.

2. Automated judicial determinations shall remain subject to the applicable legal requirements for issuing warrants.

3. The system shall not independently expand the grounds upon which warrants may be issued.

4. Automated records shall remain subject to verification and correction.

5. An individual affected by an allegedly unlawful automated determination may seek human judicial review.

6. A private technology contractor shall not acquire independent warrant authority merely by operating the system.

7. Unauthorized alteration of automated warrant records shall remain subject to applicable criminal law.

## §14.8 — Warrant Execution

1. Officers executing a warrant shall remain within their lawful authority.

2. The execution of a warrant shall comply with applicable use-of-force and property protections.

3. Officers shall identify their authority and purpose where required and reasonably practicable.

4. Emergency circumstances may permit lawful deviations from ordinary procedures where authorized.

5. Property seized during warrant execution shall be documented.

6. Officers shall not knowingly seize unrelated property outside lawful grounds.

7. An individual may challenge unlawful warrant execution through appropriate judicial proceedings.

## §14.9 — Warrant Expiration and Recall

1. Warrants shall remain enforceable only according to their lawful terms and applicable procedural requirements.

2. A judicial authority may recall, modify, or quash a warrant where permitted by law.

3. Officers shall not knowingly execute a recalled or invalid warrant.

4. Administrative system errors shall be subject to correction.

5. A disputed record shall not automatically authorize indefinite detention.

6. The appropriate court may determine the legal effect of a disputed warrant.

## §14.10 — Charging Documents

1. Prosecutors may file formal charges through recognized judicial procedures.

2. Charging documents shall identify the defendant, alleged offenses, and sufficient information concerning the conduct alleged.

3. Charges shall reference the applicable Penal Code provisions.

4. Material amendments shall comply with applicable procedural safeguards.

5. A defendant shall receive legally sufficient notice of the charges.

6. The court may dismiss charges lacking an adequate legal or evidentiary basis.

## §14.11 — Prosecutorial Review

1. Prosecutors shall assess whether the available evidence supports the applicable charges.

2. Prosecutors may amend, reduce, or dismiss charges where lawful grounds exist.

3. Prosecutorial discretion shall not authorize discriminatory or retaliatory enforcement.

4. A prosecutor shall not knowingly pursue charges unsupported by required evidence.

5. Prosecutors shall disclose material evidence according to applicable legal obligations.

6. A defendant shall remain entitled to contest the allegations.

## §14.12 — Criminal Evidence Standards

1. Criminal guilt shall be established beyond a reasonable doubt.

2. Evidence shall be evaluated according to applicable rules of relevance, reliability, authenticity, and admissibility.

3. An allegation shall not become proven merely because it appears in an official database.

4. Witness testimony, digital records, physical evidence, and other admissible information may be considered.

5. Evidence obtained through unlawful conduct may be challenged.

6. Knowingly fabricated evidence shall not be used to support a conviction.

7. The court shall determine disputed evidentiary questions according to law.

## §14.13 — Chain of Custody

1. Evidence collected during criminal investigations shall be handled according to applicable chain-of-custody procedures.

2. Material transfers and handling shall be documented where necessary to establish reliability.

3. Chain-of-custody discrepancies may affect admissibility or evidentiary weight.

4. A minor administrative discrepancy shall not automatically invalidate otherwise reliable evidence.

5. Intentional substitution, destruction, or falsification may constitute an independent offense.

6. Digital evidence shall remain subject to appropriate authenticity protections.

## §14.14 — Defendant's Right to Counsel

1. An accused individual shall retain the right to legal representation as established by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

2. A clear request for an attorney shall suspend custodial interrogation as required by that Code.

3. No scripted recitation of rights shall be necessary for those rights to become effective.

4. A defendant may consult a lawfully authorized attorney.

5. The State shall not treat a request for legal representation as evidence of guilt.

6. Restrictions on legal access shall require lawful authority.

## §14.15 — Initial Judicial Appearance

1. An individual held for criminal proceedings shall receive the judicial review required by applicable law.

2. The proceeding may address identity, legal charges, detention authority, and other matters within the court's jurisdiction.

3. An initial appearance shall not independently constitute a trial or final determination of guilt.

4. Defendants shall receive appropriate notice of the proceedings.

5. Judicial availability requirements shall be administered consistently with the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

6. Automated judicial assistance may occur only within delegated authority.

## §14.16 — Bail and Pretrial Release

1. Courts may authorize bail, release conditions, or other lawful pretrial dispositions where established by law.

2. Conditions shall be reasonably related to legitimate appearance, safety, or judicial concerns.

3. Bail shall not be used as an unauthorized criminal fine.

4. Pretrial detention shall not be imposed solely because an individual is unable to satisfy an excessive financial demand.

5. A defendant may seek review of applicable release conditions.

6. A person released pending trial shall remain subject to valid judicial orders.

7. Violation of a lawful release condition may result in further proceedings as authorized by law.

## §14.17 — Plea Agreements

1. Prosecutors and defendants may enter lawful plea agreements subject to applicable judicial approval.

2. A plea shall be voluntary, informed, and supported by a legally sufficient factual basis.

3. A defendant shall not be compelled to plead guilty through unlawful threats or coercion.

4. Plea agreements may concern qualifying charges, sentencing recommendations, restitution, or other authorized terms.

5. A court shall not impose penalties outside lawful limits merely because the parties agree to them.

6. Life imprisonment and capital punishment shall remain subject to their separate mandatory judicial safeguards.

7. An agreement shall not authorize conduct prohibited by Rosefire's community rules.

## §14.18 — Criminal Trials

1. A defendant contesting criminal charges shall be entitled to an appropriate judicial proceeding.

2. The prosecution shall bear the burden of proving the offenses beyond a reasonable doubt.

3. The defendant shall retain the rights to challenge evidence and present a defense according to law.

4. The court shall determine guilt based upon admissible evidence and applicable criminal law.

5. Judicial proceedings shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

6. A defendant shall not be convicted solely because they exercise the right to remain silent.

## §14.19 — Sentencing Factors

1. Courts shall determine sentences within the limits established by the applicable offenses.

2. Relevant factors may include the nature of the offense, proven harm, criminal intent, prior qualifying convictions, and legally recognized mitigating circumstances.

3. Sentencing shall remain individualized.

4. A defendant shall not receive an unauthorized enhancement based solely upon wealth, reputation, political beliefs, or unrelated associations.

5. Restitution and lawful noncustodial conditions may be imposed where authorized.

6. The court shall identify the statutory basis for any exceptional sentence.

## §14.20 — Mitigating Circumstances

1. A court may consider legally relevant mitigating circumstances when selecting a sentence within the authorized range.

2. Mitigation may include voluntary restitution, genuine cooperation, an otherwise limited criminal history, reduced culpability, or other appropriate circumstances.

3. The absence of an admission of guilt shall not independently be treated as an aggravating factor.

4. A defendant shall not be required to surrender fundamental rights to receive lawful sentencing consideration.

5. Mitigating evidence shall be evaluated according to the circumstances of the case.

## §14.21 — Aggravating Circumstances

1. A court may consider aggravating circumstances supported by admissible evidence.

2. Aggravating circumstances may include:

   a. Deliberate planning of a serious offense.

   b. Proven leadership of a coordinated criminal operation.

   c. Intentional infliction of serious bodily harm.

   d. Substantial danger to multiple persons.

   e. Abuse of entrusted public authority.

   f. Another circumstance expressly authorized by law.

3. Aggravating factors shall not automatically authorize a sentence beyond the offense's statutory maximum.

4. An extraordinary enhancement shall require specific statutory authority.

5. The same aggravating conduct shall not be counted repeatedly in violation of applicable merger or sentencing rules.

## §14.22 — Repeat-Offender Enhancements

1. A repeat-offender enhancement may apply only where established by a specific offense or another valid statutory provision.

2. Prior arrests shall not constitute convictions.

3. The State shall establish the prior qualifying conviction and its legal relevance.

4. A prior conviction shall not automatically make a new offense life-eligible.

5. Ordinary repeat-offender enhancements shall not exceed the authorized maximum for the offense unless an applicable statute expressly permits such an increase.

6. Any enhanced fixed-term sentence shall remain subject to the seven-day maximum.

## §14.23 — Weapon-Related Sentencing Enhancements

1. Weapon use may be considered where an applicable offense expressly authorizes an enhancement.

2. Mere lawful weapon ownership shall not constitute an aggravating circumstance.

3. The State shall establish the actual prohibited weapon-related conduct.

4. A weapon element already necessary to establish aggravated robbery or another offense shall not automatically be counted again as a separate enhancement.

5. Enhancements shall remain within the applicable statutory limits.

## §14.24 — Organized Crime Sentencing Enhancements

1. An offense committed as part of a qualifying criminal enterprise may be subject to enhanced sentencing where expressly authorized.

2. The State shall establish intentional individual participation and the applicable criminal enterprise elements.

3. Mere association with a gang, organization, business, or club shall not justify enhanced sentencing.

4. Criminal organization reputation shall not substitute for evidence.

5. Enhancements shall remain subject to the applicable fixed-term maximum and statutory offense limits.

## §14.25 — Exceptional Fixed-Term Sentences

1. An exceptional sentence exceeding six real-world hours shall require express authorization in the applicable offense provision.

2. The sentencing authority shall identify the legal basis for the exceptional term.

3. An authorized human judge shall approve the sentence.

4. Exceptional sentences shall remain subject to all applicable due process protections.

5. An exceptional sentence shall not independently establish eligibility for life imprisonment.

6. Ordinary Class A Felonies shall retain their normal sentencing limits unless an applicable offense expressly provides otherwise.

## §14.26 — Seven-Day Maximum Fixed-Term Imprisonment

1. No fixed-term sentence imposed at a single sentencing event shall exceed seven real-world days, equivalent to 168 elapsed hours.

2. The cap shall apply to the total fixed-term imprisonment imposed at that sentencing event, including consecutive sentences for multiple convictions.

3. Courts shall not divide related convictions into artificial proceedings for the purpose of circumventing the maximum.

4. Fixed-term imprisonment shall advance according to actual elapsed time, including periods when the sentenced individual is not connected to the State's correctional systems.

5. Sentence completion shall not require the presence of a correctional employee.

6. Life imprisonment shall remain a distinct sentencing category and shall not be converted into a seven-day term.

7. Escape, parole violations, and other subsequent offenses shall be handled according to applicable law without retroactive expansion of a completed fixed-term sentence.

## §14.27 — Life Imprisonment

1. Life imprisonment may be imposed only for an offense expressly designated as life-eligible.

2. A life sentence shall require an authorized human judicial determination.

3. The Judicial Intelligence Service shall not independently impose life imprisonment.

4. Life imprisonment shall not operate as an ordinary fixed-term sentence.

5. A person serving life imprisonment shall remain subject to the parole, clemency, judicial review, and correctional procedures established by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

6. An escaped life prisoner shall not automatically become eligible for capital punishment solely because of the escape.

## §14.28 — Capital Punishment Eligibility

1. Capital punishment shall remain subject to the exceptional requirements established under Civil Rights & Justice Code Section 8.

2. Eligibility shall require a prior valid life sentence and a subsequent, separately committed offense independently qualifying for life imprisonment under applicable law.

3. The subsequent offense shall require a separate lawful conviction.

4. Capital eligibility shall not make a death sentence automatic.

5. An authorized human judge shall conduct the required capital sentencing proceedings.

6. The defendant shall retain applicable rights to legal representation, appeals, stays, and clemency review.

7. The Judicial Intelligence Service shall not independently impose capital punishment.

8. A death sentence shall not be executed without a valid human judicial warrant and completion of required legal procedures.

## §14.29 — Capital Sentencing and Character Death

1. A lawful execution following a valid capital sentence shall constitute permanent character death under the established legal and community procedures.

2. The State shall not treat ordinary character injury or incapacitation as a judicial execution.

3. A person shall not be executed solely because an automated system designates them as eligible.

4. Death row custody shall remain subject to the Department of Corrections and applicable judicial oversight.

5. No correctional official shall independently order an execution.

6. Executions shall remain subject to all required appeals, stays, and clemency determinations.

7. Community conduct requirements applicable to permanent character death shall remain independently binding.

## §14.30 — Sentence Credits and Rehabilitation

1. Courts and correctional authorities may recognize lawful sentence credits or rehabilitative programs where authorized.

2. Administrative sentence credits shall not extend a sentence beyond its lawful maximum.

3. Programs may include education, rehabilitation, and other approved correctional activities.

4. The Department of Corrections shall not arbitrarily alter judicial sentences.

5. A defendant may seek review of an allegedly incorrect sentence record.

6. Release shall occur when the applicable lawful custody authority has ended.

## §14.31 — Parole and Conditional Release

1. Parole may be authorized where applicable law permits.

2. Parole decisions shall consider the applicable sentence and relevant legal requirements.

3. Parole conditions shall be reasonably related to legitimate correctional and public safety purposes.

4. An alleged parole violation shall be reviewed according to lawful procedures.

5. A parole violation shall not independently authorize capital punishment.

6. Life-sentence parole shall remain governed by its applicable specialized procedures.

## §14.32 — Judicial Appeals

1. A defendant may seek judicial review or appeal according to applicable law.

2. Grounds may include material legal error, unlawful sentencing, disputed evidence, or other recognized claims.

3. An appeal shall not automatically reverse a conviction unless law provides otherwise.

4. A valid stay shall suspend the affected action according to its terms.

5. The State shall maintain accurate records of appellate decisions.

6. Human judicial review shall remain available for qualifying automated determinations.

## §14.33 — Correction of Criminal Records

1. Material errors in criminal records may be corrected through authorized procedures.

2. Corrections shall identify the legal basis and responsible authority.

3. A correction shall not be treated as fraudulent merely because it changes a prior accusation or disposition.

4. Unauthorized deletion or falsification of records may constitute a criminal offense.

5. An individual shall not be presumed guilty solely because an official record was altered by another person.

6. Disputes concerning sentence completion, warrants, convictions, or release status may be reviewed by an appropriate court.

## §14.34 — Automated Judicial Record Disputes

1. Judicial records maintained by the Judicial Intelligence Service shall remain subject to verification and lawful correction.

2. An apparently authenticated record may be relied upon by authorized officials within the limits established by law.

3. A mere unsubstantiated allegation of digital alteration shall not automatically authorize indefinite detention.

4. Where substantial evidence establishes a material record-integrity dispute, an authorized judicial authority may determine the legal effect of the disputed record.

5. The system's contractor shall not independently determine criminal guilt or impose custody beyond expressly delegated authority.

6. A judicial determination invalidating a fraudulent record may restore the enforceability of a lawful underlying order where applicable.

7. An individual shall retain applicable rights to challenge such determinations.

## §14.35 — Prosecutorial Misconduct

1. A prosecutor shall not knowingly present fabricated evidence.

2. A prosecutor shall not knowingly pursue a criminal conviction through material false statements.

3. Prosecutors shall comply with applicable evidence disclosure obligations.

4. Misconduct allegations may be referred for authorized disciplinary or criminal investigation.

5. A criminal conviction obtained through material prosecutorial misconduct may be subject to judicial review.

6. Prosecutorial authority shall not exempt an individual from applicable criminal laws.

## §14.36 — Judicial Misconduct

1. A human judge shall exercise judicial authority according to law.

2. No judge shall knowingly accept a bribe to alter a judicial decision.

3. No judge shall knowingly impose punishment beyond lawful sentencing authority.

4. Judicial misconduct may be subject to authorized disciplinary, removal, or criminal proceedings.

5. A judge shall not be criminally prosecuted solely because another court disagrees with a good-faith legal interpretation.

6. Materially corrupt judicial decisions may be challenged through appropriate review procedures.

## §14.37 — Private Judicial Contractor Accountability

1. A private contractor maintaining judicial technology shall remain subject to applicable criminal and commercial laws.

2. Contracted technical duties shall not independently confer judicial powers.

3. A contractor shall not knowingly manipulate official judicial records for an unlawful purpose.

4. A contractor shall not independently impose criminal penalties beyond expressly delegated authority.

5. Material corruption, fraud, or unauthorized interference may be prosecuted under the applicable Penal Code provisions.

6. Government contracts shall not exempt private companies or their personnel from lawful investigation.

7. Official judicial acts shall remain subject to state legal authority and judicial oversight.

## §14.38 — Protection Against Duplicative Punishment

1. No person shall receive unlawful duplicative punishment for the same criminal offense.

2. Separate offenses may be charged where they contain independently established elements and applicable law permits.

3. A court shall apply merger rules where one offense is legally included within another or where separate punishment is otherwise prohibited.

4. The State shall not multiply substantially identical charges solely to increase a defendant's sentence.

5. Sentencing for multiple convictions shall remain subject to the statutory maximum.

6. Restitution shall not be duplicated for the same established financial loss.

## §14.39 — Effective Sentencing Authority

1. Sentencing authority shall be exercised only by duly authorized persons or judicial systems.

2. Police may issue lawful citations and other expressly delegated routine dispositions.

3. Prosecutors may negotiate or recommend lawful dispositions.

4. Courts shall resolve contested criminal liability and impose sentences within their authorized jurisdiction.

5. Automated judicial systems may perform only specifically delegated functions.

6. Life imprisonment and capital punishment shall remain reserved for authorized human judges.

7. No government contractor shall acquire sentencing authority solely by controlling judicial software.

## §14.40 — Criminal Procedure Accountability

1. Criminal procedure shall protect public safety while preserving the legal rights of accused individuals.

2. Government officials shall not knowingly falsify charges, warrants, evidence, or sentence records.

3. Official errors shall be corrected through appropriate lawful procedures.

4. A defendant shall retain access to applicable judicial review.

5. No criminal charge shall authorize unlawful punishment or prohibited community conduct.

6. The Department of Justice shall administer criminal proceedings according to the applicable statutory framework.

7. These procedural provisions shall remain enforceable unless amended or repealed through lawful state authority.
