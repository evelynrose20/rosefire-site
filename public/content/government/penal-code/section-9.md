# ROSEFIRE PENAL CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.5 — Sections 9–10**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing criminal offenses and penalties concerning wildlife conservation, hunting, fishing, animal protection, aircraft operation, aviation safety, maritime navigation, vessel operation, and associated commercial activities throughout the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–8 remain enacted and in force. Sections 9–10 constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Penal Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code, particularly Sections 14–15 concerning conservation, aviation, and maritime law.

---

# SECTION 9 — HUNTING, FISHING & WILDLIFE OFFENSES

## §9.1 — Hunting Without a Required License

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly engaging in hunting activities requiring a valid hunting license without possessing the required authorization.
2. The State shall establish that the activity requires licensing.
3. Possession of hunting equipment alone shall not constitute hunting.
4. A valid licensing record shall be recognized even where the individual cannot immediately produce a physical document.
5. Lawful exemptions shall remain applicable.
6. Wildlife products may be seized only where lawful grounds exist.

## §9.2 — Fishing Without a Required License

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly engaging in fishing activities requiring a valid fishing license without possessing the applicable authorization.
2. The State shall establish the licensing requirement.
3. Merely possessing a fishing rod or traveling near a body of water shall not constitute fishing.
4. A valid license shall not be considered invalid solely because a physical document is unavailable.
5. Applicable lawful exemptions shall remain recognized.

## §9.3 — Hunting During a Closed Season

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly hunting a regulated species during a legally established closed season.
2. The State shall establish the applicable species classification and season.
3. Hunting seasons shall be maintained in an officially adopted wildlife schedule.
4. Emergency hunting restrictions shall be enforceable only when lawfully enacted and effective.
5. Lawful defensive action against dangerous wildlife shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.

## §9.4 — Exceeding a Hunting Harvest Limit

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly harvesting wildlife beyond a legally established limit.
2. The State shall establish the applicable limit and qualifying harvest.
3. Mere possession of multiple pieces of processed meat shall not independently establish unlawful harvesting.
4. Commercial operators may be subject to separate lawful documentation requirements.
5. Harvest limits shall not be created or altered solely through an officer's informal instruction.

## §9.5 — Hunting in a Prohibited Area

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly hunting within a legally designated no-hunting area.
2. Such areas shall be identified through applicable geographic schedules or lawful notices.
3. The State shall establish the restriction and its applicability.
4. An individual shall not be convicted based solely on an unpublished restriction.
5. Separate trespass or weapons offenses may apply where independently established.

## §9.6 — Use of a Prohibited Hunting Method

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using a hunting method expressly prohibited by law.
2. Prohibited methods shall be identified in the official conservation schedule.
3. The State may restrict designated explosives, unlawful traps, poisons, or other prohibited methods.
4. Ordinary lawful possession of equipment capable of another use shall not independently establish the offense.
5. Lawful wildlife management operations shall remain subject to applicable exemptions.

## §9.7 — Hunting a Protected Species

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly hunting or unlawfully killing a species expressly designated as protected.
2. The State shall establish the applicable protected classification.
3. Protected species shall be identified through an officially adopted schedule.
4. Lawful self-defense and authorized conservation operations shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.
5. The State shall not classify an animal as protected retroactively to create criminal liability.

## §9.8 — Aggravated Poaching

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits aggravated poaching by knowingly engaging in substantial or repeated unlawful harvesting of protected wildlife, or by participating in a coordinated commercial poaching operation.
2. The State shall establish the aggravating circumstances.
3. A single minor licensing violation shall not automatically constitute aggravated poaching.
4. Criminal participation shall be established individually.
5. Restitution and lawful conservation remedies may be ordered where applicable.

## §9.9 — Organized Wildlife Trafficking

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly participating in a coordinated unlawful commercial operation involving protected wildlife or prohibited wildlife products.
2. The State shall establish intentional participation and qualifying trafficking conduct.
3. Lawful commercial sale of properly harvested wildlife products shall not constitute trafficking.
4. A business shall not be presumed criminally responsible solely because an independent supplier committed an offense without the business's knowledge.
5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
6. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

## §9.10 — Possession of Unlawfully Harvested Wildlife

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing wildlife products unlawfully harvested or acquired.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful origin and the individual's relevant knowledge.
3. Lawful possession of meat, hides, fish, or other animal products shall remain protected.
4. Mere possession of a wildlife product shall not automatically establish poaching.
5. Products may be held as evidence where authorized by law.

## §9.11 — Sale of Protected Wildlife Products

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly selling or commercially distributing wildlife products whose sale is expressly prohibited.
2. The State shall identify the applicable protected classification.
3. Lawful commercial sales shall remain permitted within authorized conditions.
4. Criminal knowledge and participation shall be established through evidence.
5. Administrative licensing consequences shall remain separate from criminal punishment.

## §9.12 — Unlawful Commercial Hunting

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly conducting a regulated commercial hunting operation without the specific authorization required by law.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying commercial activity and licensing requirement.
3. A hunter's isolated lawful sale of a permitted product shall not automatically establish a commercial operation.
4. Ordinary hunting license violations shall remain subject to the appropriate lesser offense.
5. Commercial record inspection shall remain limited to lawful authority.

## §9.13 — Fishing During a Closed Season

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly fishing for a species during a legally established closed season.
2. The State shall identify the species and applicable restriction.
3. The official fishing schedule shall determine effective seasons.
4. Accidental capture shall be evaluated according to applicable catch-and-release requirements.
5. Unpublished seasonal restrictions shall not independently create criminal liability.

## §9.14 — Exceeding a Fishing Catch Limit

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly retaining fish beyond a legally established catch or possession limit.
2. The State shall establish the relevant species, limit, and amount retained.
3. Lawful commercial exemptions shall remain applicable.
4. A person shall not be criminally punished under a limit that was not legally effective.
5. Fish may be seized where the applicable evidentiary requirements are satisfied.

## §9.15 — Taking Protected Fish

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly taking or retaining a fish species protected against such activity by law.
2. The State shall establish the species classification and prohibited conduct.
3. Accidental capture followed by compliance with required release procedures shall not automatically constitute the offense.
4. Lawful scientific and conservation authorizations shall remain recognized.
5. Applicable fishing schedules shall establish the protected species.

## §9.16 — Illegal Fishing Methods

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using a fishing method expressly prohibited under state law.
2. Prohibited methods may include designated explosive, poisonous, or otherwise unlawful harvesting techniques.
3. Ordinary possession of lawful equipment shall not establish illegal fishing.
4. The State shall identify the prohibited method and evidence of its use.
5. Additional offenses may apply for independently established property damage or danger to persons.

## §9.17 — Fishing in Restricted Waters

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly fishing in waters legally designated as restricted.
2. Restricted waters shall be identified through applicable geographic schedules.
3. The State shall establish that the applicable restriction was effective and reasonably identifiable.
4. Temporary restrictions shall remain limited to their lawful purpose and duration.
5. A general fishing license shall not automatically authorize fishing in prohibited waters.

## §9.18 — Unlawful Commercial Fishing

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly conducting commercial fishing without authorization specifically required by law.
2. The State shall establish the regulated commercial activity.
3. An ordinary recreational catch shall not automatically constitute commercial fishing.
4. Commercial fishing operations shall remain subject to applicable maritime safety requirements.
5. Vessel seizure shall require independent lawful grounds.

## §9.19 — Falsification of Wildlife Harvest Records

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly falsifying legally required hunting, fishing, or wildlife commercial records for an unlawful purpose.
2. The State shall establish the applicable recordkeeping requirement and material falsification.
3. Ordinary clerical errors shall not automatically constitute criminal conduct.
4. Authorized corrections shall remain lawful.
5. Records obtained during investigations shall remain subject to applicable legal protections.

## §9.20 — Unlawful Wildlife Transport

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly transporting designated wildlife or wildlife products contrary to an express legal restriction.
2. The State shall identify the restriction and relevant products.
3. Ordinary lawful transport of harvested meat, fish, hides, or other products shall not constitute an offense.
4. A missing document shall not automatically establish unlawful wildlife trafficking.
5. Vehicle and cargo searches shall comply with applicable law.

## §9.21 — Damage to a Protected Wildlife Habitat

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly causing substantial unlawful damage to a designated protected wildlife habitat.
2. The State shall establish the protected designation and qualifying damage.
3. Ordinary lawful access and authorized land use shall remain protected.
4. Civil restoration or remediation orders may be imposed where appropriate.
5. Duplicative punishment under the general property crime provisions shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §9.22 — Unlawful Animal Cruelty

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally inflicting unnecessary suffering upon an animal through conduct expressly prohibited by law.
2. Lawful hunting, fishing, veterinary care, and authorized wildlife management shall not constitute cruelty solely because an animal is injured or killed during the activity.
3. The State shall establish intentional prohibited conduct.
4. Applicable community conduct restrictions shall remain controlling.
5. Nothing in this provision authorizes prohibited animal-related roleplay.

## §9.23 — Aggravated Animal Cruelty

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits aggravated animal cruelty by knowingly engaging in extraordinary unlawful mistreatment causing severe injury or death under circumstances expressly designated as aggravated.
2. The State shall prove the required intent and aggravating circumstances.
3. Lawful animal husbandry, humane euthanasia, and authorized wildlife management shall remain protected.
4. This provision shall not authorize conduct prohibited by Rosefire's community conduct rules.

## §9.24 — Wildlife Enforcement Obstruction

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and unlawfully materially interfering with a duly authorized wildlife enforcement officer performing lawful duties.
2. The State shall establish the officer's relevant authority.
3. Mere disagreement, lawful observation, refusal to consent to a search, or protected silence shall not constitute obstruction.
4. Non-law-enforcement conservation employees shall not acquire general police powers through this provision.

## §9.25 — Impersonating a Wildlife Officer

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and falsely claiming wildlife law enforcement authority for the purpose of exercising apparent official powers or obtaining an unlawful benefit.
2. The State shall establish intentional misrepresentation and qualifying unlawful purpose.
3. Ordinary clothing or lawful employment in conservation shall not independently establish impersonation.
4. Duplicative punishment under general official impersonation laws shall remain subject to applicable merger rules.

## §9.26 — Unlawful Wildlife Evidence Destruction

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly destroying, concealing, substituting, or falsifying material wildlife evidence with intent to obstruct an actual or reasonably anticipated investigation.
2. The State shall establish the evidence and required corrupt intent.
3. Ordinary lawful processing of animal products without the prohibited intent shall not constitute evidence destruction.
4. Duplicative charges shall remain subject to applicable merger rules.

## §9.27 — Wildlife Licensing and Property Protections

1. Wildlife convictions may result in license suspension or revocation where authorized by applicable law.
2. A criminal conviction shall not automatically cause permanent loss of every hunting or fishing privilege.
3. Temporary seizure of wildlife products or equipment shall remain distinct from permanent forfeiture.
4. Vehicles, boats, firearms, and unrelated business property shall not be automatically forfeited because of a wildlife offense.
5. Applicable innocent-owner protections shall remain effective.
6. Civil restitution and conservation remediation shall remain separate from criminal fines.
7. All licensing actions shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.
