# SECTION 10 — AVIATION & MARITIME OFFENSES

## §10.1 — Operating an Aircraft Without a Required Pilot License

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating an aircraft requiring a pilot license without possessing the required legal authorization.
2. The State shall establish the aircraft classification and applicable license requirement.
3. Mere ownership of an aircraft shall not establish unlawful operation.
4. A pilot shall not be convicted solely because a valid licensing record is temporarily unavailable.
5. Emergency operation necessary to prevent imminent loss of life shall be evaluated under applicable defenses.

## §10.2 — Operating an Aircraft Outside License Classification

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating an aircraft outside the classifications or endorsements authorized by their pilot license.
2. The State shall establish the applicable endorsement requirement.
3. A valid general driver's license shall not substitute for a required pilot qualification.
4. An administrative classification error shall not automatically establish guilt.
5. Applicable licensing restrictions may be enforced separately.

## §10.3 — Operating an Unregistered Aircraft

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating an aircraft subject to registration without valid registration.
2. The State shall establish the registration requirement.
3. Mere possession of an unregistered aircraft that is not unlawfully operated shall not constitute this offense.
4. Aircraft operation may be restricted until the lawful registration requirements are satisfied.
5. Permanent forfeiture shall require separate legal authority.

## §10.4 — Falsification of Aircraft Registration

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, altering, or using materially false aircraft registration or ownership documentation for an unlawful purpose.
2. The State shall establish the relevant falsification and criminal intent.
3. Ordinary clerical or administrative mistakes shall not constitute this offense.
4. The lawful aircraft owner shall retain applicable property rights.

## §10.5 — Reckless Operation of an Aircraft

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk to persons or property while operating an aircraft.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying reckless conduct.
3. A technical flight error or ordinary accidental maneuver shall not automatically constitute recklessness.
4. Unauthorized low-altitude maneuvers creating substantial danger may be considered where applicable aviation restrictions exist.
5. Resulting serious injury or death may support additional charges where independently established.

## §10.6 — Aggravated Reckless Aviation

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. Aggravated reckless aviation occurs when reckless aircraft operation creates an imminent and substantial risk of death or serious bodily injury to multiple persons.
2. The State shall establish the heightened danger.
3. Ordinary aviation negligence shall not automatically establish the offense.
4. Additional homicide offenses may apply where independently established.
5. Aviation license suspension shall remain subject to applicable legal procedures.

## §10.7 — Unauthorized Landing

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits unauthorized landing by knowingly landing an aircraft in a location where landing is expressly prohibited by law.
2. The State shall establish the restriction and applicable location.
3. A reasonable emergency landing to protect life shall not independently constitute criminal conduct.
4. Landing upon private property may give rise to separate trespass or property offenses where independently established.
5. An aircraft may be recovered through lawful procedures.

## §10.8 — Violation of Restricted Airspace

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly entering or operating an aircraft within legally restricted airspace contrary to an applicable restriction.
2. Restricted airspace shall be identified through an officially adopted aviation schedule or effective lawful notice.
3. The State shall establish the restriction and knowing violation.
4. Emergency deviations reasonably necessary to protect life shall be evaluated according to applicable law.
5. Unpublished or ineffective restrictions shall not create criminal liability.

## §10.9 — Aggravated Restricted Airspace Violation

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly violating restricted airspace while creating a substantial danger to a protected government facility, aircraft, or persons.
2. The State shall establish the underlying airspace violation and qualifying aggravated danger.
3. Mere geographic entry without the additional elements shall remain subject to the ordinary offense.
4. An unauthorized aircraft shall not automatically be subject to destruction or lethal force.

## §10.10 — Failure to Comply With Lawful Air Traffic Instructions

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly disobeying a lawful, applicable, and reasonably clear instruction from authorized aviation control personnel.
2. The State shall establish the issuing authority and instruction.
3. A pilot shall not be required to obey an instruction that would create an immediate unreasonable danger to life where a lawful emergency deviation is justified.
4. An instruction shall not become lawful solely because the issuing person claims official authority.

## §10.11 — Aviation Operation Under the Influence

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by operating an aircraft while unlawfully impaired by alcohol, controlled substances, or another qualifying intoxicant.
2. The State shall establish impairment and operation.
3. Lawful possession of alcohol or a controlled substance shall not independently establish impairment.
4. Testing shall comply with applicable legal requirements.
5. Pilot license suspension may be pursued under authorized procedures.

## §10.12 — Aggravated Aviation Impairment

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits aggravated aviation impairment when operating an aircraft while unlawfully impaired and creating a substantial risk of serious bodily injury or death.
2. The State shall establish both impairment and qualifying danger.
3. A person shall not be convicted solely because a substance is present in their possession.
4. Resulting injuries may support other offenses where independently established.

## §10.13 — Unauthorized Commercial Aviation

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating commercial passenger or cargo aviation services without the legally required commercial authorization.
2. The State shall establish the commercial activity and applicable licensing requirement.
3. Ordinary private flights shall not automatically constitute commercial aviation.
4. Lawful charter, emergency, and government operations shall remain subject to applicable exemptions.
5. License restrictions shall remain subject to judicial and administrative review.

## §10.14 — Falsification of Aviation Cargo Documentation

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly falsifying material aviation cargo records legally required for commercial transportation.
2. The State shall establish the applicable recordkeeping requirement.
3. Ordinary administrative mistakes shall not constitute criminal falsification.
4. Lawful cargo documentation may be corrected through authorized procedures.
5. Cargo searches shall remain subject to applicable law.

## §10.15 — Aviation Cargo Smuggling

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits aviation cargo smuggling by knowingly transporting prohibited cargo through an aircraft as part of an unlawful commercial or criminal distribution activity.
2. The State shall establish the prohibited cargo and intentional criminal involvement.
3. A pilot shall not be presumed guilty solely because another person concealed unlawful goods without the pilot's knowledge.
4. Lawful commercial aviation shall remain protected.
5. Aircraft forfeiture shall require independent legal authority.

## §10.16 — Interference With Aircraft Operations

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully interfering with an aircraft's operation in a manner creating a substantial risk to flight safety.
2. The State shall establish the prohibited interference and qualifying danger.
3. Lawful maintenance and authorized aviation control actions shall not constitute this offense.
4. Mere verbal disagreement with aviation personnel shall not automatically establish the offense.

## §10.17 — Aircraft Hijacking

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits aircraft hijacking by knowingly seizing or attempting to seize operational control of an aircraft through force or an immediate threat of force.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying unlawful control and force or threat.
3. Ordinary unauthorized aircraft use without force shall be prosecuted under applicable theft or unauthorized operation offenses.
4. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.
5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.
6. Any independently established homicide shall be prosecuted according to the applicable homicide provisions.

## §10.18 — Aviation Sabotage

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits aviation sabotage by intentionally and unlawfully damaging, disabling, or corrupting an aircraft or essential aviation system with the purpose of causing substantial operational disruption or serious danger.
2. The State shall establish the intentional unlawful interference and qualifying circumstances.
3. Ordinary equipment failure shall not constitute criminal sabotage.
4. Authorized repair, testing, and maintenance shall remain protected.
5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.
6. Sentencing shall require human judicial authorization.

## §10.19 — Aviation Homicide

**Classification:** Capital Felony  
**Fine:** Up to $75,000  
**Sentence:** 6 hours to 7 real-world days, or life imprisonment following authorized human judicial determination

1. A person commits aviation homicide under this provision by intentionally or knowingly using an aircraft to unlawfully cause another person's death under circumstances satisfying the criminal homicide requirements established by law.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful killing, causation, and required mental state.
3. An ordinary aviation accident shall not constitute aviation homicide.
4. Reckless fatalities lacking the required intentional or knowing homicide elements shall be prosecuted under an applicable manslaughter provision.
5. Life imprisonment shall require an authorized human judicial proceeding.
6. Capital punishment shall remain subject to the separate eligibility and procedural safeguards established by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §10.20 — Operating a Vessel Without Required Authorization

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating a vessel requiring a maritime operator license or endorsement without possessing the necessary authorization.
2. The State shall establish that the particular vessel or operation requires such authorization.
3. Ordinary recreational boating shall not automatically require a commercial maritime license.
4. A valid license record shall remain recognized despite the temporary absence of a physical document.
5. Lawful emergency operation shall be evaluated according to applicable defenses.

## §10.21 — Operating an Unregistered Vessel

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $250–$1,000  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by operating a vessel subject to legal registration requirements without valid registration.
2. The State shall establish the applicable requirement.
3. Ownership of a vessel shall not independently establish unlawful operation.
4. Permanent vessel forfeiture shall not result automatically from this offense.
5. Operating restrictions may apply where lawfully established.

## §10.22 — Falsification of Vessel Registration

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, altering, or using materially false vessel registration or ownership documentation for an unlawful purpose.
2. The State shall establish the material falsification and required intent.
3. Ordinary administrative errors shall not establish criminal liability.
4. Lawful ownership interests shall remain protected.

## §10.23 — Reckless Vessel Operation

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits reckless vessel operation by consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk to persons or property while operating a boat or other vessel.
2. The State shall establish qualifying reckless conduct.
3. Ordinary navigational mistakes shall not automatically constitute criminal recklessness.
4. The absence of a collision shall not prevent prosecution where the substantial danger is otherwise established.
5. Actual injury may support an additional offense where independently established.

## §10.24 — Aggravated Reckless Vessel Operation

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. Aggravated reckless vessel operation occurs when qualifying reckless navigation creates an imminent and substantial danger of death or serious bodily injury.
2. The State shall establish the underlying recklessness and heightened danger.
3. A collision alone shall not conclusively establish this offense.
4. Additional injury or homicide offenses may be charged where independently established.

## §10.25 — Maritime Operation Under the Influence

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by operating a vessel while unlawfully impaired by alcohol, controlled substances, or another qualifying intoxicant.
2. The State shall establish impairment and vessel operation.
3. Lawful possession of an intoxicating substance shall not independently establish impairment.
4. Applicable testing and enforcement shall remain subject to civil rights protections.
5. Vessel operation may be temporarily restricted where an immediate danger exists.

## §10.26 — Aggravated Maritime Impairment

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits aggravated maritime impairment when operating a vessel while unlawfully impaired and creating a substantial danger of serious bodily injury.
2. The State shall establish both impairment and qualifying danger.
3. Ordinary impairment without the aggravating circumstance shall remain subject to the lesser offense.
4. Resulting injury or death may support separately established offenses.

## §10.27 — Violation of Maritime Navigation Rules

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $150–$1,000  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by operating a vessel contrary to an applicable and legally established navigation rule.
2. Such rules may govern right of way, designated channels, passage, and safe vessel movement.
3. The State shall identify the rule violated.
4. Emergency deviations reasonably necessary to protect life may be considered under applicable law.
5. A navigational violation shall not automatically authorize vessel search or forfeiture.

## §10.28 — Violation of Maritime Speed Restrictions

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $150–$1,000  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by operating a vessel above a legally established maritime speed limit.
2. The State shall establish the applicable restriction.
3. Speed zones shall be identified through lawful schedules or applicable notices.
4. Speeding alone shall not automatically constitute reckless vessel operation.
5. Qualifying dangerous conduct may support a separate charge where independently established.

## §10.29 — Unauthorized Entry Into Restricted Waters

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating a vessel within waters legally designated as restricted or prohibited.
2. The State shall establish the restriction and relevant area.
3. A temporary emergency restriction shall remain limited to its lawful scope.
4. Emergency navigation to prevent imminent serious harm may be considered as a defense.
5. Unauthorized entry shall not automatically justify destructive force against a vessel.

## §10.30 — Obstruction of a Navigational Channel

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly or recklessly obstructing a designated navigational channel in violation of applicable law.
2. An accidental breakdown shall not independently establish criminal obstruction.
3. Authorized docking, marine construction, and emergency operations may be exempt where lawful.
4. Officers may arrange lawful removal of an immediate navigation hazard.
5. Permanent forfeiture shall require separate authority.

## §10.31 — Failure to Render Required Maritime Assistance

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly failing to perform a legally established duty to provide reasonable assistance following a maritime collision or comparable incident.
2. Such duties shall be limited by reasonable safety and capability.
3. A person shall not be required to create an imminent substantial danger to themselves or others to provide assistance.
4. The State shall establish the applicable duty and knowing failure.
5. Ordinary inability to provide effective assistance shall not independently constitute an offense.

## §10.32 — Leaving the Scene of a Maritime Collision

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly leaving a qualifying maritime collision without performing required reporting, identification, or assistance duties.
2. The State shall establish the legal duty and knowing violation.
3. Temporary departure to obtain emergency help may be justified where reasonable.
4. The collision itself shall not automatically establish criminal fault.
5. Civil maritime liability shall remain distinct from criminal charges.

## §10.33 — Unauthorized Commercial Maritime Operation

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating a designated commercial vessel service without the authorization expressly required by law.
2. The State shall establish the regulated commercial activity and licensing requirement.
3. Ordinary recreational boating shall not automatically constitute commercial maritime operation.
4. Lawful charter, fishing, cargo, and passenger services shall remain protected when properly authorized.

## §10.34 — Falsification of Maritime Cargo Records

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly falsifying legally required maritime cargo documents for an unlawful purpose.
2. The State shall establish the material falsification and applicable record requirement.
3. Ordinary clerical mistakes shall not automatically constitute this offense.
4. Searches of cargo shall remain subject to applicable law.
5. Separate fraud offenses may apply where independently established.

## §10.35 — Maritime Cargo Smuggling

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly transporting prohibited cargo by vessel as part of an unlawful commercial or criminal operation.
2. The State shall establish knowledge and participation.
3. Mere employment aboard a vessel shall not establish criminal responsibility for concealed unlawful cargo.
4. Lawful commercial cargo shall remain protected against arbitrary seizure.
5. Vessel forfeiture shall require appropriate legal authority.

## §10.36 — Maritime Armed Robbery

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by unlawfully taking property from an occupied vessel through force or an immediate threat of force involving a deadly weapon.
2. The State shall prove the unlawful taking and qualifying weapon-related conduct.
3. Ordinary theft from an unattended vessel shall remain subject to the applicable theft offense.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §10.37 — Vessel Hijacking

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits vessel hijacking by knowingly seizing operational control of an occupied vessel through force or an immediate threat of force.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful seizure and required coercive conduct.
3. Ordinary vessel theft without force shall not constitute hijacking.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
6. Independently established serious injury or death may support additional charges.

## §10.38 — Maritime Sabotage

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits maritime sabotage by intentionally and unlawfully damaging or disabling a vessel, port, navigation system, or other designated maritime infrastructure under circumstances creating widespread disruption or substantial danger.
2. The State shall establish the intentional conduct and qualifying disruption or danger.
3. Ordinary equipment failure or legitimate maintenance shall not constitute sabotage.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.
5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §10.39 — Maritime Homicide

**Classification:** Capital Felony  
**Fine:** Up to $75,000  
**Sentence:** 6 hours to 7 real-world days, or life imprisonment following authorized human judicial determination

1. A person commits maritime homicide under this provision by intentionally or knowingly using a vessel to unlawfully cause another person's death under circumstances satisfying the criminal homicide requirements.
2. The State shall prove causation and the applicable criminal mental state.
3. An accidental drowning, collision, or other maritime tragedy shall not automatically constitute homicide.
4. Reckless fatal conduct lacking intentional or knowing killing shall be prosecuted under an applicable manslaughter provision.
5. Life imprisonment shall require authorized human judicial proceedings.
6. Capital punishment shall remain subject to the separate eligibility and procedural requirements established by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §10.40 — Interference With Maritime Rescue

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and materially interfering with lawful maritime rescue or emergency operations in a manner creating a substantial danger to persons.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying intentional interference.
3. Mere observation, peaceful communication, or protected refusal to consent to an unrelated search shall not constitute this offense.
4. Additional offenses may apply where independently established violence occurs.

## §10.41 — Aviation and Maritime Evidence Tampering

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly destroying, concealing, altering, or falsifying material evidence concerning an actual or reasonably anticipated aviation or maritime investigation with intent to obstruct that proceeding.
2. The State shall establish the prohibited interference and required corrupt intent.
3. Authorized equipment repair, accident recovery, and evidence preservation shall not constitute tampering.
4. Duplicative punishment under general evidence-tampering provisions shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §10.42 — Aviation and Maritime Seizure Protections

1. Aircraft and vessels may be temporarily secured or seized where lawful grounds exist.
2. Temporary evidence seizure shall not automatically establish permanent forfeiture.
3. Permanent forfeiture shall require appropriate legal authority and due process.
4. The State shall consider lawful ownership, leasing, financing, and third-party interests.
5. Ordinary registration or licensing violations shall not automatically authorize permanent confiscation.
6. Affected individuals may seek review and return of unlawfully retained property.
7. Officials shall not personally appropriate seized aircraft, vessels, equipment, or cargo.
