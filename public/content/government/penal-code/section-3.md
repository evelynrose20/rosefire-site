# ROSEFIRE PENAL CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.2 — Sections 3–4**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act defining criminal offenses against property, land, buildings, public infrastructure, businesses, financial institutions, and lawful ownership; and establishing penalties for trespassing, destruction, burglary, robbery, theft, and related criminal conduct.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–2 remain enacted and in force. Sections 3–4 form a continuation of the Rosefire Penal Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

---

# SECTION 3 — CRIMES AGAINST PROPERTY

## §3.1 — Criminal Trespass

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,000  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits criminal trespass by knowingly entering or remaining upon property without lawful authorization after receiving legally sufficient notice that entry or continued presence is prohibited.

2. Notice may be established through direct communication, legally effective signage, or another recognized means.

3. Ordinary entry into property generally open to the public shall not constitute trespass during authorized access.

4. A person shall receive a reasonable opportunity to leave where practicable.

5. Lawful government authority or another recognized legal justification may permit entry.

## §3.2 — Aggravated Trespass

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits aggravated trespass when they knowingly enter or remain upon restricted property while intending to commit an independently unlawful act or while creating a substantial safety threat.

2. Restricted property may include secure commercial premises, occupied private property, and other designated locations.

3. The State shall establish the aggravating circumstances.

4. Mere entry into an unfamiliar location shall not independently establish aggravated trespass.

5. Trespass upon specially protected government or correctional property may be subject to a separate offense.

## §3.3 — Criminal Mischief

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits criminal mischief by intentionally damaging or interfering with another person's property without lawful authority.

2. This offense applies to property damage not meeting the requirements of a more serious offense.

3. Ordinary accidental damage without the required criminal fault shall not constitute criminal mischief.

4. Restitution may be ordered for established losses.

5. Property ownership disputes shall not automatically establish criminal intent.

## §3.4 — Vandalism

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits vandalism by intentionally defacing, marking, damaging, or altering property belonging to another without lawful permission.

2. Vandalism may include unauthorized graffiti, destruction of signs, defacement of buildings, and similar conduct.

3. Authorized artwork or alterations permitted by the lawful property holder shall not constitute vandalism.

4. The State shall establish intentional unauthorized conduct.

5. Documented restoration costs may be considered for restitution.

## §3.5 — Significant Property Destruction

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits significant property destruction by intentionally causing substantial physical damage to another person's property.

2. Substantial damage shall be established by the nature, extent, and reasonably documented cost of the loss.

3. A minor cosmetic defect shall not independently establish substantial destruction.

4. Where the conduct creates a serious danger to life, additional or aggravated offenses may apply.

5. Restitution may be imposed independently of the criminal fine.

## §3.6 — Destruction of High-Value Property

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally destroying or rendering substantially unusable property of significant established value.

2. Qualifying property may include vehicles, commercial equipment, aircraft, vessels, industrial machinery, or major building systems.

3. The prosecution shall establish the property damage, relevant value, and required intent.

4. Lawful repossession, authorized demolition, and other legally permitted acts shall not constitute this offense.

5. The State may seek restitution for established loss.

## §3.7 — Arson

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits arson by intentionally setting an unlawful fire that damages a building, vehicle, structure, or other protected property.

2. The State shall establish the intentional unlawful ignition and resulting damage.

3. An accidental fire shall not independently constitute arson.

4. Lawfully authorized burning shall not constitute arson when conducted within applicable legal restrictions.

5. Additional offenses may apply when the fire causes independently established injury or death.

## §3.8 — Aggravated Arson

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. Aggravated arson occurs when qualifying arson knowingly creates a substantial risk of death or serious bodily injury.

2. Aggravating circumstances may include setting fire to an occupied structure or intentionally obstructing evacuation.

3. The prosecution shall establish the relevant aggravating facts.

4. The mere existence of significant financial damage shall not independently constitute aggravated arson.

5. Separate homicide charges may be brought where their elements are independently established.

## §3.9 — Arson Resulting in Death

**Classification:** Capital Felony  
**Fine:** Up to $75,000  
**Sentence:** 6 hours to 7 real-world days, or life imprisonment following an authorized human judicial determination

1. A person commits arson resulting in death when they intentionally commit arson under circumstances establishing a knowing or intentional lethal danger, and that conduct causes another person's death.

2. The State shall prove the underlying arson, required mental state, causation, and resulting death.

3. An accidental fire shall not qualify.

4. Life imprisonment shall require the judicial procedures established by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

5. Capital punishment shall not result automatically from conviction.

6. Any death sentence shall require independent eligibility and compliance with the capital sentencing requirements of Civil Rights & Justice Code §8.7.

## §3.10 — Reckless Destruction of Property

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits reckless destruction by consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk of significant property damage, resulting in such damage.

2. The conduct must constitute a gross deviation from reasonable care.

3. Ordinary negligence or unavoidable accident shall not independently establish this offense.

4. Where the conduct causes serious injury, the applicable offense against persons may also be considered.

## §3.11 — Tampering With Property

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits property tampering by knowingly interfering with another person's equipment, machinery, access systems, or other property without authorization and with intent to impair its lawful operation.

2. Tampering may occur without permanent physical damage.

3. Lawful maintenance, authorized repair, and permitted modification shall not constitute this offense.

4. The State shall establish the required intent and unauthorized interference.

## §3.12 — Sabotage of Commercial Operations

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits commercial sabotage by intentionally and unlawfully damaging or disabling a business's equipment, facilities, or essential operational systems for the purpose of materially disrupting its activities.

2. Ordinary commercial competition, employee resignation, or lawful labor disputes shall not constitute sabotage.

3. Breach of contract alone shall not establish this offense.

4. The State shall prove intentional unlawful interference and its qualifying purpose.

5. Civil damages may be sought separately for established losses.

## §3.13 — Sabotage of Public Infrastructure

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–6 hours

1. A person commits public infrastructure sabotage by knowingly and unlawfully damaging or disabling infrastructure essential to public transportation, emergency response, communications, power, water, or another designated public service.

2. The conduct must cause or be intended to cause substantial disruption.

3. Authorized maintenance or lawful service interruption shall not constitute this offense.

4. An offense creating an extraordinary danger to life may qualify for an enhanced sentence where expressly provided by law.

5. Financial loss alone shall not automatically establish a capital offense.

## §3.14 — Extraordinary Infrastructure Attack

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally conducting a coordinated or large-scale unlawful attack against designated critical infrastructure that causes widespread disruption and a substantial risk of serious bodily injury or death.

2. The State shall prove the intentional attack, qualifying scale, and substantial danger.

3. An ordinary outage, isolated equipment failure, or routine act of vandalism shall not constitute this offense.

4. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require human judicial authorization.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence for this offense shall be 48 real-world hours.

## §3.15 — Destruction of Government Property

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally causing substantial unauthorized damage to property lawfully owned or operated by the State.

2. Government property may include public buildings, government vehicles, equipment, and designated public facilities.

3. Minor accidental damage shall not independently establish this offense.

4. The State shall establish the damage and required criminal intent.

5. Damage affecting essential services may qualify for a more serious offense where its elements are established.

## §3.16 — Destruction of Emergency Service Equipment

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully destroying or materially disabling equipment used for police, fire, medical, or emergency rescue operations.

2. The State shall establish knowledge of the equipment's protected function and intentional interference.

3. Ordinary accidental damage during an emergency shall not independently establish this offense.

4. Conduct creating an immediate danger to life may support additional charges where independently established.

## §3.17 — Unlawful Occupation of Property

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits unlawful occupation by knowingly occupying premises without legal right and refusing to leave following legally sufficient notice or an enforceable possession order.

2. A good-faith ownership or tenancy dispute shall not independently establish criminal occupation.

3. Lawful tenants shall retain their rights under Section 16 of the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

4. Landlords shall not use this offense to circumvent lawful eviction proceedings.

5. A court may resolve disputed possession.

## §3.18 — Unlawful Interference With Real Property

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and unlawfully obstructing another individual's lawful access to or use of real property through physical interference.

2. Ordinary contractual disagreements shall not independently constitute criminal interference.

3. An individual's exercise of a valid ownership or possession right shall not constitute this offense.

4. The State shall establish the unlawful interference and required intent.

## §3.19 — Illegal Dumping

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 0–20 minutes

1. A person commits illegal dumping by knowingly disposing of waste or unwanted material in a location where such disposal is prohibited.

2. The State may establish lawful waste collection and disposal locations.

3. Ordinary accidental littering may be subject to a lesser offense.

4. Significant environmental contamination may qualify for a more serious offense where independently established.

5. An authorized waste collection or transport activity shall not constitute illegal dumping when performed lawfully.

## §3.20 — Hazardous Illegal Dumping

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits hazardous illegal dumping by knowingly disposing of designated hazardous material unlawfully and creating a substantial danger to persons, property, or protected environments.

2. The State shall identify the material and applicable disposal restriction.

3. Lawful industrial or commercial disposal performed under valid authorization shall not constitute this offense.

4. Restitution or lawful cleanup obligations may be imposed where appropriate.

## §3.21 — Damage to Protected Natural Resources

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly causing substantial unlawful destruction to designated protected natural resources or conservation property.

2. Protected status shall be established by effective state law or official schedules.

3. Ordinary lawful gathering, hunting, fishing, or authorized land use shall not constitute this offense.

4. Wildlife-specific offenses shall remain governed by the applicable conservation provisions.

## §3.22 — Property Crime Restitution and Liability

1. A court may order restitution for qualifying property loss caused by criminal conduct.

2. Restitution may include reasonably established repair, replacement, or restoration costs.

3. Restitution shall remain distinct from the criminal fine.

4. A person shall not automatically receive duplicate restitution for the same loss.

5. Property disputes lacking criminal intent shall remain subject to appropriate civil proceedings.

6. Government authorities shall not seize unrelated property merely to guarantee restitution without lawful authority.
