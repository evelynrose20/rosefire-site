# SECTION 12 — ORGANIZED CRIME, RACKETEERING & CRIMINAL ENTERPRISES

## §12.1 — Participation in a Criminal Enterprise

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and intentionally participating in the operation of an organized group through a qualifying pattern of independently unlawful conduct.

2. The State shall establish the defendant's individual participation and the qualifying criminal activity.

3. Mere membership in a social club, motorcycle club, business association, gang, or other organization shall not independently constitute criminal participation.

4. The name, clothing, reputation, or associations of a group shall not establish that it is a criminal enterprise.

5. Lawful businesses and organizations shall retain their civil rights and property protections.

## §12.2 — Criminal Enterprise Leadership

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing, coordinating, or managing a qualifying criminal enterprise's unlawful activities.

2. The State shall establish actual criminal leadership or operational control.

3. Ownership of a business or leadership of a social organization shall not independently establish criminal enterprise leadership.

4. The defendant's responsibility shall be based upon proven participation in prohibited conduct.

5. Individual criminal liability shall not be presumed from organizational rank alone.

## §12.3 — Racketeering

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits racketeering by knowingly participating in a criminal enterprise through a qualifying pattern of specified criminal offenses conducted for unlawful financial, territorial, or organizational benefit.

2. Qualifying predicate offenses may include designated extortion, bribery, serious fraud, trafficking, robbery, or other offenses expressly adopted by law.

3. A qualifying pattern shall require at least two distinct predicate criminal acts connected to the enterprise, with at least one occurring after the enterprise's formation or establishment of its relevant criminal purpose.

4. Mere allegations or arrests without proof shall not establish predicate offenses.

5. The State shall establish the enterprise, pattern, and individual's knowing participation.

6. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

7. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §12.4 — Aggravated Racketeering

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. Aggravated racketeering occurs when qualifying racketeering involves substantial coordinated violence, large-scale financial corruption, or another expressly designated aggravated circumstance.

2. The State shall establish the underlying racketeering pattern and qualifying aggravation.

3. Mere membership in a suspected criminal organization shall not constitute aggravated racketeering.

4. A sentence shall reflect the defendant's proven responsibility.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.

6. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §12.5 — Extraordinary Criminal Enterprise

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $40,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a criminal enterprise engaged in an extraordinary pattern of serious violent or financially destructive offenses affecting multiple victims or major public institutions.

2. The State shall establish the qualifying pattern, extraordinary scope, and defendant's individual criminal participation.

3. A group shall not qualify solely because of its size, reputation, political influence, or commercial success.

4. A single ordinary offense shall not establish an extraordinary criminal enterprise.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.

6. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

7. This offense shall not independently authorize life imprisonment.

## §12.6 — Criminal Conspiracy Within an Enterprise

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly agreeing with one or more other persons to commit a designated felony on behalf of a criminal enterprise, followed by an overt act in furtherance of that agreement.

2. Mere discussion without a qualifying agreement and overt act shall not constitute conspiracy.

3. The State shall establish individual intentional participation.

4. An accused person shall not be responsible for every offense committed by an associated organization.

5. Where the same agreement is charged under Penal Code §1.7, applicable merger rules shall govern.

## §12.7 — Organized Extortion

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits organized extortion by knowingly participating in a coordinated operation to obtain money, property, services, or other benefits through unlawful threats or coercion.

2. The State shall establish the coercive conduct and intentional participation.

3. Lawful commercial negotiations and authorized debt collection shall not constitute extortion.

4. Mere association with the individuals committing extortion shall not establish guilt.

5. A qualifying pattern may support racketeering charges where separately established.

## §12.8 — Protection Racket

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly demanding money or another benefit from a business or individual through a threat of unlawful harm if the demand is refused.

2. The threatened harm may be physical, financial, or directed at lawful property interests where the applicable extortion elements are established.

3. Legitimate private security agreements shall not constitute a protection racket solely because payment is required for contracted services.

4. The State shall establish the unlawful threat and coercive demand.

5. Businesses subjected to extortion may pursue appropriate criminal complaints and civil remedies.

## §12.9 — Criminal Loan-Sharking

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits criminal loan-sharking by knowingly operating or enforcing a lending arrangement through unlawful threats, violence, or coercive criminal conduct.

2. An unfavorable but lawfully disclosed interest rate shall not independently constitute loan-sharking.

3. Ordinary lawful lending and collections shall remain governed by applicable contract and banking laws.

4. The State shall establish the unlawful coercion.

5. Separate financial fraud or assault offenses may apply where independently established.

## §12.10 — Criminal Debt Collection

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using unlawful threats, property destruction, or other prohibited criminal conduct to compel payment of an actual or alleged financial obligation.

2. The existence of a genuine debt shall not justify unlawful force.

3. Lawful civil collection and judicial enforcement procedures shall remain permitted.

4. The State shall establish the prohibited conduct and criminal purpose.

5. A borrower shall not be imprisoned solely because of inability to pay an ordinary lawful debt.

## §12.11 — Organized Theft Operation

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly participating in a coordinated criminal operation involving repeated or substantial unlawful theft of property.

2. The State shall establish the criminal coordination and individual participation.

3. A lawful resale business shall not be presumed criminal solely because it purchased property later discovered to be stolen.

4. Ordinary isolated theft shall remain subject to the applicable theft offense.

5. Duplicative punishment for identical acts shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §12.12 — Organized Vehicle Theft Ring

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in an organized operation to unlawfully steal, conceal, alter, or distribute multiple motor vehicles.

2. The State shall establish the organized activity and individual criminal involvement.

3. Lawful vehicle sales, repairs, impound recovery, repossession, and transportation shall not constitute an organized vehicle theft ring.

4. Mere employment at an automotive business shall not establish criminal participation.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

6. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §12.13 — Chop Shop Operation

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating or substantially participating in a facility used to dismantle, alter, or process stolen vehicles for unlawful commercial gain.

2. The State shall establish knowledge of the stolen status and qualifying criminal operation.

3. Lawful scrapyards, salvage facilities, vehicle repair businesses, and recycling operations shall remain protected.

4. Possession of automotive tools or vehicle parts shall not independently establish this offense.

5. Vehicles and equipment may be seized only upon lawful authority.

## §12.14 — Organized Cargo Theft Ring

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in an organized operation to steal, conceal, or unlawfully distribute substantial commercial cargo.

2. The State shall establish the coordinated criminal activity and defendant's individual participation.

3. Lawful commercial transportation shall not independently establish involvement.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §12.15 — Organized Armed Robbery Enterprise

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in an ongoing coordinated criminal enterprise responsible for multiple armed robberies.

2. The State shall establish the qualifying pattern of armed robberies and individual criminal participation.

3. Participation in one isolated robbery shall not independently establish an ongoing enterprise.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.

5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. Independently established homicides shall remain subject to the applicable homicide provisions.

## §12.16 — Criminal Enterprise Money Laundering

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly participating in a coordinated scheme to conceal the unlawful source, ownership, or control of proceeds generated by a qualifying criminal enterprise.

2. The State shall establish the underlying unlawful proceeds and defendant's intentional participation in concealment.

3. Ordinary business revenue and lawful banking transactions shall not independently establish money laundering.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §12.17 — Operation of a Fraudulent Business Front

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating a materially fraudulent business intended to conceal an ongoing criminal enterprise or facilitate qualifying unlawful commercial activity.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent operation and relevant criminal purpose.

3. A legitimate business shall not be presumed fraudulent solely because its owner or employee has been accused of an unrelated offense.

4. Ordinary business insolvency or accounting discrepancies shall not independently constitute a fraudulent front.

5. License and property enforcement shall remain subject to applicable law.

## §12.18 — Criminal Enterprise Use of a Legitimate Business

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using an otherwise legitimate commercial operation to materially facilitate qualifying criminal enterprise activities.

2. The State shall establish intentional use of the business for prohibited conduct.

3. An innocent business owner, employee, or customer shall not be criminally responsible solely because another person misused the business.

4. Lawful commercial operations shall remain protected.

5. The use of a business account shall not independently establish criminal enterprise participation.

## §12.19 — Criminal Enterprise Corruption of a Public Official

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using bribery, unlawful coercion, or material fraud to corrupt a public official for the purpose of facilitating a qualifying criminal enterprise.

2. The State shall establish the underlying corrupt conduct and enterprise-related purpose.

3. Lawful political advocacy and ordinary government-contract applications shall not constitute corruption.

4. Public officials and private participants may be charged according to their proven conduct.

5. Duplicative punishment for the same bribery shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §12.20 — Public Official Participation in Organized Crime

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A public official commits this offense by knowingly and materially participating in the operation of a criminal enterprise while using entrusted government authority to assist or conceal its activities.

2. The State shall establish both the criminal enterprise involvement and misuse of official authority.

3. An official shall not be presumed complicit solely because an organization operates within their jurisdiction.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. Authorized employment discipline may proceed separately under applicable law.

## §12.21 — Organized Witness Intimidation

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated operation to threaten unlawful harm against witnesses or complainants for the purpose of obstructing qualifying criminal proceedings.

2. The State shall establish the intentional coordinated intimidation.

3. Lawful legal advocacy and ordinary contact with witnesses shall remain protected.

4. The prosecution shall establish the defendant's individual participation.

5. Applicable merger rules shall prevent duplicative punishment for identical threats.

## §12.22 — Organized Evidence Destruction

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in coordinated unlawful destruction, concealment, or falsification of material evidence to obstruct investigation or prosecution of a qualifying criminal enterprise.

2. The State shall establish the qualifying evidence interference and corrupt intent.

3. Authorized evidence preservation, record correction, and lawful document management shall not constitute criminal destruction.

4. The offense shall not be established by ordinary refusal to provide voluntary statements.

## §12.23 — Criminal Organization Intimidation of a Business

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly participating in coordinated unlawful threats intended to force a business to cease trading, surrender property, pay an unauthorized demand, or enter a criminal arrangement.

2. The State shall establish the intimidation and prohibited purpose.

3. Lawful commercial competition, peaceful demonstrations, and ordinary negotiations shall not constitute criminal intimidation.

4. Businesses may seek judicial protection and appropriate civil remedies.

## §12.24 — Criminal Territorial Extortion

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly demanding payment, property, or another benefit through unlawful threats based upon a claim of criminal control over a geographic territory.

2. No criminal organization shall acquire lawful governmental authority merely by asserting territorial control.

3. The State shall establish the unlawful demand and coercive threat.

4. Ordinary private property rights and lawful business territory agreements shall remain protected.

5. Mere association with a neighborhood or organization shall not constitute this offense.

## §12.25 — Organized Illegal Weapons Trade

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated criminal operation to unlawfully distribute restricted or prohibited weapons.

2. The State shall establish the organized conduct and relevant weapon classification.

3. Lawful licensed firearm commerce shall not constitute the offense.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. Where identical conduct is punished under §7.14 or §7.15, applicable merger rules shall govern.

## §12.26 — Organized Controlled Substance Distribution

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated criminal operation distributing prohibited controlled substances.

2. The State shall establish the qualifying organized activity and defendant's knowing involvement.

3. Licensed cannabis commerce and authorized medical distribution shall not constitute this offense.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. Where identical conduct is punishable under §8.17–§8.19, applicable merger rules shall govern.

## §12.27 — Criminal Enterprise Interference With Government Institutions

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated criminal enterprise operation that materially disrupts essential government services through qualifying unlawful interference.

2. The State shall establish the underlying criminal conduct and resulting substantial disruption.

3. Peaceful protest, political opposition, lawful litigation, and ordinary criticism of government institutions shall not constitute the offense.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.

5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §12.28 — Extraordinary Organized Criminal Attack

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $40,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in an exceptionally serious coordinated criminal operation involving widespread unlawful violence or substantial disruption of essential public services.

2. The State shall establish the qualifying extraordinary scale and defendant's individual criminal involvement.

3. A single isolated offense shall not automatically establish an extraordinary organized criminal attack.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.

5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. Life imprisonment shall not be imposed unless a separately established life-eligible capital offense applies.

## §12.29 — Criminal Enterprise Property Seizure

1. Property materially connected to qualifying criminal enterprise offenses may be seized where lawful grounds exist.

2. An allegation of criminal organization membership shall not independently authorize seizure of every asset owned by an individual.

3. Permanent forfeiture shall require appropriate legal authority and due process.

4. The State shall distinguish criminal proceeds from lawful business income and unrelated personal property.

5. Innocent owners, legitimate employees, secured lenders, and other lawful third parties shall retain their protected interests.

6. Businesses shall not automatically be permanently closed merely because an employee is accused of organized crime.

7. Property may be returned following appropriate judicial review.

## §12.30 — Individual Responsibility and Civil Rights

1. Criminal responsibility shall be established individually.

2. Mere membership in a gang, club, corporation, association, or other group shall not independently establish criminal guilt.

3. Association with a person convicted of a crime shall not create presumed accomplice liability.

4. A group's reputation or publicly claimed territorial control shall not substitute for evidence of a qualifying offense.

5. Lawful political organizations, labor associations, private clubs, businesses, and community groups shall retain their civil rights.

6. Investigations shall comply with lawful search, seizure, detention, and arrest procedures.

7. Criminal enterprises shall not acquire governmental authority merely through financial or political influence.

8. Duly established corporate control of a lawful government institution shall not independently establish organized crime without proof of prohibited criminal conduct.
