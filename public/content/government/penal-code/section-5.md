# ROSEFIRE PENAL CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.3 — Sections 5–6**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act defining offenses involving motor vehicles, commercial transportation, traffic safety, public order, government institutions, law enforcement operations, correctional custody, public corruption, and interference with the administration of justice.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–4 remain enacted and in force. Sections 5–6 form a continuation of the Rosefire Penal Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

---

# SECTION 5 — VEHICLE & TRAFFIC OFFENSES

## §5.1 — Speeding

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $150–$750  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits speeding by operating a motor vehicle above the legally established speed limit.
2. Applicable speed limits shall be established through state law, posted traffic controls, or an authorized roadway schedule.
3. The degree of the violation may be considered when imposing a fine within the statutory range.
4. Speeding alone shall not automatically constitute reckless driving.
5. An officer shall establish a reasonable evidentiary basis for the alleged speed violation.
6. Emergency vehicle exemptions shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §5.2 — Excessive Speeding

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits excessive speeding by exceeding a lawful speed limit by an amount designated as excessive under the official traffic schedule.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying speed threshold through a published schedule.
3. Excessive speeding may be charged independently of reckless driving only where the elements of each offense are separately established.
4. The absence of a collision shall not automatically defeat an excessive speeding charge.
5. An officer shall not invent or alter the applicable threshold during an enforcement encounter.

## §5.3 — Failure to Obey Traffic Control Devices

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $100–$500  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly or negligently failing to comply with a lawful traffic signal, sign, or other designated traffic control device.
2. Applicable traffic controls must be legally established and reasonably identifiable.
3. Conduct reasonably necessary to avoid an immediate danger may be considered under applicable defenses.
4. Authorized emergency vehicle operations may be exempt where law permits.
5. A traffic control violation shall not independently authorize a vehicle search.

## §5.4 — Failure to Yield

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $150–$750  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits failure to yield by unlawfully refusing or failing to provide the right of way required by applicable traffic law.
2. This offense may apply at intersections, pedestrian crossings, roadway merges, and other designated locations.
3. The State shall establish the relevant duty to yield.
4. A collision shall not be required where the underlying traffic violation is established.
5. A collision shall not automatically establish that every involved driver failed to yield.

## §5.5 — Improper Lane Use

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $100–$500  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits improper lane use by operating a vehicle contrary to applicable lane markings, roadway restrictions, or other legally established lane-use requirements.
2. Authorized deviations reasonably necessary to avoid immediate danger may be considered.
3. Conduct involving substantial danger may qualify for reckless driving where independently established.
4. An officer shall identify the applicable traffic rule supporting the citation.

## §5.6 — Unsafe Lane Change

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $150–$750  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits an unsafe lane change by changing lanes without reasonable regard for other road users or contrary to an applicable roadway requirement.
2. The State shall establish the relevant unsafe conduct.
3. Failure to use a required signal may be considered where such a requirement is legally established.
4. Mere dissatisfaction with another driver's conduct shall not establish an offense.

## §5.7 — Failure to Maintain Safe Following Distance

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $150–$750  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by following another vehicle more closely than is reasonable under the circumstances.
2. Relevant circumstances may include speed, visibility, roadway conditions, and traffic.
3. A collision shall not automatically establish the offense without evidence of an applicable breach.
4. Deliberate dangerous tailgating may qualify for a more serious offense where its elements are established.

## §5.8 — Improper Parking

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $100–$500  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits improper parking by leaving a motor vehicle in a location where parking is prohibited under applicable law.
2. The restriction must be legally established.
3. Vehicles may be subject to authorized towing where the requirements of Civil Rights & Justice Code Section 11 are satisfied.
4. Improper parking shall not automatically authorize permanent vehicle forfeiture.
5. An authorized emergency exemption may apply where established by law.

## §5.9 — Obstruction of a Roadway

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits roadway obstruction by knowingly or recklessly placing, operating, or leaving a vehicle or object in a manner that materially and unlawfully obstructs public traffic.
2. Authorized road closures, emergency operations, and lawful transportation work shall not constitute this offense.
3. An accidental vehicle breakdown shall not independently establish criminal obstruction.
4. Officers may arrange lawful removal of a hazardous obstruction.
5. Material interference with emergency access may qualify for a separate offense.

## §5.10 — Driving Without a Valid License

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating a motor vehicle requiring a driver's license without possessing the necessary valid authorization.
2. A forgotten physical document shall not automatically establish that the underlying driving authorization is invalid.
3. The State shall verify the applicable licensing requirement.
4. An individual shall not be convicted solely because of an inaccurate or unavailable licensing database.
5. Continued driving may be lawfully restricted until an authorized operator is available.

## §5.11 — Driving While License Suspended or Revoked

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating a vehicle while their applicable driving privileges are lawfully suspended or revoked.
2. The State shall establish the validity of the restriction and the required knowledge.
3. An expired, invalid, or unlawfully imposed restriction shall not support conviction.
4. Applicable license reinstatement procedures shall remain governed by Civil Rights & Justice Code Section 10.
5. The vehicle shall not automatically be forfeited.

## §5.12 — Operating an Unregistered Vehicle

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $250–$1,000  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by operating a vehicle requiring registration without valid registration.
2. The State shall establish the applicable registration requirement.
3. A temporary administrative database error shall not independently establish criminal intent.
4. A vehicle may be subject to lawful operating restrictions or impoundment where independently authorized.
5. Ownership shall not automatically be forfeited.

## §5.13 — False or Altered Vehicle Registration

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using materially false, altered, or fraudulent vehicle registration information.
2. The State shall establish the accused's knowledge and intentional unlawful use.
3. A genuine administrative mistake shall not independently constitute criminal falsification.
4. Forgery or other separately established criminal offenses may be charged where appropriate.

## §5.14 — Illegal License Plates

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly displaying a stolen, counterfeit, altered, or unlawfully assigned license plate.
2. The State shall establish the applicable registration discrepancy and criminal knowledge.
3. An accidentally damaged or illegible plate shall not automatically establish intentional wrongdoing.
4. Plates may be seized as evidence where lawful grounds exist.

## §5.15 — Reckless Driving

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits reckless driving by consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk to persons or property while operating a motor vehicle.
2. Reckless conduct may include dangerous maneuvers, excessive speed under hazardous conditions, or other qualifying behavior.
3. Speeding alone shall not automatically establish recklessness.
4. The State shall identify the facts supporting the alleged substantial risk.
5. Additional offenses may apply when serious injury or death results.

## §5.16 — Aggravated Reckless Driving

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. Aggravated reckless driving occurs when reckless vehicle operation creates an imminent and substantial risk of death or serious bodily injury.
2. The State shall establish both the reckless conduct and heightened danger.
3. The mere absence of a collision shall not defeat an otherwise established offense.
4. A collision shall not automatically establish aggravated recklessness.
5. Relevant driving evidence may be introduced according to applicable evidentiary law.

## §5.17 — Driving Under the Influence

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits driving under the influence by operating a motor vehicle while unlawfully impaired by alcohol, controlled substances, or another qualifying intoxicant.
2. Impairment shall be established through admissible evidence.
3. Lawful possession of alcohol or cannabis shall not independently establish impaired driving.
4. Investigations and testing shall comply with Civil Rights & Justice Code §5.6.
5. License suspension may be imposed through the procedures established by law.
6. The vehicle may be temporarily secured where continued operation presents an immediate danger.

## §5.18 — Aggravated Driving Under the Influence

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. Aggravated DUI occurs when an individual commits DUI while engaging in additional conduct creating a substantial risk of serious bodily injury or death.
2. The State shall establish the unlawful impairment and applicable aggravating circumstances.
3. Impairment alone shall not automatically constitute aggravated DUI.
4. Serious resulting injuries may support additional charges where independently established.
5. Driver's license restrictions shall remain subject to Section 10 of the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §5.19 — Hit and Run — Property Damage

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly leaving the scene of a vehicle collision involving property damage without performing duties expressly required by law.
2. Required duties may include stopping, providing appropriate identifying information, and complying with applicable reporting obligations.
3. An operator may leave temporarily where reasonably necessary to obtain emergency assistance or avoid immediate danger, subject to applicable reporting requirements.
4. The collision itself shall not automatically establish criminal fault.
5. Civil liability may be considered separately.

## §5.20 — Hit and Run — Injury

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly leaving a collision involving injury without performing the duties required under applicable law.
2. The State shall establish the relevant knowledge, collision, and failure to comply.
3. A driver shall not be required to remain in an immediately life-threatening location when a lawful and reasonable alternative exists.
4. Separate offenses may apply where the driver independently caused injury through criminal conduct.

## §5.21 — Vehicular Manslaughter

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits vehicular manslaughter by causing another person's death through criminally reckless operation of a motor vehicle.
2. The State shall establish causation and the required criminal mental state.
3. A fatal collision shall not automatically establish criminal manslaughter.
4. Lawful emergency vehicle operations shall be evaluated under their applicable safety obligations.
5. Intentional use of a vehicle to kill may constitute murder rather than manslaughter.

## §5.22 — Failure to Yield to an Emergency Vehicle

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $250–$1,000  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by failing to yield to an authorized emergency vehicle where legally required.
2. The State shall establish the applicable duty and surrounding circumstances.
3. A driver shall not be required to perform a maneuver that would create an immediate unreasonable danger.
4. Intentional obstruction of an emergency response may constitute a separate offense.

## §5.23 — Failure to Stop for Law Enforcement

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly failing to comply with a lawful and reasonably identifiable police direction to stop a motor vehicle.
2. The State shall establish that the direction was lawful and sufficiently communicated.
3. A driver reasonably continuing a short distance to reach a safe stopping location shall not automatically commit this offense.
4. The offense shall remain distinct from felony evasion.
5. Failure to recognize an unmarked or unidentified vehicle shall be evaluated according to the circumstances.

## §5.24 — Evading Law Enforcement

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits evasion by knowingly using a motor vehicle to flee an officer attempting a lawful detention or arrest after a reasonably identifiable direction to stop.
2. The State shall establish knowing flight.
3. A brief delay without intent to evade shall not establish this offense.
4. Dangerous driving during evasion may support an aggravated charge where its elements are established.
5. An officer's pursuit shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §5.25 — Felony Evasion

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. Felony evasion occurs when an individual knowingly flees a lawful vehicle stop or arrest and, during the flight, operates the vehicle in a manner creating a substantial risk of serious bodily injury.
2. The prosecution shall establish knowing flight and the required dangerous conduct.
3. Speed alone shall not automatically establish the felony.
4. The underlying police action must possess lawful authority.
5. Vehicle impoundment and license restrictions may apply where authorized.

## §5.26 — Aggravated Felony Evasion

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–12 hours

1. Aggravated felony evasion occurs when qualifying felony evasion involves intentional use of a vehicle as a weapon, serious bodily injury, or another expressly designated extraordinary danger.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying aggravated circumstances.
3. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.
4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.
5. Escape from police pursuit shall not independently authorize lethal force.

## §5.27 — Illegal Street Racing

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits illegal street racing by knowingly participating as a driver in an unauthorized competitive speed contest upon public roads.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying competitive conduct.
3. Ordinary spirited driving without an unlawful contest shall not independently constitute street racing.
4. Organizers and other participants may be charged where a separate applicable offense is established.
5. Vehicles may be impounded only according to lawful procedures.

## §5.28 — Organizing an Illegal Street Race

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly organizing or directing an unauthorized competitive speed event on public roads under circumstances prohibited by law.
2. The State shall establish intentional organization and the qualifying event.
3. Mere attendance at a gathering shall not constitute organization.
4. Lawful motorsport events conducted with required authorization shall not constitute this offense.
5. Additional offenses may apply for independently established dangerous conduct.

## §5.29 — Driving an Unsafe Vehicle

**Classification:** Infraction  
**Fine:** $250–$1,000  
**Imprisonment:** None

1. A person commits this offense by operating a vehicle with a safety defect prohibited under applicable vehicle standards.
2. The State shall identify the applicable defect and relevant standard.
3. Ordinary cosmetic damage shall not independently constitute an unsafe vehicle.
4. Officers may restrict continued operation where the vehicle presents an immediate danger.
5. Lawful repairs may be required before the vehicle returns to the road.

## §5.30 — Unauthorized Emergency Vehicle Equipment

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly installing or operating restricted emergency lighting, sirens, or other designated emergency identification equipment without legal authorization.
2. The State shall identify the equipment and applicable restriction.
3. Mere possession of ordinary amber hazard equipment shall not independently constitute the offense where such equipment is lawful.
4. Impersonation of emergency personnel may be charged separately where established.

## §5.31 — Operating a Commercial Vehicle Without a CDL

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating a vehicle for which a Commercial Driver's License is legally required without the applicable authorization.
2. The State shall establish that the particular vehicle or operation requires a CDL.
3. Commercial employment alone shall not establish that a CDL is necessary.
4. The vehicle or trailer may be temporarily secured where continued operation cannot lawfully proceed.
5. The lawful owner shall retain applicable recovery rights.

## §5.32 — Commercial Transport Documentation Violation

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly failing to maintain or present commercial transportation documentation expressly required by law.
2. Documents may include designated waybills, cargo manifests, or other legally required records.
3. The State shall identify the applicable document requirement.
4. A record-service failure or good-faith administrative mistake shall not automatically establish criminal intent.
5. Cargo shall not be permanently forfeited solely because required paperwork is missing.

## §5.33 — Falsification of Commercial Cargo Records

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly falsifying material information within a legally required commercial cargo document for an unlawful purpose.
2. The State shall prove the material falsification and criminal intent.
3. Ordinary clerical errors shall not independently establish the offense.
4. Cargo searches shall remain subject to applicable legal standards.

## §5.34 — Overloaded or Unsafe Commercial Transport

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly or recklessly operating a commercial vehicle in violation of an applicable load, securement, or transportation safety requirement.
2. The State shall identify the specific safety standard.
3. Officers may prohibit continued unsafe operation where authorized.
4. Cargo or vehicle forfeiture shall not automatically follow.
5. Additional offenses may apply when serious injury is independently caused.

## §5.35 — Unauthorized Vehicle Towing or Recovery

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly towing, transporting, or retaining another person's vehicle without lawful authority and with intent to unlawfully deprive the owner of possession.
2. Lawful DOT recovery, consensual towing, and valid repossession shall not constitute the offense.
3. The State shall establish the lack of authority and required criminal intent.
4. Ordinary billing or contractual disputes shall not automatically constitute unauthorized towing.
5. Additional theft offenses may apply where their elements are independently established.

## §5.36 — Fraudulent Vehicle Repossession

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits fraudulent repossession by knowingly using a forged, materially false, or otherwise fraudulent claim of authority to recover a vehicle.
2. The State shall establish the material deception and intentional unauthorized recovery.
3. A good-faith legal dispute concerning a valid financing agreement shall not independently constitute this offense.
4. The lawful owner may seek return and appropriate restitution.
5. Repossession procedures shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §5.37 — Reckless Towing or Recovery Operations

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by recklessly conducting a towing or vehicle recovery operation that creates a substantial and unjustifiable danger to persons or property.
2. The State shall establish the qualifying reckless conduct.
3. Ordinary accidental equipment failure without criminal recklessness shall not establish the offense.
4. Authorized DOT work shall remain subject to applicable roadway safety requirements.
5. Resulting property damage may support restitution where appropriate.

## §5.38 — Unauthorized Use of a Motor Vehicle

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits unauthorized use by knowingly operating another person's motor vehicle without permission under circumstances that do not establish the intent to deprive required for theft.
2. Lawful rental, employment, borrowing, and recovery arrangements shall not constitute unauthorized use.
3. The State shall establish the lack of authorization and required knowledge.
4. More serious theft or robbery offenses may apply where separately established.
