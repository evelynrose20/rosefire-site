# ROSEFIRE PENAL CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.7 — Sections 13–14**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing criminal offenses relating to fraudulent identification, forged documents, false licenses, identity deception, and fraudulent professional authority; and providing uniform criminal procedures concerning charges, warrants, evidence, plea agreements, sentencing enhancements, judicial review, and the lawful administration of criminal justice throughout the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–12 remain enacted and in force. Sections 13–14 constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Penal Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

---

# SECTION 13 — FRAUD, FORGERY & IDENTITY OFFENSES

## §13.1 — Forgery of an Official Document

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, materially altering, or presenting a forged official document with intent to deceive another person or institution concerning its legal validity.

2. Official documents may include identification, licenses, authorizations, certificates, and other government-issued records.

3. The State shall establish the falsification and required fraudulent intent.

4. Authorized administrative corrections shall not constitute forgery.

5. The mere possession of an inaccurate document shall not independently establish criminal intent.

6. Where the forged document is a judicial order, the specific offense established under Penal Code §6.36 shall govern.

## §13.2 — Possession of a Forged Government Document

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a materially forged government document with intent to use it fraudulently.

2. Possession without knowledge of the document's fraudulent status shall not establish this offense.

3. A document shall not be deemed forged merely because the government's electronic records contain an error.

4. The State shall establish the document's fraudulent nature and the accused's intent.

5. Lawful possession for evidence preservation or authorized investigation shall remain protected.

## §13.3 — Use of a Counterfeit Identification Card

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly presenting a counterfeit identification card as valid identification for an unlawful purpose.

2. The State shall establish the document's counterfeit status and intentional use.

3. A damaged, expired, or otherwise invalid genuine identification document shall not automatically be considered counterfeit.

4. An individual's lawful use of a recognized alias shall not independently constitute this offense.

5. Where the identification is used to commit financial identity theft, the applicable specific offense may govern.

## §13.4 — Identity Misrepresentation for an Unlawful Benefit

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly assuming or misrepresenting another identifiable person's identity to unlawfully obtain property, access, authority, or another material benefit.

2. The State shall establish the deceptive representation and unlawful purpose.

3. Ordinary use of a nickname, pseudonym, stage name, or lawful alias shall not constitute identity misrepresentation.

4. Anonymous communication shall not independently establish criminal conduct.

5. Financial identity theft shall remain governed by the applicable provisions of Section 11.

## §13.5 — Forgery of a Driver's License

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, materially altering, selling, or using a forged driver's license with intent to misrepresent lawful driving authorization.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent document and required intent.

3. Operating a vehicle without valid authorization may constitute a separate offense where independently established.

4. A genuine license that has expired or been lawfully suspended shall not automatically constitute a forged license.

5. Relevant licensing records shall remain subject to verification and correction.

## §13.6 — Forgery of a Commercial Driver's License

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, materially altering, or fraudulently presenting a Commercial Driver's License to falsely establish commercial driving authorization.

2. The State shall establish the counterfeit credential and required intent.

3. Possession of an ordinary driver's license shall not establish CDL authorization.

4. A legitimate CDL holder shall not be criminally punished solely because licensing records temporarily fail to display their authorization.

5. Unlawful commercial vehicle operation may constitute a separate offense.

## §13.7 — Forgery of a Firearm License

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, materially altering, or fraudulently presenting a firearm license or restricted-weapon authorization.

2. The State shall establish the falsification and fraudulent purpose.

3. A forged firearm authorization shall not confer lawful possession or carrying privileges.

4. An actual valid firearm license shall remain effective despite unrelated administrative errors.

5. Separate unlawful weapon possession charges may apply where independently established.

## §13.8 — Forgery of a Professional License

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating or fraudulently using a forged license authorizing a regulated professional activity.

2. Protected credentials may include medical, legal, aviation, commercial, real estate, or other professional licenses expressly recognized by law.

3. The State shall establish the applicable licensing requirement and forged credential.

4. Ordinary employment without a legally required license shall not constitute this offense.

5. Separate offenses may apply where a person unlawfully performs regulated professional functions.

## §13.9 — Unlawful Practice of a Regulated Profession

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly performing designated professional services requiring a legal license while falsely representing that they possess the necessary authorization.

2. The State shall establish the applicable professional licensing requirement, prohibited conduct, and intentional misrepresentation.

3. Ordinary advice, lawful assistance, and activities not requiring professional licensing shall remain protected.

4. A person shall not be convicted under a professional qualification requirement that was not legally effective at the time of the conduct.

5. Actual professional misconduct may support additional charges where independently established.

## §13.10 — Fraudulent Medical Credentials

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly presenting materially false medical qualifications or authorization with intent to perform regulated medical duties or unlawfully obtain a benefit.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent credential and required intent.

3. Legitimate emergency assistance by an unlicensed bystander shall not automatically constitute fraudulent medical practice.

4. A person shall not be presumed medically licensed solely because they possess medical equipment.

5. Patient injury or independently established fraud may support additional offenses.

## §13.11 — Fraudulent Legal Credentials

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly falsely claiming authorization to practice law, serve as a prosecutor, or perform another legally restricted judicial profession for an unlawful purpose.

2. The State shall establish the applicable professional requirement and intentional fraudulent representation.

3. Ordinary legal discussion, public legal information, and self-representation shall not constitute this offense.

4. Impersonation of a sitting judge or government official may be prosecuted under the applicable specific provision.

5. A defendant shall not lose the right to legal representation because another person fraudulently claimed professional status.

## §13.12 — Fraudulent Government Employment Credentials

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating or presenting forged employment credentials for a recognized government institution with intent to obtain unauthorized access, authority, property, or another unlawful benefit.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent credential and prohibited purpose.

3. Ordinary government employment applications shall not constitute this offense.

4. Fraudulent exercise of apparent police or government authority may be prosecuted under Section 6.

5. A legitimate government employee shall not be presumed fraudulent solely because an official personnel record is temporarily inaccurate.

## §13.13 — Fraudulent Business License

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, materially altering, or presenting a forged business license for the purpose of conducting or obtaining authority for regulated commercial activity unlawfully.

2. The State shall establish the applicable license requirement and fraudulent document.

3. A lapsed license shall not automatically constitute a forged license.

4. Unauthorized business activity may constitute a separate licensing offense where established.

5. Business ownership rights shall remain protected under the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §13.14 — Forgery of a Weed Seller License

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, materially altering, or fraudulently presenting a Weed Seller License to falsely establish authority for regulated cannabis sales.

2. The State shall establish the counterfeit authorization and required intent.

3. Lawful cannabis possession shall not independently constitute this offense.

4. The applicable criminal cannabis sales provisions shall govern any separate unlawful commercial transaction.

5. Licensing revocation or reinstatement shall remain subject to applicable law.

## §13.15 — Fraudulent Property Ownership Records

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, modifying, or presenting materially false property ownership records with intent to obtain or unlawfully transfer a property interest.

2. The State shall establish the false ownership representation and required criminal intent.

3. An ordinary property-boundary disagreement shall not constitute fraudulent ownership.

4. Authorized corrections to official ownership records shall remain lawful.

5. Mortgage and real estate fraud offenses may apply where separately established.

6. Where the same act is punishable as fraudulent real estate transfer under Section 11, merger rules shall prevent duplicative punishment.

## §13.16 — Forgery of a Lease Agreement

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly fabricating or materially altering a lease agreement with intent to obtain an unlawful legal or financial benefit.

2. The State shall establish the forged agreement and required intent.

3. An ordinary disagreement over contractual interpretation shall not constitute forgery.

4. Lawful amendments accepted by the relevant parties shall remain valid.

5. Tenancy and eviction disputes shall remain subject to applicable civil protections.

## §13.17 — Forgery of an Aircraft or Vessel Registration

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly creating, materially altering, or fraudulently using aircraft or vessel registration documents.

2. The State shall establish the false registration and criminal purpose.

3. Lawful registration amendments shall not constitute forgery.

4. Aircraft and vessel ownership shall remain subject to the protections established under Section 15 of the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

5. Where the same conduct is punishable under §10.4 or §10.22, applicable merger rules shall govern.

## §13.18 — Sale of Counterfeit Government Documents

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly engaging in the unlawful commercial sale or distribution of counterfeit government identification, licenses, permits, or other official documents.

2. The State shall establish the counterfeit nature of the documents and knowing commercial distribution.

3. Lawful printing, administrative services, and authorized document production shall remain protected.

4. Possession of ordinary printing or digital production equipment shall not independently establish guilt.

5. The State shall establish the accused's individual criminal participation.

## §13.19 — Organized Document Forgery Operation

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in a coordinated criminal operation producing or distributing counterfeit official documents.

2. The State shall establish the organized activity and the defendant's individual participation.

3. An isolated forged document shall not independently establish an organized operation.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. A sentence exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §13.20 — Counterfeiting Currency

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits counterfeiting currency by knowingly manufacturing, materially altering, or distributing false monetary instruments intended to be accepted as genuine currency.

2. The State shall establish the counterfeit nature and required intent.

3. Clearly identified theatrical or novelty currency not presented as genuine money shall not independently constitute counterfeiting.

4. Possession of printing equipment alone shall not establish the offense.

5. Fraudulent financial transactions involving counterfeit currency may support other charges where independently established.

## §13.21 — Passing Counterfeit Currency

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using counterfeit currency as genuine payment in a transaction.

2. The State shall establish the individual's knowledge that the currency was counterfeit.

3. Unknowing receipt or use shall not constitute the offense.

4. Counterfeit currency may be seized as evidence under lawful procedures.

5. The State shall not presume criminal intent solely from an individual's possession of a counterfeit note.

## §13.22 — Fraudulent Certification or Inspection

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly issuing or presenting a materially false certification or inspection record required by law for an unlawful purpose.

2. Qualifying records may concern vehicle safety, commercial equipment, aviation, maritime operations, or other regulated activities.

3. The State shall establish the applicable requirement and false representation.

4. A good-faith inspection mistake shall not automatically establish criminal fraud.

5. Where identical conduct is punishable under another specific offense, applicable merger rules shall govern.

## §13.23 — Fraudulent Use of a Government Seal

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using a counterfeit or unauthorized government seal, emblem, or official certification mark to falsely represent legal authority or authenticity for an unlawful purpose.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent use and prohibited intent.

3. Ordinary commentary, artistic depiction, and protected expression shall not independently constitute fraudulent use.

4. A genuine government document lawfully displaying a government seal shall remain valid.

## §13.24 — Identity-Based Access Fraud

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using another person's identifying credentials or a fabricated official identity to gain unauthorized access to protected premises, confidential records, or restricted institutional systems for an unlawful purpose.

2. The State shall establish the identity deception, unauthorized access, and required intent.

3. Ordinary lawful account sharing, authorized delegation, or legitimate professional access shall remain protected.

4. Mere possession of an access card shall not automatically establish criminal conduct.

5. Computer-specific offenses may apply where their elements are independently established.

## §13.25 — False Identity in a Judicial Proceeding

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly assuming another individual's identity or presenting materially false identity documentation in a judicial proceeding with intent to alter an official legal result.

2. The State shall establish intentional identity deception and the prohibited purpose.

3. Lawful witness-protection procedures and authorized identity confidentiality shall remain protected.

4. Ordinary errors in identification shall not automatically establish the offense.

5. Judicial record manipulation and perjury offenses may apply where independently established.

## §13.26 — Fraudulent Criminal Record Clearance

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully obtaining or causing the fraudulent alteration of official criminal records to falsely indicate dismissal, acquittal, completion of sentence, discharge, or other legally significant clearance.

2. The State shall establish the fraudulent intervention and required criminal intent.

3. A legitimate judicial acquittal, pardon, sentence completion, or authorized record correction shall not constitute this offense.

4. An individual shall not be presumed responsible solely because their criminal record contains an unauthorized alteration.

5. A disputed clearance shall be subject to appropriate judicial verification and review.

6. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

7. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

8. Identical record alteration charged under §6.32 shall remain subject to the applicable merger rules.

## §13.27 — Document Forgery Evidence and Restitution

1. Documents seized during forgery investigations shall be preserved according to applicable evidentiary procedures.

2. A disputed document shall not automatically be deemed fraudulent solely because an officer suspects falsification.

3. Government records may be verified through the appropriate issuing authority.

4. The State shall not knowingly rely upon altered or fabricated records.

5. Restitution may be ordered for established financial or property loss caused by a qualifying forgery offense.

6. Innocent holders of documents obtained in good faith shall retain applicable legal protections.

7. Unauthorized confiscation of unrelated property shall remain prohibited.
