# ROSEFIRE PENAL CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.1**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act defining criminal offenses, establishing criminal classifications and authorized penalties, and providing for the consistent enforcement of criminal law throughout the State of San Andreas.*

---

# SECTION 1 — GENERAL PROVISIONS & CRIMINAL CLASSIFICATIONS

## §1.1 — Authority of the Penal Code

1. This Code shall establish the criminal offenses recognized by the State of San Andreas.

2. All criminal charges shall be supported by an offense established under this Code or another duly enacted criminal law.

3. No law enforcement officer, prosecutor, judge, or government official shall create a criminal offense or penalty without lawful authority.

4. All criminal proceedings shall remain subject to the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

5. No individual shall be convicted under a criminal provision enacted after the alleged conduct occurred.

6. Where a conflict exists, the fundamental civil rights and sentencing limitations established by the Civil Rights & Justice Code shall remain controlling.

## §1.2 — Criminal Classifications

Criminal offenses shall be classified as follows:

**Infraction**

A minor violation ordinarily punishable by a monetary fine or another authorized noncustodial penalty.

**Class C Misdemeanor**

A minor criminal offense ordinarily punishable by a monetary fine and, where authorized, a short custodial sentence.

**Class B Misdemeanor**

A moderate criminal offense involving more serious unlawful conduct, a repeated qualifying violation, or a greater threat to public order.

**Class A Misdemeanor**

A serious misdemeanor that may involve violence, substantial danger, or significant unlawful interference with persons or property.

**Class C Felony**

A lower-level felony involving significant criminal conduct, property loss, violence, or public danger.

**Class B Felony**

A serious felony involving substantial violence, serious injury, organized criminal conduct, or other grave wrongdoing.

**Class A Felony**

A major felony involving extraordinary violence, severe criminal harm, or another offense designated for enhanced judicial sentencing.

**Capital Felony**

An exceptionally serious offense expressly designated as eligible for life imprisonment or capital punishment under applicable law.

Classification alone shall not establish guilt or authorize a penalty exceeding the specific offense provision.

## §1.3 — General Penalty Schedule

Unless a specific offense establishes a different lawful penalty range, the following schedule shall apply:

| Classification | Authorized Fine | Imprisonment |
|---|---|---|
| Infraction | $100–$1,000 | None |
| Class C Misdemeanor | $500–$2,500 | 0–10 minutes |
| Class B Misdemeanor | $1,000–$5,000 | 5–20 minutes |
| Class A Misdemeanor | $2,500–$10,000 | 10–40 minutes |
| Class C Felony | $5,000–$15,000 | 20–60 minutes |
| Class B Felony | $10,000–$30,000 | 45–120 minutes |
| Class A Felony | $20,000–$60,000 | 90 minutes–6 hours |
| Capital Felony | As prescribed by offense | Judicially determined |

1. These ranges establish the ordinary sentencing limits for each classification.

2. A specific offense may establish a more limited or otherwise expressly authorized penalty.

3. Criminal fines shall be imposed according to the applicable offense and sentencing authority.

4. Monetary penalties shall not be increased arbitrarily according to an individual's wealth.

5. Restitution may be imposed separately where authorized and supported by evidence of compensable harm.

6. Multiple fixed-term sentences imposed in the same proceeding shall remain subject to the seven-real-world-day maximum established under the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

7. Life imprisonment and capital punishment shall be imposed only when expressly authorized for a qualifying offense and following the applicable human judicial proceedings.

8. A sentence exceeding six hours of imprisonment shall require an offense-specific authorization and an appropriate judicial determination.

9. No fixed-term sentence shall exceed seven real-world days, equivalent to 168 hours, for a single sentencing event.

## §1.4 — Elements of Criminal Liability

1. A criminal conviction shall require proof of each element of the charged offense.

2. Where an offense requires intentional conduct, the prosecution shall establish the applicable criminal intent.

3. Where an offense requires knowledge, recklessness, or negligence, that requirement shall be established through admissible evidence.

4. Mere suspicion, reputation, association, or prior arrest shall not independently establish guilt.

5. An individual's exercise of lawful civil rights shall not constitute criminal conduct solely because an officer disapproves of that exercise.

6. Criminal guilt shall be established beyond a reasonable doubt.

## §1.5 — Attempted Offenses

1. A person commits a criminal attempt when, acting with the intent required for a designated offense, they take a substantial step toward its commission but fail to complete it.

2. Mere preparation, discussion, or an unacted-upon intention shall not independently constitute an attempt.

3. Attempted offenses may be prosecuted where recognized by the applicable criminal provision.

4. An attempted offense shall not automatically carry the same penalty as a completed offense unless expressly authorized.

5. Where no specific attempted-offense penalty is provided, the maximum authorized imprisonment and fine shall not exceed one-half of the completed offense's ordinary maximum, unless another law provides otherwise.

## §1.6 — Accomplice Liability

1. A person who knowingly and intentionally assists another in committing a criminal offense may be charged as an accomplice where the applicable elements are established.

2. Accomplice liability shall require evidence of intentional participation or assistance.

3. Mere presence at a crime scene shall not independently establish accomplice liability.

4. Mere association with a criminal organization shall not establish responsibility for every offense committed by its members.

5. An accomplice's sentence shall reflect the applicable offense and their established criminal participation.

## §1.7 — Criminal Conspiracy

1. A criminal conspiracy occurs when two or more persons knowingly agree to commit a designated criminal offense and an overt act in furtherance of the agreement occurs.

2. Mere discussion of unlawful activity without an agreement and qualifying act shall not establish conspiracy.

3. Participation shall be established individually.

4. A conspiracy charge shall not automatically establish that the underlying offense was completed.

5. Penalties shall be determined by the applicable conspiracy offense or authorized sentencing provisions.

## §1.8 — Multiple Criminal Charges

1. A person may be charged with multiple distinct offenses arising from a criminal incident.

2. Each offense shall require proof of its separate elements.

3. The State shall not impose duplicative punishment for the same offense contrary to the protection against double jeopardy.

4. Where offenses merge under applicable law, punishment shall be imposed only as permitted by the controlling legal rule.

5. Consecutive fixed-term sentences arising from one sentencing event shall remain subject to the statutory seven-day maximum.

6. Separate charging documents shall not be used to circumvent the seven-day limit for offenses adjudicated as part of the same criminal proceeding.

## §1.9 — Prior Convictions and Repeat Offenses

1. Prior convictions may be considered at sentencing where expressly authorized.

2. A prior arrest without conviction shall not be treated as a prior criminal conviction.

3. Repeat-offender enhancements shall apply only where established by the specific offense or another duly enacted provision.

4. Prior convictions shall not independently authorize life imprisonment or capital punishment.

5. A subsequent serious conviction following life imprisonment may establish eligibility for capital sentencing only under the requirements established by Section 8 of the Civil Rights & Justice Code and the applicable capital offense.

6. The existence of a prior conviction shall not relieve the prosecution of its burden to prove a new charge.

7. Escape from correctional custody shall not, by itself, constitute grounds for capital punishment.

## §1.10 — Sentencing Authority

1. Authorized law enforcement officers may issue fines and other routine dispositions expressly delegated to them by law.

2. Officers shall not unilaterally adjudicate contested offenses requiring a trial.

3. Contested charges shall be referred to an authorized judicial authority.

4. Class A and Class B felony convictions requiring custodial sentencing shall receive judicial authorization unless a separately enacted procedure expressly permits a qualifying admitted disposition.

5. Class A felony sentences involving extraordinary confinement shall require human judicial review.

6. Life imprisonment and capital punishment shall require a qualifying conviction and human judicial proceedings.

7. Automated judicial systems may act only within their specifically delegated powers.

8. Prosecutors may recommend sentences but shall not independently exercise authority reserved to judges.

9. No law enforcement officer, prosecutor, or correctional officer shall independently impose a sentence exceeding their legally delegated authority.

## §1.11 — Fines and Financial Penalties

1. Criminal fines shall be determined under the applicable statutory schedule.

2. An individual shall be informed of the charges and financial penalties imposed.

3. Fines shall be recorded through an authorized government payment and criminal records system.

4. No officer shall personally collect or retain criminal fines outside a lawful payment process.

5. A fine shall not automatically result in imprisonment solely because the individual cannot immediately pay.

6. Courts may establish lawful payment arrangements or other authorized remedies.

7. Criminal fines shall remain distinct from civil restitution, commercial debt, towing charges, and license fees.

## §1.12 — Restitution

1. A court may order restitution for qualifying financial loss, property damage, medical expenses, or other legally recognized harm arising from criminal conduct.

2. Restitution shall be based upon reasonably established loss.

3. Restitution shall not be used as an unauthorized additional criminal fine.

4. The person ordered to pay restitution may challenge the amount through appropriate judicial procedures.

5. Restitution orders may be enforced through lawful civil collection procedures.

6. Inability to pay restitution shall not independently create an additional criminal offense.

## §1.13 — Criminal Record Accuracy

1. All criminal charges and final dispositions shall be accurately recorded.

2. Arrest records shall distinguish allegations from convictions.

3. Dismissed or acquitted charges shall not be recorded as convictions.

4. Sentence completion and lawful release shall be reflected in the applicable records.

5. Unauthorized alteration of criminal records may constitute an independent offense.

6. Individuals may challenge inaccurate records through the Department of Justice.

## §1.14 — Prohibited Conduct and Community Standards

1. No provision of the Rosefire Penal Code shall be interpreted as authorizing conduct expressly prohibited by the State's established community conduct rules.

2. The existence of a criminal offense within state law shall not independently constitute permission to portray that offense in roleplay.

3. Where a form of criminal roleplay is expressly prohibited, the applicable community conduct restrictions shall take precedence over any apparent roleplay permission arising from this Code.

4. Criminal investigations and judicial proceedings shall not be used to compel or facilitate prohibited roleplay scenarios.

5. Enforcement of community conduct rules shall remain separate from in-character criminal prosecution.

6. The Department of Justice shall not recognize an in-character criminal proceeding as authority to disregard established community conduct restrictions.

## §1.15 — Exceptional Fixed-Term Sentences

1. The State recognizes exceptional fixed-term sentences for designated serious criminal offenses.

2. Exceptional sentences may exceed the ordinary six-hour Class A Felony maximum only where the applicable offense expressly authorizes such punishment.

3. A sentence exceeding six hours shall require an appropriate judicial determination.

4. Exceptional fixed-term sentences may be imposed within the following statutory ranges when expressly authorized:

   a. **Exceptional Felony:** More than six hours, up to 24 hours.

   b. **Extraordinary Serious Offense:** More than 24 hours, up to seven real-world days.

5. The designation of an offense as exceptional shall not independently establish eligibility for life imprisonment.

6. A fixed-term sentence shall never exceed 168 hours for a single sentencing event.

7. Life imprisonment and capital punishment shall remain separate sentencing categories governed by the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

8. An exceptional fixed-term sentence shall progress according to actual elapsed time, including periods when the sentenced individual is not present within the State, subject to an expressly applicable lawful interruption.
