# SECTION 8 — CONTROLLED SUBSTANCE OFFENSES

## §8.1 — Unlawful Possession of a Restricted Controlled Substance

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a controlled substance requiring specific legal authorization without possessing that authorization.

2. The State shall establish the substance's classification and applicable restriction.

3. Authorized medical, scientific, or commercial possession shall not constitute this offense.

4. Personal possession shall not automatically establish intent to distribute.

5. The substance must be identified through an appropriate evidentiary basis.

## §8.2 — Possession of a Prohibited Controlled Substance

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a substance legally designated as prohibited for ordinary civilian possession.

2. The State shall establish the substance's prohibited classification.

3. The mere presence of a prohibited substance near an individual shall not automatically establish possession.

4. Authorized exceptions shall remain applicable.

5. The offense shall not automatically establish manufacturing or trafficking intent.

## §8.3 — Unlawful Possession of Cannabis

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing cannabis in circumstances expressly prohibited under the applicable cannabis regulatory schedule.

2. Lawful personal possession shall not constitute this offense.

3. An authorized cannabis seller may lawfully possess qualifying commercial inventory within the scope of their authorization.

4. The State shall establish the specific restriction violated.

5. Cannabis possession alone shall not constitute probable cause where such possession is lawful.

## §8.4 — Unlawful Public Consumption of a Controlled Substance

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly consuming a designated controlled substance in a location where such consumption is expressly prohibited by law.

2. The State shall establish the substance and applicable location restriction.

3. Consumption permitted by law shall not constitute an offense.

4. Mere appearance of intoxication shall not establish unlawful consumption.

5. Medical emergencies shall be addressed according to applicable emergency response procedures.

## §8.5 — Unlawful Distribution of a Restricted Controlled Substance

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly selling, supplying, or distributing a restricted controlled substance outside applicable legal authorization.

2. The State shall establish the relevant substance classification and prohibited distribution.

3. Authorized medical and commercial transfers shall not constitute this offense.

4. Mere possession shall not independently establish distribution.

5. Separate trafficking provisions may apply where their additional elements are established.

## §8.6 — Distribution of a Prohibited Controlled Substance

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly selling, supplying, or distributing a substance prohibited from ordinary civilian distribution.

2. The State shall establish the prohibited substance and actual distribution conduct.

3. A completed financial transaction shall not be necessary where unlawful supply is otherwise established.

4. Mere possession of a prohibited substance shall not establish distribution.

5. Medical exceptions and other lawful authorizations shall remain applicable where expressly recognized.

## §8.7 — Possession With Intent to Distribute

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a controlled substance with the specific intent to distribute it unlawfully.

2. Intent may be established through admissible evidence concerning quantity, packaging, transactions, communications, or other relevant circumstances.

3. Quantity alone shall not conclusively establish distribution intent.

4. Ordinary lawful commercial inventory shall not constitute unlawful possession with intent to distribute.

5. The prosecution shall prove the required intent beyond a reasonable doubt.

## §8.8 — Unlicensed Cannabis Sales

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly engaging in commercial cannabis sales without possessing the Weed Seller License or other authorization required by law.

2. The State shall establish the applicable licensing requirement and qualifying sale.

3. Lawful personal possession or a noncommercial transfer permitted by law shall not constitute this offense.

4. A general business license shall not automatically substitute for a Weed Seller License.

5. Administrative licensing violations lacking the required criminal elements shall remain subject to appropriate administrative proceedings.

## §8.9 — Sale of Cannabis Outside Licensed Conditions

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A licensed cannabis seller commits this offense by knowingly conducting a regulated cannabis sale in violation of a specific legally established sales restriction.

2. Applicable restrictions may concern locations, product classifications, authorized business operations, or other published licensing conditions.

3. The State shall identify the restriction violated.

4. An ordinary administrative mistake shall not automatically establish intentional criminal wrongdoing.

5. License suspension or revocation shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §8.10 — Unlawful Cannabis Cultivation

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly cultivating cannabis contrary to an applicable licensing or cultivation restriction.

2. Cultivation authorizations shall be established through the official cannabis regulatory schedule.

3. A Weed Seller License shall not automatically authorize unrestricted commercial cultivation.

4. Lawful personal cultivation, where expressly permitted, shall not constitute the offense.

5. The State shall establish the applicable violation and relevant criminal knowledge.

## §8.11 — Unlawful Commercial Cannabis Production

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly conducting commercial-scale cannabis production or processing without the required legal authorization.

2. The State shall establish the regulated activity and applicable authorization requirement.

3. Mere possession of agricultural tools or equipment shall not establish unlawful production.

4. Lawful cultivation and processing operations shall remain protected.

5. Commercial inventory may be seized only upon appropriate legal grounds.

## §8.12 — Unlawful Manufacturing of Restricted Substances

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly manufacturing or processing a restricted controlled substance without the authorization required by law.

2. The State shall establish the substance classification, regulated manufacturing activity, and lack of authorization.

3. Lawful medical and scientific production shall not constitute the offense.

4. Ordinary possession of dual-purpose equipment shall not establish manufacturing.

5. Hazardous operations may be subject to lawful emergency intervention.

## §8.13 — Manufacturing a Prohibited Controlled Substance

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly manufacturing, refining, or processing a prohibited controlled substance without applicable legal authority.

2. The State shall establish the prohibited classification and qualifying manufacturing conduct.

3. The presence of suspicious equipment alone shall not establish guilt.

4. Authorized scientific or other specifically permitted activities shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.

5. Separate offenses may apply for independently established hazardous conduct or resulting harm.

## §8.14 — Operation of a Hazardous Illegal Drug Laboratory

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating an unlawful controlled-substance manufacturing location that creates a substantial and unjustifiable danger to persons or property.

2. The State shall establish the prohibited manufacturing activity and hazardous circumstances.

3. Ordinary lawful industrial operations shall not constitute this offense merely because chemicals or specialized equipment are present.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §8.15 — Transportation of a Prohibited Controlled Substance

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly transporting a prohibited controlled substance contrary to law.

2. The State shall establish the knowing transportation and prohibited classification.

3. Mere presence as a passenger shall not independently establish criminal participation.

4. Lawfully authorized transport shall remain exempt within its scope.

5. Searches of vehicles and cargo shall comply with applicable law.

## §8.16 — Unlawful Commercial Drug Transportation

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly transporting controlled substances as part of an unlawful commercial distribution operation.

2. The State shall establish knowledge of the cargo and participation in the prohibited transport.

3. A commercial driver shall not be presumed guilty solely because unlawful cargo was concealed by another person.

4. A valid CDL shall not authorize prohibited substance transportation.

5. Lawful cargo shall remain protected against arbitrary permanent forfeiture.

## §8.17 — Controlled Substance Trafficking

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits controlled-substance trafficking by knowingly participating in the unlawful commercial-scale production, transportation, supply, or distribution of designated controlled substances.

2. The State shall establish the qualifying scale and intentional criminal participation.

3. Mere possession of a substance shall not automatically constitute trafficking.

4. Lawful cannabis commerce and authorized controlled-substance distribution shall not constitute trafficking.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

6. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §8.18 — Organized Drug Trafficking

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits organized drug trafficking by knowingly participating in a coordinated criminal enterprise engaged in substantial unlawful controlled-substance distribution or production.

2. The State shall establish intentional participation and qualifying organized activity.

3. Mere friendship, association, or presence near suspected traffickers shall not establish guilt.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.

5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §8.19 — Extraordinary Drug Trafficking Operation

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $40,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly directing or substantially participating in an exceptionally large unlawful controlled-substance production or distribution operation involving coordinated criminal activity and significant established scale.

2. The State shall establish the enterprise's qualifying scope and the defendant's individual responsibility.

3. Ordinary unlicensed personal sales shall not constitute this offense.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.

5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. This offense shall not independently authorize life imprisonment.

## §8.20 — Distribution to a Legally Prohibited Recipient

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly distributing a regulated controlled substance to an individual whom applicable law expressly prohibits from receiving it.

2. The State shall identify the eligibility restriction and establish the accused's knowledge.

3. A recipient's apparent age or status alone shall not conclusively establish knowing unlawful distribution.

4. Lawful medical administration and other authorized activities shall remain protected.

5. This provision shall not authorize conduct prohibited by Rosefire's community conduct rules.

## §8.21 — Falsification of Controlled Substance Records

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly falsifying material records legally required for controlled-substance production, possession, transportation, or sale.

2. The State shall establish the applicable recordkeeping requirement and material falsification.

3. Ordinary administrative errors shall not automatically constitute criminal falsification.

4. Authorized corrections shall not constitute this offense.

5. Separate fraud charges may apply where independently established.

## §8.22 — Diversion of Medical Controlled Substances

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly diverting lawfully held restricted medical substances for unauthorized distribution or other prohibited use.

2. The State shall establish the lawful custody, unauthorized diversion, and required criminal intent.

3. Ordinary lawful medical administration shall not constitute diversion.

4. Medical personnel shall not be presumed guilty solely because they handle regulated substances.

5. Protected medical records shall remain subject to applicable confidentiality laws.

## §8.23 — Unlawful Possession of Drug Manufacturing Materials With Criminal Intent

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing specifically regulated manufacturing materials with the intent to use them in a designated unlawful controlled-substance manufacturing offense.

2. The State shall establish the applicable material restriction and specific criminal intent.

3. Ordinary possession of legal tools, chemicals, or agricultural supplies shall not independently constitute an offense.

4. Lawful industrial, agricultural, and medical activities shall remain protected.

## §8.24 — Controlled Substance Distribution Through a Fraudulent Business

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly using a materially fraudulent business operation to conceal or facilitate unlawful controlled-substance distribution.

2. The State shall establish the underlying prohibited distribution and intentional fraudulent business use.

3. A lawful business shall not be criminally responsible solely because a customer or independent supplier commits an unrelated offense.

4. Business records shall remain subject to applicable search protections.

5. License revocation and asset forfeiture shall require separate lawful authority.

## §8.25 — Drug-Related Money Laundering

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly conducting financial transactions intended to conceal the unlawful origin, ownership, or control of proceeds from qualifying controlled-substance crimes.

2. The State shall establish the underlying unlawful proceeds and required concealment intent.

3. Ordinary banking transactions shall not independently constitute money laundering.

4. The mere use of cash or a financial institution shall not establish criminal liability.

5. Financial records shall be obtained according to applicable law.

## §8.26 — Organized Drug Money Laundering

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly participating in a coordinated scheme to conceal or transfer substantial proceeds of organized controlled-substance crimes.

2. The State shall establish the organized financial conduct and qualifying criminal proceeds.

3. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

4. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

5. Lawful financial institutions shall not be presumed complicit solely because their services were used.

## §8.27 — Controlled Substance Evidence Tampering

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly altering, substituting, destroying, or falsifying controlled-substance evidence with intent to impair an actual or reasonably anticipated investigation or judicial proceeding.

2. The State shall establish the corrupt intent and relevant evidence interference.

3. Authorized evidence testing and disposal shall not constitute tampering.

4. Public officials and private persons may be charged where the required elements exist.

5. Duplicative punishment with the general evidence-tampering offense shall remain subject to merger rules.

## §8.28 — Theft of Controlled Substances From a Medical Facility

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully taking designated controlled substances from a recognized medical facility.

2. The State shall establish the unlawful taking and relevant protected substances.

3. Lawful medical inventory transfer shall not constitute theft.

4. Violence or separately established serious criminal conduct may support additional charges.

5. Medical privacy and evidence protections shall remain applicable.

## §8.29 — Controlled Substance Property Forfeiture

1. Property connected to qualifying controlled-substance offenses may be temporarily seized where lawful grounds exist.

2. Temporary evidence seizure shall not automatically establish permanent forfeiture.

3. Permanent forfeiture shall require appropriate legal authority and due process.

4. Innocent-owner and lawful third-party property interests shall remain protected.

5. A financial account shall not automatically be forfeited solely because its owner is suspected of drug activity.

6. Legitimate business property unrelated to an offense shall not be confiscated without lawful authority.

7. Individuals may petition for return of unlawfully seized property.

## §8.30 — Medical Treatment and Rehabilitation

1. A person suspected or convicted of a controlled-substance offense shall remain entitled to applicable medical protections.

2. Emergency medical assistance shall not be denied solely because the individual is suspected of unlawful drug use.

3. Courts may consider treatment or rehabilitation as an authorized sentencing alternative where lawful.

4. Treatment participation shall not automatically excuse independently established serious criminal conduct.

5. An individual shall not be compelled to confess to a criminal offense merely to obtain emergency treatment.

6. Rehabilitation and correctional programs shall remain subject to the Civil Rights & Justice Code.
