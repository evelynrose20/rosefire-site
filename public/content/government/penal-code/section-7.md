# ROSEFIRE PENAL CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.4 — Sections 7–8**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing criminal penalties for unlawful possession, handling, trafficking, and use of weapons and firearms; and for unlawful controlled-substance possession, cultivation, manufacture, distribution, transportation, and trafficking within the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–6 remain enacted and in force. Sections 7–8 constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Penal Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code, including its firearm regulations under Section 12 and controlled-substance regulations under Section 13.

---

# SECTION 7 — WEAPONS & FIREARM OFFENSES

## §7.1 — Unlawful Possession of a Class I Firearm

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a Class I firearm without the license or other authorization required by law.

2. The State shall establish the firearm's classification and the applicable licensing requirement.

3. Lawful possession under a recognized exception shall not constitute this offense.

4. The mere inability to immediately produce a physical license shall not establish that no valid authorization exists.

5. A firearm may be temporarily secured where lawful grounds exist.

## §7.2 — Unlawful Possession of a Class II Firearm

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a Class II restricted firearm without the authorization required for that classification.

2. An ordinary Class I firearm license shall not automatically authorize Class II possession unless expressly recognized by law.

3. The prosecution shall establish the restricted classification and lack of lawful authorization.

4. Lawful occupational, governmental, or specially licensed possession shall remain protected.

5. Seizure and forfeiture shall follow applicable legal procedures.

## §7.3 — Unlawful Possession of a Class III Weapon

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a Class III automatic or specially restricted weapon without lawful authorization.

2. The official firearm classification schedule shall determine which weapons fall within Class III.

3. A person's ordinary firearm license shall not independently authorize unrestricted possession of Class III weapons.

4. Authorized government personnel and other specifically approved holders shall remain subject to the conditions of their authorization.

5. The State shall prove possession, classification, and the absence of applicable authority.

## §7.4 — Possession of a Prohibited Class IV Weapon

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a Class IV prohibited military-grade weapon outside an expressly authorized exception.

2. The State shall establish that the weapon appears on the legally effective Class IV schedule.

3. A firearm shall not be classified as Class IV solely because an officer considers it unusually dangerous.

4. Authorized military or other lawful government custody shall be evaluated according to applicable law.

5. Permanent confiscation shall remain subject to the appropriate legal process.

## §7.5 — Carrying a Firearm Without Required Authorization

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly carrying a firearm in a manner requiring a license or endorsement that they do not lawfully possess.

2. Open and concealed carrying shall be evaluated according to the actual licensing requirements established by law.

3. Lawful open carry shall not independently constitute reasonable suspicion or probable cause.

4. Concealed carry shall not be presumed unlawful without evidence of an applicable violation.

5. A person shall not be convicted merely because an officer is unfamiliar with a valid authorization.

## §7.6 — Carrying a Firearm in a Prohibited Location

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly carrying a firearm in a location where such carrying is expressly prohibited by law.

2. Restricted locations may include designated correctional facilities, secure courtrooms, and other legally established firearm exclusion areas.

3. The applicable restriction shall be legally effective and reasonably identifiable.

4. Authorized law enforcement and other recognized exemptions shall remain applicable.

5. Ordinary government property shall not automatically constitute a prohibited firearm location.

## §7.7 — Unlawful Brandishing of a Weapon

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits unlawful brandishing by intentionally displaying or presenting a weapon in a threatening manner without lawful justification.

2. Merely carrying a lawfully possessed firearm shall not constitute brandishing.

3. The State shall establish the threatening conduct and lack of lawful justification.

4. Defensive presentation of a weapon may be justified under the applicable self-defense provisions.

5. Brandishing shall remain distinct from aggravated assault unless the latter offense's additional elements are established.

## §7.8 — Reckless Discharge of a Firearm

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits reckless discharge by firing a weapon while consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk to persons or property.

2. The State shall establish the reckless conduct.

3. An accidental discharge without the required criminal recklessness shall not automatically establish this offense.

4. Authorized training, lawful hunting, and justified defensive uses shall be evaluated according to their circumstances.

5. Resulting injury or death may support additional charges where independently established.

## §7.9 — Unlawful Discharge Within a Restricted Area

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly discharging a firearm within an area where discharge is lawfully prohibited.

2. Restricted discharge areas shall be established through applicable legislation or official geographic schedules.

3. Lawful self-defense and authorized emergency operations may provide recognized exceptions.

4. The mere possession of a firearm within such an area shall not establish unlawful discharge.

5. The State shall prove that an applicable restriction was in effect.

## §7.10 — Discharging a Firearm From a Motor Vehicle

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully discharging a firearm from or within an occupied motor vehicle.

2. The State shall establish the discharge, applicable criminal intent, and unlawful circumstances.

3. Authorized law enforcement action and legally justified defense shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.

4. Serious injury or homicide may support separate charges where independently established.

5. Lawful transportation of a secured firearm shall not constitute this offense.

## §7.11 — Unlawful Use of a Firearm During a Criminal Offense

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally using or threatening immediate use of a firearm to facilitate an independently established criminal offense.

2. Mere firearm ownership or lawful possession shall not satisfy this provision.

3. The State shall prove the weapon-related conduct and the underlying criminal offense.

4. Where the underlying offense already includes identical firearm use as an element, applicable merger and double-jeopardy rules shall prevent duplicative punishment.

5. A separate charge may proceed only where independently punishable conduct is established.

## §7.12 — Unlawful Firearm Transfer

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly transferring a firearm in violation of a legally established transfer restriction.

2. Applicable restrictions may concern weapon classification, recipient eligibility, or required transfer authorization.

3. Lawful transfers between authorized parties shall not constitute the offense.

4. The State shall establish the applicable restriction and knowing violation.

5. A clerical discrepancy shall not automatically establish criminal intent.

## §7.13 — Unlicensed Firearm Dealing

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly engaging in commercial firearm sales or distribution without the required dealer authorization.

2. The State shall establish an actual commercial dealing activity and the applicable licensing requirement.

3. An isolated lawful personal transfer shall not independently constitute commercial dealing.

4. A general business license shall not automatically substitute for a specifically required firearm dealer authorization.

5. Commercial inventory may be seized only where lawful grounds exist.

## §7.14 — Firearm Trafficking

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits firearm trafficking by knowingly participating in a coordinated unlawful commercial operation to supply, transport, or distribute restricted or prohibited firearms.

2. The State shall establish intentional participation and qualifying unlawful distribution.

3. Ordinary lawful transportation of privately owned firearms shall not constitute trafficking.

4. Merely possessing multiple firearms shall not automatically establish a trafficking operation.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

## §7.15 — Trafficking of Class IV Weapons

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly participating in the unlawful commercial supply or coordinated distribution of weapons designated as Class IV.

2. The State shall prove the prohibited classification and qualifying trafficking conduct.

3. Authorized government transport shall not constitute this offense.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.

5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §7.16 — Possession of a Stolen Firearm

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a firearm with knowledge that it was unlawfully stolen.

2. Possession alone shall not establish knowledge of the firearm's stolen status.

3. The State shall prove the firearm's unlawful origin and the accused's relevant knowledge.

4. Good-faith acquisition may be considered according to applicable law.

5. The firearm may be held as evidence and returned through lawful procedures.

## §7.17 — Possession of a Firearm With an Altered Identifier

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a firearm whose legally required identifying markings have been intentionally removed or altered for an unlawful purpose.

2. The State shall establish the applicable identification requirement.

3. Ordinary cosmetic modifications shall not automatically constitute unlawful alteration.

4. A genuine manufacturing or maintenance defect shall not independently establish criminal conduct.

5. The required knowledge and unlawful circumstances shall be proven.

## §7.18 — Unlawful Modification of a Firearm

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly modifying a firearm into a prohibited configuration contrary to applicable law.

2. Prohibited modifications shall be established through the official weapons schedule.

3. Authorized maintenance, repair, and lawful cosmetic modification shall not constitute this offense.

4. A modification shall not be deemed unlawful merely because it changes appearance or improves ordinary performance.

5. The State shall establish the prohibited configuration and knowing conduct.

## §7.19 — Unlawful Manufacture of Firearms

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly manufacturing firearms in violation of applicable licensing or weapon classification restrictions.

2. Authorized commercial manufacturing and lawful repair shall remain protected.

3. Mere possession of mechanical tools shall not establish unlawful manufacture.

4. The State shall prove the regulated manufacturing activity and applicable violation.

5. Criminal intent and classification requirements shall be evaluated according to the specific offense.

## §7.20 — Unlawful Possession of Restricted Ammunition

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing ammunition expressly designated as restricted or prohibited without required authorization.

2. Ordinary ammunition lawful for civilian use shall not constitute restricted ammunition solely because it is capable of causing serious injury.

3. Ammunition restrictions shall be established through an official schedule.

4. Authorized professional and governmental possession shall remain subject to applicable exceptions.

## §7.21 — Possession of an Unlawful Explosive Device

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing an explosive device expressly prohibited by law without applicable authorization.

2. Authorized industrial, commercial, and governmental activities shall remain subject to specific exemptions.

3. The State shall establish the device's prohibited classification and the accused's knowing possession.

4. Ordinary lawful tools, equipment, or commercial materials shall not independently constitute an explosive device.

5. Emergency safety actions may be authorized where an immediate danger exists.

## §7.22 — Unlawful Use of an Explosive Device

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and unlawfully detonating or deploying an explosive device in a manner presenting substantial danger to persons or property.

2. The State shall establish the intentional unlawful use and qualifying danger.

3. Authorized demolition, industrial work, and emergency operations shall not constitute this offense when conducted lawfully.

4. Resulting serious injury or death may support independent charges.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

## §7.23 — Extraordinary Explosive Attack

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally carrying out a major unlawful explosive attack that causes widespread destruction and creates a substantial risk of multiple deaths or serious bodily injuries.

2. The State shall establish the qualifying scale, intentional act, and substantial danger.

3. An isolated minor weapons violation shall not constitute an extraordinary explosive attack.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.

5. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

6. Life imprisonment shall not be imposed unless a separately established life-eligible offense is proven.

## §7.24 — Unlawful Weapon Possession by a Disqualified Person

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing a firearm while subject to a valid legal prohibition against such possession.

2. The State shall establish the existence, legal validity, and applicable scope of the prohibition.

3. A prior arrest without an enforceable disqualification shall not independently establish this offense.

4. Temporary seizure of a firearm shall not automatically constitute permanent disqualification.

5. Disqualification and restoration procedures shall remain subject to applicable law.

## §7.25 — Unlawful Use of a Weapon by a Public Official

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A public official commits this offense by knowingly using an issued or otherwise possessed weapon for an unlawful purpose through misuse of official authority.

2. The State shall establish the unlawful conduct and required criminal intent.

3. Lawful and proportionate use of force shall not constitute this offense.

4. Official employment shall not exempt a person from ordinary criminal weapons laws.

5. Additional official misconduct charges may apply where independently established.

## §7.26 — Firearm Evidence and Forfeiture

1. Weapons seized during criminal investigations shall be documented and handled according to applicable evidence procedures.

2. Seizure as evidence shall not automatically transfer ownership to the State.

3. Lawfully possessed weapons shall be returned when no lawful retention basis remains.

4. Weapons designated as prohibited contraband may be disposed of according to applicable law.

5. Permanent forfeiture shall require appropriate statutory or judicial authority.

6. Lawful third-party ownership interests shall be considered where applicable.

7. Officers shall not personally appropriate seized weapons.
