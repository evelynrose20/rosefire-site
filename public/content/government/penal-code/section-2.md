# SECTION 2 — CRIMES AGAINST PERSONS

## §2.1 — Simple Assault

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$4,000  
**Imprisonment:** 5–20 minutes

1. A person commits simple assault by intentionally causing another person reasonably to fear imminent unlawful physical harm through a threatening act.

2. Mere verbal criticism or an insult, without conduct satisfying the offense, shall not constitute assault.

3. Lawful defensive conduct shall not constitute criminal assault.

4. The prosecution shall establish intentional threatening conduct and the required apprehension of imminent harm.

## §2.2 — Battery

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$7,500  
**Imprisonment:** 10–35 minutes

1. A person commits battery by intentionally making unlawful harmful or offensive physical contact with another person.

2. Battery does not require permanent injury.

3. Ordinary accidental contact without the required criminal fault shall not constitute battery.

4. Lawful physical contact undertaken in justified self-defense or another recognized legal capacity shall not constitute criminal battery.

## §2.3 — Aggravated Assault

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$12,500  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. Aggravated assault occurs when a person commits assault while:

   a. Using or threatening immediate use of a deadly weapon.

   b. Creating a substantial risk of serious bodily injury.

   c. Engaging in another aggravating circumstance expressly established by law.

2. A weapon's mere lawful presence shall not independently establish aggravated assault.

3. The State shall establish the relevant threatening conduct and aggravating circumstances.

## §2.4 — Aggravated Battery

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$25,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits aggravated battery when they intentionally inflict serious bodily injury upon another through unlawful physical force.

2. Serious bodily injury may include substantial risk of death, major bodily impairment, or significant traumatic injury.

3. Use of a deadly weapon may constitute an aggravating circumstance where the required elements are established.

4. Injuries shall be supported by appropriate evidence.

5. Lawful and proportionate self-defense shall remain an available defense.

## §2.5 — Criminal Threats

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$6,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–30 minutes

1. A person commits criminal threats by knowingly communicating a serious threat of unlawful violence against an identifiable person with the intent to place that person in reasonable fear of its execution.

2. Threats may be communicated verbally, in writing, or through electronic means.

3. Mere political criticism, insulting language, or nonthreatening disagreement shall not constitute this offense.

4. The context and circumstances shall be considered in determining whether a genuine criminal threat occurred.

## §2.6 — Unlawful Restraint

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$12,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits unlawful restraint by knowingly restricting another individual's freedom of movement without consent or lawful authority.

2. Restraint may be physical or accomplished by a credible immediate threat of force.

3. A brief lawful detention by an authorized officer shall not constitute unlawful restraint.

4. A restraint undertaken under a recognized legal justification shall not constitute this offense.

## §2.7 — Kidnapping

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $12,500–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 60–120 minutes

1. A person commits kidnapping by knowingly abducting, transporting, or unlawfully confining another person through force, threats, or deception with the required criminal intent.

2. Kidnapping may include detention for ransom, coercion, unlawful confinement, or another designated unlawful purpose.

3. The prosecution shall establish the unlawful taking or confinement and the applicable intent.

4. Separate qualifying violence or extortion offenses may be charged where independently supported.

5. This provision shall not authorize conduct prohibited under Rosefire's community conduct rules.

## §2.8 — Aggravated Kidnapping

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$55,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. Aggravated kidnapping occurs when a qualifying kidnapping involves one or more of the following:

   a. A credible threat to kill the victim.

   b. Intentional infliction of serious bodily injury.

   c. A deadly weapon used to maintain confinement.

   d. A substantial ransom demand accompanied by a credible threat of serious violence.

   e. Another circumstance expressly designated as aggravating.

2. Each aggravating circumstance shall require sufficient evidence.

3. A court may consider the duration, harm, and manner of confinement within the authorized sentencing range.

4. This provision shall not authorize conduct prohibited under Rosefire's community conduct rules.

## §2.9 — Reckless Endangerment

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits reckless endangerment by consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk of serious bodily injury to another person.

2. The conduct must represent a gross deviation from reasonable care under the circumstances.

3. Actual injury shall not be required where the statutory danger is established.

4. Mere accidental conduct without the required recklessness shall not constitute this offense.

## §2.10 — Involuntary Manslaughter

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$45,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–5 hours

1. A person commits involuntary manslaughter when they unlawfully cause another person's death through criminally reckless conduct, without the intent required for murder.

2. The State shall establish causation and the applicable criminal mental state.

3. An accidental death shall not automatically constitute manslaughter.

4. Death resulting from vehicular conduct may be charged under the applicable vehicular homicide provision where established.

## §2.11 — Voluntary Manslaughter

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–6 hours

1. Voluntary manslaughter occurs when a person intentionally or knowingly causes another person's death under circumstances of legally adequate provocation or another recognized mitigating condition that reduces an otherwise qualifying murder offense.

2. Mere anger, jealousy, or ordinary disagreement shall not automatically establish adequate provocation.

3. The State shall establish the underlying unlawful killing.

4. Any legally recognized mitigation shall be determined through the appropriate judicial process.

## §2.12 — Second-Degree Murder

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 4–6 hours

1. A person commits second-degree murder when they unlawfully cause another person's death with the criminal mental state required for murder, without establishing the additional elements of first-degree murder.

2. A killing committed knowingly or with extreme disregard for human life may constitute second-degree murder where the applicable elements are established.

3. The prosecution shall prove the unlawful killing, causation, and required mental state beyond a reasonable doubt.

4. The existence of a prior dispute shall not automatically establish murder.

5. Life imprisonment shall not be imposed for this offense unless another duly enacted provision expressly makes the established conduct life-eligible.

## §2.13 — First-Degree Murder

**Classification:** Capital Felony  
**Fine:** Up to $75,000  
**Sentence:** 6 hours to 7 real-world days, or life imprisonment upon authorized judicial determination

1. A person commits first-degree murder by intentionally and unlawfully causing another person's death with premeditation and deliberation.

2. Premeditation shall require evidence of a conscious decision to kill formed before the fatal act.

3. The State shall prove the unlawful killing, causation, intent, and premeditation beyond a reasonable doubt.

4. A conviction shall require human judicial determination before life imprisonment may be imposed.

5. The court may consider the circumstances of the killing, relevant aggravating factors, and mitigating evidence when determining a lawful sentence.

6. Capital punishment shall not be imposed for a first conviction solely because the offense is classified as a Capital Felony.

7. Capital sentencing following a subsequent qualifying offense shall remain subject to §8.7 of the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

8. Any fixed-term sentence imposed under this provision shall not exceed seven real-world days.

## §2.14 — Attempted Murder

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$50,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits attempted murder when, acting with the specific intent to unlawfully kill another person, they take a substantial step toward causing that person's death.

2. The offense does not require the intended victim to die.

3. Mere threats or preparation without a substantial step shall not independently establish attempted murder.

4. The applicable mental state and substantial step shall be proven beyond a reasonable doubt.

5. A conviction under this provision shall not independently authorize life imprisonment.

## §2.15 — Torture

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–6 hours

1. A person commits torture by intentionally inflicting severe physical pain or suffering upon another person for punishment, intimidation, coercion, or another prohibited purpose.

2. Torture may occur during unlawful confinement, interrogation, criminal intimidation, or other qualifying conduct.

3. A person's status as a prisoner, suspect, employee, or associate shall not justify torture.

4. A public official who commits torture may be charged under this section and other applicable official misconduct provisions.

5. Claims of consent shall not excuse conduct where the law otherwise prohibits it.

6. This provision shall not authorize roleplay involving conduct prohibited by Rosefire's community conduct rules.

## §2.16 — Coercion to Commit a Criminal Offense

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally using unlawful threats, violence, or restraint to compel another individual to participate in a criminal offense.

2. The prosecution shall establish the unlawful coercion and the criminal conduct the accused intended to compel.

3. Mere persuasion, lawful negotiation, or an individual's voluntary association with another person shall not constitute this offense.

4. This offense shall not authorize any form of roleplay prohibited by the State's established community conduct rules.

5. Separate kidnapping, assault, extortion, or other offenses may be charged where independently established.

## §2.17 — Stalking

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits stalking by knowingly engaging in a repeated course of unwanted conduct directed at an identifiable individual that would cause a reasonable person substantial fear for personal safety and does cause such fear.

2. Conduct may include repeated following, surveillance, threatening communications, or other qualifying acts.

3. A single lawful encounter shall not ordinarily establish a course of conduct.

4. Lawful journalism, protected expression, and authorized investigations shall not independently constitute stalking.

5. Existing protective orders may be relevant where lawfully issued.

## §2.18 — Harassment

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 0–20 minutes

1. A person commits harassment by intentionally engaging in a repeated course of targeted conduct involving unlawful threats, intimidation, or substantial interference with another person's safety or lawful activities.

2. Mere insults, criticism, disagreement, or protected expression shall not independently constitute criminal harassment.

3. The State shall establish the prohibited conduct and required criminal intent.

4. Criminal harassment shall remain distinguishable from ordinary civil disputes and protected communication.

## §2.19 — Violation of a Protective Order

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$10,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly violating a valid and enforceable judicial protective order after receiving legally sufficient notice of its restrictions.

2. The prosecution shall establish the order's validity, the accused's knowledge, and the prohibited conduct.

3. An expired or invalid protective order shall not independently support conviction.

4. Separate criminal acts committed during a violation may be charged where independently established.

## §2.20 — Abuse of a Vulnerable Person

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly or recklessly inflicting serious unlawful harm upon an individual whose dependence, disability, age, or other legally recognized vulnerability creates a duty of care or significant inability to protect themselves.

2. The applicable duty, conduct, and criminal mental state shall be established through evidence.

3. Ordinary lawful caregiving decisions shall not independently constitute criminal abuse.

4. Conduct satisfying another serious offense may be prosecuted according to applicable charging rules.

5. This provision shall not authorize any form of abuse roleplay prohibited under Rosefire's community conduct rules.

## §2.21 — Criminal Neglect

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits criminal neglect when, while under a legally established duty of care, they knowingly or recklessly fail to provide necessary protection or assistance, creating a substantial risk of serious bodily harm.

2. The State shall establish the applicable duty and required criminal mental state.

3. Inability to provide assistance despite reasonable efforts shall not automatically constitute criminal neglect.

4. The mere existence of a personal relationship shall not independently establish a legal duty of care.

## §2.22 — Interference With Emergency Medical Care

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally and unlawfully obstructing authorized emergency medical personnel while they perform necessary emergency treatment or rescue duties.

2. The interference must materially impede the lawful emergency activity.

3. Protected speech or reasonable questioning shall not independently constitute this offense.

4. A person shall not be required to consent to medical treatment solely because emergency personnel request it, except where lawful emergency treatment authority applies.

5. Violence against medical personnel may constitute a separate offense where established.
