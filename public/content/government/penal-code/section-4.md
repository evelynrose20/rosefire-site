# SECTION 4 — ROBBERY, BURGLARY & THEFT

## §4.1 — Petty Theft

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$4,000  
**Imprisonment:** 0–20 minutes

1. A person commits petty theft by knowingly taking or exercising unauthorized control over property belonging to another with intent to deprive the lawful owner of it.

2. This offense applies to theft involving property below the threshold established for grand theft.

3. Mere accidental possession or a good-faith ownership dispute shall not constitute theft.

4. An unfulfilled contractual payment obligation shall not automatically constitute theft.

5. Restitution may be ordered for established loss.

## §4.2 — Grand Theft

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits grand theft by knowingly taking property of substantial recognized value without lawful authorization and with intent to deprive the owner.

2. The applicable property-value threshold shall be established in the official property offense schedule.

3. The prosecution shall establish ownership or lawful possessory interest, unauthorized taking, required intent, and qualifying value.

4. Property acquired through a valid transaction shall not constitute theft merely because a later contract dispute arises.

## §4.3 — Theft From a Person

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$12,500  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits theft from a person by intentionally taking property directly from another individual's person without permission and without the force or threatened force required for robbery.

2. This offense may include qualifying pickpocketing and similar taking.

3. If the taking involves force or threats satisfying robbery, the applicable robbery offense shall govern.

4. The State shall prove the unauthorized taking and required intent.

## §4.4 — Shoplifting

**Classification:** Class C Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $500–$2,500  
**Imprisonment:** 0–10 minutes

1. A person commits shoplifting by knowingly taking merchandise offered for sale from a commercial establishment without payment or lawful authorization.

2. An accidental failure to pay without the required intent shall not constitute shoplifting.

3. Higher-value or aggravated conduct may be charged under the applicable theft provision.

4. Businesses may recover qualifying loss through lawful restitution or civil proceedings.

## §4.5 — Theft of Services

**Classification:** Class B Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $1,000–$5,000  
**Imprisonment:** 0–20 minutes

1. A person commits theft of services by knowingly obtaining paid services through material deception, unauthorized means, or intentional evasion of an obligation to pay at the time the service is obtained.

2. Services may include transportation, lodging, repair work, and other qualifying commercial activities.

3. An ordinary billing dispute shall not automatically constitute theft.

4. The State shall establish the criminal intent required by this offense.

## §4.6 — Possession of Stolen Property

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing or controlling property stolen from another with knowledge of its stolen status.

2. Knowledge may be established through reliable evidence and relevant circumstances.

3. Possession alone, without the required criminal knowledge, shall not establish guilt.

4. Property lawfully obtained without knowledge of theft shall remain subject to appropriate ownership and return procedures.

## §4.7 — Receiving or Selling Stolen Property

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$25,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly receiving, purchasing for resale, selling, or distributing property known to be stolen.

2. The State shall establish knowledge and intentional commercial participation.

3. A lawful merchant shall not be presumed guilty solely because stolen property was unknowingly acquired.

4. Property may be seized and returned according to applicable evidence and ownership procedures.

## §4.8 — Burglary

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits burglary by knowingly entering or unlawfully remaining within a building or secured structure with intent to commit theft or another designated criminal offense therein.

2. The intended offense need not be completed.

3. Mere criminal trespass without the additional required intent shall not constitute burglary.

4. Entry with lawful permission shall not constitute burglary unless the applicable unlawful-remaining elements are established.

## §4.9 — Residential Burglary

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits residential burglary by knowingly entering or unlawfully remaining within an occupied or ordinarily inhabited dwelling with intent to commit theft or another designated criminal offense.

2. The prosecution shall establish the protected residential character of the premises.

3. Unlawful entry into a dwelling shall not automatically establish the criminal intent required for burglary.

4. Violence or threats committed during the burglary may support additional charges where independently established.

## §4.10 — Aggravated Burglary

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. Aggravated burglary occurs when qualifying burglary involves:

   a. Use or threatened immediate use of a deadly weapon.

   b. Intentional infliction of serious bodily injury.

   c. Another specifically designated aggravating circumstance.

2. The State shall prove the burglary and applicable aggravating conduct.

3. A weapon's mere lawful ownership shall not independently establish aggravated burglary.

4. Additional criminal offenses may be charged where separately established.

## §4.11 — Robbery

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$25,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits robbery by unlawfully taking property from another person's possession or immediate presence through force or an immediate threat of force.

2. The force or threat must be connected to the taking.

3. Mere theft without the required force or immediate threat shall not constitute robbery.

4. The State shall establish the unlawful taking and required intent.

## §4.12 — Armed Robbery

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$50,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits armed robbery when they commit robbery while intentionally using or threatening immediate use of a deadly weapon.

2. The State shall establish the weapon-related aggravating conduct.

3. Mere ownership of a firearm shall not establish armed robbery.

4. A robbery involving a weapon represented as dangerous may be considered according to the applicable offense and evidence.

5. Separate injuries may support additional charges where independently established.

## §4.13 — Convenience Store Robbery

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$25,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by unlawfully taking or attempting to take money or property from a retail convenience business through force or an immediate threat of force.

2. The State shall establish the underlying robbery and qualifying commercial premises.

3. An armed offense may be prosecuted under §4.12 where the applicable elements are established.

4. The State shall not impose duplicative punishment under this section and §4.11 for the same taking.

## §4.14 — Commercial Establishment Robbery

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$50,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits commercial establishment robbery by knowingly using force or immediate threats to unlawfully take money, merchandise, or other property from a designated commercial business.

2. Qualifying businesses may include jewelry retailers, specialized commercial establishments, and other businesses designated by the Penal Code.

3. The prosecution shall establish the underlying robbery and qualifying circumstances.

4. Minor retail theft without violence shall not constitute this offense.

5. Restitution may be ordered for established losses.

## §4.15 — Bank Robbery

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits bank robbery by knowingly taking or attempting to take money or other property from a recognized financial institution through force or an immediate threat of force.

2. A bank robbery may involve interference with bank employees, customers, secured financial assets, or designated financial facilities.

3. The State shall establish the underlying unlawful taking or criminal attempt and the required force or threat.

4. A financial institution's mere loss of money through an ordinary commercial dispute shall not constitute bank robbery.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require an authorized human judicial determination.

6. The maximum fixed-term sentence under this offense shall be 12 real-world hours.

## §4.16 — Aggravated Bank Robbery

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. Aggravated bank robbery occurs when qualifying bank robbery involves:

   a. A hostage taken or unlawfully confined during the robbery.

   b. Intentional infliction of serious bodily injury.

   c. Coordinated use of deadly force presenting a substantial threat to multiple persons.

   d. Another expressly designated aggravating circumstance.

2. The State shall prove both the underlying robbery and applicable aggravating conduct.

3. Merely acting with another person shall not independently establish aggravated bank robbery.

4. A sentence exceeding six hours shall require human judicial authorization.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.

## §4.17 — Major Financial Institution Heist

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $40,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 12–72 hours

1. A person commits this offense by intentionally participating in a coordinated large-scale robbery of a designated major financial institution involving unlawful taking by force or immediate threat and substantial interference with the institution's secured operations.

2. The offense requires evidence of both a qualifying robbery and the extraordinary scale or circumstances prescribed by law.

3. Ordinary unauthorized entry, an unsuccessful minor theft, or a single routine robbery shall not independently constitute a major financial institution heist.

4. Participation shall be established individually.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 72 real-world hours.

6. Sentencing shall require authorized human judicial determination.

## §4.18 — Armored Transport Robbery

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits armored transport robbery by using force or immediate threats to unlawfully take money, valuables, or secured cargo from a designated armored transportation operation.

2. The State shall establish the protected transport operation and underlying robbery.

3. Ordinary commercial cargo theft without force shall be prosecuted under the applicable theft offense.

4. Serious injuries or independently established aggravated conduct may support other lawful charges.

## §4.19 — Organized Armed Robbery

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 4–12 hours

1. A person commits organized armed robbery by knowingly participating in a coordinated robbery involving two or more persons and the intentional use or threatened immediate use of deadly weapons.

2. The State shall establish the defendant's knowing participation and the qualifying coordinated conduct.

3. Mere association with another suspect shall not establish participation.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

5. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require an authorized human judicial determination.

## §4.20 — Motor Vehicle Theft

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits motor vehicle theft by knowingly taking or exercising unauthorized control over another person's motor vehicle with intent to deprive the lawful owner.

2. Motor vehicle theft shall remain distinct from ordinary traffic violations and contractual repossession disputes.

3. A good-faith ownership or lawful possession claim shall be considered under the applicable criminal intent requirement.

4. Recovered vehicles shall remain subject to lawful evidence and return procedures.

## §4.21 — Grand Theft Auto

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits grand theft auto by unlawfully taking a motor vehicle of substantial recognized value with intent to deprive the lawful owner.

2. Qualifying value or designated circumstances shall be established through the official property offense schedule.

3. A vehicle's status as a luxury vehicle shall not independently establish that the defendant committed the theft.

4. Vehicle financing and repossession disputes shall remain subject to applicable property law.

## §4.22 — Carjacking

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$50,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits carjacking by unlawfully taking or attempting to take a motor vehicle from the possession or immediate control of another individual through force or an immediate threat of force.

2. The offense shall require proof of the unlawful taking and relevant force or threat.

3. Ordinary unattended vehicle theft shall not constitute carjacking.

4. The use of a deadly weapon may be considered where relevant to the applicable offense and sentencing provisions.

## §4.23 — Aggravated Carjacking

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. Aggravated carjacking occurs when qualifying carjacking involves serious bodily injury, the intentional use of a deadly weapon, or another expressly designated aggravated circumstance.

2. The State shall prove the underlying carjacking and applicable aggravating conduct.

3. A sentence exceeding six hours shall require human judicial authorization.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

## §4.24 — Theft of Commercial Cargo

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits commercial cargo theft by knowingly taking cargo from a lawful transport or storage operation without authorization and with intent to deprive the owner.

2. Cargo may include commercial goods, transported materials, equipment, and other legally held products.

3. The State shall establish the unlawful taking and required intent.

4. A legitimate dispute over delivery performance shall not automatically constitute cargo theft.

## §4.25 — Organized Cargo Theft

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits organized cargo theft by knowingly participating in a coordinated criminal operation to unlawfully take substantial commercial cargo.

2. The State shall establish intentional participation and the qualifying coordinated conduct.

3. Mere presence near a stolen trailer or cargo shipment shall not establish guilt.

4. Lawful cargo transport and commercial recovery operations shall remain protected.

## §4.26 — Theft of Government Property

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits theft of government property by knowingly taking property lawfully possessed or owned by a recognized government institution with intent to deprive that institution.

2. The State shall establish the relevant property interest and unlawful taking.

3. Ordinary mistakes involving government-issued equipment shall not independently constitute theft.

4. Theft involving restricted weapons or protected records may qualify for separate charges where independently established.

## §4.27 — Theft of Emergency Service Equipment

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly stealing equipment designated for emergency medical, fire, police, or rescue operations.

2. The State shall establish the equipment's relevant protected function and the unlawful taking.

3. The use of stolen equipment to commit another offense may support separate lawful charges.

4. Damage resulting from the theft may be considered for restitution.

## §4.28 — Safecracking and Unauthorized Vault Entry

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully defeating or entering a secured safe, vault, or designated secure storage area with intent to commit theft or another criminal offense.

2. Authorized locksmith, maintenance, recovery, and security work shall not constitute this offense.

3. A person shall not be convicted solely because they possess lawful tools capable of opening locks or secured containers.

4. Where the conduct forms part of a qualifying bank robbery or burglary, applicable charging and merger rules shall govern.

## §4.29 — Possession of Burglary Tools With Criminal Intent

**Classification:** Class A Misdemeanor  
**Fine:** $2,500–$8,000  
**Imprisonment:** 10–40 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly possessing equipment with the specific intent to use it in a designated burglary or unlawful entry.

2. Ordinary possession of lockpicks, repair tools, or other dual-purpose equipment shall not independently constitute a criminal offense.

3. The State shall establish the relevant criminal intent through evidence.

4. Professional locksmiths, mechanics, and other lawful workers shall remain entitled to possess authorized tools.

## §4.30 — Theft by Deception

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits theft by deception by knowingly obtaining another person's money or property through a material false representation made with intent to deprive the owner.

2. The State shall establish intentional material deception and the resulting unlawful acquisition.

3. An ordinary failed investment, broken promise, or contractual dispute shall not independently establish deception.

4. Larger financial schemes may qualify for additional fraud offenses under applicable law.

## §4.31 — Embezzlement

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits embezzlement by knowingly converting property or funds entrusted to their lawful care or control for unauthorized personal or other unlawful use.

2. The State shall establish the entrusted relationship, unauthorized conversion, and required intent.

3. Ordinary accounting errors without criminal intent shall not constitute embezzlement.

4. Business owners, employees, government officials, and financial personnel may be subject to this offense where its elements are established.

## §4.32 — Major Financial Embezzlement

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 3–12 hours

1. A person commits major financial embezzlement by knowingly converting substantial entrusted funds or financial assets through an organized or materially deceptive scheme.

2. Qualifying value or circumstances shall be established in the official financial offense schedule.

3. The State shall prove the underlying unlawful conversion and applicable qualifying circumstances.

4. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require human judicial authorization.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

## §4.33 — Theft From Financial Accounts

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly and unlawfully obtaining or transferring funds from a financial account without the authorization of the lawful account holder or another legally empowered person.

2. The offense may involve unauthorized account access, fraudulent transactions, or other unlawful electronic transfer.

3. The State shall establish the unauthorized financial taking and required intent.

4. A bank error or disputed authorized transaction shall not automatically constitute criminal theft.

5. Financial records shall be obtained and handled according to applicable law.

## §4.34 — Organized Financial Theft

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Extraordinary Serious Offense  
**Fine:** $30,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 6–48 hours

1. A person commits organized financial theft by knowingly participating in a coordinated operation unlawfully transferring or obtaining substantial funds from financial institutions or multiple protected accounts through fraud or unauthorized access.

2. The State shall establish the coordinated criminal conduct, unlawful acquisition, and qualifying scale.

3. A person shall not be criminally responsible solely because they maintain a financial account used by another person.

4. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 48 real-world hours.

5. Sentencing shall require human judicial authorization.

## §4.35 — Theft of Aircraft or Vessels

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$30,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly taking or exercising unauthorized control over another person's aircraft or vessel with intent to deprive the lawful owner.

2. Lawful rental, charter, repossession, and recovery operations shall not constitute theft when conducted within their authorization.

3. Additional offenses may apply for independently established dangerous operation or violence.

4. Aircraft and vessel ownership and seizure rights shall remain subject to Section 15 of the Civil Rights & Justice Code.

## §4.36 — Robbery of an Aircraft or Vessel

**Classification:** Class A Felony  
**Fine:** $20,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 2–6 hours

1. A person commits this offense by unlawfully taking or attempting to take control of an aircraft or vessel through force or an immediate threat of force.

2. The prosecution shall prove the unlawful taking, force or threat, and required criminal intent.

3. Ordinary theft of an unoccupied aircraft or vessel shall be prosecuted under the applicable theft provision.

4. Kidnapping, injury, or other independent criminal conduct may be charged where supported.

## §4.37 — Criminal Use of Stolen Vehicles

**Classification:** Class C Felony  
**Fine:** $5,000–$15,000  
**Imprisonment:** 20–60 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly operating or using a stolen motor vehicle for a qualifying criminal purpose.

2. The State shall establish knowledge of the stolen status and the additional criminal use.

3. Mere presence as a passenger shall not independently establish knowing criminal use.

4. Applicable charges shall remain subject to the rules against duplicative punishment.

## §4.38 — Theft During an Emergency

**Classification:** Class B Felony  
**Fine:** $10,000–$25,000  
**Imprisonment:** 45–120 minutes

1. A person commits this offense by knowingly exploiting an active disaster, evacuation, emergency response, or comparable public emergency to steal property from persons or premises affected by that emergency.

2. The State shall establish the theft and knowing exploitation of the emergency.

3. Ordinary theft occurring at the same time as an unrelated emergency shall not automatically qualify.

4. Lawful emergency recovery or safekeeping of property shall not constitute theft.

## §4.39 — Robbery Resulting in Serious Bodily Injury

**Classification:** Class A Felony — Exceptional Felony  
**Fine:** $25,000–$60,000  
**Imprisonment:** 4–12 hours

1. A person commits this offense when a qualifying robbery intentionally or knowingly causes serious bodily injury to a victim.

2. The State shall prove the robbery, injury, causation, and applicable mental state.

3. An ordinary minor injury shall not automatically establish the aggravated offense.

4. Sentences exceeding six hours shall require human judicial authorization.

5. The maximum fixed-term sentence shall be 12 real-world hours.

## §4.40 — Robbery Resulting in Death

**Classification:** Capital Felony  
**Fine:** Up to $75,000  
**Sentence:** 6 hours to 7 real-world days, or life imprisonment following an authorized human judicial determination

1. A person commits robbery resulting in death when, during the commission of a qualifying robbery, they intentionally or knowingly cause another person's death under circumstances satisfying the applicable criminal homicide elements.

2. The State shall prove the underlying robbery, unlawful killing, causation, and required criminal mental state.

3. A death occurring during a robbery shall not automatically establish guilt for every participant without the legally required individual responsibility.

4. Life imprisonment shall require a qualifying human judicial proceeding.

5. Capital punishment shall remain subject to the separate eligibility and procedural requirements established by Civil Rights & Justice Code §8.7.

6. A fixed-term sentence shall not exceed seven real-world days.
