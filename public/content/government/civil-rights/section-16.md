# SECTION 16 — PROPERTY, BUSINESS & COMMERCIAL RIGHTS

## §16.1 — General Property Rights

1. Every individual and legally recognized entity may lawfully acquire, own, possess, lease, sell, transfer, and inherit property within the State of San Andreas.

2. Property rights shall remain subject to applicable law and valid contractual obligations.

3. No individual shall be deprived of lawful property ownership without appropriate legal authority and due process.

4. Criminal allegations shall not independently extinguish property ownership.

5. The State may regulate property use for legitimate safety, land-use, and other lawful purposes.

6. A person's wealth, occupation, or social standing shall not independently diminish their property rights.

7. Property disputes may be brought before an authorized court.

## §16.2 — Types of Property

For the purposes of this Code, property may include:

1. Residential real estate.

2. Commercial real estate.

3. Land and recognized property interests.

4. Motor vehicles, aircraft, and vessels.

5. Business assets and commercial equipment.

6. Financial accounts and lawful monetary interests.

7. Personal belongings and other movable property.

8. Contractual rights and qualifying intellectual property.

9. Other interests expressly recognized under applicable law.

Ownership and control of different property categories may be subject to distinct legal requirements.

## §16.3 — Residential Property Ownership

1. Individuals may lawfully purchase and own residential properties.

2. Residential ownership shall be established through recognized ownership records and valid transactions.

3. A lawful property owner may occupy, lease, transfer, or sell the property in accordance with applicable law.

4. Ownership may be subject to valid financing agreements, liens, or other recognized encumbrances.

5. An owner shall not be deprived of their property solely because of an unrelated criminal allegation.

6. Property ownership disputes may be resolved through the courts.

7. The State shall recognize valid property records maintained through authorized property registration procedures.

## §16.4 — Commercial Property Ownership

1. Individuals and businesses may lawfully own commercial premises.

2. Commercial properties may be used for lawful activities permitted by applicable licensing and property restrictions.

3. Ownership of a commercial building shall not automatically authorize operation of every regulated business.

4. Commercial activity may require separate business licenses or professional permits.

5. Commercial property may be leased or transferred through lawful agreements.

6. Ownership may be subject to recognized financial interests.

7. Commercial property shall not automatically be forfeited solely because a business license has been suspended or revoked.

## §16.5 — Real Estate Transactions

1. Property may be transferred through lawful sale, inheritance, judicial order, or another recognized legal process.

2. A valid transfer shall identify the property, relevant parties, and agreed consideration or other legal basis.

3. Property transactions shall be accurately recorded through the recognized property administration system.

4. Material misrepresentation concerning ownership, liens, or transaction terms may constitute fraud where the applicable legal elements are established.

5. A purchaser shall be entitled to receive the ownership interest described by a valid completed transaction.

6. Sellers shall not knowingly transfer property interests they have no lawful authority to convey.

7. Disputes concerning property transfers may be brought before the Department of Justice.

## §16.6 — Licensed Real Estate Services

1. The State may authorize licensed real estate businesses to assist with property listing, leasing, purchase, and sale.

2. Licensed real estate representatives may advertise available properties, present lawful transaction terms, and assist with recognized property agreements.

3. A real estate representative shall not knowingly falsify ownership or transaction documentation.

4. A real estate business shall not acquire ownership of a client's property solely by acting as a listing or transaction representative.

5. Representatives shall disclose material conflicts of interest where required by law.

6. Real estate services may charge lawful fees established by agreement or applicable regulation.

7. Disputes involving real estate services may be addressed through appropriate civil or licensing proceedings.

## §16.7 — Recognition of D8 Real Estate Services

1. The State recognizes D8 as an authorized real estate service through which designated properties may be listed, marketed, leased, purchased, and transferred.

2. D8 may facilitate transactions within the scope of its lawful business authorization and applicable agreements.

3. D8 shall not independently exercise judicial foreclosure powers merely by acting as a real estate representative.

4. Property ownership, financing, and lease interests shall remain subject to applicable law.

5. D8 shall maintain appropriate transaction records for properties administered through its services.

6. A D8 representative shall not knowingly misrepresent a property's availability, price, ownership, or encumbrances.

7. D8's involvement shall not invalidate the rights of owners, tenants, purchasers, lenders, or other lawful parties.

8. The State may authorize other real estate businesses according to applicable licensing law.

## §16.8 — Property Listings and Disclosure

1. Property listings shall accurately identify the property offered for sale or lease.

2. Sellers and authorized representatives shall not knowingly conceal material facts where disclosure is required by law.

3. Advertised sale and lease terms shall not be intentionally misleading.

4. A listing shall not independently constitute a completed property transfer.

5. An interested purchaser or tenant shall receive the material terms necessary to enter the transaction.

6. Property advertisements may be withdrawn or amended according to applicable agreements.

7. Disputes concerning misleading listings may give rise to civil or administrative proceedings.

## §16.9 — Residential Leasing

1. Property owners may lawfully lease residential premises to tenants.

2. A lease shall establish the parties, premises, payment obligations, occupancy rights, and other material terms.

3. The tenant shall receive the lawful right to occupy the property for the agreed period, subject to the lease and applicable law.

4. A landlord shall not unlawfully enter occupied residential premises.

5. A tenant shall not intentionally damage property or violate lawful material lease conditions.

6. Lease payments and obligations shall be governed by the agreement and applicable law.

7. A lease dispute shall not automatically constitute a criminal offense.

8. Landlords and tenants may seek appropriate judicial remedies.

## §16.10 — Commercial Leasing

1. Commercial property owners may lease premises for lawful business purposes.

2. Commercial leases may establish rent, permitted use, maintenance obligations, improvements, and other agreed conditions.

3. A commercial tenant shall not acquire ownership of the property solely by entering a lease.

4. A property owner shall not arbitrarily terminate a valid lease outside applicable legal or contractual authority.

5. A business license suspension shall not automatically cancel a commercial lease unless a lawful lease provision or independent legal ground applies.

6. Commercial lease disputes may be resolved through appropriate civil proceedings.

7. Property owners and tenants shall comply with valid judicial orders affecting occupancy or possession.

## §16.11 — Landlord Entry and Tenant Privacy

1. A residential tenant shall retain lawful privacy rights within the leased premises.

2. A landlord may enter according to the lease and applicable law for legitimate purposes, including necessary repairs, inspections, or emergencies.

3. Entry shall be conducted with required notice where applicable, except in circumstances lawfully permitting immediate entry.

4. Ownership of a property shall not grant a landlord unrestricted authority to search a tenant's private belongings.

5. Landlords shall not enter solely to intimidate, harass, or retaliate against a tenant.

6. Police searches of leased premises shall remain governed by Section 4.

7. Disputes concerning unlawful entry may be brought before the courts.

## §16.12 — Lease Defaults and Termination

1. A lease may be terminated according to its lawful terms or another recognized legal ground.

2. Grounds may include material nonpayment, expiration, serious contractual breach, or other legally recognized circumstances.

3. A landlord shall provide the notice required by applicable law.

4. A tenant shall have the opportunity to challenge an unlawful termination.

5. A landlord shall not employ unlawful force, threats, or unauthorized seizure of belongings to remove a tenant.

6. Disputed possession may be resolved through an appropriate judicial proceeding.

7. Termination of a lease shall not automatically transfer ownership of the tenant's personal property to the landlord.

## §16.13 — Eviction Proceedings

1. Eviction is the lawful removal of an occupant from premises following termination of their legal right to occupy them.

2. Contested eviction shall require an appropriate legal proceeding or enforceable judicial order.

3. Eviction proceedings shall identify the property, relevant parties, and grounds for removal.

4. A tenant shall receive reasonable notice and an opportunity to respond.

5. No landlord shall conduct an unlawful self-help eviction through threats, violence, or deliberate deprivation of essential access.

6. Authorized personnel may enforce a valid possession order within the scope of the order.

7. Personal property remaining after eviction shall be handled according to applicable law.

8. An eviction shall not independently constitute a criminal conviction.

## §16.14 — Mortgage Financing

1. Residential and commercial properties may be purchased through lawful mortgage financing.

2. A mortgage agreement shall identify the borrower, lender, secured property, principal obligation, interest terms, repayment requirements, and other material conditions.

3. The lender may hold a lawful security interest in the property until the secured obligation is satisfied or otherwise discharged.

4. A mortgage shall not automatically transfer unrestricted ownership or occupancy rights to the lender.

5. Borrowers shall receive disclosure of the applicable annual percentage rate, required down payment, repayment obligations, and other material financing terms before entering the agreement.

6. A lender shall not knowingly misrepresent material borrowing costs.

7. Borrowers shall remain responsible for valid contractual obligations.

8. Mortgage disputes may be submitted to the appropriate civil court.

## §16.15 — Mortgage Applications and Credit Assessment

1. Licensed financial institutions may assess mortgage applications according to lawful lending criteria.

2. Lenders may consider the applicant's credit history, existing obligations, available funds, collateral, and other legitimate financial information.

3. Loan terms may vary according to lawful risk assessment and financial circumstances.

4. An applicant shall not be entitled to a specific interest rate or loan merely because a property is publicly listed.

5. Lending criteria shall not be applied in violation of the equal protection requirements established under Section 1.

6. Applications shall not automatically constitute approval or an enforceable lending commitment.

7. Applicants may challenge fraudulent, discriminatory, or otherwise unlawful conduct through appropriate proceedings.

## §16.16 — Mortgage Approval and Execution

1. Mortgage financing shall become binding only through a lawful agreement accepted by the lender and borrower.

2. A real estate listing or proposed purchase shall not independently constitute loan approval.

3. A lender may require the borrower to complete authorized credit assessment and approval procedures.

4. A down payment shall be applied according to the agreed transaction.

5. The financing agreement shall identify applicable interest and repayment terms.

6. The lender and borrower shall receive or retain an appropriate record of the executed agreement.

7. Property transfer and financing records shall be maintained through recognized state systems.

8. Material disputes concerning approval, payment, or contractual obligations may be resolved through civil proceedings.

## §16.17 — Interest Rates and Lending Terms

1. Financial institutions may offer different interest rates and loan conditions according to lawful lending criteria.

2. Annual percentage rates and other material borrowing costs shall be disclosed before execution of a financing agreement.

3. A lender shall not arbitrarily alter a fixed contractual interest rate outside a lawful agreement.

4. Variable-rate financing may be offered where the applicable adjustment mechanism is clearly disclosed and lawful.

5. Late-payment charges and other fees shall require contractual or statutory authority.

6. Lenders shall not impose undisclosed or unauthorized charges.

7. Disputes concerning interest calculations or repayment obligations may be reviewed by an appropriate court.

## §16.18 — Mortgage Default

1. A borrower may be considered in default when the applicable legal and contractual conditions for default have been satisfied.

2. Default may arise from failure to make required payments or another material breach recognized by the agreement.

3. A lender shall provide required notice before initiating foreclosure or other designated recovery proceedings.

4. An ordinary missed payment shall not automatically authorize immediate seizure or eviction unless a lawful provision expressly establishes such a consequence.

5. Borrowers may satisfy overdue obligations, negotiate lawful modification, or exercise available cure rights where permitted.

6. Default shall not automatically constitute criminal fraud.

7. The lender shall retain only those recovery powers established by law and the financing agreement.

## §16.19 — Foreclosure

1. Foreclosure is the lawful enforcement of a secured real estate obligation following qualifying default.

2. Foreclosure shall require satisfaction of the applicable contractual and legal prerequisites.

3. Where judicial foreclosure is required, the lender shall obtain an appropriate order.

4. A borrower shall receive the notice and opportunity to contest foreclosure required by law.

5. A lender shall not employ unlawful threats, physical force, or unauthorized entry to acquire possession.

6. A foreclosure may result in lawful transfer, sale, or recovery of the secured property according to the controlling order or procedure.

7. Valid ownership, lien, and third-party interests shall be considered.

8. A foreclosure shall not automatically authorize confiscation of unrelated personal or business property.

9. Disputes may be brought before an authorized court.

## §16.20 — Foreclosed Property and Resale

1. Property recovered through lawful foreclosure may be offered for resale or lease according to applicable law.

2. A foreclosed property shall not become freely available for sale until the required legal recovery and transfer procedures have been completed.

3. Authorized real estate businesses, including D8 where applicable, may list eligible foreclosed properties.

4. Listings shall accurately disclose the property's status and material sale conditions.

5. Financial institutions shall retain appropriate records of recovery, ownership transfer, and disposition.

6. A purchaser acquiring foreclosed property shall receive the legal interest conveyed by the completed transaction.

7. Property disputes arising from foreclosure or resale may be resolved through appropriate judicial proceedings.

## §16.21 — Banking and Financial Institutions

1. Individuals and lawful businesses may maintain financial accounts with authorized banking institutions.

2. Banks may provide lawful deposit, withdrawal, transfer, payment, credit, and financing services.

3. Account holders shall retain recognized rights concerning funds lawfully held in their accounts.

4. Banks shall maintain accurate account records and comply with valid legal obligations.

5. No law enforcement officer or government official shall independently appropriate private account funds without lawful authority.

6. Account restrictions, seizures, or judicial holds shall require an appropriate legal basis.

7. Financial institutions shall comply with lawful judicial orders within their authorized scope.

8. Financial disputes may be brought before an appropriate court.

## §16.22 — Financial Privacy

1. Private banking information and financial records shall remain protected against unreasonable government access.

2. Law enforcement access to nonpublic financial records shall require lawful authority appropriate to the investigation.

3. A business relationship with a person suspected of criminal conduct shall not independently establish wrongdoing.

4. Authorized banks may maintain records necessary for lawful financial operations.

5. Banks shall not knowingly disclose protected customer information outside applicable legal authorization.

6. Judicial orders compelling disclosure shall identify the relevant scope of the required production.

7. Individuals may challenge unlawful financial record access through appropriate legal proceedings.

## §16.23 — Credit and Lending

1. Authorized financial institutions may provide lawful credit services.

2. Credit agreements shall establish the material repayment and borrowing conditions.

3. Borrowers shall remain responsible for obligations validly undertaken.

4. Lenders may use lawful creditworthiness criteria when evaluating applications.

5. Financial institutions shall not knowingly falsify a borrower's credit records.

6. Borrowers may dispute materially inaccurate credit information through the applicable procedure.

7. Inability to repay an ordinary lawful debt shall not independently constitute a criminal offense.

8. Fraudulent borrowing and intentional financial deception may be prosecuted where the required criminal elements are established.

## §16.24 — Commercial Business Rights

1. Individuals and lawful entities may establish and operate businesses within the State of San Andreas, subject to applicable licensing laws.

2. Businesses may engage in lawful commerce, employ workers, acquire assets, lease property, and enter contracts.

3. Commercial activity shall remain subject to applicable criminal, licensing, and property laws.

4. A business shall not be permanently closed or forfeited solely because its owner has been arrested.

5. Business license suspension and revocation shall remain governed by Section 10.

6. The State may restrict or prohibit designated unlawful commercial activity.

7. Business owners may challenge improper enforcement actions through appropriate proceedings.

## §16.25 — Business Ownership and Management

1. A business may be owned by an individual, partnership, corporation, or another legally recognized entity.

2. Business ownership interests shall be established through valid agreements and recognized records.

3. Owners may appoint authorized managers and employees.

4. Management authority shall not automatically convey ownership.

5. An employee shall not acquire ownership of business assets solely by working for the business.

6. Owners and managers shall comply with applicable laws governing commercial activity.

7. Ownership disputes may be resolved through appropriate civil proceedings.

## §16.26 — Business Sale and Transfer

1. A lawful business may be sold or transferred in accordance with applicable agreements and licensing requirements.

2. A transfer shall identify the relevant business interests, assets, and material obligations included.

3. Transfer of business ownership shall not automatically eliminate valid contractual debts or property interests.

4. Licenses that are nontransferable by law shall require separate application or approval by the new operator.

5. Sellers shall not knowingly conceal material ownership disputes or encumbrances where disclosure is legally required.

6. Buyers shall receive the rights described in the completed lawful transaction.

7. Disputes may be brought before an appropriate court.

## §16.27 — Business Licensing and Regulated Activities

1. Businesses shall maintain licenses required for their designated activities.

2. Certain industries may require additional professional, commercial, or restricted-activity authorizations.

3. Possession of a general business license shall not independently authorize prohibited or separately regulated activities.

4. Licensing authorities may investigate applicable violations within their lawful scope.

5. Administrative suspension and revocation shall remain subject to Section 10.

6. A licensing investigation shall not automatically authorize unrestricted searches.

7. Businesses may contest unlawful licensing decisions through appropriate proceedings.

## §16.28 — Commercial Contracts

1. Individuals and businesses may enter lawful commercial contracts.

2. Contracts may concern goods, services, employment, transportation, property, financing, supply, or other legitimate commercial matters.

3. Enforceable agreements shall be interpreted according to their lawful terms and applicable law.

4. Material fraud, coercion, or unlawful subject matter may affect enforceability.

5. A party alleging breach of contract may seek appropriate civil remedies.

6. A contractual breach shall not automatically constitute criminal fraud.

7. Courts may award lawful damages or other remedies supported by the evidence.

8. Contractual rights shall not override protections established by this Code.

## §16.29 — Employment and Compensation

1. Individuals and businesses may lawfully employ persons to perform authorized work.

2. Employment agreements may establish duties, compensation, schedules, and other lawful terms.

3. Employers shall honor valid compensation obligations.

4. Employees shall perform their duties according to applicable agreements and law.

5. Wages may be established by agreement, subject to applicable legal requirements.

6. A business shall not knowingly misrepresent material employment conditions to obtain labor through fraud.

7. Employment disputes may be addressed through appropriate civil or administrative proceedings.

8. Employment shall not independently grant an individual ownership of business property.

## §16.30 — Employee Payment and Business Accounts

1. Privately owned businesses shall be responsible for compensation owed to their employees.

2. Employee payments may be administered through authorized business financial accounts or other lawful payment methods.

3. A business shall not knowingly represent that wages have been paid when the payment has not occurred.

4. Insufficient business funds shall not extinguish a valid wage obligation.

5. An owner or manager shall not knowingly divert funds held for a legally restricted purpose in violation of law.

6. Disputes concerning unpaid wages may be brought before the appropriate court.

7. The State shall not automatically assume the private payroll obligations of a commercially operated business.

8. State employment and public-service compensation shall be administered according to separate lawful public employment authority.

## §16.31 — Commercial Supply and Delivery

1. Businesses may enter lawful agreements for the supply, transport, or delivery of commercial goods.

2. Supply agreements may establish product specifications, quantities, prices, delivery conditions, and payment obligations.

3. Commercial transport shall remain subject to applicable vehicle, maritime, aviation, and controlled-substance regulations.

4. A supplier shall not knowingly misrepresent the contents of a shipment.

5. A purchaser shall not knowingly evade a valid payment obligation through fraudulent conduct.

6. Breach of a delivery agreement shall ordinarily constitute a civil matter unless criminal conduct is independently established.

7. Parties may seek appropriate remedies under applicable contract law.

## §16.32 — Consumer Transactions

1. Businesses offering goods or services to the public shall not knowingly engage in fraudulent commercial practices.

2. Material terms of a sale or service agreement shall not be intentionally misrepresented.

3. Customers shall receive the goods or services described in a valid completed transaction, subject to lawful conditions.

4. Businesses may establish reasonable return, cancellation, and service policies consistent with applicable law.

5. Commercial disputes may be resolved through appropriate civil proceedings.

6. Criminal fraud shall require proof of the relevant statutory elements.

7. A customer's dissatisfaction with a lawful purchase shall not independently establish criminal wrongdoing.

## §16.33 — Business Financial Records

1. Businesses may maintain financial records concerning income, expenses, sales, payroll, financing, and contractual obligations.

2. Records required by applicable law shall be maintained accurately.

3. Knowingly falsifying material financial records for a fraudulent purpose may constitute a criminal offense.

4. Private business records shall remain protected against unreasonable search and seizure.

5. Law enforcement may obtain protected records through appropriate legal authority.

6. Courts may compel production of relevant records through lawful judicial procedures.

7. A business shall not be required to disclose unrelated confidential information beyond the scope of an authorized order.

## §16.34 — Business Insolvency

1. A business may become insolvent when it cannot satisfy its financial obligations according to applicable law.

2. Insolvency shall not independently constitute a criminal offense.

3. Creditors may seek lawful remedies concerning unpaid obligations.

4. Business assets may remain subject to valid liens, secured interests, and judicial orders.

5. Owners shall not unlawfully conceal or transfer assets for the purpose of defeating valid creditor rights.

6. A court may determine the appropriate disposition of disputed assets and obligations.

7. Insolvency proceedings shall not automatically transfer unrelated personal property to creditors.

## §16.35 — Commercial Fraud

1. Knowingly obtaining money, property, goods, or services through material deception may constitute fraud under the Penal Code.

2. Fraud may involve falsified ownership records, fictitious transactions, forged agreements, or other prohibited misrepresentations.

3. Criminal fraud shall require evidence satisfying the elements established by law.

4. Failure to fulfill a contract shall not automatically establish fraudulent intent.

5. Victims may seek appropriate restitution or civil remedies.

6. Criminal investigations shall comply with applicable search, seizure, and due process requirements.

7. Financial or property forfeiture shall require lawful authority.

## §16.36 — Business Partnerships and Corporate Interests

1. Individuals may establish lawful partnerships and other recognized commercial entities.

2. Business agreements may define ownership shares, management authority, distributions, and other rights.

3. A partner shall not exercise powers beyond those granted by law or the governing agreement.

4. Ownership interests may be transferred according to applicable contractual and legal restrictions.

5. Material partnership disputes may be resolved through civil proceedings.

6. Knowingly misappropriating business property may constitute an offense where the applicable legal elements are established.

7. A corporate entity shall remain subject to applicable state laws.

## §16.37 — Private Corporate Contractors

1. The State may engage independent private businesses to provide designated government services.

2. Holding a government contract shall not independently grant legislative, judicial, or law enforcement powers.

3. Contracted services shall remain subject to applicable law and the terms of the governing agreement.

4. Contractors shall not unlawfully interfere with judicial proceedings or official records.

5. Government contractors may be investigated for fraud, corruption, or other prohibited conduct.

6. Contractual arrangements shall not exempt private businesses or their employees from criminal liability.

7. The State shall retain legal responsibility for the exercise of powers that cannot lawfully be delegated.

## §16.38 — Government Property and Public Facilities

1. The State may own and operate property necessary for public institutions and authorized services.

2. Government property may include courthouses, police stations, correctional facilities, public offices, transportation infrastructure, and other designated premises.

3. Access to secure government facilities may be restricted according to law.

4. Government ownership shall not eliminate applicable civil rights protections.

5. Unauthorized occupation, destruction, or theft of government property may constitute an offense where established by law.

6. Disposal or transfer of government property shall require appropriate legal authority.

7. Government officials shall not knowingly appropriate public property for unauthorized personal use.

## §16.39 — Eminent Domain and Public Acquisition

1. The State may acquire private property for a lawful public purpose where expressly authorized.

2. Compulsory acquisition shall require appropriate notice and due process.

3. The owner shall be entitled to the compensation or other protections required under applicable law.

4. Property shall not be acquired under the false representation of public purpose solely to benefit a favored private individual or business.

5. The owner may challenge the legal basis or terms of compulsory acquisition.

6. The State shall not employ unlawful force or unauthorized seizure to circumvent required proceedings.

7. Public acquisition authority shall remain subject to judicial review.

## §16.40 — No General Taxation or Mandatory Insurance Requirement

1. No general income, sales, property, or vehicle tax is established by this Code.

2. Ownership of property or operation of a lawful business shall not independently create a general taxation obligation under this Code.

3. No mandatory insurance requirement is established for property ownership, vehicle operation, business activity, or ordinary commercial transactions under this Code.

4. Voluntary private commercial agreements may establish lawful risk allocation, guarantees, or other financial arrangements.

5. Lawful licensing fees, service charges, contractual payments, fines, and court-ordered obligations shall remain distinct from general taxation.

6. No agency shall impose a general tax or mandatory insurance obligation merely through an administrative policy or informal demand.

## §16.41 — Commercial Rights and Equal Protection

1. Individuals and lawful businesses shall receive equal protection under applicable commercial and property laws.

2. Government authorities shall not unlawfully discriminate in licensing, property administration, judicial proceedings, or commercial enforcement.

3. A business owner's political disagreement with the government shall not independently justify revocation of lawful property or commercial rights.

4. A private corporation shall not obtain preferential judicial authority solely because it maintains a government contract.

5. Individuals may seek appropriate judicial review of unlawful governmental interference with their property or business.

6. No official shall demand unauthorized personal benefits as a condition of providing a lawful government service.

## §16.42 — Property and Commercial Record Integrity

1. Official property, licensing, financial, and commercial records shall be maintained by their recognized responsible authorities.

2. Unauthorized alteration, deletion, or falsification of official records may constitute an offense.

3. Individuals may petition for correction of materially inaccurate records.

4. The State may use authorized electronic systems to administer property transfers, business registration, and other commercial records.

5. A technology contractor shall not acquire ownership of private property merely because it maintains the records system.

6. Disputed records shall remain subject to appropriate judicial review.

7. Government technology systems shall not independently override valid legal ownership without lawful authority.

## §16.43 — Judicial Remedies in Property and Commercial Disputes

1. Individuals and lawful entities may bring property and commercial claims before the appropriate court.

2. Courts may consider disputes involving:

   a. Property ownership.

   b. Real estate transactions.

   c. Residential and commercial leases.

   d. Mortgage financing.

   e. Foreclosure.

   f. Banking and credit agreements.

   g. Business ownership and partnerships.

   h. Employment and unpaid compensation.

   i. Commercial contracts and supply agreements.

   j. Fraudulent business practices.

   k. Other legally recognized property or commercial claims.

3. Courts may award lawful damages, restitution, possession orders, declaratory relief, injunctions, or other appropriate remedies.

4. No person shall be imprisoned solely for inability to pay an ordinary civil debt.

5. Criminal proceedings may arise independently where the conduct satisfies an applicable criminal offense.

6. All proceedings shall remain subject to the due process protections established in Section 1.

# LEGISLATIVE CONTINUITY AND AUTHORITY

1. Sections 15–16 shall be interpreted consistently with Sections 1–14 of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

2. Civil rights established under Section 1 shall remain applicable to aviation, maritime, property, financial, and commercial matters.

3. Legal standards concerning reasonable suspicion, probable cause, detention, and arrest shall remain governed by Sections 2–3.

4. All investigative searches and seizures shall remain subject to Section 4.

5. Law enforcement authority shall remain governed by Section 5.

6. Use of force shall remain subject to Section 6.

7. Criminal processing and prosecution shall remain governed by Sections 7 and 9.

8. Sentencing and correctional custody shall remain governed by Section 8.

9. License suspension and revocation shall remain subject to Section 10.

10. Vehicle repossession, impoundment, and forfeiture shall remain governed by Section 11 where applicable.

11. Weapons transported by aircraft or vessel shall remain subject to Section 12.

12. Controlled substances and wildlife commerce shall remain subject to Sections 13–14.

13. Aircraft and vessel ownership shall not independently excuse unlawful operation.

14. Lawful property ownership shall remain protected against arbitrary government seizure.

15. Mortgage default, business insolvency, and ordinary contractual disputes shall not independently constitute criminal offenses.

16. Real estate and financial transactions shall remain subject to applicable contractual rights and judicial procedures.

17. Government contracts shall not independently confer law enforcement, judicial, or legislative authority upon private companies.

18. No general taxation or mandatory insurance system is established by these provisions.

19. Official aviation, maritime, property, business, and commercial records shall remain subject to lawful verification, correction, and judicial review.

20. Subsequent legislation and authorized regulatory schedules shall establish specific aviation and maritime classifications, licensing qualifications, restricted locations, procedural deadlines, and applicable criminal penalties.
