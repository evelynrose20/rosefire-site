# ROSEFIRE CIVIL RIGHTS & JUSTICE CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.7 — Sections 15–16**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing the laws governing aviation, maritime navigation, aircraft and vessel ownership, transportation safety, commercial transportation, property ownership, real estate transactions, leasing, mortgages, foreclosure, banking, business operations, commercial agreements, and the rights of individuals and enterprises within the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–14 remain enacted and in force. Sections 15–16 constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the fundamental civil rights, judicial procedures, property protections, licensing standards, and enforcement limitations previously established.

---

# SECTION 15 — AVIATION & MARITIME LAW

## §15.1 — General Aviation Authority

1. The State of San Andreas shall regulate the lawful operation of aircraft within its recognized jurisdiction.

2. Aviation laws shall govern aircraft ownership, registration, licensing, flight operations, airspace restrictions, landing facilities, and aviation-related public safety.

3. The State may establish designated aviation authorities responsible for licensing, safety oversight, and administrative enforcement.

4. Law enforcement agencies may investigate aviation-related criminal offenses within their lawful jurisdiction.

5. Administrative aviation authority shall not independently confer unrestricted police powers.

6. Aviation regulations shall remain subject to the civil rights and judicial protections established under this Code.

7. Specific aircraft classifications, flight restrictions, and operational requirements shall be maintained through officially adopted aviation schedules.

## §15.2 — Aircraft Ownership and Registration

1. Individuals and lawful businesses may purchase, own, lease, sell, and transfer aircraft in accordance with state law.

2. Aircraft operating within regulated state airspace shall maintain applicable registration.

3. Registration shall identify the recognized owner or authorized registrant.

4. Aircraft ownership shall not independently establish authorization to operate an aircraft without the required pilot qualifications.

5. Aircraft may be subject to lawful financing agreements, leases, liens, and other recognized property interests.

6. Knowingly falsifying aircraft ownership or registration documentation may constitute a criminal offense.

7. An aircraft shall not be permanently confiscated solely because its owner has been accused of a criminal offense.

8. Disputes concerning ownership, registration, or financial interests may be resolved through appropriate judicial proceedings.

## §15.3 — Pilot Licensing

1. Individuals operating aircraft requiring a pilot's license shall possess valid authorization appropriate to the aircraft and activity.

2. Pilot licensing may distinguish between fixed-wing aircraft, helicopters, commercial aviation, and other recognized classifications.

3. Additional qualifications or endorsements may be required for designated aircraft or professional operations.

4. A standard driver's license shall not independently authorize aircraft operation.

5. A pilot's license shall not authorize the operation of aircraft outside the holder's approved classification.

6. Licenses shall be issued, suspended, revoked, and reinstated according to Section 10.

7. The State may require appropriate examinations or competency demonstrations as a condition of licensing.

8. An individual may contest an unlawful licensing determination through the Department of Justice.

## §15.4 — Lawful Aircraft Operation

1. Aircraft shall be operated with reasonable care for the safety of passengers, persons on the ground, other aircraft, and property.

2. Pilots shall comply with applicable airspace restrictions, aviation procedures, and lawful instructions issued by authorized aviation personnel.

3. Reckless operation of an aircraft may constitute a criminal offense.

4. A pilot shall not deliberately operate an aircraft in a manner presenting an unjustified imminent danger of serious bodily injury or death.

5. Pilots shall not intentionally use an aircraft to threaten or endanger persons without lawful justification.

6. Aircraft shall be operated only where the pilot possesses the applicable legal authorization.

7. Criminal penalties shall be established by the Penal Code.

## §15.5 — Airports, Airfields, and Heliports

1. The State may recognize designated airports, airfields, heliports, and other authorized aviation facilities.

2. Aircraft may take off and land at facilities appropriate to their classification and lawful use.

3. Restricted operational areas may be established for passenger safety, aircraft movement, and security.

4. Individuals shall not unlawfully enter designated secure aviation areas.

5. Airport and aviation personnel may enforce lawful access restrictions within their assigned authority.

6. Unauthorized entry into a restricted aviation area may constitute an offense where established by law.

7. An airport operator shall not obtain general police powers solely through operation of an aviation facility.

8. Official aviation facility designations shall be maintained through applicable maps and regulatory schedules.

## §15.6 — Unauthorized Landings

1. Landing an aircraft in a location where landing is prohibited by law may constitute an offense.

2. The State may designate prohibited or restricted landing areas.

3. Pilots shall not knowingly land in a manner creating an unreasonable threat to persons or property.

4. Emergency landings reasonably necessary to protect life shall be evaluated according to the circumstances.

5. A lawful emergency landing shall not independently constitute criminal conduct merely because it occurred outside an ordinary aviation facility.

6. Officers may investigate whether an alleged emergency landing was reasonably justified.

7. Aircraft removal or recovery shall require appropriate legal authority.

## §15.7 — Restricted Airspace

1. The State may establish restricted or prohibited airspace for lawful security, public safety, and aviation management purposes.

2. Restricted airspace may include designated government facilities, correctional institutions, emergency response areas, and other locations expressly identified by law.

3. Restrictions shall identify the relevant area and applicable operational limitations.

4. Pilots shall comply with lawfully established airspace restrictions.

5. Emergency restrictions may be imposed by authorized officials where immediate public safety needs justify such action.

6. Restrictions shall remain limited to their lawful purpose and duration.

7. An officer shall not independently designate permanent prohibited airspace without legal authority.

8. Official restricted-airspace boundaries shall be identified through recognized aviation notices and maps.

## §15.8 — Air Traffic Instructions

1. Pilots shall comply with lawful instructions issued by authorized aviation control personnel.

2. Such instructions may concern takeoff, landing, movement, holding patterns, and separation from other aircraft.

3. Instructions shall be issued for legitimate aviation safety or operational purposes.

4. An instruction shall not be lawful merely because the issuing person claims aviation authority.

5. Emergency circumstances may justify necessary deviations from ordinary instructions to protect life.

6. Knowingly disregarding a valid safety instruction without lawful justification may constitute an offense.

7. Aviation control personnel shall not knowingly issue dangerous or fraudulent operational instructions.

## §15.9 — Aircraft Operating Under the Influence

1. Operating an aircraft while unlawfully impaired by alcohol, controlled substances, or another intoxicating substance may constitute a criminal offense.

2. Aviation impairment investigations shall require appropriate factual and legal grounds.

3. A pilot suspected of impairment may be subject to lawful testing procedures where authorized.

4. The State may impose applicable pilot license suspension or revocation through the procedures established in Section 10.

5. Aircraft may be temporarily secured where continued operation presents an immediate and substantial danger.

6. Aircraft ownership shall not automatically be forfeited following an impairment-related offense.

7. Criminal proceedings shall remain subject to applicable due process protections.

## §15.10 — Commercial Aviation

1. Individuals and businesses may engage in lawful commercial aviation when possessing the necessary authorizations.

2. Commercial aviation may include passenger transportation, cargo delivery, aircraft charter, and other recognized services.

3. Commercial operators shall comply with applicable aviation licensing, safety, and operational requirements.

4. Commercial pilots may require qualifications beyond those applicable to ordinary private aircraft operation.

5. Commercial transport documents may be required where prescribed by law.

6. A commercial operator shall not knowingly transport unlawful cargo.

7. Lawful commercial aviation shall remain protected against arbitrary seizure, closure, or interference.

8. Commercial disputes may be resolved through the courts under Sections 9 and 16.

## §15.11 — Aviation Cargo and Transportation

1. Aircraft transporting cargo may be subject to applicable commercial documentation requirements.

2. Cargo documentation shall accurately identify information required by law.

3. Knowingly falsifying commercial aviation cargo records may constitute an offense.

4. Transportation of prohibited weapons, controlled substances, or other unlawful cargo may constitute a criminal offense.

5. Aircraft searches shall remain subject to Section 4 and applicable aviation inspection authority.

6. Commercial cargo unrelated to an offense shall not automatically be subject to permanent forfeiture.

7. Lawful ownership and third-party interests shall be considered during seizure and forfeiture proceedings.

## §15.12 — Emergency Aircraft Operations

1. Authorized emergency aviation operations may be conducted for medical response, law enforcement, rescue, firefighting, and other designated purposes.

2. Emergency aircraft may receive operational exceptions expressly permitted by law.

3. Such exceptions shall not authorize reckless disregard for human life.

4. Emergency personnel shall exercise reasonable care when operating near populated areas or other aircraft.

5. Emergency landing authority shall be limited to circumstances reasonably requiring immediate action.

6. Government aircraft shall remain subject to applicable public safety and accountability requirements.

7. Unauthorized use of emergency aircraft privileges may constitute official misconduct.

## §15.13 — Aviation Accidents and Investigations

1. Aviation accidents involving significant injury, death, or substantial property damage may be investigated by the appropriate authorities.

2. Investigators may secure an accident scene where lawful grounds exist.

3. Aircraft wreckage and relevant records may be preserved as evidence.

4. Investigative access to protected property shall remain subject to applicable legal standards.

5. A pilot shall not automatically be presumed criminally responsible solely because an aviation accident occurred.

6. Criminal liability shall require evidence satisfying the applicable offense.

7. Aircraft owners and other affected parties may pursue appropriate civil remedies.

## §15.14 — Aircraft Seizure and Impoundment

1. Aircraft may be temporarily secured, detained, or impounded where authorized by law.

2. Qualifying circumstances may include:

   a. Operation creating an immediate danger.

   b. Serious aviation-related criminal conduct.

   c. Lawful evidence preservation.

   d. Operation without required authorization where continued flight cannot lawfully proceed.

   e. Other circumstances expressly designated by law.

3. Temporary impoundment shall not constitute permanent transfer of ownership.

4. Permanent forfeiture shall require appropriate judicial authority.

5. Lawful financing, leasing, and third-party ownership interests shall be considered.

6. Aircraft owners may challenge allegedly unlawful seizure or impoundment.

7. Authorized authorities shall maintain appropriate seizure and release records.

## §15.15 — General Maritime Authority

1. The State of San Andreas shall regulate lawful maritime and inland-waterway activity within its recognized jurisdiction.

2. Maritime law shall govern vessel operation, registration, navigation, commercial transport, safety, docking, and designated marine activities.

3. The State may authorize maritime enforcement and emergency response personnel.

4. Maritime personnel exercising criminal enforcement powers shall possess the appropriate legal authorization.

5. Vessel owners and operators shall retain the property and civil rights established under this Code.

6. Specific maritime requirements shall be established through officially adopted schedules and regulations.

## §15.16 — Vessel Ownership and Registration

1. Individuals and businesses may lawfully purchase, own, lease, sell, and transfer boats and other recognized vessels.

2. Vessels may be subject to registration requirements according to their classification and use.

3. Registration shall identify the lawful owner or authorized registrant.

4. Vessel ownership shall not independently establish authorization to operate a vessel where a separate operator qualification is required.

5. Vessel ownership may be subject to lawful financing agreements, leases, and liens.

6. Knowingly falsifying vessel ownership or registration information may constitute an offense.

7. Ownership disputes may be resolved through appropriate judicial proceedings.

## §15.17 — Maritime Operator Authorization

1. The State may require designated vessels or commercial maritime activities to be operated by properly licensed individuals.

2. Licensing requirements may vary according to vessel size, classification, or commercial purpose.

3. Recreational vessels may be subject to distinct operating requirements.

4. An ordinary driver's license shall not automatically authorize operation of a vessel requiring specialized maritime certification.

5. Licensing suspension and revocation shall remain subject to Section 10.

6. A person shall not be criminally punished for lacking a maritime credential that has not been legally required for the relevant operation.

## §15.18 — Safe Vessel Operation

1. Vessel operators shall exercise reasonable care toward passengers, swimmers, other vessels, and persons near waterways.

2. Operators shall comply with applicable navigational rules and lawful safety requirements.

3. Reckless vessel operation may constitute a criminal offense.

4. Operators shall not intentionally use a vessel to endanger persons or property without lawful justification.

5. The State may establish speed restrictions in designated waterways.

6. Such restrictions shall be officially identified and enforced according to applicable law.

7. Maritime offenses shall be subject to the applicable Penal Code.

## §15.19 — Maritime Navigation and Right of Way

1. Vessel operators shall comply with officially established navigation rules.

2. Navigational requirements may govern passage, right of way, restricted channels, and safe operation near docks or shorelines.

3. Operators shall take reasonable action to avoid collisions.

4. A vessel shall not deliberately obstruct a designated navigation channel without lawful authority.

5. Emergency vessels may receive lawful operational priority during emergency response.

6. Navigational restrictions shall not be enforced beyond the authority established by law.

## §15.20 — Ports, Marinas, and Docks

1. The State may recognize public and privately operated ports, marinas, and docking facilities.

2. Facility operators may establish lawful access and operational conditions.

3. Restricted commercial or secure areas shall be identified according to applicable law.

4. Unauthorized entry into a restricted facility may constitute an offense where established by law.

5. Private dock ownership shall not independently authorize unlawful obstruction of public waterways.

6. Commercial dock use may be subject to lawful agreements and service charges.

7. Disputes concerning marina services, docking rights, and commercial agreements may be resolved through civil proceedings.

## §15.21 — Maritime Commercial Operations

1. Individuals and businesses may operate lawful maritime transportation and commercial services.

2. Such services may include passenger transport, cargo delivery, fishing operations, charter services, and other authorized activities.

3. Commercial operators shall possess required authorizations and comply with applicable safety requirements.

4. Cargo records may be required where expressly prescribed by law.

5. Commercial maritime operations shall remain subject to applicable property, contract, and business laws.

6. Criminal investigation of commercial operations shall require lawful grounds.

7. Commercial vessels shall not be permanently confiscated solely upon an unproven allegation.

## §15.22 — Maritime Cargo and Smuggling

1. Knowingly transporting prohibited cargo by vessel may constitute a criminal offense.

2. The State may establish specific offenses relating to unlawful maritime transportation and trafficking.

3. A commercial vessel operator shall not knowingly falsify required transportation documents.

4. Maritime cargo inspections shall remain subject to applicable legal authority.

5. The mere possession of commercial cargo shall not establish smuggling.

6. Lawful cargo unrelated to an offense shall remain protected against arbitrary seizure.

7. Vessels used in qualifying serious criminal activity may be subject to judicial forfeiture under applicable law.

## §15.23 — Operating a Vessel Under the Influence

1. Operating a vessel while unlawfully impaired may constitute a criminal offense.

2. Impairment investigations shall be conducted according to applicable legal standards.

3. A vessel may be temporarily secured where continued operation presents an immediate safety danger.

4. An operator may be subject to applicable criminal or licensing consequences.

5. Ownership of the vessel shall not automatically be forfeited.

6. Affected individuals shall retain appropriate judicial review rights.

## §15.24 — Maritime Emergencies and Rescue

1. Authorized maritime and emergency personnel may respond to persons or vessels in distress.

2. Emergency response may include rescue, medical assistance, hazard removal, and other lawful protective measures.

3. Vessel operators shall not intentionally interfere with lawful emergency rescue operations.

4. Emergency personnel may take necessary and proportionate actions to protect life.

5. Emergency authority shall not independently permit unrelated criminal searches.

6. Emergency vessel operations shall be conducted with reasonable regard for public safety.

7. Individuals receiving emergency assistance shall retain their civil rights.

## §15.25 — Vessel Accidents and Collisions

1. Significant vessel collisions may be investigated by authorized authorities.

2. Operators involved in collisions shall comply with applicable duties to stop, provide information, and render reasonable assistance where required by law.

3. Accident scenes may be secured where lawful grounds exist.

4. The State may preserve vessels, equipment, and records as evidence when authorized.

5. An accident shall not automatically establish criminal guilt.

6. Civil liability may arise from negligent operation or other legally recognized conduct.

7. Disputes may be resolved through the appropriate judicial process.

## §15.26 — Maritime Search and Seizure

1. Vessel searches shall remain subject to the legal protections established under Section 4.

2. A maritime inspection authorized for safety or regulatory purposes shall remain limited to its lawful scope.

3. Private cabins, personal belongings, and unrelated cargo shall not automatically be subject to unrestricted inspection.

4. Officers may conduct lawful searches pursuant to a valid warrant, consent, probable cause under an applicable exception, or another recognized legal basis.

5. Seized vessels and cargo shall be documented.

6. Individuals may challenge unlawful maritime searches or seizures.

7. Permanent vessel forfeiture shall require appropriate legal authority.

## §15.27 — Maritime and Aviation Enforcement Accountability

1. Officials exercising aviation or maritime enforcement powers shall remain accountable under this Code.

2. No officer shall knowingly fabricate aviation or maritime charges.

3. No official shall unlawfully seize aircraft, vessels, or commercial cargo for personal benefit.

4. No private airport, port, or transportation contractor shall acquire unrestricted police authority through commercial operation.

5. Alleged misconduct may be referred for appropriate investigation.

6. Affected individuals may seek judicial review of unlawful enforcement actions.

7. Civil rights and property protections shall remain applicable throughout aviation and maritime proceedings.
