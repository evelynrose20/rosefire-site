# SECTION 3 — DETENTION, IDENTIFICATION, ARREST, AND CORRECTIONAL CUSTODY

## §3.1 — Lawful Investigative Detention

1. An on-duty law enforcement officer may temporarily detain an individual when reasonable suspicion of criminal activity exists.

2. The detention shall be limited to the time reasonably necessary to confirm or dispel the officer's suspicion.

3. A detained individual shall not be transported to a correctional facility or placed into correctional custody solely upon reasonable suspicion.

4. An officer shall identify themselves as law enforcement when reasonably practicable.

5. An officer shall inform the detained individual of the general reason for detention as soon as doing so is safe and reasonably practicable.

6. An investigative detention shall end when reasonable suspicion has been dispelled and no other lawful grounds for continued detention exist.

7. An investigative detention shall not be prolonged solely for the purpose of securing a confession or compelling cooperation.

## §3.2 — Identification Requirements

1. An individual lawfully detained upon reasonable suspicion may be required to provide their correct legal name and sufficient identifying information to establish identity.

2. An individual lawfully operating a motor vehicle may be required to present a driver's license and documents prescribed by law during a legitimate traffic stop.

3. Commercial vehicle operators may be required to provide applicable commercial driving credentials and transport documentation during lawful inspections.

4. Individuals engaged in licensed activities may be required to present relevant credentials during a lawful inspection or investigation concerning that activity.

5. No individual shall be required to identify themselves solely because an officer wishes to engage in voluntary conversation.

6. Knowingly providing false identifying information during a lawful identification demand may constitute an offense under the Penal Code.

7. An officer shall not demand unrelated personal records merely to establish identity when sufficient identification has already been provided.

## §3.3 — Voluntary Police Encounters

1. Law enforcement officers may approach individuals and request voluntary conversation without reasonable suspicion.

2. An individual participating in a voluntary encounter may decline to answer questions and may leave unless lawful grounds for detention arise.

3. Officers shall not knowingly represent a voluntary encounter as mandatory detention.

4. Refusal to participate in a voluntary conversation shall not, by itself, establish grounds for detention.

## §3.4 — Lawful Arrest

An individual may be arrested when:

1. A law enforcement officer possesses probable cause that the individual committed a criminal offense for which arrest is authorized.

2. A valid judicial arrest warrant has been issued.

3. Another specific provision of law expressly authorizes the arrest.

An arrest shall be conducted using reasonable force, and the individual shall be informed of the general grounds for arrest as soon as reasonably practicable.

## §3.5 — Rights Following Arrest

1. An arrested individual shall be informed of the offenses for which they are being held.

2. An arrested individual retains the right to remain silent and request legal counsel.

3. An arrested individual shall receive reasonable access to necessary medical treatment.

4. Officers shall not use unnecessary force, intimidation, or coercive punishment against a person in custody.

5. The arresting agency shall maintain an appropriate record of the arrest and the grounds supporting it.

6. An individual shall not be held indefinitely without charges or other lawful authority.

7. No formal recital of rights shall be required to establish the protections afforded under Section 1.

## §3.6 — Arrest Without a Warrant

1. Officers may arrest without a warrant when probable cause exists and warrantless arrest is otherwise authorized by law.

2. Warrantless arrest shall not automatically authorize entry into a private dwelling.

3. Entry into a dwelling for the purpose of arrest shall require a valid warrant, lawful consent, exigent circumstances, or another expressly applicable exception.

4. An officer shall not intentionally use an unlawful entry to create the circumstances necessary for an arrest.

## §3.7 — Temporary Restraints and Protective Measures

1. During lawful detention, officers may impose temporary restraints when specific circumstances reasonably indicate a threat to safety, an immediate risk of flight, or another substantial operational danger.

2. Handcuffs or other restraints shall not automatically convert every detention into an arrest, but the total extent and duration of restraint must remain justified.

3. Officers shall discontinue unnecessary restraints when the relevant safety justification has ended.

4. No individual may be restrained solely as punishment for exercising a lawful right.

5. Officers shall account for known medical conditions and immediate safety concerns when applying restraints.

## §3.8 — Release from Detention

1. An individual shall be released from investigative detention when officers lack a lawful basis to continue holding them.

2. Where an investigation establishes probable cause, officers may proceed with lawful arrest.

3. Where arrest is not authorized or warranted, officers may issue an appropriate citation or warning when permitted by law.

4. An individual's prior arrest record, reputation, or known associations shall not independently justify continued detention.

## §3.9 — Investigative Detention and Custody Limitations

1. Investigative detention shall continue only for the period reasonably necessary to investigate the circumstances establishing reasonable suspicion.

2. An individual shall not be transported to a correctional facility or placed into correctional custody solely upon reasonable suspicion.

3. Upon establishing probable cause for an arrestable offense, an officer may arrest the individual and begin lawful criminal processing.

4. Where exceptional circumstances require an extended investigation of an individual already lawfully arrested, the responsible law enforcement agency shall obtain supervisory authorization and promptly seek judicial review for continued custody beyond ordinary processing.

5. Supervisory authorization shall not permit indefinite detention without sufficient legal grounds.

6. An individual's refusal to answer investigative questions or exercise of protected civil rights shall not independently justify prolonging detention.

7. A supervisor shall not authorize continued custody where no lawful basis for arrest or detention remains.

8. The State shall establish and publish applicable custody-review deadlines through subsequent criminal procedure legislation.

## §3.10 — Department of Corrections Authority

1. The Department of Corrections shall be the recognized authority responsible for the lawful custody, supervision, care, and management of persons committed to state correctional facilities.

2. The Department of Corrections may receive and hold an individual pursuant to:

   a. A lawfully imposed criminal sentence.

   b. A valid judicial remand order.

   c. A lawful temporary custody or transfer order.

   d. Another commitment expressly authorized by state law.

3. Correctional officers shall possess the authority reasonably necessary to maintain institutional safety, prevent escapes, conduct lawful security inspections, and enforce correctional regulations.

4. Individuals committed to correctional custody retain all civil rights not lawfully restricted by their sentence, custody status, or necessary institutional security measures.

5. Correctional officers may conduct authorized searches, supervise inmate movement, establish reasonable security restrictions, and respond to threats within correctional facilities.

6. The Department of Corrections may coordinate with law enforcement, courts, attorneys, and medical personnel concerning inmate transportation, judicial appearances, treatment, and lawful release.

7. No person shall remain incarcerated beyond the expiration of their lawful commitment without a new and valid legal authorization.

8. The Department of Corrections shall not independently impose additional criminal sentences or overturn judicial decisions.

## §3.11 — Correctional Custody and Sentence Administration

1. The State recognizes standard and extended correctional custody as lawful forms of imprisonment.

2. **Standard Correctional Custody:** Individuals serving ordinary criminal sentences shall be eligible for release upon completion of the imposed sentence without requiring the physical attendance or authorization of a correctional officer.

3. **Extended Correctional Custody:** Individuals convicted of qualifying serious offenses may receive an extended sentence pursuant to lawful judicial authority. Such sentences may include correctional supervision, rehabilitative programs, parole eligibility, and scheduled judicial review.

4. The Department of Corrections shall administer correctional sentences but shall not independently increase, indefinitely extend, or overturn a sentence imposed by a lawful sentencing authority.

5. Correctional officers may establish reasonable institutional schedules, work assignments, housing arrangements, visitation procedures, and rehabilitative opportunities consistent with established regulations.

6. Participation in approved correctional programs may be considered during parole, sentence modification, or rehabilitation proceedings where authorized by law.

7. No individual shall be denied an otherwise lawful release solely because a correctional officer, attorney, or judge is unavailable.

8. Individuals held pending trial shall not be treated as convicted prisoners and shall retain the legal protections afforded to persons awaiting adjudication.

9. Institutional disciplinary violations may result in reasonable correctional sanctions. Additional criminal imprisonment shall require an independently lawful charge, sentence, or judicial order.

10. Correctional commitments, sentence modifications, judicial holds, and releases shall be documented by the responsible authority.

## §3.12 — Maximum Term of Imprisonment

1. No individual sentenced to a fixed term of imprisonment shall be required to serve more than seven consecutive real-world days for a single sentencing event.

2. The maximum period established under this section shall include all consecutive fixed-term sentences imposed as part of the same proceeding.

3. Fixed-term sentences shall continue to progress according to actual elapsed time whether the individual is present within the State or temporarily absent, unless a lawful interruption expressly applies.

4. The expiration of a lawful fixed-term sentence shall entitle the individual to release from correctional custody unless a separate and independently valid judicial commitment remains in force.

5. No correctional officer, law enforcement officer, or government official may extend a fixed-term sentence beyond its lawful maximum through administrative punishment or discretionary detention.

6. Life imprisonment may be imposed only following a qualifying criminal conviction and an authorized judicial proceeding.

7. Individuals sentenced to life imprisonment shall remain eligible for judicial review, rehabilitation consideration, clemency, parole, or another lawful release process established under this Code.

8. An individual shall not be sentenced to life imprisonment solely because the total penalties associated with multiple ordinary offenses exceed the maximum fixed-term sentence.

9. The specific criminal offenses qualifying for life imprisonment shall be established under the Penal Code.

## §3.13 — Escape and Continuing Sentences

1. Escape from lawful correctional custody shall constitute a separate criminal offense.

2. An escape shall not, by itself, invalidate, satisfy, or extinguish the underlying criminal sentence.

3. An escaped individual shall remain subject to lawful arrest and recommitment pursuant to their existing sentence and any subsequent lawful conviction, unless their enforceable commitment has been lawfully terminated or otherwise altered.

4. The Department of Corrections may recommend additional charges arising from an escape but shall not independently impose additional criminal imprisonment.

5. An individual serving a life sentence who escapes custody shall remain legally subject to that sentence unless subsequently pardoned, paroled, or otherwise released by lawful judicial authority.

6. All correctional authorities shall maintain appropriate records of an escape, including the individual's commitment, last known custody status, and applicable warrants.

7. Allegations concerning a previously terminated or altered commitment shall be resolved according to the applicable judicial record and review procedures.

## §3.14 — Automated Judicial Services

1. The State of San Andreas may establish an authorized automated judicial service for the administration of designated judicial and legal proceedings.

2. Such a service may receive applications, examine submitted evidence, maintain judicial records, and exercise only those functions expressly delegated to it by law.

3. Every automated judicial determination shall be recorded and identify the legal authority supporting the determination.

4. Decisions affecting an individual's liberty, property, or protected rights shall remain subject to judicial review.

5. Automated judicial services shall not impose life imprisonment or exercise extraordinary sentencing powers unless expressly authorized by subsequent legislation.

6. No automated judicial system may create criminal offenses, independently amend enacted law, or impose penalties not authorized by the Penal Code.

7. Individuals shall retain the right to challenge an automated judicial determination through the Department of Justice.

8. Where a human judge and an automated judicial service possess overlapping authority, disputes concerning jurisdiction or conflicting orders shall be resolved through the Department of Justice.

9. The use of automated judicial services shall not diminish the civil rights guaranteed under Section 1.

## §3.15 — Judicial Record Integrity and Correctional Authority

1. All correctional commitments, criminal convictions, warrants, sentence modifications, and orders of release shall be recorded within the judicial records system recognized by the State of San Andreas.

2. The Department of Corrections and recognized law enforcement agencies may rely upon authenticated judicial records when determining an individual's custody status or the existence of an enforceable warrant.

3. An individual whose official correctional commitment has been marked satisfied, discharged, or otherwise terminated shall not remain in correctional custody without separate lawful authority.

4. Unauthorized alteration, falsification, deletion, or manipulation of judicial records shall constitute an offense under the applicable criminal laws of San Andreas.

5. Where credible evidence establishes that a judicial record has been unlawfully altered, the Department of Justice may initiate proceedings to review and correct the affected record.

6. An allegation of record manipulation shall not independently authorize indefinite detention or the automatic restoration of a previously terminated correctional commitment.

7. The authenticity and legal effect of disputed judicial records shall be determined through an authorized judicial proceeding.

8. The State may maintain independent evidentiary records, audit records, and investigative records for the purpose of examining suspected judicial fraud, subject to applicable law.

9. An individual shall not be presumed guilty of record manipulation solely because a record concerning them has been altered.

## §3.16 — Contracted Judicial Technology and State Authority

1. The State of San Andreas may contract independent private corporations to develop, maintain, and operate technological systems supporting the administration of justice.

2. Any judicial technology operated under a government contract shall remain subject to the laws of San Andreas and the lawful oversight of the Department of Justice.

3. A private contractor shall not acquire independent legislative, prosecutorial, or judicial authority solely by virtue of holding a government contract.

4. Judicial decisions issued through an authorized automated judicial system shall carry only the authority expressly delegated under state law.

5. The contracting corporation shall be responsible for maintaining the security, integrity, and operational reliability of the systems under its administration.

6. Unauthorized access to, interference with, or manipulation of judicial technology or official records shall be subject to applicable criminal law.

7. The Department of Justice shall retain authority to investigate alleged irregularities, review automated decisions, and order corrective measures where permitted by law.

8. No private agreement shall exempt a government contractor or its employees from criminal liability for unlawful conduct.

9. Judicial contractors shall not independently alter criminal sentences, issue binding arrest warrants, or terminate lawful commitments outside the authority delegated to the judicial system.

10. The existence of a government technology contract shall not transfer ultimate responsibility for the lawful administration of justice away from the State.
