# SECTION 14 — HUNTING, FISHING & WILDLIFE CONSERVATION

## §14.1 — Purpose and Conservation Authority

1. The State of San Andreas shall regulate hunting, fishing, wildlife harvesting, and designated conservation activities for the protection of wildlife, natural habitats, public safety, and lawful recreational and commercial use.

2. Wildlife regulations shall apply to activities conducted within the territorial jurisdiction of San Andreas.

3. The State may designate protected species, hunting seasons, fishing restrictions, conservation areas, and lawful harvesting methods.

4. Conservation requirements shall be established through officially adopted laws and regulatory schedules.

5. No individual shall be punished for violating a wildlife restriction that was not legally effective at the time of the alleged conduct.

6. Wildlife enforcement shall remain subject to the fundamental civil rights and legal standards established under this Code.

## §14.2 — Wildlife Enforcement Authority

1. The State may designate authorized wildlife officers, game wardens, or other conservation enforcement personnel.

2. The legal powers of wildlife enforcement personnel shall be determined by their recognized appointment and applicable law.

3. Duly authorized wildlife law enforcement officers may investigate hunting, fishing, poaching, and related conservation offenses.

4. Personnel serving in non-law-enforcement conservation roles shall not acquire general arrest or search powers solely because of their employment.

5. Authorized wildlife officers may cooperate with municipal, county, and state law enforcement agencies.

6. Wildlife officers exercising police powers shall remain subject to the applicable provisions of Sections 1–6.

7. Wildlife enforcement authority shall not permit unrestricted searches of individuals, vehicles, residences, or businesses.

## §14.3 — Hunting Licenses

1. An individual engaging in hunting activities requiring a license shall possess valid hunting authorization.

2. Hunting licenses shall be issued through the applicable state licensing procedure.

3. A hunting license may authorize designated species, methods, locations, or other activities according to applicable regulations.

4. A license holder shall comply with applicable wildlife restrictions.

5. Possession of a hunting license shall not independently authorize use of prohibited firearms or other illegal weapons.

6. Hunting license suspension and revocation shall remain subject to Section 10.

7. An individual may contest allegedly unlawful licensing action through appropriate proceedings.

## §14.4 — Fishing Licenses

1. An individual engaging in fishing activities requiring a license shall possess valid fishing authorization.

2. Fishing licenses shall be administered by the appropriate state authority.

3. A fishing license may specify lawful methods, locations, species, and other applicable conditions.

4. Commercial fishing may require authorization beyond an ordinary recreational fishing license.

5. Individuals shall comply with applicable catch limits, protected-species restrictions, and conservation areas.

6. Fishing license suspension and revocation shall remain subject to Section 10.

7. A license shall not authorize fishing activities expressly prohibited by another applicable law.

## §14.5 — Protected Wildlife Species

1. The State may designate animal species as protected, restricted, or otherwise regulated.

2. Protected status shall be identified through an officially adopted wildlife schedule.

3. Knowingly hunting, killing, capturing, selling, or transporting a protected species contrary to law may constitute an offense.

4. Protection may apply to the animal, designated body parts, hides, meat, eggs, or other animal products.

5. An individual shall not be criminally punished for possession of an animal product where the State fails to establish that its possession is unlawful under the applicable classification.

6. Authorized scientific, conservation, or emergency activities may be exempt where expressly permitted.

7. Wildlife classification disputes may be considered through an appropriate judicial proceeding.

## §14.6 — Hunting Seasons

1. The State may designate lawful hunting seasons for specified wildlife species.

2. Hunting outside an applicable season may constitute an offense where prohibited by law.

3. The official hunting schedule shall identify the species and periods subject to seasonal restrictions.

4. Emergency conservation restrictions may be imposed where expressly authorized and lawfully published.

5. Officers shall not independently invent or extend a hunting season restriction.

6. Individuals may rely upon officially published hunting information when determining whether a designated season is open.

7. Applicable emergency restrictions shall take effect according to their lawful publication and effective date.

## §14.7 — Hunting Methods and Equipment

1. Hunters shall employ weapons and methods permitted by applicable wildlife regulations.

2. Lawful hunting methods may include authorized firearms, bows, and other designated equipment.

3. The State may prohibit hunting methods that present unreasonable danger or violate conservation requirements.

4. Restricted methods may include:

   a. Unlawful explosive devices.

   b. Prohibited traps or snares.

   c. Unauthorized use of vehicles to pursue or kill wildlife.

   d. Unlawful poisoning of wildlife.

   e. Other methods expressly designated as prohibited.

5. Ordinary possession of hunting equipment shall not independently establish poaching or unlawful hunting.

6. The use of firearms for hunting shall remain subject to Section 12.

7. Officers shall distinguish lawful hunting equipment from prohibited weapons according to applicable law.

## §14.8 — Hunting Near Roads and Populated Areas

1. Hunting activities shall not create unreasonable danger to motorists, pedestrians, residents, or nearby property.

2. The State may designate no-hunting areas around populated locations and other sensitive sites.

3. Discharging a weapon toward an occupied building, public roadway, or person without lawful justification may constitute an offense.

4. Hunters shall comply with applicable firearm discharge restrictions.

5. Hunting-related property damage may give rise to civil liability or criminal charges where the required elements are established.

6. A hunting license shall not authorize trespassing upon private property.

7. Designated restrictions shall be identified through applicable law and official maps.

## §14.9 — Hunting on Private Property

1. Hunting on privately owned or lawfully occupied property shall require permission where applicable law establishes such a requirement.

2. A property owner's permission to enter land shall not exempt a hunter from state wildlife or firearm regulations.

3. Hunting without required permission may constitute trespass or another applicable offense.

4. Property ownership shall not independently authorize hunting protected wildlife in violation of state law.

5. Officers investigating hunting offenses shall respect the search protections applicable to private property.

6. A private landowner may establish lawful restrictions governing hunting access to their premises.

## §14.10 — Poaching

1. Poaching shall mean knowingly engaging in hunting, capture, taking, possession, or commercial exploitation of wildlife in violation of designated conservation laws.

2. Poaching offenses may include:

   a. Taking protected wildlife.

   b. Hunting during a prohibited season.

   c. Exceeding established harvest limits.

   d. Hunting in designated prohibited areas.

   e. Using prohibited hunting methods.

   f. Unlawfully trafficking protected animal products.

3. Penalties shall be established by the Penal Code.

4. A poaching charge shall require evidence of the applicable unlawful conduct.

5. Mere possession of lawful hunting equipment shall not establish poaching.

6. Wildlife products may be seized as evidence where lawful grounds exist.

7. Repeated serious poaching offenses may support hunting license revocation under Section 10.

## §14.11 — Wildlife Harvest Limits

1. The State may establish lawful harvest limits for designated animal species.

2. Harvest limits may apply by individual, hunting period, location, or other authorized classification.

3. Limits shall be published through the official wildlife schedule.

4. Knowingly exceeding an applicable harvest limit may constitute an offense.

5. Lawfully harvested wildlife products shall not be treated as illegal solely because an individual possesses multiple pieces of processed meat or other products.

6. Enforcement shall account for the applicable species, method of acquisition, and relevant documentation.

7. Harvest limits shall not be created or altered solely through informal officer instruction.

## §14.12 — Processing and Possession of Wildlife Products

1. Individuals may lawfully process, store, transport, and possess wildlife products obtained in compliance with applicable law.

2. Products may include meat, hides, pelts, bones, antlers, fish, and other designated materials.

3. The State may establish restrictions concerning protected wildlife products.

4. Lawfully acquired products may be sold or transferred where permitted by law.

5. Possession of a wildlife product shall not automatically establish unlawful hunting.

6. The State may require documentation for designated commercial or protected products.

7. Seizure of wildlife products shall require appropriate legal authority.

## §14.13 — Commercial Hunting and Wildlife Trade

1. Commercial sale of wildlife products may be conducted where authorized by law.

2. Individuals and businesses engaged in regulated wildlife commerce shall possess any applicable commercial or hunting-related authorization.

3. The State may establish requirements for commercial acquisition, processing, transport, and sale.

4. Knowingly selling products derived from unlawfully taken protected wildlife may constitute an offense.

5. A lawful business shall not automatically be criminally responsible for a supplier's independent violation without evidence satisfying the applicable offense.

6. Commercial records may be inspected only within the authority granted by law.

7. Permanent closure or license revocation shall require the procedures established under Section 10.

## §14.14 — Fishing Methods and Equipment

1. Fishing shall be conducted using methods authorized by applicable law.

2. Permitted methods may include designated rods, lines, nets, traps, and other equipment according to the official regulatory schedule.

3. The State may restrict methods that present substantial conservation or public safety risks.

4. Prohibited fishing methods may include unlawful explosives, poisoning, or other methods expressly banned by law.

5. Ordinary possession of fishing equipment shall not independently establish unlawful fishing.

6. Fishing equipment lawfully possessed shall not be permanently confiscated without appropriate legal authority.

7. Commercial fishing methods may require separate permits or endorsements.

## §14.15 — Fishing Seasons, Catch Limits, and Protected Fish

1. The State may designate fishing seasons, catch limits, and protected aquatic species.

2. Applicable restrictions shall be published through an official fishing schedule.

3. Knowingly taking protected fish contrary to law may constitute an offense.

4. Knowingly exceeding an established catch limit may constitute an offense.

5. Where catch-and-release requirements apply, individuals shall comply with the relevant conservation rules.

6. The accidental taking of a protected species shall be evaluated according to applicable law and the individual's subsequent conduct.

7. Officers shall not independently establish new fishing limits or protected classifications.

## §14.16 — Commercial Fishing

1. Commercial fishing shall be permitted where applicable licensing and conservation requirements are satisfied.

2. Commercial operators may be required to maintain catch records and other designated documentation.

3. Commercial fishing vessels shall comply with applicable maritime and vessel safety laws.

4. A recreational fishing license shall not automatically authorize unrestricted commercial fishing.

5. Lawfully caught fish may be sold or transported according to applicable commercial regulations.

6. Commercial fishing violations may result in administrative or criminal proceedings.

7. Fishing vessels, gear, and cargo shall remain protected against unlawful search and confiscation.

## §14.17 — Fishing Vessels and Maritime Activities

1. Fishing activities conducted from boats or other vessels shall remain subject to applicable maritime law.

2. Operators shall comply with relevant vessel registration, licensing, and safety requirements.

3. Wildlife enforcement personnel may conduct lawful fishing-related inspections within their delegated jurisdiction.

4. Such authority shall not automatically permit unrestricted searches of private cabins, personal effects, or unrelated cargo.

5. Vessels may be temporarily detained where a specific law authorizes enforcement of applicable fishing requirements.

6. Permanent vessel forfeiture shall require independent lawful grounds and appropriate legal process.

7. Additional maritime rules shall be established under Section 15.

## §14.18 — Wildlife Conservation Areas

1. The State may designate protected wildlife reserves, conservation areas, and restricted habitats.

2. Activities within protected areas may be restricted according to applicable conservation regulations.

3. Restrictions may concern hunting, fishing, vehicle access, camping, construction, resource extraction, or other designated activities.

4. Conservation boundaries shall be identified through officially adopted maps or other lawful notices.

5. Individuals shall not be criminally punished for violating an unpublished or legally ineffective restriction.

6. Authorized conservation, emergency, and government activities may be permitted under applicable law.

7. Public access shall not be restricted beyond the scope established by law.

## §14.19 — Wildlife Habitat Protection

1. Knowingly damaging protected wildlife habitats in violation of applicable law may constitute an offense.

2. Protected habitats may include designated nesting grounds, spawning areas, breeding sites, and conservation lands.

3. Lawful construction, land use, and commercial operations shall remain subject to applicable environmental restrictions.

4. Ordinary property ownership shall not automatically exempt a person from valid habitat protection laws.

5. Habitat-related enforcement shall respect applicable property rights and judicial procedures.

6. Restoration or remediation orders may be issued where authorized by law.

7. Criminal penalties shall require conduct satisfying the applicable offense.

## §14.20 — Wildlife Endangerment and Unlawful Cruelty

1. Deliberate acts of unlawful cruelty toward animals may constitute an offense where established by the Penal Code.

2. Lawful hunting, fishing, veterinary treatment, and authorized wildlife management shall not constitute cruelty solely because an animal is injured or killed during the activity.

3. Individuals shall not intentionally cause unnecessary animal suffering through methods prohibited by law.

4. Injured wildlife may be treated, relocated, or humanely euthanized by authorized personnel where lawful and necessary.

5. An officer shall distinguish lawful wildlife harvesting from prohibited animal cruelty according to the circumstances and applicable law.

6. Violations may be referred for criminal or conservation enforcement.

## §14.21 — Dangerous Wildlife

1. Individuals may take reasonable action to protect themselves or others from an immediate threat posed by dangerous wildlife.

2. Lawful defensive action shall be evaluated according to the circumstances and applicable self-defense and wildlife laws.

3. Killing a protected animal solely for convenience or commercial gain shall not automatically qualify as lawful defensive action.

4. A person who lawfully acts to prevent imminent serious injury shall not be presumed guilty of poaching solely because a protected animal was killed.

5. Authorized wildlife officers may remove, relocate, or otherwise manage dangerous animals where permitted by law.

6. Emergency wildlife control measures shall remain subject to applicable public safety requirements.

## §14.22 — Wildlife Rescue and Rehabilitation

1. The State may authorize designated individuals or organizations to rescue and rehabilitate injured or endangered wildlife.

2. Such activities may require applicable professional or conservation permits.

3. Authorized rehabilitation shall not automatically confer ownership of protected wildlife.

4. Unlawful possession or commercial sale of protected animals shall not be excused solely by an unsupported claim of rescue.

5. Wildlife authorities may establish lawful release, transport, and care procedures.

6. Authorized conservation work shall not independently permit unlawful entry onto private property.

## §14.23 — Wildlife Transportation

1. Lawfully harvested wildlife products may be transported according to applicable law.

2. Transport of designated protected species or products may require special authorization.

3. Commercial transport may require lawful documentation where prescribed.

4. Wildlife transportation shall remain subject to applicable vehicle and commercial transport regulations under Section 11.

5. An ordinary hunter or fisherman shall not be presumed to engage in commercial trafficking solely because lawful products are transported in a vehicle.

6. Searches of vehicles and cargo shall remain subject to Section 4.

7. Unlawful wildlife products may be seized where the applicable legal requirements are satisfied.

## §14.24 — Wildlife-Related Property Seizure

1. Authorized officers may seize unlawful wildlife products, prohibited hunting equipment, and other evidence where lawful grounds exist.

2. Temporary evidence seizure shall be distinguished from permanent property forfeiture.

3. Firearms used in qualifying wildlife offenses may be held as evidence under applicable law.

4. A hunting or fishing violation shall not automatically authorize permanent confiscation of an unrelated vehicle, boat, or business.

5. Permanent forfeiture shall require appropriate judicial or statutory authority.

6. Lawful ownership and third-party interests shall be considered where applicable.

7. Affected individuals may petition for review and return of unlawfully retained property.

## §14.25 — Wildlife Searches and Inspections

1. Wildlife enforcement officers may conduct searches and inspections only within the authority granted by law.

2. An officer may request applicable licenses and required harvest documentation during a lawful wildlife enforcement encounter.

3. A wildlife license shall not constitute blanket consent to search a residence, vehicle, or unrelated personal property.

4. Commercial wildlife operations may be subject to specifically authorized administrative inspections.

5. Restricted business areas and private residences shall remain protected under Section 4.

6. Reasonable suspicion shall not independently authorize a full evidentiary search.

7. Evidence discovered through an unlawful wildlife search may be challenged before the Department of Justice.

## §14.26 — Wildlife Enforcement Detention and Arrest

1. A duly authorized wildlife law enforcement officer may conduct investigative detention where reasonable suspicion of a qualifying offense exists and the officer possesses lawful detention authority.

2. Arrest shall require probable cause or other lawful authority as established by Sections 2 and 3.

3. Wildlife officers shall inform detained individuals of the general reason for enforcement action when reasonably practicable.

4. Individuals shall retain their civil rights during wildlife investigations.

5. A suspected licensing violation shall not automatically authorize custodial arrest unless the applicable law permits it.

6. Wildlife enforcement officers shall not impose criminal sentences beyond their delegated authority.

7. Cases requiring judicial determination shall be referred through appropriate criminal procedures.

## §14.27 — Wildlife Licensing Penalties

1. Hunting and fishing licenses may be suspended or revoked for qualifying conservation offenses.

2. Permanent revocation shall require the procedures established under Section 10.

3. An arrest alone shall not establish permanent disqualification.

4. Serious or repeated offenses may support enhanced licensing sanctions where authorized by law.

5. Administrative licensing penalties shall not independently constitute criminal imprisonment.

6. Affected individuals may seek judicial review.

7. Reinstatement shall remain subject to the applicable licensing procedure.

## §14.28 — Wildlife Evidence and Records

1. Wildlife officers shall maintain appropriate records concerning significant enforcement actions.

2. Records may identify the alleged offense, relevant species, location, seized property, and supporting evidence.

3. Protected wildlife classifications shall be verified through the applicable official schedule.

4. Officers shall not knowingly falsify harvest records, protected-species identifications, or evidence.

5. Lawfully seized evidence shall be preserved according to applicable procedures.

6. Individuals may challenge the accuracy or admissibility of wildlife enforcement evidence through authorized judicial proceedings.

## §14.29 — Conservation and Commercial Rights

1. The State shall recognize lawful hunting, fishing, and wildlife-related commerce as legitimate activities when conducted in compliance with applicable law.

2. Conservation regulations shall not be used as a pretext for arbitrary seizure of lawful property.

3. Licensed businesses and individuals shall retain access to administrative and judicial review.

4. The State may establish lawful conservation restrictions necessary to protect designated wildlife resources.

5. Wildlife conservation authority shall not independently confer ownership of privately held land or lawfully acquired commercial property upon the State.

6. Environmental and conservation disputes may be resolved through the appropriate legal process.

## §14.30 — Wildlife Enforcement Accountability

1. Wildlife enforcement personnel shall remain accountable for their official conduct.

2. No officer shall fabricate poaching charges or knowingly misrepresent wildlife classifications.

3. No officer shall personally appropriate seized wildlife products or equipment outside lawful procedures.

4. Officers shall not unlawfully enter private property or conduct unauthorized searches under the pretext of conservation enforcement.

5. Allegations of misconduct may be referred for departmental or judicial investigation.

6. A lawful wildlife investigation shall not exempt an officer from applicable civil rights or use-of-force requirements.

7. The Department of Justice may review disputed wildlife enforcement actions, licensing decisions, and property seizures.

# LEGISLATIVE CONTINUITY AND AUTHORITY

1. Sections 13–14 shall be interpreted consistently with Sections 1–12 of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

2. Fundamental civil rights under Section 1 shall remain applicable to all controlled-substance and wildlife enforcement proceedings.

3. Investigative standards, detention, and arrest shall remain governed by Sections 2–3.

4. Searches and seizures shall remain subject to Section 4.

5. Law enforcement powers shall remain subject to Section 5.

6. All uses of force shall remain subject to Section 6.

7. Criminal processing and prosecution shall remain governed by Sections 7 and 9.

8. Criminal sentencing shall remain subject to the restrictions established in Section 8.

9. License suspension and revocation shall remain subject to Section 10.

10. Vehicle, commercial transport, and property seizure provisions shall remain subject to Section 11.

11. Firearm possession, carrying, and use shall remain subject to Section 12.

12. Licensed cannabis commerce shall be distinguished from unlawful controlled-substance distribution.

13. Lawful possession of a regulated substance shall not independently justify criminal arrest or unrestricted search.

14. Lawful hunting and fishing activities shall not independently establish poaching or other criminal conduct.

15. No law enforcement agency, licensing authority, or private contractor may create criminal offenses or penalties outside duly enacted law.

16. The State shall maintain official controlled-substance, wildlife, hunting, and fishing schedules identifying applicable classifications, restrictions, and licensing requirements.

17. Administrative regulations shall not override the civil rights, judicial procedures, or property protections established by this Code.

18. All significant enforcement, seizure, and licensing actions shall remain subject to lawful review.
