# SECTION 6 — POLICE USE OF FORCE

## §6.1 — General Principles

1. Law enforcement officers shall use only such force as is objectively reasonable, necessary, and proportionate to the circumstances confronting them.
2. The purpose of force shall be to accomplish a lawful objective, protect life, prevent injury, or maintain lawful control.
3. Force shall not be used as punishment, retaliation, intimidation, or a means of compelling a confession.
4. Officers shall consider the severity of the offense, the immediate threat presented, and whether the individual is actively resisting or attempting to evade lawful custody.
5. Force shall be reduced or discontinued when its lawful justification no longer exists.
6. Every use of force shall remain subject to review under this Code.

## §6.2 — Verbal Commands and De-escalation

1. Where reasonably practicable, officers shall attempt to resolve confrontations through communication, warnings, and reasonable opportunities for voluntary compliance.
2. Officers shall identify themselves and communicate lawful commands before using force when the circumstances safely permit.
3. De-escalation may include reasonable distance, time, cover, assistance from additional officers, or other appropriate measures.
4. An officer shall not be required to delay necessary action where an immediate threat of serious bodily harm or death exists.
5. The failure of an individual to immediately comply shall not automatically justify the use of significant physical force.
6. Officers shall account for apparent injury, disability, confusion, communication difficulties, or other circumstances affecting an individual's ability to understand or comply.

## §6.3 — Physical Control and Restraint

1. Officers may use reasonable physical control to conduct a lawful arrest, detention, or other authorized enforcement action.
2. Physical control may include guiding, holding, handcuffing, or other approved restraint methods.
3. The degree of force used shall be limited to what is reasonably necessary.
4. Restraints shall not be applied or maintained solely to punish an individual.
5. Officers shall monitor restrained individuals for signs of medical distress where reasonably practicable.
6. Physical force against a restrained individual shall require an independent and continuing lawful justification.

## §6.4 — Less-Lethal Force

1. Less-lethal force may be used when reasonably necessary to control an individual presenting a genuine safety threat, actively resisting lawful custody, or engaging in conduct that creates a substantial risk of injury.
2. Approved less-lethal equipment may include conducted-energy devices, batons, less-lethal projectiles, and other equipment authorized by the relevant law enforcement agency.
3. Less-lethal force shall not be used solely in response to verbal disagreement, insults, or the lawful exercise of civil rights.
4. Officers shall consider whether the selected method presents an unreasonable risk of serious injury under the circumstances.
5. Repeated applications of less-lethal force shall require a continuing and independently justifiable need.
6. Less-lethal force shall cease when the individual is safely controlled and no continuing threat justifies further application.

## §6.5 — Lethal Force

1. Lethal force may be used only when an officer reasonably believes that such force is immediately necessary to protect an individual from an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury.
2. The existence of a weapon alone shall not automatically justify lethal force.
3. An individual's prior criminal record, reputation, or suspected involvement in serious criminal activity shall not independently justify lethal force.
4. Lethal force shall not be used solely to prevent an unarmed individual from escaping custody.
5. Where reasonably practicable, officers shall provide a clear warning before employing lethal force.
6. An officer shall discontinue lethal force when the imminent threat has ended.
7. Every use of lethal force shall be subject to appropriate investigation and review.

## §6.6 — Force Against Fleeing Individuals

1. An individual's attempt to flee a lawful detention or arrest shall not independently authorize lethal force.
2. Officers may employ reasonable and proportionate physical or less-lethal measures to prevent escape where lawful and necessary.
3. Lethal force against a fleeing individual shall be permissible only where the requirements of §6.5 are independently satisfied.
4. Officers shall consider the danger presented to uninvolved persons when attempting to stop a fleeing individual.
5. Force shall not be escalated solely because an officer is frustrated by a suspect's refusal to surrender.

## §6.7 — Force Against Occupants of Vehicles

1. A motor vehicle may constitute a deadly threat when operated in a manner presenting an imminent danger of death or serious bodily injury.
2. Officers shall not use lethal force against a vehicle or its occupants solely because the driver refuses to stop or attempts to evade police.
3. Lethal force involving a vehicle shall require an objectively reasonable belief that such force is immediately necessary to prevent death or serious bodily injury.
4. Officers shall account for the danger posed to passengers, pedestrians, and other road users.
5. The use of roadblocks, vehicle intervention techniques, or other methods intended to forcibly stop a vehicle shall be governed by applicable departmental procedures and the general requirements of necessity and proportionality.
6. Intentional use of a vehicle as a weapon by an officer shall be assessed under the same legal standards applicable to other uses of potentially lethal force.

## §6.8 — Force Against Restrained or Incapacitated Individuals

1. No officer shall use force against a restrained or incapacitated individual except where reasonably necessary to address an immediate and continuing threat.
2. An individual who is unconscious, incapacitated, or otherwise incapable of resisting shall not be subjected to punitive force.
3. Officers shall not deliberately withhold necessary medical assistance as punishment.
4. Continued use of a weapon or restraint after an individual has ceased presenting a lawful threat may constitute excessive force.
5. Officers shall take reasonable steps to protect individuals in custody from foreseeable violence or serious injury.

## §6.9 — Medical Assistance Following Force

1. Officers shall request or arrange appropriate medical assistance when an individual suffers or reasonably appears to have suffered significant injury following the use of force.
2. Officers shall not intentionally delay necessary medical care for the purpose of punishment or extracting information.
3. Medical personnel shall be permitted reasonable access to injured individuals when conditions are sufficiently safe.
4. Officers shall communicate known relevant injuries and the circumstances of force to responding medical personnel where necessary for treatment.
5. An individual's status as a suspect or prisoner shall not independently justify denial of necessary medical treatment.

## §6.10 — Warning Shots and Reckless Discharge

1. Officers shall not discharge firearms solely to intimidate an individual or compel compliance.
2. Warning shots shall not be used as a substitute for lawful verbal commands.
3. Firearms shall not be discharged into an area where the officer cannot reasonably account for the danger to other persons.
4. Reckless or unnecessary discharge of a firearm may constitute official misconduct or a criminal offense.
5. The discharge of a firearm shall remain subject to departmental reporting requirements, regardless of whether injury occurs.

## §6.11 — Intervention Against Excessive Force

1. An officer who observes another officer employing clearly unlawful or excessive force shall, when reasonably capable of doing so without creating an unreasonable danger, take appropriate action to prevent or stop the violation.
2. An officer shall report observed serious misconduct through established departmental or judicial channels.
3. Supervisory officers shall not knowingly conceal serious violations of this section.
4. No officer shall be subjected to unlawful retaliation solely for making a truthful report of excessive force.
5. Knowingly falsifying allegations or deliberately concealing material evidence concerning a use-of-force incident may constitute official misconduct.

## §6.12 — Reporting and Review of Force

1. Significant uses of force shall be documented according to established departmental procedures.
2. Reports shall accurately describe the circumstances, perceived threats, force employed, and resulting injuries known to the reporting officer.
3. Uses of lethal force shall be referred for appropriate supervisory and investigative review.
4. The Department of Justice may investigate alleged violations of this section.
5. Evidence of criminal conduct by an officer may be referred for prosecution.
6. Administrative findings shall not independently determine criminal guilt but may be considered in lawful disciplinary or judicial proceedings.

## §6.13 — Department of Corrections Use of Force

1. Correctional officers exercising lawful authority within correctional facilities shall be subject to the fundamental use-of-force principles established under this section.
2. Correctional officers may employ reasonable physical control and approved less-lethal measures to maintain institutional safety, prevent assaults, respond to disturbances, and prevent unlawful escapes.
3. Lethal force shall be governed by the requirements of §6.5.
4. An inmate's refusal to participate in an institutional activity shall not independently justify excessive force.
5. Correctional disciplinary authority shall not permit corporal punishment, torture, or retaliatory violence.
6. Significant uses of force within correctional facilities shall be documented and subject to appropriate review.
7. The Department of Corrections may adopt institutional use-of-force procedures consistent with this Code, but such procedures shall not diminish the protections established herein.

## §6.14 — Government Accountability and Judicial Remedies

1. An individual subjected to an allegedly unlawful use of force may file an appropriate complaint or petition before the Department of Justice.
2. Courts may review the legality of force employed by law enforcement or correctional personnel.
3. A finding of unlawful force may result in applicable disciplinary action, criminal proceedings, or civil remedies.
4. No officer shall be exempt from legal accountability solely because the conduct occurred during an official operation.
5. A lawful arrest or criminal conviction shall not automatically excuse an independently unlawful use of force.
6. Government authorities shall not knowingly obstruct a lawful investigation into official use-of-force misconduct.

---

# LEGISLATIVE CONTINUITY

1. Sections 5–6 shall be read in conjunction with Sections 1–4 of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.
2. The rights established in Section 1 apply automatically and do not require a formal verbal warning.
3. The standards governing reasonable suspicion and probable cause remain established under Section 2.
4. All detention and arrest powers remain subject to Section 3.
5. All search and seizure activity remains subject to Section 4.
6. No policy issued by an individual law enforcement agency or correctional institution shall supersede the requirements of this Code.
7. Alleged violations may be referred to the Department of Justice through the appropriate judicial process.
