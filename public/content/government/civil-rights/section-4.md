# SECTION 4 — SEARCH AND SEIZURE

## §4.1 — General Search Authority

1. A law enforcement officer may conduct a search only where authorized by this Code or another applicable law.

2. Every search shall remain within the scope justified by a valid warrant, lawful consent, or another recognized legal exception.

3. Property or persons unrelated to the lawful purpose of a search shall not be searched without an independent legal basis.

4. Officers shall not intentionally circumvent warrant requirements by dividing, disguising, or misrepresenting an investigation.

5. The existence of probable cause concerning one individual shall not automatically establish probable cause concerning another individual.

## §4.2 — Protective Searches During Detention

1. During a lawful investigative detention, an officer may conduct a limited protective pat-down when specific, articulable facts reasonably indicate that the individual is armed and dangerous.

2. Such a search shall be restricted to identifying weapons or objects presenting an immediate safety threat.

3. A protective pat-down shall not authorize the examination of wallets, phones, bags, or personal belongings for unrelated evidence.

4. Objects whose incriminating nature becomes immediately apparent during a lawful protective search may be seized where permitted by law.

5. A protective search shall not be used as a substitute for an evidentiary search requiring probable cause or a warrant.

## §4.3 — Searches Incident to Lawful Arrest

1. Following a lawful custodial arrest, officers may search the arrested individual and areas within their immediate control for weapons, evidence, or items presenting a safety or escape risk.

2. Officers may seize evidence and prohibited items lawfully discovered during such a search.

3. Search incident to arrest shall not automatically permit a general search of an entire residence, business, or vehicle.

4. Additional searches shall require independent lawful justification.

5. An individual's placement in handcuffs shall not automatically authorize a search of unrelated property.

## §4.4 — Vehicle Searches

1. A vehicle may be searched with valid consent, a lawful warrant, or probable cause that the vehicle contains evidence or contraband connected to a criminal offense, subject to applicable exceptions.

2. A probable-cause vehicle search shall be limited to locations and containers reasonably capable of holding the object of the search.

3. A routine traffic violation shall not independently authorize a search of the vehicle.

4. An officer may perform a limited protective inspection of accessible areas of a vehicle when specific circumstances reasonably indicate an immediate danger involving a weapon.

5. When a vehicle is lawfully impounded, officers may conduct a documented inventory pursuant to standardized impound procedures.

6. Inventory searches shall not be used as a pretext for an otherwise unauthorized criminal investigation.

7. Commercial vehicles remain subject to lawful credential, registration, and cargo-document inspections. Such inspections shall not automatically authorize unrestricted searches of private belongings or unrelated compartments.

8. A search of a commercial vehicle's cargo shall require lawful authority appropriate to the circumstances.

## §4.5 — Search Warrants

1. A search warrant must be issued by a judge or another judicial authority expressly empowered by law to issue the warrant.

2. Applications must establish probable cause and identify the location, property, or evidence sought.

3. A warrant shall clearly describe the premises or property subject to search and the lawful purpose of the search.

4. Officers shall execute warrants within their authorized scope and any conditions imposed by the issuing authority.

5. A warrant authorizing the search of one residence or business shall not automatically authorize a search of neighboring properties.

6. A warrant may be challenged before the Department of Justice when its issuance or execution is alleged to have been unlawful.

7. A warrant shall not provide authority beyond the scope specifically authorized in the judicial order.

8. A warrant issued through an authorized automated judicial service shall remain subject to the same legal standards and judicial review as a warrant issued by a human judge.

## §4.6 — Residential Searches

A private dwelling may be searched only where one or more of the following conditions apply:

1. A valid judicial search warrant authorizes entry and search.

2. A legally authorized occupant voluntarily consents to the search within the scope of their authority.

3. Exigent circumstances justify immediate entry or action.

4. Another exception expressly recognized by law applies.

5. An arrest occurring near a residence shall not automatically permit officers to search the residence.

6. Officers lawfully entering a residence for a limited purpose shall not expand their search beyond the authorized purpose without further lawful justification.

## §4.7 — Commercial Property Searches

1. Publicly accessible areas of a business may be entered by law enforcement during normal public access, subject to lawful restrictions.

2. Restricted offices, employee areas, storage rooms, private records, and other non-public business spaces remain protected against unreasonable search.

3. Officers may search protected business areas with a warrant, valid consent, exigent circumstances, or another specific lawful authorization.

4. A business owner's refusal to consent to an otherwise unauthorized search shall not constitute obstruction of justice.

5. Administrative inspections expressly authorized by law shall remain limited to their lawful regulatory purposes.

6. Ownership or management of a licensed business shall not constitute blanket consent to criminal investigation searches.

## §4.8 — Consent Searches

1. An individual with lawful authority over property may voluntarily consent to its search.

2. Officers shall respect limitations placed upon the scope of consent.

3. Where practicable, consent should be recorded or documented.

4. Consent obtained from a person without lawful authority over the property shall not authorize the search.

5. Consent may not be inferred solely from silence or failure to resist an officer.

6. Withdrawal of consent shall be respected unless independent lawful grounds authorize continuation of the search.

7. Officers shall not knowingly misrepresent the existence of a warrant or legal authority to obtain consent.

## §4.9 — Emergency Entry and Hot Pursuit

1. Officers may enter protected property without a warrant where objectively reasonable circumstances establish an immediate threat to life or serious bodily safety.

2. Officers may enter property during qualifying hot pursuit where the underlying offense, immediacy of pursuit, and surrounding circumstances establish a lawful emergency exception.

3. Entry shall be limited to addressing the emergency, locating the fleeing suspect, or securing persons from immediate danger.

4. A lawful emergency entry shall not automatically authorize a general evidentiary search after the emergency has ended.

5. Where the circumstances no longer justify continued warrantless searching, officers shall obtain appropriate legal authority before expanding the investigation.

## §4.10 — Plain-View Evidence

1. An officer who is lawfully present may seize evidence or contraband in plain view when its incriminating character is immediately apparent and the officer has lawful access to it.

2. Officers shall not manipulate, open, or otherwise investigate concealed property merely to establish that it contains illegal items without additional legal authority.

3. Plain-view discovery shall not authorize an unrestricted search of the surrounding premises.

4. Evidence lawfully observed but not immediately accessible may support an application for a judicial warrant.

## §4.11 — Search and Seizure of Electronic Devices

1. Mobile phones, computers, digital storage devices, and private electronic communications are protected against unreasonable search.

2. The physical seizure of an electronic device during a lawful arrest or investigation shall not automatically authorize examination of its private digital contents.

3. Access to private stored digital information shall require a judicial warrant, valid informed consent, or a specifically applicable lawful emergency exception.

4. Warrants authorizing electronic searches shall identify the relevant investigative purpose and scope of examination.

5. Officers shall not access unrelated private communications or records beyond the authorized scope without additional legal authority.

6. Government-controlled judicial records shall remain subject to applicable judicial integrity, evidence, and investigative procedures.

## §4.12 — Property Seizure and Evidence Handling

1. Officers may seize evidence, prohibited items, and property lawfully subject to forfeiture or impoundment.

2. Seized items shall be documented in an official property or evidence record.

3. Property not lawfully retained as evidence, contraband, or forfeited property shall be eligible for return to its lawful owner.

4. Individuals may petition the Department of Justice for the return of unlawfully seized or improperly retained property.

5. Permanent forfeiture of valuable property shall require the applicable legal process and authorization established by law.

6. Government authorities shall take reasonable measures to preserve evidence entrusted to their custody.

7. Officers shall not knowingly destroy, conceal, alter, or unlawfully dispose of seized evidence.

8. Property seized under an invalidated warrant shall be subject to judicial review concerning its continued retention.

## §4.13 — Unlawful Searches and Remedies

1. An individual may challenge an allegedly unlawful search, seizure, or arrest through the Department of Justice.

2. A court may suppress evidence obtained through a substantial violation of this Code, subject to applicable legal exceptions.

3. A court may order the return of unlawfully retained property.

4. Deliberate misconduct or serious violations by an officer may be referred for disciplinary or criminal proceedings.

5. A violation of this section shall not automatically invalidate unrelated lawfully obtained evidence or independently established charges.

6. Officers shall not be exempt from judicial review solely because they acted during an official investigation.

7. The Department of Justice shall retain authority to examine the legality of searches and seizures conducted under automated judicial warrants.
