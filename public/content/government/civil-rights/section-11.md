# ROSEFIRE CIVIL RIGHTS & JUSTICE CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.5 — Sections 11–12**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing the lawful regulation of motor vehicles, commercial transportation, towing, impoundment, repossession, and vehicle forfeiture; and governing the possession, classification, carrying, registration, use, and seizure of firearms and other regulated weapons within the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–10 remain enacted and in force. Sections 11–12 shall constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code and shall be interpreted consistently with all previously established civil rights, property protections, enforcement limitations, judicial procedures, and licensing requirements.

---

# SECTION 11 — VEHICLES, TOWING, IMPOUND & SEIZURE

## §11.1 — General Vehicle Rights and Responsibilities

1. Every individual may lawfully own, lease, purchase, sell, and operate motor vehicles within the State of San Andreas, subject to applicable laws.

2. Vehicle ownership shall not independently establish authorization to operate the vehicle upon public roads without the required license and registration.

3. Motor vehicles shall be operated in a manner that does not create an unreasonable danger to other persons or property.

4. Vehicle owners shall comply with applicable registration, licensing, insurance-independent financial obligations, and roadway safety requirements established by law.

5. No individual shall be deprived of lawful vehicle ownership solely because of an arrest, criminal allegation, or unpaid citation without appropriate legal authority.

6. Vehicle ownership, financial encumbrances, impoundment, and permanent forfeiture shall remain distinct legal matters.

7. All vehicle searches shall remain subject to Section 4 of this Code.

## §11.2 — Vehicle Registration and Ownership

1. Motor vehicles intended for lawful public-road operation shall maintain valid registration where required by law.

2. Registration shall identify the recognized owner or other legally authorized registrant.

3. Vehicle ownership may be established through lawful purchase, transfer, inheritance, court order, or another recognized means.

4. A vehicle's registration shall not automatically extinguish a valid financial lien, lease interest, or other lawful encumbrance.

5. Knowingly falsifying vehicle registration or ownership documentation may constitute a criminal offense.

6. An individual who lawfully purchases or receives a vehicle shall be entitled to have the ownership transfer properly recorded.

7. Disputes concerning registered ownership may be brought before an authorized court.

8. Unauthorized alteration of official registration records shall be prohibited.

## §11.3 — Vehicle Identification and License Plates

1. Registered vehicles shall display lawful identification plates where required.

2. A license plate shall correspond to the vehicle to which it has been lawfully assigned.

3. Knowingly displaying a stolen, falsified, altered, or unlawfully assigned plate may constitute a criminal offense.

4. Knowingly removing or altering a vehicle identification number for the purpose of concealing criminal conduct or ownership may constitute an offense.

5. A damaged or missing license plate shall not automatically establish intentional criminal misconduct.

6. An officer may investigate discrepancies in registration or identification where lawful grounds exist.

7. Permanent seizure of a vehicle shall not occur solely because a minor registration defect has been discovered.

## §11.4 — Lawful Vehicle Operation

1. Individuals operating motor vehicles upon public roads shall possess the applicable driving authorization.

2. Drivers shall observe posted speed limits, traffic control devices, and other applicable roadway regulations.

3. Drivers shall exercise reasonable care toward pedestrians, cyclists, other motorists, and property.

4. Reckless or dangerous operation may result in criminal charges and applicable licensing consequences.

5. Driving under the influence shall remain subject to the applicable Penal Code and the procedures established under §5.6.

6. An officer may order an individual to discontinue vehicle operation when lawful circumstances establish an immediate safety concern.

7. Temporary prohibition from continued driving shall not automatically constitute permanent loss of vehicle ownership or driving privileges.

## §11.5 — Commercial Vehicle Operations

1. Commercial vehicles shall be operated by individuals holding the licenses required for their designated vehicle classification.

2. Commercial operators shall comply with applicable vehicle safety, cargo transportation, and commercial documentation requirements.

3. A Commercial Driver's License shall be required only for vehicles or commercial operations expressly designated under applicable licensing law.

4. The State may recognize multiple classes or endorsements of commercial driving authority.

5. Commercial operators may be required to present relevant licensing and transportation documents during lawful inspections.

6. Commercial operation without required authorization may result in criminal or administrative penalties.

7. A commercial violation shall not automatically justify seizure of the vehicle or its cargo.

8. Commercial vehicle enforcement shall remain subject to the protections established under Sections 1, 4, 5, and 10.

## §11.6 — Commercial Cargo and Waybills

1. Commercial cargo transportation may require a valid waybill, manifest, or equivalent transportation document where prescribed by law.

2. A waybill may identify the transporting company, operator, cargo classification, origin, destination, and other required commercial information.

3. Knowingly falsifying commercial transportation documentation may constitute a criminal offense.

4. An operator's inability to immediately present a document may justify temporary verification where authorized by law.

5. Failure to possess required documentation may justify restricting continued commercial transport until the violation is corrected.

6. Temporary detention of a commercial trailer shall not automatically constitute permanent confiscation of the trailer or its cargo.

7. Cargo searches shall require lawful authority under Section 4 or a specifically authorized commercial inspection procedure.

8. Lawfully owned cargo unrelated to an offense shall not be permanently forfeited without independent legal grounds.

## §11.7 — Department of Transportation Authority

1. The State recognizes authorized Department of Transportation personnel and properly licensed towing operators as persons permitted to conduct designated roadway recovery and transportation services.

2. Authorized personnel may perform:

   a. Vehicle towing and recovery.

   b. Roadside assistance.

   c. Disabled vehicle removal.

   d. Lawfully requested vehicle transport.

   e. Accident scene clearance under appropriate authority.

   f. Authorized impound transportation.

   g. Other recognized transportation and roadway services.

3. Transportation personnel shall not acquire general law enforcement powers solely through their employment.

4. DOT personnel may cooperate with law enforcement, emergency medical personnel, and other authorized public services.

5. DOT employees shall comply with applicable roadway safety requirements when performing official duties.

6. A DOT employee shall not intentionally damage, unlawfully confiscate, or misappropriate a vehicle entrusted to their care.

7. DOT personnel shall remain subject to applicable criminal and civil laws.

## §11.8 — Authorized Towing Operations

1. A motor vehicle may be towed by an authorized operator with lawful grounds for removal.

2. Lawful grounds may include:

   a. Consent or request of the lawful owner or authorized operator.

   b. Police-directed towing.

   c. Removal of an abandoned vehicle.

   d. Removal of a vehicle obstructing traffic or emergency access.

   e. Removal from a location where parking is lawfully prohibited and towing is authorized.

   f. Lawful property recovery or repossession.

   g. Another towing authorization expressly established by law.

3. A towing operator shall not remove a vehicle merely because the operator wishes to obtain possession of it.

4. Towing operations shall be conducted using reasonable care to prevent unnecessary vehicle damage.

5. Tow operators shall maintain appropriate records of vehicles removed under official or commercial authority.

6. The lawful owner shall retain applicable rights to recover a vehicle from authorized storage.

7. Disputes concerning improper towing may be brought through an appropriate administrative or judicial proceeding.

## §11.9 — Parking Enforcement and Vehicle Removal

1. Vehicles may be removed when parked in violation of a lawful restriction authorizing towing.

2. Towing may be authorized where a vehicle:

   a. Obstructs a public roadway.

   b. Blocks emergency vehicle access.

   c. Blocks a lawful driveway or access point.

   d. Occupies a designated emergency access area.

   e. Is abandoned in a prohibited location.

   f. Creates an immediate traffic or public safety hazard.

   g. Otherwise satisfies an expressly established towing condition.

3. Authorized DOT personnel may remove qualifying vehicles without first locating the owner where immediate removal is lawful.

4. A vehicle shall not be permanently forfeited solely because it was illegally parked.

5. Property owners may request removal of unauthorized vehicles from private premises according to applicable law.

6. A towing operator shall not intentionally misrepresent a vehicle as illegally parked to justify unauthorized removal.

7. Improper towing fees may be contested through the appropriate process.

## §11.10 — Abandoned Vehicles

1. A vehicle may be classified as abandoned when the circumstances satisfy applicable statutory abandonment requirements.

2. The mere absence of the owner from a parked vehicle shall not establish abandonment.

3. Authorities may remove vehicles reasonably believed to be abandoned where lawful conditions exist.

4. The owner shall retain appropriate notice and recovery rights.

5. Abandoned vehicles shall not automatically become the property of the towing operator.

6. Disposal, auction, or transfer of an unclaimed vehicle shall require the applicable notice and legal procedures.

7. A vehicle connected to a criminal investigation may be held under separate evidentiary authority.

## §11.11 — Roadside Assistance and Recovery

1. Authorized towing and transportation personnel may provide roadside assistance to motorists.

2. Services may include vehicle recovery, battery assistance, tire service, fuel delivery, lockout assistance, and other lawful roadside operations.

3. A roadside service provider shall not knowingly perform an unauthorized vehicle entry or transport.

4. Permission from the vehicle owner or another legally authorized person shall ordinarily be required for voluntary roadside services.

5. Emergency circumstances or lawful government orders may authorize otherwise permitted intervention.

6. Service providers shall exercise reasonable care when handling customer vehicles and property.

7. Disputes involving service charges, vehicle damage, or contractual performance may be resolved through appropriate civil proceedings.

## §11.12 — Emergency Towing and Accident Recovery

1. Law enforcement may direct authorized towing personnel to remove vehicles following collisions, criminal incidents, or other roadway emergencies.

2. Emergency towing shall be limited to the purposes reasonably necessary to restore roadway safety, preserve evidence, or protect persons and property.

3. A vehicle may be transported to an authorized storage or impound facility where circumstances justify such action.

4. A police-requested tow shall not automatically establish permanent seizure of the vehicle.

5. Towing personnel shall comply with reasonable lawful instructions concerning scene safety and evidence preservation.

6. Law enforcement shall not require a towing operator to perform an unlawful seizure.

7. An individual whose vehicle is removed shall retain applicable recovery and judicial review rights.

## §11.13 — Standard Vehicle Impoundment

1. Standard impoundment is the temporary lawful removal and storage of a motor vehicle.

2. Standard impoundment may be authorized when:

   a. The vehicle is abandoned or unlawfully parked.

   b. The driver cannot lawfully or safely continue operating the vehicle and no reasonable alternative arrangement is available.

   c. The vehicle presents a continuing public safety hazard.

   d. The vehicle requires temporary storage following lawful recovery.

   e. Another legal provision expressly permits impoundment.

3. Standard impoundment shall not constitute transfer of vehicle ownership.

4. The owner or authorized claimant may recover the vehicle upon satisfying applicable lawful release conditions.

5. Authorities shall not impose arbitrary impound fees unrelated to an authorized fee schedule.

6. An individual may challenge an allegedly improper impound.

## §11.14 — Extended Vehicle Impoundment

1. Extended impoundment may be imposed for designated serious offenses involving the use or operation of a motor vehicle.

2. Qualifying offenses may include:

   a. Felony evasion.

   b. Driving under the influence.

   c. Serious reckless driving.

   d. Vehicular manslaughter.

   e. Participation in prohibited street racing.

   f. Use of the vehicle to commit a qualifying violent offense.

   g. Repeated serious vehicle-related violations.

3. Extended impoundment shall require the lawful authority established by the applicable offense and impoundment schedule.

4. The duration of impoundment shall be specified in the applicable law or authorized order.

5. The vehicle owner shall retain the right to contest an unlawful impound.

6. Extended impoundment shall not automatically extinguish financing agreements, ownership interests, or valid liens.

7. Where the owner was not involved in the underlying offense, that circumstance shall be considered under applicable innocent-owner protections.

## §11.15 — Impoundment of Commercial Vehicles and Trailers

1. Commercial vehicles and trailers may be impounded where lawful grounds exist.

2. Grounds may include:

   a. Operation without a required CDL.

   b. Serious commercial transport safety violations.

   c. Transportation of unlawfully held cargo.

   d. Use in qualifying criminal activity.

   e. Failure to produce required transportation documents where continued operation is lawfully prohibited.

3. A commercial trailer shall not be permanently confiscated solely because a driver lacks proper credentials.

4. Where practicable, lawful arrangements may be made for a properly licensed replacement operator to remove the vehicle or trailer.

5. Commercial cargo unrelated to criminal conduct shall remain protected against unlawful confiscation.

6. Cargo requiring preservation, refrigeration, or other special handling shall receive reasonable care during authorized impoundment.

7. The lawful interests of a separate trailer owner, cargo owner, or financing company shall be considered where applicable.

## §11.16 — Vehicle Impound Records

1. Every official vehicle impound shall be documented.

2. Records shall identify:

   a. The vehicle and its registration.

   b. The reason for impoundment.

   c. The authorizing officer or authority.

   d. The towing operator responsible for removal.

   e. The receiving storage facility.

   f. Applicable lawful release conditions.

   g. The date and time of removal.

3. Authorities shall maintain reasonable procedures for notifying the registered owner.

4. Official impound records shall not be knowingly falsified or altered.

5. Individuals may seek correction of materially inaccurate records through the appropriate process.

## §11.17 — Vehicle Release and Recovery

1. A lawfully impounded vehicle shall be eligible for release when the legal grounds for continued impoundment have expired or otherwise been satisfied.

2. The vehicle may be released to its lawful owner or another person possessing recognized authority to receive it.

3. Applicable lawful towing and storage charges may be required where permitted by law.

4. An officer or towing operator shall not demand unauthorized personal payment as a condition of vehicle release.

5. A vehicle subject to an independent evidentiary hold or judicial seizure may remain detained under that separate authority.

6. Disputes concerning release may be referred to the appropriate judicial or administrative authority.

7. The expiration of an impound period shall not automatically extinguish an independently valid repossession or ownership order.

## §11.18 — Vehicle Seizure and Forfeiture

1. Permanent vehicle forfeiture is the lawful deprivation of an owner's property interest pursuant to appropriate judicial authority.

2. Vehicle forfeiture may be considered when a vehicle is substantially connected to qualifying serious criminal activity.

3. Qualifying circumstances may include:

   a. Repeated use in serious felony offenses.

   b. Knowing use in qualifying drug trafficking.

   c. Knowing use in qualifying weapons trafficking.

   d. Use as an instrument of a serious violent offense.

   e. Other circumstances expressly designated under the Penal Code.

4. Permanent forfeiture shall require a judicial proceeding or other expressly authorized legal process.

5. An arresting officer shall not independently transfer ownership of a seized vehicle to the State.

6. The owner shall receive notice and an opportunity to challenge the forfeiture.

7. Lawful ownership, financing, and innocent third-party interests shall be considered.

8. A vehicle shall not be permanently forfeited solely because its owner has been accused of a criminal offense.

## §11.19 — Innocent Owner and Third-Party Protections

1. A person holding a lawful ownership or financial interest in a vehicle may contest forfeiture affecting that interest.

2. The State shall consider whether the claimant knowingly participated in, authorized, or facilitated the relevant unlawful use.

3. Ownership by an innocent third party shall not automatically establish criminal responsibility for another person's conduct.

4. A rental, lease, or financed vehicle may contain legal interests belonging to persons or businesses other than the operator.

5. A court may order appropriate return, release, or protection of a qualifying innocent party's interest.

6. Fraudulent ownership transfers intended to defeat a lawful forfeiture may be challenged.

7. Any final distribution of forfeited property shall comply with the applicable judicial order.

## §11.20 — Vehicle Repossession

1. A vehicle subject to a valid financing agreement, security interest, or other lawful repossession arrangement may be recovered when the legal conditions for repossession have been satisfied.

2. Repossession shall be conducted according to the applicable agreement and state law.

3. A creditor, financing institution, or authorized recovery company shall not obtain greater authority than permitted by law.

4. A vehicle shall not be repossessed solely because a payment is disputed where the legal grounds for recovery have not been established.

5. Repossession personnel shall not use unlawful violence, threats, or unauthorized entry into a dwelling to obtain possession.

6. A repossessed vehicle shall remain subject to valid ownership, lien, redemption, and sale procedures.

7. Repossession shall not independently constitute a criminal conviction or permanent confiscation by the State.

8. Disputes concerning repossession may be brought before an authorized court.

## §11.21 — Repossession and Department of Transportation Personnel

1. Authorized vehicle recovery operators may perform repossessions on behalf of lawful creditors or owners where permitted.

2. Recovery personnel shall possess valid authorization for the particular vehicle being recovered.

3. DOT employment alone shall not authorize a person to repossess any vehicle of their choosing.

4. Recovery operators shall document the legal basis and disposition of a repossessed vehicle.

5. An operator shall not knowingly recover a vehicle pursuant to a forged or invalid authorization.

6. Law enforcement may intervene where a repossession creates a breach of the peace or involves independently suspected criminal conduct.

7. Ordinary contractual repossession disputes shall remain civil matters unless evidence supports a separate criminal offense.

## §11.22 — Vehicle Financing and Liens

1. A lawfully financed vehicle may remain subject to the creditor's valid security interest until the applicable obligation is satisfied or lawfully discharged.

2. Financing obligations shall be determined by the governing agreement and applicable law.

3. A creditor shall not arbitrarily alter agreed financial obligations outside lawful contractual provisions.

4. Default procedures shall comply with applicable notice and recovery requirements.

5. A vehicle transfer shall not automatically extinguish a valid recorded lien.

6. A court may resolve disputes concerning unpaid obligations, repossession, title, and conflicting ownership interests.

7. Criminal law shall not be used solely to imprison an individual for inability to satisfy an ordinary contractual debt.

## §11.23 — Vehicle Storage, Auctions, and Disposal

1. Authorized impound and recovery facilities may store vehicles lawfully entrusted to their custody.

2. Unclaimed vehicles may be subject to lawful disposal or sale after satisfaction of applicable notice and waiting requirements.

3. Vehicle auctions shall not be conducted in violation of ownership, lien, or judicial restrictions.

4. Proceeds from authorized sales shall be distributed according to applicable legal priorities.

5. A vehicle shall not be transferred to an employee or public official merely because that person arranged its impoundment.

6. Records of official disposal or sale shall be maintained.

7. Owners and lawful lienholders may challenge improper disposal through the appropriate judicial process.

## §11.24 — Unauthorized Towing and Vehicle Theft

1. Knowingly towing, transporting, taking, or retaining another person's vehicle without lawful authority may constitute a criminal offense.

2. A person shall not falsely claim government authority to obtain possession of a vehicle.

3. A towing operator shall not knowingly conceal the location of a recovered vehicle to demand unauthorized payment.

4. Vehicle theft, unauthorized use, fraudulent repossession, and unlawful impoundment shall be governed by applicable criminal law.

5. A private contractual dispute shall not automatically establish criminal intent.

6. Individuals affected by unauthorized vehicle removal may seek appropriate civil and criminal remedies.

## §11.25 — Judicial Review of Vehicle Actions

1. Individuals may challenge unlawful towing, impoundment, repossession, seizure, or forfeiture through appropriate legal proceedings.

2. A reviewing authority may examine the legality of the action and the supporting evidence.

3. Courts may order release of a vehicle, correction of official records, or other lawful relief.

4. A vehicle shall not be permanently forfeited while an applicable judicial stay expressly prohibits disposition.

5. The State shall not retaliate against an individual solely for contesting an official vehicle action.

6. No officer, DOT employee, or private towing contractor shall be exempt from applicable liability for unlawful conduct.
