# SECTION 12 — WEAPONS & FIREARMS

## §12.1 — General Right to Lawful Weapon Possession

1. Individuals within the State of San Andreas may lawfully possess firearms and other weapons subject to applicable licensing and regulatory requirements.

2. Lawful weapon ownership shall not independently constitute criminal conduct.

3. The State may regulate the possession, acquisition, transfer, carrying, and use of designated weapons.

4. Individuals shall not possess weapons prohibited by applicable law unless expressly authorized.

5. Firearm ownership shall not exempt an individual from laws concerning threats, assault, reckless discharge, or other criminal conduct.

6. The lawful possession of a firearm shall not, by itself, establish probable cause to search or arrest its possessor.

7. Firearm regulation shall be administered consistently with the civil rights established under Section 1.

## §12.2 — Firearm Classifications

For purposes of licensing and enforcement, firearms shall be categorized according to the classifications established below.

**Class I — Standard Civilian Firearms**

1. Ordinary handguns, permitted civilian rifles, and other firearms designated for general lawful possession.

2. Class I firearms may be possessed and carried in accordance with applicable licensing laws.

**Class II — Restricted Civilian Firearms**

1. Firearms designated as restricted due to their configuration, capacity, destructive capability, or specialized purpose.

2. Class II firearms may require additional authorization, registration, or professional qualification.

**Class III — Automatic and Specialized Firearms**

1. Firearms capable of sustained automatic fire and other weapons designated for restricted professional or governmental use.

2. Civilian possession shall be prohibited unless expressly authorized under applicable law.

**Class IV — Prohibited Military-Grade Firearms**

1. Firearms and specialized weapon systems designated by law as prohibited from ordinary civilian possession.

2. Such weapons shall be restricted to expressly authorized governmental or other designated operations.

3. The State shall maintain an official firearm classification schedule identifying the weapons and configurations assigned to each class.

4. A firearm shall not be classified solely according to its appearance, manufacturer, or cosmetic modifications.

5. Disputes concerning classification may be submitted for authorized administrative or judicial review.

## §12.3 — Firearm Licensing

1. Individuals shall possess the required firearm license when engaging in activities for which such licensing is mandated.

2. Firearm licensing shall be administered by an authorized state authority.

3. A license may specify the classes of firearms and types of possession or carry authorized.

4. Possession of a standard firearm license shall not automatically authorize restricted or prohibited firearms.

5. An applicant shall provide accurate identifying and qualification information.

6. A license may be denied, suspended, or revoked only upon lawful grounds.

7. License suspension and revocation shall remain subject to Section 10.

8. An individual may seek judicial review of an allegedly unlawful licensing decision.

## §12.4 — Firearm Acquisition and Registration

1. Firearms may be acquired through lawful commercial sale, authorized private transfer, inheritance, or another recognized method.

2. Transfers shall comply with applicable registration, licensing, and prohibited-person requirements.

3. Where registration is required, the official firearm record shall identify the relevant weapon and lawful registrant.

4. Knowingly falsifying firearm registration or serial number information may constitute an offense.

5. A purchaser shall not knowingly obtain a firearm on behalf of a person legally prohibited from possessing it.

6. Firearms transferred under unlawful circumstances may be subject to seizure according to law.

7. An individual's firearm license shall not independently establish ownership of every firearm in their possession.

## §12.5 — Open Carry

1. Open carry is the visible possession of a firearm upon an individual's person in a public location.

2. Open carry of qualifying civilian firearms shall be permitted where the individual possesses the authorization required by law.

3. Open carry shall not independently constitute brandishing or criminal threatening.

4. Individuals carrying openly shall comply with applicable restrictions concerning government facilities, secure premises, and designated prohibited locations.

5. An officer may conduct a lawful licensing inquiry where the circumstances and applicable inspection authority justify such action.

6. Open carry shall not automatically authorize a full evidentiary search.

7. The intentional threatening display of a firearm shall remain subject to applicable criminal law.

## §12.6 — Concealed Carry

1. Concealed carry is the possession of a firearm upon an individual's person in a manner not ordinarily visible to the public.

2. Concealed carry shall be permitted for qualifying firearms when authorized by the applicable firearm license or law.

3. A person shall not carry a firearm while subject to a lawful prohibition against possession.

4. Concealed possession shall not exempt a person from lawful searches, licensing requirements, or secure-facility restrictions.

5. The mere discovery of a lawfully concealed firearm shall not independently establish criminal intent.

6. A firearm license shall not authorize possession of otherwise prohibited weapons or modifications.

## §12.7 — Brandishing and Threatening Conduct

1. Brandishing shall mean intentionally displaying, presenting, pointing, or handling a weapon in a threatening manner without lawful justification.

2. Brandishing may constitute a criminal offense where the required elements are established under the Penal Code.

3. The lawful carrying of a holstered or otherwise safely carried firearm shall not independently constitute brandishing.

4. An individual may lawfully draw or display a weapon when reasonably necessary for lawful self-defense or defense of others.

5. A claim of self-defense shall be evaluated according to the circumstances and applicable criminal law.

6. An officer shall not classify ordinary lawful firearm possession as brandishing solely to establish grounds for an otherwise unauthorized search.

## §12.8 — Firearm Safety and Responsible Handling

1. Firearms shall be handled in a manner that does not recklessly endanger other persons.

2. Reckless discharge may constitute an offense under the Penal Code.

3. Individuals shall not intentionally discharge firearms into occupied buildings, crowded public locations, or other areas where such conduct unlawfully endangers persons.

4. Lawful discharge may be authorized for designated hunting, training, sporting, or defensive purposes.

5. The State may designate restricted discharge areas and lawful shooting facilities.

6. A firearm owner may be subject to applicable civil or criminal liability for unlawful conduct involving the weapon.

## §12.9 — Use of Firearms in Self-Defense

1. An individual may employ a firearm in lawful self-defense or defense of another person when the applicable legal requirements are satisfied.

2. The use of potentially lethal force shall require a reasonable belief that such force is necessary to prevent an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury.

3. Mere verbal disagreement, insults, or ordinary property disputes shall not independently justify lethal force.

4. The legality of defensive force shall be evaluated according to the circumstances known to the individual at the time.

5. An individual who knowingly creates an unlawful violent confrontation shall not automatically acquire an unrestricted right to employ lethal force.

6. Nothing in this section shall authorize retaliatory violence after an immediate threat has ended.

7. Law enforcement shall investigate suspected unlawful violence according to the standards established by this Code.

## §12.10 — Restricted Locations

1. Firearms may be restricted within designated secure government facilities, correctional institutions, judicial facilities, and other locations expressly established by law.

2. Authorized law enforcement and correctional personnel may possess approved duty weapons within their lawful operational duties.

3. Private property owners and businesses may establish lawful conditions governing entry with weapons, subject to applicable law.

4. A person who refuses to comply with a lawful property access condition may be required to leave.

5. Refusal to comply with a lawful order to leave may constitute trespass or another applicable offense.

6. The presence of a firearm in a restricted location shall be evaluated according to the applicable licensing, authorization, and criminal provisions.

7. Restricted locations shall be sufficiently identified through law or official notice.

## §12.11 — Restricted and Prohibited Firearms

1. Possession of Class II, III, or IV firearms shall be regulated according to their classification and applicable authorization requirements.

2. Unauthorized possession of a restricted firearm may constitute a criminal offense.

3. Prohibited firearms shall not be lawfully possessed by ordinary civilians unless a specific statutory exception applies.

4. An individual shall not knowingly manufacture, acquire, transfer, or possess a prohibited firearm outside lawful authorization.

5. Possession by authorized law enforcement or other designated personnel shall remain limited to the scope of official duties and applicable regulations.

6. Firearm classification and prohibited-weapon schedules shall be formally published.

7. An individual may challenge a disputed weapon classification through the appropriate legal process.

## §12.12 — Illegal Firearm Modifications

1. Firearms modified in a manner expressly prohibited by law shall be classified as unlawful weapons.

2. Prohibited modifications may include:

   a. Unlawful conversion to automatic fire.

   b. Removal or falsification of identifying serial numbers.

   c. Unauthorized barrel alterations resulting in a prohibited configuration.

   d. Unauthorized suppressors or sound-reduction devices where prohibited.

   e. Other modifications expressly designated as unlawful.

3. Cosmetic modifications shall not constitute unlawful weapon alteration unless specifically prohibited.

4. The State may permit designated modifications through restricted licenses or professional authorization.

5. An officer shall not declare a weapon illegal merely because its appearance differs from a standard commercial model.

6. Disputed modifications may be examined through appropriate evidentiary or technical procedures.

## §12.13 — Ammunition and Explosive Materials

1. Ammunition shall be subject to applicable possession, sale, and transportation regulations.

2. The State may designate certain ammunition types as restricted or prohibited.

3. Possession of ordinary lawful ammunition shall not independently constitute criminal conduct.

4. Explosive devices, destructive weapons, and specialized munitions may be subject to separate criminal restrictions.

5. Unauthorized possession, manufacture, or distribution of prohibited destructive devices may constitute a serious criminal offense.

6. Lawful commercial or professional possession of designated materials shall remain subject to applicable authorization requirements.

7. Detailed explosive and destructive-device classifications shall be established under the Penal Code and associated regulatory schedules.

## §12.14 — Non-Firearm Weapons

1. Knives, blunt instruments, and other non-firearm weapons shall be regulated according to applicable law.

2. Ordinary tools shall not automatically become illegal weapons solely because they are capable of causing injury.

3. Possession of a tool with lawful occupational or recreational purpose shall not independently constitute criminal conduct.

4. Certain specially designated weapons may be restricted or prohibited.

5. Brandishing or unlawfully using a non-firearm weapon may constitute a criminal offense.

6. Weapon classification shall consider the item's nature, configuration, and relevant circumstances.

7. A person may not be arrested solely because they possess an ordinary lawful tool.

## §12.15 — Firearm Possession by Prohibited Persons

1. The State may prohibit firearm possession by individuals subject to qualifying convictions, judicial orders, or other restrictions expressly established by law.

2. A firearm prohibition shall identify the relevant legal basis and applicable duration.

3. An individual shall not be deemed prohibited solely because they have previously been investigated or arrested.

4. Lawful firearm restrictions associated with parole or probation shall remain subject to the applicable judicial order.

5. Individuals may seek review of allegedly improper firearm prohibitions.

6. Restoration of firearm privileges shall require satisfaction of the applicable legal requirements.

7. Possession contrary to a valid prohibition may constitute a criminal offense.

## §12.16 — Firearms During Parole and Probation

1. A person subject to parole or probation may be prohibited from possessing firearms where the applicable conditions lawfully establish such a restriction.

2. Firearm restrictions shall be communicated as part of the applicable order.

3. Violation of a lawful restriction may constitute a parole or probation violation.

4. The existence of parole or probation shall not automatically authorize unrestricted searches of unrelated property.

5. Searches involving supervised individuals shall remain governed by the applicable supervision order and law.

6. Firearm rights may be restored following the expiration or lawful modification of the relevant restriction.

## §12.17 — Commercial Firearm Sales

1. Individuals or businesses conducting regulated firearm sales shall possess any licenses required by law.

2. Authorized dealers shall comply with applicable restrictions concerning prohibited purchasers and restricted firearms.

3. A dealer shall not knowingly falsify a firearm transaction record.

4. A dealer shall not knowingly sell a firearm to an individual legally prohibited from possessing it.

5. Commercial firearm records shall be maintained where required by law.

6. Lawful commercial activity shall remain protected against arbitrary seizure or closure.

7. Violations may result in criminal charges or applicable license sanctions.

## §12.18 — Private Firearm Transfers

1. Private firearm transfers may occur where permitted by law.

2. Parties shall comply with applicable licensing, registration, and prohibited-person requirements.

3. A transfer shall not be valid where prohibited by applicable firearm law.

4. An individual shall not knowingly transfer a weapon to a prohibited person.

5. Transfers involving restricted firearms shall require any additional authorization prescribed by law.

6. Fraudulent firearm transfers or falsified ownership records may constitute criminal conduct.

7. Ownership disputes may be reviewed through appropriate judicial proceedings.

## §12.19 — Firearm Theft and Unlawful Possession

1. Knowingly stealing another person's firearm may constitute a criminal offense.

2. Knowingly possessing or transferring a stolen firearm may constitute an offense where the applicable elements are established.

3. An individual shall not be presumed guilty of firearm theft solely because a weapon's registration is disputed.

4. Suspected stolen firearms may be seized during lawful investigations.

5. Lawful owners may petition for the return of recovered property.

6. Stolen firearms retained as criminal evidence shall remain subject to applicable evidence-handling requirements.

7. An officer shall not personally retain a recovered firearm outside lawful evidence or property procedures.

## §12.20 — Firearm Seizure by Law Enforcement

1. Officers may seize firearms when supported by lawful authority.

2. Grounds may include:

   a. Possession of a prohibited weapon.

   b. Use of a firearm in a criminal offense.

   c. Lawful seizure pursuant to a warrant.

   d. Evidence discovered during an authorized search.

   e. Temporary safety measures during a lawful detention or arrest.

   f. Another expressly recognized legal ground.

3. Temporary securing of a firearm shall not automatically constitute permanent confiscation.

4. Firearms lawfully held as evidence shall be appropriately documented.

5. Firearms no longer subject to lawful retention shall be eligible for return to their lawful owner.

6. Permanent forfeiture shall require applicable legal authority and process.

7. Seizure may be challenged through the Department of Justice.

## §12.21 — Firearm Evidence and Identification

1. Firearms involved in criminal investigations may be examined for relevant identifying and forensic evidence.

2. Authorized examinations may include serial number verification, firearm identification, and other lawful forensic procedures.

3. Officers shall maintain appropriate records of firearms transferred into evidence custody.

4. Evidence shall not knowingly be altered, fabricated, substituted, or unlawfully destroyed.

5. A firearm's presence at a crime scene shall not automatically establish the guilt of its registered owner.

6. Forensic findings shall remain subject to appropriate judicial examination and challenge.

7. Disputed firearms evidence may be presented during authorized court proceedings.

## §12.22 — Law Enforcement Firearms

1. Authorized law enforcement officers may possess and carry departmental firearms necessary for lawful duties.

2. Duty weapon authorization shall remain subject to departmental regulations and state law.

3. Officers shall not use departmental weapons for unlawful personal purposes.

4. Police use of force shall remain governed by Section 6.

5. Departmental weapon possession shall not exempt officers from criminal liability for unlawful firearm use.

6. Firearm discharges involving officers shall be documented and reviewed according to applicable law.

7. A police officer shall not permanently confiscate a civilian firearm solely for personal or departmental use without lawful authority.

## §12.23 — Department of Corrections Firearms

1. Authorized correctional personnel may possess designated weapons and security equipment necessary for lawful correctional operations.

2. Weapon authorization may vary according to facility security requirements and assigned duties.

3. Correctional officers shall remain subject to the use-of-force requirements of §6.13.

4. The Department of Corrections may restrict weapon possession within designated inmate housing and other secure areas.

5. Unauthorized introduction of firearms or prohibited weapons into a correctional facility may constitute a serious criminal offense.

6. Correctional personnel shall not use weapons as instruments of unlawful punishment or intimidation.

7. Significant weapon-related incidents shall be documented and subject to review.

## §12.24 — Private Security Firearms

1. Licensed private security personnel may possess designated firearms where authorized by applicable law.

2. Private security employment shall not independently confer general police powers.

3. A private security company may be subject to professional licensing and weapon registration requirements.

4. Security personnel shall remain subject to ordinary criminal laws governing weapon possession and use.

5. Use of a firearm by private security personnel shall be evaluated according to the same applicable legal standards governing civilian self-defense, except where specific lawful authority provides otherwise.

6. A private business shall not independently authorize possession of a firearm otherwise prohibited by state law.

7. Violations may result in criminal charges and applicable licensing sanctions.

## §12.25 — Weapons in Commercial Transportation

1. Commercial vehicle operators shall comply with applicable firearm possession and transportation laws.

2. Lawful commercial employment shall not automatically prohibit otherwise lawful firearm possession.

3. Restricted or prohibited firearms shall not be transported without required authorization.

4. Commercial cargo may be inspected when lawful grounds exist under Sections 4 and 11.

5. Transporting unlawful weapons as commercial cargo may constitute a criminal offense.

6. A driver's possession of a CDL shall not independently authorize commercial weapons transportation.

7. A vehicle or trailer used in qualifying weapons trafficking may be subject to impoundment or judicial forfeiture under Section 11.

## §12.26 — Weapon Manufacturing and Modification

1. Commercial manufacture or modification of firearms may require applicable professional licensing.

2. Authorized gunsmiths and manufacturers shall comply with applicable weapon classifications and prohibited-modification laws.

3. Unauthorized manufacture of prohibited firearms may constitute a criminal offense.

4. Ordinary lawful firearm maintenance shall not independently constitute illegal manufacturing.

5. A person shall not knowingly remove identifying marks to conceal a firearm's ownership or criminal history.

6. Commercial businesses shall not knowingly supply unlawful weapons outside applicable authorization.

7. Violations may result in criminal proceedings and applicable business licensing consequences.

## §12.27 — Firearm License Suspension and Revocation

1. Firearm licenses may be suspended or revoked only under lawful grounds.

2. Qualifying grounds may include designated violent convictions, prohibited weapons offenses, or other restrictions established by law.

3. Arrest alone shall not automatically result in permanent revocation.

4. Temporary safety restrictions may be imposed where expressly authorized.

5. Permanent revocation shall require the applicable administrative or judicial procedure.

6. An individual shall receive reasonable notice and an opportunity for review.

7. Reinstatement shall be governed by Section 10 and applicable firearm legislation.

## §12.28 — Weapon Forfeiture and Disposal

1. Weapons lawfully forfeited under a final judicial order may be disposed of according to established state procedures.

2. Weapons retained as evidence shall not be disposed of while a lawful evidence hold remains in effect.

3. Lawfully owned weapons shall not be forfeited solely because they were temporarily secured during an arrest.

4. Forfeited weapons shall not be transferred into private possession by an official outside authorized procedures.

5. Disposal records shall be maintained by the responsible authority.

6. Affected individuals may challenge improper forfeiture through the appropriate judicial process.

## §12.29 — Judicial Review of Firearm Enforcement

1. Individuals may petition the Department of Justice concerning allegedly unlawful firearm confiscation, license revocation, or weapon classification.

2. Courts may examine the legal grounds supporting an enforcement action.

3. A court may order correction of records, return of lawfully held property, or other authorized relief.

4. Government authorities shall not retaliate against individuals solely for contesting firearm enforcement actions.

5. Law enforcement officers shall not be exempt from accountability for unlawful weapon seizures or misuse.

6. Firearm enforcement shall remain subject to all applicable civil rights protections.

# LEGISLATIVE CONTINUITY AND AUTHORITY

1. Sections 11–12 shall be interpreted consistently with Sections 1–10 of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

2. All individuals retain the civil rights guaranteed under Section 1.

3. Investigative detention, probable cause, and arrest authority shall remain governed by Sections 2–3.

4. Vehicle and weapon searches shall remain subject to Section 4.

5. Law enforcement powers and lawful orders shall remain governed by Section 5.

6. Use of force involving vehicles or weapons shall remain subject to Section 6.

7. Criminal charges, processing, and prosecution shall remain governed by Section 7.

8. Criminal sentencing and correctional punishment shall remain governed by Section 8.

9. Civil disputes, judicial orders, forfeiture proceedings, and appeals shall remain governed by Section 9.

10. Driver, commercial driver, firearm, and professional license suspensions and revocations shall remain subject to Section 10.

11. Vehicle impoundment shall not automatically constitute permanent forfeiture.

12. Contractual vehicle repossession shall not independently constitute criminal punishment or seizure by the State.

13. No DOT employee or private towing operator shall acquire general law enforcement authority merely through employment or commercial licensing.

14. Lawful firearm ownership and carry shall not independently establish criminal guilt or probable cause for an unrestricted search.

15. No government department or private contractor shall permanently confiscate vehicles, weapons, or other protected property without lawful authority.

16. Specific traffic offenses, impound durations, firearm classifications, weapon restrictions, and applicable penalties shall be established through the Penal Code and officially adopted regulatory schedules.

17. All official vehicle, weapon, licensing, and judicial records shall remain subject to lawful verification, correction, and review.
