# ROSEFIRE CIVIL RIGHTS & JUSTICE CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**\
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.1 — Sections 5–6**\
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing the lawful powers of peace officers, the requirements governing official commands and enforcement actions, and the limitations upon the use of physical, less-lethal, and lethal force within the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–4 remain in force as previously enacted. Sections 5–6 form a continuation of the same Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the fundamental protections established therein.

---

# SECTION 5 — POLICE POWERS & LAWFUL ORDERS

## §5.1 — Scope of Law Enforcement Authority

1. The powers established under this section may be exercised only by duly authorized law enforcement officers acting within the lawful scope of their duties.
2. A law enforcement officer shall possess only such authority as is granted by the laws of San Andreas and the jurisdiction of their employing agency.
3. Possession of a badge, uniform, identification card, or departmental equipment shall not independently authorize an officer to act outside the law.
4. An off-duty officer shall not exercise ordinary enforcement powers solely by virtue of their employment, except where expressly authorized by law or when acting under the same emergency protections available to other individuals.
5. Law enforcement officers shall remain personally accountable for unlawful conduct committed under the appearance of official authority.
6. No departmental policy, verbal instruction, or order from a superior officer shall authorize conduct prohibited under this Code.

## §5.2 — Identification of Law Enforcement Officers

1. An officer exercising official authority shall identify themselves as law enforcement when reasonably practicable.
2. Identification may be established through a recognized law enforcement uniform, badge, official identification, or clear verbal announcement.
3. An officer operating in plain clothes shall identify themselves when issuing an enforcement order, unless immediate identification would present a substantial and articulable danger to an ongoing lawful operation.
4. An individual shall not be criminally punished for failing to comply with an order when the circumstances would not reasonably permit them to recognize the issuing person as a lawful officer.
5. Officers shall provide their name, badge number, or other recognized identifying information upon reasonable request, except where a specific lawful undercover or protective exception applies.
6. Officers shall not knowingly impersonate personnel from another law enforcement agency to obtain authority they do not possess.

## §5.3 — Lawful Police Orders

1. A lawful police order is a command issued by an authorized officer acting within the lawful scope of their duties for a legitimate enforcement or public safety purpose.
2. A lawful order must be reasonably clear, capable of compliance, and proportionate to the circumstances.
3. An officer may issue lawful orders for the purpose of:
   - Securing a crime scene.
   - Conducting a lawful detention or arrest.
   - Preventing immediate injury or danger.
   - Controlling traffic during emergencies.
   - Executing a valid judicial warrant.
   - Protecting evidence from destruction or interference.
   - Maintaining safety during lawful police operations.
4. An order shall not be lawful solely because it was issued by a law enforcement officer.
5. An individual shall not be required to comply with an order that clearly exceeds the officer's legal authority.
6. Officers shall provide individuals a reasonable opportunity to comply where the circumstances permit.
7. No officer shall deliberately issue contradictory, impossible, or unnecessarily confusing commands for the purpose of creating grounds for arrest.

## §5.4 — Failure to Comply and Obstruction

1. Knowingly refusing a lawful police order may constitute an offense where established by the Penal Code.
2. An individual's lawful silence, refusal to consent to a search, request for legal counsel, or decision to challenge an officer's conduct shall not independently constitute obstruction.
3. Verbal disagreement, criticism, or questioning of an officer's actions shall not constitute obstruction unless accompanied by conduct satisfying the elements of an applicable criminal offense.
4. Physical interference with a lawful arrest, deliberate destruction of evidence, or intentional obstruction of an active investigation may constitute criminal conduct.
5. An officer shall not threaten arrest merely to compel voluntary cooperation where no lawful enforcement authority exists.

## §5.5 — Traffic Enforcement Authority

1. Law enforcement officers may stop a motor vehicle when they possess reasonable suspicion of a traffic violation or other criminal offense.
2. Officers may conduct lawful traffic enforcement concerning:
   - Driver licensing and vehicle registration.
   - Speed restrictions.
   - Reckless or dangerous operation.
   - Driving under the influence.
   - Vehicle safety violations.
   - Commercial transportation requirements.
   - Other offenses established under traffic law.
3. During a lawful traffic stop, an officer may request documents required by law and conduct reasonable investigative inquiries related to the stop.
4. A traffic stop shall not be prolonged beyond its lawful purpose without an independent legal justification.
5. Officers may direct a driver or occupant to exit or remain within a vehicle when reasonably necessary for officer safety or lawful enforcement.
6. A routine traffic stop shall not automatically authorize a vehicle search.
7. Vehicle searches shall remain governed by §4.4 of this Code.

## §5.6 — Driving Under the Influence

1. An officer may investigate suspected impaired driving when specific observations or other reliable information reasonably indicate impairment.
2. Indicators may include unsafe driving, observable impairment, admissions, or other relevant circumstances.
3. Officers may conduct standardized roadside sobriety assessments consistent with established departmental procedures.
4. A chemical breath or other impairment test may be required only where expressly authorized by applicable law.
5. An officer shall inform an individual of the lawful basis for a compulsory impairment test.
6. Refusal to participate in a voluntary assessment shall not, by itself, establish guilt.
7. Refusal of a lawfully required test may carry only those consequences expressly established by legislation.
8. An officer shall not fabricate impairment findings or knowingly misrepresent test results.

## §5.7 — Law Enforcement Jurisdiction

1. Recognized law enforcement agencies shall exercise authority within the jurisdictions established by state law.
2. State-level law enforcement agencies may exercise statewide authority where expressly authorized.
3. Municipal and county agencies shall operate primarily within their assigned jurisdictions.
4. Officers may continue a lawful pursuit beyond ordinary jurisdictional boundaries when authorized by law.
5. Law enforcement agencies may provide mutual assistance when requested or when immediate circumstances justify emergency intervention.
6. An officer operating outside their ordinary jurisdiction shall remain subject to applicable state law and interagency procedures.
7. Jurisdictional boundaries shall be identified in the official maps and jurisdiction schedules adopted under Section 17 of this Code.

## §5.8 — Crime Scenes and Public Safety Areas

1. Officers may establish reasonable temporary boundaries around active crime scenes, hazardous locations, and emergency operations.
2. Such boundaries shall be limited to areas reasonably necessary to preserve evidence, protect individuals, or permit emergency services to operate safely.
3. Individuals may be ordered to leave or remain outside an established lawful perimeter.
4. The establishment of a crime scene shall not automatically suspend the civil rights of all persons present.
5. Officers shall not search or arrest an individual solely because they are within or near a crime scene.
6. Public access shall be restored when the lawful grounds for maintaining the restriction have ended.

## §5.9 — Checkpoints and Road Closures

1. Law enforcement may establish temporary road closures when reasonably necessary to respond to emergencies, hazardous conditions, active criminal incidents, or threats to public safety.
2. Traffic checkpoints intended for routine enforcement shall require authorization under applicable law and established departmental procedures.
3. Checkpoints shall not be established solely to target individuals on the basis of protected personal characteristics.
4. A lawful checkpoint shall not independently authorize an unrestricted search of persons or vehicles.
5. Vehicles may be temporarily directed, stopped, or diverted where necessary to maintain the lawful operation of the checkpoint.
6. Officers shall not use a checkpoint as a pretext for arbitrary detention or harassment.

## §5.10 — Pursuit Authority

1. Officers may pursue an individual when lawful grounds exist to arrest or detain that individual and the pursuit is consistent with applicable departmental safety requirements.
2. The continuation of a pursuit shall account for:
   - The seriousness of the suspected offense.
   - The immediate danger posed by the suspect.
   - Roadway conditions and traffic density.
   - The likelihood of injury to uninvolved persons.
   - Reasonable alternatives to continued pursuit.
3. A pursuit shall not automatically authorize lethal force.
4. Officers shall not intentionally create unreasonable danger to the public solely to prevent a suspect from escaping.
5. Pursuits may be terminated or restricted by an authorized supervisor where continued pursuit presents a disproportionate risk.
6. A suspect's decision to flee shall not eliminate their protections under this Code.

## §5.11 — Police Authority Concerning Weapons

1. Officers may lawfully carry and possess departmental weapons and equipment necessary for their official duties.
2. Authorized possession of restricted equipment shall not exempt officers from laws governing unlawful use, excessive force, or official misconduct.
3. An officer may temporarily secure a weapon during a lawful detention or arrest when reasonably necessary for safety.
4. Lawfully possessed weapons shall not be permanently confiscated solely because they were temporarily secured during an investigation.
5. Weapons used in criminal offenses, unlawfully possessed weapons, and weapons lawfully subject to evidentiary seizure may be retained according to applicable law.
6. Firearm licensing, classifications, prohibited attachments, and public carry restrictions shall be governed by Section 12 and the applicable Penal Code.

## §5.12 — Emergency Vehicle Authority

1. Authorized emergency vehicles may exercise limited exemptions from ordinary traffic restrictions when responding to a legitimate emergency.
2. Emergency driving shall be conducted with due regard for the safety of other road users.
3. Emergency lights and sirens shall be used where required by departmental regulations and applicable law.
4. Authorized officers may conduct emergency or covert operations without audible warning devices where necessary and lawful.
5. An emergency vehicle exemption shall not grant permission to operate a vehicle with reckless disregard for human life.
6. Emergency vehicles may temporarily stop or park in restricted locations when reasonably necessary for official duties.
7. Improper or personal use of emergency vehicle privileges may constitute official misconduct.

## §5.13 — Searches, Seizures, and Confiscation

1. All searches conducted by law enforcement shall comply with Section 4 of this Code.
2. Officers may seize prohibited items, criminal evidence, and property lawfully subject to confiscation.
3. Property seized for evidentiary or investigative purposes shall be documented and handled according to established evidence procedures.
4. Lawful property shall not be permanently confiscated without appropriate legal authorization.
5. An officer shall not seize personal property for private use, retaliation, or punishment outside the lawful sentencing process.
6. Disputed seizures shall remain subject to judicial review.

## §5.14 — Supervisory Authority and Accountability

1. Supervisory officers may direct lawful operations, coordinate personnel, and review enforcement decisions within their assigned authority.
2. A superior officer shall not order a subordinate to perform an unlawful act.
3. An officer shall remain responsible for knowingly carrying out an unlawful order.
4. Supervisory approval shall not independently legalize an otherwise unlawful search, detention, arrest, or use of force.
5. Officers shall accurately document significant enforcement actions as required by law and departmental procedure.
6. Knowingly falsifying official reports, evidence, or material investigative records may constitute official misconduct and a criminal offense.

## §5.15 — Private Security and Non-Police Personnel

1. Private security personnel shall not possess general law enforcement powers solely by virtue of private employment.
2. Private security companies may protect property, enforce lawful access restrictions, and undertake other activities permitted under applicable law.
3. Private security personnel shall not independently conduct police-style searches, arrests, or criminal investigations unless a specific law authorizes the action.
4. Private security personnel remain subject to the same criminal laws as other individuals.
5. Government contractors shall not obtain independent law enforcement authority merely through a commercial agreement.
6. DOC personnel exercising correctional authority shall be governed by their lawful correctional jurisdiction and the provisions of this Code.
