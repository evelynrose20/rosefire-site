# SECTION 10 — LICENSE SUSPENSION & REVOCATION

## §10.1 — General Licensing Authority

1. The State of San Andreas may require licenses, permits, or certifications for activities designated by law.

2. Licenses shall be issued, maintained, suspended, revoked, and reinstated according to established legal procedures.

3. A license shall authorize only the activities expressly covered by its classification.

4. Possession of one license shall not automatically satisfy requirements for another professional or regulated activity.

5. Licensing authorities shall maintain accurate records concerning the current status of licenses.

6. No public official shall arbitrarily deny, suspend, or revoke a license without lawful authority.

7. Individuals shall retain the right to challenge licensing decisions through appropriate judicial procedures.

## §10.2 — Recognized License Categories

The State may recognize the following license and permit categories:

1. **Standard Driver's License:** Authorizes the operation of qualifying ordinary motor vehicles.

2. **Commercial Driver's License (CDL):** Authorizes operation of designated commercial vehicles subject to applicable qualifications.

3. **Firearm License:** Authorizes designated firearm possession or carry where required by law.

4. **Hunting License:** Authorizes lawful hunting activities subject to wildlife regulations.

5. **Fishing License:** Authorizes lawful fishing activities subject to applicable regulations.

6. **Pilot's License:** Authorizes designated aircraft operations according to aviation law.

7. **Business License:** Authorizes lawful commercial operation subject to applicable requirements.

8. **Weed Seller License:** Authorizes designated lawful cannabis-related commercial activity within applicable limits.

9. **Professional Legal Authorization:** Permits an individual to practice law within the State.

10. **Other Professional or Special Permits:** Additional credentials expressly established by legislation.

Recognition of a license category shall not require that every category be administered by the same issuing office.

## §10.3 — Licensing Records and Verification

1. State-issued licenses shall be associated with the lawful identity of the holder.

2. The State shall maintain recognized records of license issuance, expiration, suspension, revocation, and reinstatement.

3. Authorized officials may verify relevant licensing credentials when conducting lawful enforcement or administrative duties.

4. Individuals may be required to present licenses during activities for which possession or presentation is required by law.

5. Licensing verification shall not automatically authorize unrelated searches or seizure of private property.

6. Unauthorized alteration or falsification of licensing records may constitute a criminal offense.

7. An individual shall not be presumed unlicensed solely because of a temporary failure of the licensing records service where other reliable evidence of valid authorization exists.

## §10.4 — Temporary License Suspension

1. A license may be temporarily suspended where specific lawful grounds justify restricting the licensed activity.

2. Temporary suspension may be imposed through an authorized judicial, administrative, or emergency procedure.

3. Emergency suspension may be permitted where continued licensed activity presents an immediate and substantial danger to public safety.

4. The suspension shall identify its legal basis and applicable duration or review condition.

5. An individual subject to suspension shall receive notice as soon as reasonably practicable.

6. Temporary suspension shall not automatically constitute permanent revocation.

7. A suspended individual may seek review according to applicable law.

8. An emergency suspension shall be subject to prompt review by the appropriate licensing or judicial authority.

## §10.5 — Permanent License Revocation

1. Permanent revocation shall require lawful grounds and an authorized determination.

2. Revocation may be imposed for designated serious offenses, repeated qualifying violations, proven professional misconduct, fraud, or other grounds expressly established by law.

3. An arrest or unproven criminal allegation shall not independently justify permanent license revocation.

4. The individual shall receive notice of the proposed revocation and a reasonable opportunity to contest the determination.

5. Permanent revocation shall not occur solely because a law enforcement officer believes the individual is unsuitable to hold the license.

6. Revocation orders shall be recorded by the issuing or responsible licensing authority.

7. Reinstatement after permanent revocation shall require the procedure expressly established for the relevant license category.

8. A court may review whether a revocation was imposed lawfully.

## §10.6 — Driver's License Suspension and Revocation

1. A driver's license may be suspended or revoked following qualifying serious traffic offenses or other grounds established by law.

2. Such grounds may include:

   a. Driving under the influence.

   b. Vehicular manslaughter.

   c. Felony evasion involving a motor vehicle.

   d. Serious hit-and-run offenses.

   e. Repeated dangerous or reckless driving.

   f. Other designated offenses involving substantial danger to road users.

3. An officer may temporarily restrict an individual's continued operation of a motor vehicle when lawful grounds establish that continued driving presents an immediate danger.

4. Temporary restriction from driving shall not automatically constitute permanent revocation.

5. Suspension or revocation shall require the applicable administrative or judicial procedure.

6. An individual whose license is lawfully suspended or revoked shall not operate a vehicle requiring that license until authorization is restored.

7. Reinstatement may require satisfaction of lawful conditions, including payment of applicable fees, completion of required training, or other prescribed measures.

8. An individual may appeal a suspension or revocation through the appropriate judicial process.

## §10.7 — Commercial Driver's License

1. A Commercial Driver's License shall authorize the operation of designated commercial vehicles.

2. Commercial drivers shall comply with applicable commercial vehicle safety, transportation, and documentation requirements.

3. CDL suspension or revocation may result from serious offenses committed while operating a commercial vehicle.

4. Qualifying offenses may include:

   a. Driving under the influence.

   b. Vehicular manslaughter.

   c. Reckless commercial vehicle operation.

   d. Felony evasion.

   e. Serious hit-and-run offenses.

   f. Use of a commercial vehicle in qualifying drug or weapons trafficking offenses.

   g. Repeated serious commercial transport violations.

5. Operation of a commercial vehicle without the required CDL may result in enforcement action under the Penal Code.

6. A commercial vehicle or trailer may be temporarily impounded where authorized by law.

7. CDL suspension shall not automatically revoke an individual's ordinary driver's license unless a separate legal basis exists.

8. Permanent CDL revocation shall require authorized review and shall not be imposed solely at the discretion of the investigating officer.

9. Commercial driving privileges may be reinstated according to applicable licensing procedures.

## §10.8 — Firearm License Suspension and Revocation

1. Firearm licensing shall remain subject to applicable weapons legislation.

2. A firearm license may be suspended or revoked following qualifying violent offenses, prohibited firearm conduct, or other legally designated grounds.

3. Law enforcement may temporarily secure firearms during lawful investigations where authorized under Sections 4 and 5.

4. Temporary seizure of a firearm shall not independently establish permanent loss of licensing privileges.

5. Permanent firearm license revocation shall require an authorized legal determination.

6. The State may prohibit individuals subject to specific convictions, protective orders, or lawful restrictions from possessing designated firearms.

7. An individual may seek judicial review of an allegedly unlawful suspension or revocation.

8. Reinstatement shall be governed by applicable weapons licensing law.

## §10.9 — Hunting License Suspension and Revocation

1. Hunting licenses may be suspended or revoked for designated violations of wildlife and conservation laws.

2. Qualifying violations may include:

   a. Poaching.

   b. Hunting protected wildlife.

   c. Hunting with prohibited weapons.

   d. Unlawful trafficking of protected animal products.

   e. Repeated serious hunting violations.

3. Authorized wildlife enforcement personnel and recognized law enforcement officers may investigate suspected hunting offenses within their jurisdiction.

4. Temporary seizure of hunting equipment or animal products shall remain subject to applicable search and seizure law.

5. A hunting license shall not be permanently revoked solely upon an unsupported allegation.

6. Authorized licensing or judicial authorities shall determine permanent revocation.

7. Reinstatement may require satisfaction of lawful conditions or completion of prescribed conservation training.

## §10.10 — Fishing License Suspension and Revocation

1. Fishing licenses may be suspended or revoked following qualifying violations of fishing and marine conservation law.

2. Qualifying violations may include:

   a. Poaching.

   b. Taking protected marine species.

   c. Violations of designated conservation restrictions.

   d. Illegal trafficking of protected marine wildlife.

   e. Repeated serious fishing violations.

3. Temporary confiscation of equipment or unlawful catches shall require appropriate legal authority.

4. Permanent license revocation shall require an authorized determination.

5. Affected individuals shall retain the right to seek judicial review.

6. Reinstatement shall follow the applicable licensing procedure.

## §10.11 — Pilot's License Suspension and Revocation

1. Aircraft operators shall possess the licenses required for the class of aircraft and activity involved.

2. Aircraft shall be operated according to applicable aviation laws and designated airspace restrictions.

3. Pilot licenses may be suspended or revoked for qualifying aviation offenses, including:

   a. Reckless or dangerous aircraft operation.

   b. Unauthorized entry into restricted airspace.

   c. Unlawful landing in prohibited or hazardous locations.

   d. Operating an aircraft under the influence.

   e. Deliberate endangerment of individuals through aircraft operation.

   f. Use of aircraft in qualifying serious criminal offenses.

4. Emergency restrictions may be imposed when an operator presents an immediate and substantial aviation safety danger.

5. Permanent revocation shall require an authorized legal determination.

6. An aircraft shall not be permanently confiscated solely because the pilot's license has been suspended.

7. Aircraft seizure or forfeiture shall require an independent lawful basis.

8. Reinstatement after revocation shall follow the applicable aviation licensing law.

## §10.12 — Business License Suspension and Revocation

1. Businesses operating within San Andreas shall maintain applicable commercial licenses and permits required by law.

2. Business licenses may be suspended or revoked for serious or repeated violations of applicable commercial law.

3. Qualifying grounds may include:

   a. Proven fraudulent business activity.

   b. Operation of a business for designated serious criminal purposes.

   c. Repeated knowing violations of applicable licensing restrictions.

   d. Material falsification of licensing applications.

   e. Other grounds expressly established by law.

4. An employee's criminal conduct shall not automatically establish grounds to revoke the business license unless the relevant legal requirements are satisfied.

5. A business owner shall receive notice and an opportunity to contest a proposed permanent revocation.

6. Emergency suspension may be authorized when continued operation presents a substantial and immediate danger.

7. Business license revocation shall not automatically transfer business property or ownership to the State.

8. Property seizure, foreclosure, or forfeiture shall require an independent lawful basis.

9. A business may seek reinstatement or reapplication according to applicable licensing procedures.

## §10.13 — Weed Seller License Suspension and Revocation

1. The State may authorize licensed commercial activity involving designated cannabis products under applicable law.

2. A Weed Seller License shall authorize only the cannabis-related activities expressly covered by its classification.

3. A license may be suspended or revoked for designated violations, including:

   a. Sale of prohibited substances outside the license authorization.

   b. Repeated unlawful sales.

   c. Material falsification of licensing records.

   d. Operation outside authorized licensing conditions.

   e. Other serious violations expressly established by law.

4. An individual shall not automatically lose an unrelated business or driving license solely because a Weed Seller License has been suspended.

5. Permanent revocation shall require an authorized determination.

6. Licensed operators shall retain the right to contest suspension or revocation.

7. Reinstatement or reapplication shall be governed by applicable law.

## §10.14 — Professional Legal Authorization

1. The Department of Justice may suspend or revoke authorization to practice law following proven serious professional misconduct.

2. Grounds may include:

   a. Knowingly presenting fabricated evidence.

   b. Bribery or corruption affecting judicial proceedings.

   c. Serious breach of professional duties.

   d. Misappropriation of client funds.

   e. Fraudulent representation of legal qualifications.

   f. Other conduct expressly designated under applicable professional regulations.

3. Attorneys shall receive notice of disciplinary allegations and an opportunity to respond.

4. A disciplinary proceeding shall be conducted by an authorized and sufficiently impartial reviewing authority.

5. Suspension or revocation shall be recorded by the Department of Justice.

6. An attorney may seek judicial review of a disciplinary determination.

7. Reinstatement may be granted according to applicable professional regulations.

8. Disciplinary action against an attorney shall not automatically invalidate every proceeding in which the attorney previously appeared.

## §10.15 — Seizure of Physical License Documents

1. An authorized officer may temporarily seize a physical license document where expressly permitted by law.

2. Physical seizure of a license shall not automatically constitute revocation of the underlying legal authorization.

3. Any seizure shall be documented, including the legal grounds and responsible officer.

4. Where a temporary suspension is legally authorized, the applicable licensing record shall be updated through the recognized procedure.

5. Physical license documents shall not be seized as punishment for unrelated lawful conduct.

6. An individual may contest improper seizure through the appropriate administrative or judicial process.

7. A seized document shall be returned or otherwise disposed of according to the lawful determination concerning the license.

## §10.16 — Notice and Opportunity for Review

1. Individuals subject to license suspension or revocation shall receive notice identifying the affected license and the legal grounds for action.

2. Permanent revocation shall ordinarily require an opportunity for hearing or review before taking effect.

3. Emergency suspension may take immediate effect where expressly authorized by law and necessary to address a substantial public safety threat.

4. Emergency suspensions shall be subject to prompt subsequent review.

5. Licensing authorities shall maintain records of relevant notices, decisions, and reviews.

6. Affected individuals may seek legal representation during authorized proceedings.

7. A licensing decision shall not be immune from review solely because it was made through an automated government system.

## §10.17 — Reinstatement and Reapplication

1. A suspended license may be reinstated when the applicable suspension period expires and all lawful reinstatement conditions have been satisfied.

2. Reinstatement requirements may include appropriate fees, examinations, training, or other conditions authorized for the license category.

3. Permanent revocation shall not create an automatic right to reinstatement.

4. Individuals may apply for reinstatement or reissuance where permitted by law.

5. An issuing authority shall not impose unauthorized fees or arbitrary requirements.

6. Denial of reinstatement may be challenged through the appropriate review process.

7. Reinstated privileges shall be reflected in the recognized licensing records system.

## §10.18 — Multiple Licenses and Independent Authority

1. Suspension or revocation of one license shall not automatically suspend or revoke unrelated licenses.

2. Separate licenses may be affected where the same conduct independently establishes lawful grounds under each applicable licensing provision.

3. Licensing authorities shall identify the legal basis for every affected credential.

4. No individual shall be subject to blanket revocation of all licenses solely as a general punishment unless a specific law expressly authorizes such a consequence.

5. Courts may review whether multiple licensing sanctions are legally justified.

## §10.19 — Fraudulent Licensing Records

1. Knowingly creating, possessing, presenting, or using a fraudulent government license may constitute an offense under the Penal Code.

2. Unauthorized alteration of official licensing records shall be prohibited.

3. A suspected irregularity in licensing records shall not, by itself, establish criminal guilt.

4. Authorized agencies may investigate credible evidence of licensing fraud.

5. The Department of Justice may review disputes concerning falsified, compromised, or improperly modified records.

6. Individuals shall not be held criminally responsible solely because an unauthorized third party altered their licensing information without their knowledge or participation.

7. Proven fraudulent authorization may be invalidated through applicable legal procedures.

## §10.20 — Automated Licensing Decisions

1. The State may use authorized electronic systems to process license applications, renewals, status checks, and administrative records.

2. Automated systems may issue or renew licenses where the applicable legal requirements are objectively satisfied and the function is lawfully delegated.

3. Automated licensing systems shall not independently impose extraordinary penalties beyond their authorized powers.

4. Individuals may challenge materially incorrect automated licensing decisions.

5. Government contractors maintaining licensing systems shall not independently acquire licensing or enforcement authority outside their lawful contractual responsibilities.

6. Material licensing decisions shall identify the responsible authority and applicable legal basis.

7. Suspected manipulation of electronic licensing records may be referred for investigation.

# LEGISLATIVE CONTINUITY AND AUTHORITY

1. Sections 9–10 shall be interpreted together with Sections 1–8 of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

2. The protections of Section 1 apply automatically to all individuals and do not require a formal recital by law enforcement.

3. The legal standards established in Sections 2–4 remain applicable to judicial and licensing investigations.

4. The authority of law enforcement officers remains limited by Section 5.

5. Government personnel conducting enforcement actions remain subject to the use-of-force limitations of Section 6.

6. Criminal proceedings and charging procedures shall remain governed by Section 7.

7. Criminal sentencing, correctional custody, parole, remand, and capital punishment shall remain governed by Section 8.

8. The Department of Justice shall not impose criminal punishment beyond its lawful sentencing authority.

9. The seven-real-world-day maximum for fixed-term imprisonment remains in force.

10. Life imprisonment and capital punishment remain subject to their expressly established human judicial requirements.

11. No private corporation or government technology contractor shall acquire judicial or licensing powers beyond those lawfully delegated.

12. License revocation shall not automatically constitute forfeiture of property, termination of a business lease, or cancellation of unrelated legal rights.

13. Official judicial and licensing records shall remain subject to lawful review and correction.

14. Subsequent legislation shall establish specific filing deadlines, licensing procedures, professional qualifications, administrative penalties, and other necessary implementing provisions.
