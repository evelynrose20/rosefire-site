# ROSEFIRE CIVIL RIGHTS & JUSTICE CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.8 — Section 17**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing the official territorial jurisdictions, municipal and county boundaries, governmental districts, restricted areas, and legally recognized state maps of San Andreas; providing procedures for geographic administration, interagency jurisdiction, and amendments to official territorial records.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–16 remain enacted and in force. Section 17 constitutes a continuation of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code and shall be interpreted consistently with all previously established civil rights, law enforcement powers, property protections, administrative procedures, and judicial safeguards.

---

# SECTION 17 — OFFICIAL STATE MAPS, TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION & RESTRICTED AREAS

## §17.1 — Territorial Authority of the State

1. The State of San Andreas shall exercise lawful jurisdiction over its recognized territory, including designated land areas, inland waterways, coastal waters, airspace, and public facilities, subject to applicable law.

2. The State shall establish and maintain official territorial records identifying its administrative and governmental boundaries.

3. State jurisdiction shall apply to individuals, businesses, property, and activities located within the territory, subject to applicable legal exceptions.

4. No department, agency, private corporation, or individual shall independently alter a state boundary without lawful authorization.

5. All jurisdictional authority shall remain subject to the fundamental civil rights established under Section 1.

## §17.2 — Recognized Territorial Divisions

The State recognizes the following principal administrative jurisdictions:

1. **Blaine County:** The northern county jurisdiction, encompassing the northern communities, rural settlements, wilderness, and other areas designated on the official state map.

2. **Los Santos County:** The county jurisdiction encompassing designated settlements, unincorporated communities, rural districts, and surrounding lands outside the municipal jurisdiction of the City of Los Santos.

3. **City of Los Santos:** The incorporated municipal jurisdiction comprising the designated urban area and associated districts identified on the official state map.

The City of Los Santos shall possess a distinct municipal jurisdiction notwithstanding its geographic association with Los Santos County.

The precise territorial limits of each jurisdiction shall be established in the Official Territorial Jurisdiction Map adopted under this section.

## §17.3 — Blaine County

1. Blaine County shall constitute a recognized county jurisdiction of San Andreas.

2. Blaine County authorities may exercise the county administrative powers granted by law.

3. County law enforcement may exercise authority within the recognized county boundaries.

4. State-level agencies may exercise their lawful authority within Blaine County.

5. The county may contain municipalities, townships, settlements, public lands, and restricted areas designated by law.

6. No county regulation shall override applicable state law or fundamental civil rights.

7. Blaine County boundaries shall be identified by the official territorial map.

## §17.4 — Los Santos County

1. Los Santos County shall constitute a recognized county jurisdiction of San Andreas.

2. The county shall encompass the areas officially designated outside the separate municipal limits of the City of Los Santos.

3. County authorities may administer lawful services and responsibilities within their designated jurisdiction.

4. County law enforcement may exercise authority within the recognized county territory and as otherwise authorized by law.

5. State agencies may operate within Los Santos County according to their lawful authority.

6. County jurisdiction shall not automatically extend into the separate municipal jurisdiction of the City of Los Santos except where an applicable law or interagency arrangement permits such action.

7. County boundaries shall be defined by the official territorial map.

## §17.5 — City of Los Santos

1. The City of Los Santos shall constitute a recognized municipal jurisdiction within the State of San Andreas.

2. The City may administer municipal services and exercise powers granted under applicable state law.

3. Municipal law enforcement may exercise authority within the officially designated city limits.

4. County and state agencies may operate within city limits where authorized by law.

5. The City may establish lawful municipal ordinances consistent with state legislation.

6. Municipal authority shall not supersede the civil rights and protections established by this Code.

7. The limits of the City of Los Santos shall be established through the official territorial map.

## §17.6 — Statewide Law Enforcement Jurisdiction

1. State law enforcement agencies possessing statewide authority may exercise their lawful powers throughout the recognized territory of San Andreas.

2. County and municipal law enforcement agencies shall exercise their ordinary authority within their designated jurisdictions, subject to applicable exceptions.

3. Lawful interagency agreements may authorize mutual assistance and coordinated operations.

4. Emergency response and qualifying pursuit circumstances may permit cross-jurisdictional action where established by law.

5. No agency shall claim exclusive authority over an area in contradiction of applicable state law.

6. Jurisdictional disputes between agencies may be submitted for appropriate administrative or judicial resolution.

7. An officer operating outside their ordinary jurisdiction shall remain subject to all applicable legal limitations.

## §17.7 — Mutual Aid and Interagency Assistance

1. Recognized law enforcement and emergency agencies may provide mutual assistance where lawful authorization exists.

2. Assistance may include criminal investigations, public safety operations, disaster response, medical support, and other recognized activities.

3. An agency requesting assistance shall communicate the nature and scope of the request where practicable.

4. Personnel providing assistance shall remain accountable for their conduct.

5. Mutual assistance shall not authorize officers to disregard state law, civil rights, or applicable use-of-force limitations.

6. The Department of Justice may review disputes concerning the legality of interagency operations.

## §17.8 — Pursuit Across Jurisdictional Boundaries

1. An officer engaged in a lawful pursuit may continue beyond their ordinary jurisdiction where authorized by law.

2. The pursuit shall remain subject to Section 5 and the use-of-force requirements of Section 6.

3. Officers shall coordinate with the appropriate receiving agency where reasonably practicable.

4. Crossing a jurisdictional boundary shall not independently terminate an otherwise lawful pursuit.

5. Crossing a boundary shall not automatically authorize additional search, arrest, or force powers.

6. A pursuit shall remain subject to applicable public safety limitations.

## §17.9 — Official State Maps

1. The State shall maintain recognized official maps identifying relevant territorial boundaries and designated governmental areas.

2. Official maps may include:

   a. State territorial boundaries.

   b. County jurisdictions.

   c. Municipal boundaries.

   d. Law enforcement districts.

   e. Government-owned facilities.

   f. Restricted areas.

   g. Protected wildlife and conservation lands.

   h. Designated aviation and maritime restrictions.

   i. Other geographic designations established by law.

3. An official map shall identify its issuing authority, title, effective date, and revision designation.

4. Only maps formally adopted under lawful authority shall possess binding legal effect.

5. Reference maps, unofficial diagrams, and commercial navigation maps shall not independently amend legally adopted boundaries.

## §17.10 — Official Territorial Jurisdiction Map

1. The State shall adopt an Official Territorial Jurisdiction Map identifying Blaine County, Los Santos County, and the City of Los Santos.

2. The map shall establish the legally recognized division between the three principal jurisdictions.

3. The map may contain a scale, legend, geographic labels, boundary markings, and other necessary cartographic information.

4. The Department of Justice shall maintain the official adopted version.

5. Geographic schedules may be updated through the amendment procedures established under this section.

6. A map revision shall not take effect merely because a new draft has been prepared.

7. The authoritative map shall be identified as **Schedule 17-A — Official Territorial Jurisdiction Map**.

## §17.11 — Legal Interpretation of Boundaries

1. Jurisdictional boundaries shall be interpreted according to the officially adopted geographic schedule.

2. Where a boundary follows a roadway, coastline, waterway, property line, or other geographic feature, the controlling map or accompanying description shall determine its legal position.

3. A boundary shall not be altered solely because a physical roadway or structure is modified.

4. Ambiguous boundaries may be referred to the appropriate state authority for clarification.

5. Geographic uncertainty shall not independently grant an agency powers it does not possess.

6. Disputed criminal or administrative jurisdiction may be reviewed by an authorized court.

## §17.12 — Jurisdiction of Public Roads

1. Public roads shall remain subject to applicable state traffic laws.

2. Municipal and county authorities may exercise lawful enforcement powers upon roadways within their recognized jurisdictions.

3. State law enforcement agencies may exercise authority according to their statewide powers.

4. Road maintenance responsibility shall not independently determine criminal enforcement jurisdiction.

5. Highway designations, administrative maintenance arrangements, and law enforcement jurisdiction shall remain legally distinct where applicable.

6. Temporary road closures and traffic restrictions shall remain governed by Section 5.

## §17.13 — Inland Waterways and Coastal Jurisdiction

1. Lakes, rivers, reservoirs, and coastal waters shall fall within the applicable territorial authority established by state law.

2. Maritime enforcement shall remain governed by Section 15.

3. Geographic maps may identify maritime boundaries and designated waters.

4. A shoreline shall not automatically create unrestricted private ownership over adjacent navigable public waters.

5. Conservation restrictions may apply to designated waterways under Section 14.

6. Disputes concerning maritime and county jurisdiction may be addressed through authorized administrative or judicial procedures.

## §17.14 — Airspace Jurisdiction

1. Airspace within the territorial authority of San Andreas shall remain subject to applicable aviation laws.

2. Restricted and controlled airspace may be designated by authorized aviation authorities.

3. Airspace regulations shall remain governed by Section 15.

4. County or municipal jurisdiction shall not independently permit officials to create prohibited airspace without lawful authority.

5. Aviation-related restrictions shall be maintained through recognized maps and official notices.

6. Temporary emergency airspace restrictions shall remain limited to their lawful scope and duration.

## §17.15 — Government Property and Facilities

1. The State may designate property used for governmental functions.

2. Government facilities may include courthouses, police stations, correctional facilities, medical institutions, administrative offices, and other public-service premises.

3. Designation as government property shall not automatically establish a prohibition on public access.

4. Areas requiring security restrictions shall be identified according to applicable law.

5. Government personnel may establish lawful access procedures for secure facilities.

6. Restricted access shall not eliminate applicable civil rights or judicial review.

## §17.16 — Restricted Government Facilities

1. The State may designate secure government areas requiring special access authorization.

2. Such areas may include:

   a. Correctional institutions.

   b. Secure law enforcement facilities.

   c. Restricted judicial facilities.

   d. Evidence storage areas.

   e. Authorized emergency operations facilities.

   f. Other designated government security sites.

3. Unauthorized entry may constitute an offense where established by law.

4. Restricted designation shall not authorize unnecessary force or arbitrary arrest.

5. Publicly accessible portions of a government facility shall remain distinguishable from secure operational areas.

6. Restricted facilities shall be identified through official notices or other lawful publication.

## §17.17 — Military and Special Security Areas

1. The State may recognize military installations and other designated high-security areas.

2. Entry, photography, flight operations, and other activities may be restricted where lawful authority exists.

3. Restrictions shall be defined by applicable legislation or officially adopted notices.

4. Unauthorized entry may constitute an offense under the Penal Code.

5. Security personnel shall not exercise powers beyond those lawfully assigned to them.

6. Special security designation shall not independently authorize punishment without due process.

## §17.18 — Protected Wildlife and Conservation Areas

1. The State may designate protected habitats, wildlife reserves, hunting restrictions, and conservation areas.

2. Such areas shall be identified through the appropriate official geographic schedules.

3. Conservation restrictions shall remain governed by Section 14.

4. The existence of a protected area shall not automatically prohibit all public access unless lawfully established.

5. Activities within conservation areas shall be governed by the specific restrictions applicable to the designation.

6. Wildlife officers shall exercise only their lawful enforcement authority.

## §17.19 — Temporary Restricted Areas

1. Authorized officials may establish temporary restricted areas when necessary for public safety, emergency response, active criminal investigations, or other lawful purposes.

2. Restrictions shall be proportionate to the circumstances requiring them.

3. Temporary restrictions shall not remain effective after their lawful justification has ended.

4. The responsible authority shall provide reasonable notice where practicable.

5. Temporary restrictions shall not independently authorize unrestricted searches or unlawful confiscation.

6. Individuals may challenge improperly imposed restrictions through the appropriate judicial process.

## §17.20 — Private Property Boundaries

1. Private property boundaries shall be established through recognized property records and applicable law.

2. Official government maps may identify property and land-use districts where authorized.

3. Inclusion of private property within a county or municipality shall not transfer ownership to that jurisdiction.

4. Government officials shall not enter protected private premises without lawful authority.

5. Private owners shall not independently redefine public jurisdictional boundaries.

6. Ownership and boundary disputes may be heard by an authorized court under Section 16.

## §17.21 — Designated Commercial and Administrative Districts

1. The State may designate commercial, industrial, residential, and administrative districts where authorized.

2. Designations may identify permitted activities and applicable land-use restrictions.

3. District boundaries shall be established through officially adopted maps or written legal descriptions.

4. A geographic designation shall not independently revoke an existing lawful property interest without appropriate legal process.

5. Land-use disputes may be addressed through the appropriate administrative or judicial authority.

6. Commercial licensing shall remain governed by Sections 10 and 16.

## §17.22 — Government Mapping and Records

1. The State shall maintain records of officially adopted territorial and restricted-area maps.

2. Records shall identify the version, effective date, and responsible authority.

3. Superseded maps may be retained for historical and judicial reference.

4. Unauthorized alteration or falsification of official maps may constitute an offense under applicable law.

5. Individuals may request clarification concerning official jurisdictional designations.

6. Material errors may be corrected through authorized administrative or legislative procedures.

## §17.23 — Amendments to Official Maps

1. An official map may be amended through a lawful state procedure.

2. An amendment shall identify the affected territory or designation.

3. An amendment shall specify its effective date.

4. An amendment affecting jurisdictional authority shall be formally recorded.

5. No private contractor shall independently amend legally binding jurisdictional boundaries merely because it maintains a mapping system.

6. Existing rights and obligations shall be considered where a boundary amendment affects property, licenses, or pending proceedings.

7. Amendments shall not be applied retroactively to create criminal liability for conduct that was lawful when undertaken.

## §17.24 — Jurisdictional Disputes

1. Disputes concerning county, municipal, or agency jurisdiction may be submitted for authorized administrative or judicial determination.

2. Such disputes may concern enforcement authority, property administration, government services, or other legally recognized matters.

3. An agency shall not intentionally misrepresent its jurisdiction to obtain powers it does not possess.

4. A jurisdictional dispute shall not automatically invalidate an otherwise lawful emergency intervention.

5. Actions taken without required jurisdictional authority may be challenged through the Department of Justice.

6. Appropriate remedies shall be determined according to law.

## §17.25 — Jurisdiction and Criminal Proceedings

1. Criminal proceedings shall be brought before an authority possessing appropriate legal jurisdiction.

2. A criminal offense shall ordinarily be subject to the jurisdiction in which the relevant conduct occurred, subject to applicable exceptions.

3. Offenses involving conduct in multiple jurisdictions may be prosecuted according to applicable state law.

4. Jurisdictional uncertainty shall not independently establish criminal guilt.

5. The Department of Justice may resolve procedural disputes concerning venue and applicable judicial authority.

6. Criminal proceedings shall remain subject to the protections established under Sections 1, 7, 8, and 9.

## §17.26 — Official Publication

1. The State shall publish adopted territorial jurisdiction maps and applicable public geographic restrictions through recognized government channels.

2. Official maps may be made available in printed, electronic, or other accessible form.

3. The public version shall clearly identify the relevant jurisdictions and controlling revision.

4. Sensitive security information may be withheld where lawful authority permits, without concealing restrictions whose public notice is required for enforcement.

5. Government agencies shall not knowingly represent obsolete or unofficial maps as currently binding.

6. Public access to official geographic information shall remain subject to applicable state records law.

## §17.27 — Mapping Contractors and Technology

1. The State may contract independent private organizations to create, maintain, or distribute geographic information systems.

2. Such contractors shall not independently acquire legislative or jurisdictional powers.

3. A contractor shall not alter binding jurisdictional boundaries outside lawful authorization.

4. Unauthorized manipulation of official geographic records may be subject to criminal investigation.

5. The State shall retain legal responsibility for adoption and enforcement of official territorial designations.

6. Disputed map records shall remain subject to lawful review and correction.

## §17.28 — Judicial Review of Territorial Actions

1. Individuals and legal entities may challenge unlawful territorial or restricted-area designations affecting their rights.

2. Courts may examine the legal authority supporting a disputed designation.

3. A court may order correction of an unlawful record, appropriate relief, or other remedies authorized by law.

4. An administrative map shall not independently override a valid judicial order.

5. Property owners and affected persons shall retain applicable due process protections.

6. Judicial determinations concerning map-related disputes shall be recorded by the Department of Justice.

# OFFICIAL GEOGRAPHIC SCHEDULES

The following schedules shall support the implementation of Section 17:

**Schedule 17-A — Official Territorial Jurisdiction Map**

Identifies Blaine County, Los Santos County, and the City of Los Santos, including the legal boundaries separating those jurisdictions.

**Schedule 17-B — Law Enforcement Jurisdiction Map**

Identifies ordinary law enforcement jurisdictional areas and designated operational boundaries, subject to applicable state and mutual-aid authority.

**Schedule 17-C — Government Facilities and Restricted Areas**

Identifies government facilities, correctional institutions, secure installations, and other legally designated restricted sites.

**Schedule 17-D — Wildlife and Conservation Areas**

Identifies protected habitats, conservation zones, restricted hunting areas, and other designated environmental locations.

**Schedule 17-E — Aviation and Maritime Restrictions**

Identifies applicable airspace restrictions, designated aviation facilities, maritime restrictions, and other legally defined aviation or marine areas.

**Schedule 17-F — Administrative and Commercial Districts**

Identifies geographic districts established for lawful administrative, property, and commercial purposes.

The schedules shall possess binding legal effect only upon formal adoption by the competent state authority.

# LEGISLATIVE CONTINUITY AND AUTHORITY

1. Section 17 shall be interpreted consistently with Sections 1–16 of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.

2. No territorial or restricted-area designation shall eliminate the fundamental civil rights established under Section 1.

3. All detention, arrest, search, seizure, and use-of-force authority shall remain governed by the applicable provisions of this Code.

4. Law enforcement jurisdiction shall remain subject to Section 5.

5. Judicial disputes and legal review shall remain subject to Section 9.

6. Property ownership and commercial rights shall remain protected under Section 16.

7. Aviation and maritime restrictions shall remain subject to Section 15.

8. Conservation areas shall remain subject to Section 14.

9. No private corporation or administrative contractor shall independently establish binding territorial boundaries.

10. The State shall maintain officially adopted maps and publish legally enforceable geographic restrictions through recognized government channels.

11. Map revisions shall require lawful adoption and shall not independently create retroactive criminal liability.

12. The Department of Justice shall retain authority to review disputes concerning territorial jurisdiction, official records, and the legality of geographic restrictions.
