# ROSEFIRE CIVIL RIGHTS & JUSTICE CODE

STATE OF SAN ANDREAS DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE

Version 1.3 — Section 8 Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE

An Act establishing lawful sentencing, pretrial detention, correctional imprisonment, rehabilitation, parole, capital punishment, and release procedures within the State of San Andreas.

# SECTION 8 — SENTENCING, BAIL, REMAND & PAROLE

## §8.1 — Purpose and Principles of Sentencing

1. Criminal sentencing shall serve the lawful purposes of accountability, public protection, deterrence, rehabilitation, and restitution where appropriate.
2. Penalties shall be proportionate to the seriousness of the offense and the circumstances established in the criminal proceeding.
3. Sentencing authorities shall consider the applicable Penal Code, relevant evidence, and authorized mitigating or aggravating circumstances.
4. Punishment shall not be imposed solely to humiliate, retaliate against, or unlawfully deprive an individual of protected rights.
5. No sentence shall exceed the authority granted under this Code or the applicable Penal Code.
6. The Department of Corrections shall administer sentences lawfully imposed by authorized authorities but shall not independently increase criminal punishment.

## §8.2 — Sentencing Categories

The State recognizes the following forms of criminal disposition:

1. Citation or Monetary Fine: A financial penalty lawfully imposed for an eligible offense.
2. Restitution: A lawful payment or other remedy ordered to compensate an injured party for qualifying loss or damage.
3. Standard Correctional Custody: A fixed-term sentence administered through ordinary correctional procedures.
4. Extended Correctional Custody: A longer fixed-term sentence involving correctional supervision, potential rehabilitative programs, and applicable judicial review.
5. Life Imprisonment: An indefinite custodial sentence imposed following a qualifying conviction and an authorized judicial proceeding.
6. Capital Punishment: A sentence of death imposed by an authorized human court following conviction of a qualifying capital offense and completion of all required judicial safeguards.
7. Probation: A conditional noncustodial disposition requiring compliance with specified lawful conditions.
8. Parole: Conditional release from a custodial sentence subject to authorized supervision and restrictions.
9. Other Judicial Remedies: Additional penalties or conditions expressly authorized by law.

The precise penalties associated with criminal offenses shall be established under the Penal Code.

## §8.3 — Maximum Fixed-Term Imprisonment

1. No fixed-term sentence shall exceed seven consecutive real-world days for a single sentencing event.
2. Seven real-world days shall mean 168 hours of actual elapsed time.
3. Multiple consecutive fixed-term sentences arising from a single sentencing proceeding shall remain subject to the same maximum.
4. A sentencing authority shall not artificially divide charges or proceedings for the purpose of circumventing the statutory maximum.
5. Ordinary fixed-term sentences shall progress according to actual elapsed time, whether the individual is present within the State or temporarily absent, except where a specific lawful interruption applies.
6. Expiration of a fixed-term sentence shall establish entitlement to release unless a separate lawful custody commitment remains in effect.
7. The unavailability of correctional personnel shall not prevent release upon expiration of a lawful sentence.
8. Life imprisonment and capital punishment constitute separate sentencing categories and shall not be imposed merely because accumulated fixed-term penalties exceed the seven-day limit.

## §8.4 — Standard Correctional Sentences

1. Ordinary criminal sentences may be administered as standard correctional custody where authorized by law.
2. Such sentences shall possess a definite beginning and expiration.
3. Standard sentences may be administered without requiring the continuous presence of correctional personnel.
4. Inmates may participate in available correctional work, recreation, education, and rehabilitation activities.
5. Correctional activities may provide eligibility for authorized sentence credits or other lawful benefits.
6. An inmate shall not be denied the expiration of an ordinary fixed-term sentence solely because correctional programs or staff are unavailable.
7. Standard correctional custody shall not require a separate parole hearing unless a specific lawful order establishes one.

## §8.5 — Extended Correctional Sentences

1. A court may impose extended correctional custody for qualifying offenses where authorized by the Penal Code.
2. An extended fixed-term sentence shall remain subject to the maximum established under §8.3.
3. Extended custody may include structured correctional supervision, rehabilitation programs, work assignments, institutional schedules, and parole consideration.
4. The Department of Corrections may establish housing classifications, security procedures, and supervised activities consistent with applicable law.
5. Inmates serving extended sentences shall retain their protected civil rights.
6. An extended sentence shall not be increased solely because an inmate declines optional correctional activities.
7. Eligible inmates may receive lawful sentence reductions, parole consideration, or other benefits for documented rehabilitation and good conduct.
8. Expiration of an extended fixed-term sentence shall require release unless a separate lawful commitment remains in effect.

## §8.6 — Life Imprisonment

1. Life imprisonment may be imposed only for qualifying offenses expressly designated under the Penal Code.
2. A life sentence shall require a conviction following an authorized human judicial proceeding.
3. Life imprisonment shall not be imposed through ordinary officer-issued sentencing.
4. The automated judicial intelligence system shall not independently impose a life sentence under its current delegated authority.
5. A life sentence shall not possess an automatic expiration date.
6. Individuals serving life imprisonment shall remain eligible for lawful judicial review, rehabilitation consideration, clemency, parole, or other release procedures established by law.
7. Imposition of life imprisonment shall not deprive an inmate of fundamental civil rights beyond restrictions lawfully necessary for custody and institutional security.
8. The Department of Corrections shall maintain accurate records concerning the inmate's sentence, conduct, rehabilitative participation, and parole eligibility.
9. Life imprisonment shall not prohibit subsequent lawful reduction, modification, or termination of the sentence.

## §8.7 — Capital Punishment and Death Row

### §8.7.1 — Eligibility for Capital Punishment

1. The State of San Andreas recognizes capital punishment as an extraordinary criminal sentence reserved for individuals convicted of qualifying capital offenses.
2. An individual previously sentenced to life imprisonment who subsequently commits and is convicted of another offense independently qualifying for life imprisonment may be considered eligible for capital sentencing.
3. A previous life sentence shall not independently require that a subsequent conviction result in capital punishment.
4. Escape from lawful correctional custody shall not, by itself, constitute sufficient grounds for capital punishment.
5. The death penalty shall not be imposed merely because the accumulated penalties associated with multiple offenses exceed the maximum fixed-term imprisonment limit.
6. The offenses eligible for capital punishment shall be expressly designated under the Penal Code.

### §8.7.2 — Judicial Authority

1. A sentence of death may be imposed only following a formal criminal trial before an authorized human judge.
2. No law enforcement officer, correctional officer, prosecutor, or automated judicial service shall independently impose a sentence of death.
3. A capital sentencing determination shall require a separate judicial consideration of the appropriate punishment following conviction.
4. The court shall consider the seriousness of the offense, the accused's prior qualifying convictions, aggravating and mitigating circumstances, and evidence relevant to sentencing.
5. The court may impose another lawful sentence, including life imprisonment, when capital punishment is not warranted.
6. All capital sentencing decisions shall be recorded within the recognized judicial records system.

### §8.7.3 — Rights of the Condemned

1. An individual facing capital punishment shall retain all fundamental civil rights established under Section 1.
2. The accused shall have the right to legal representation throughout capital proceedings.
3. A capital defendant shall be provided a reasonable opportunity to present mitigating evidence, challenge evidence, and contest the proposed sentence.
4. A death sentence shall remain subject to authorized appeals and judicial review.
5. An individual sentenced to death shall retain the right to petition for executive clemency.
6. No execution shall proceed while an authorized appeal or judicial stay remains pending.
7. No individual shall be executed solely on the basis of an automated judicial determination.

### §8.7.4 — Death Row Custody

1. An individual sentenced to death shall be committed to designated death row custody under the supervision of the Department of Corrections.
2. Death row inmates shall remain subject to reasonable correctional security procedures.
3. Death row custody shall not authorize torture, cruel treatment, or disciplinary punishment exceeding lawful authority.
4. Inmates shall retain access to necessary medical treatment, legal representation, judicial proceedings, and authorized visitation.
5. The Department of Corrections may establish additional security procedures for death row facilities consistent with this Code.
6. The imposition of a death sentence shall not independently authorize correctional personnel to carry out an execution without a valid final execution warrant.

### §8.7.5 — Appeals and Clemency

1. A condemned inmate may challenge their conviction or sentence through authorized judicial proceedings.
2. The reviewing court may affirm, modify, vacate, or remand the sentence as permitted by law.
3. A death sentence may be commuted to life imprisonment or another lawful disposition by an authority possessing valid clemency powers.
4. A pending authorized judicial stay shall prevent execution until the stay is lawfully lifted.
5. The Department of Corrections shall comply with any judicial order suspending, modifying, or overturning a death sentence.
6. No correctional officer may independently deny a valid appeal, clemency petition, or judicial review.

### §8.7.6 — Execution Warrants and Final Sentences

1. An execution may be carried out only pursuant to a valid final execution warrant issued by an authorized human judicial authority.
2. The warrant shall identify the condemned individual, the controlling sentence, and the lawful authorization for execution.
3. The Department of Corrections shall verify that no applicable judicial stay prohibits execution.
4. Execution procedures shall be governed by established state law and authorized correctional regulations.
5. No execution shall be conducted solely upon verbal authorization from a law enforcement officer, correctional supervisor, prosecutor, or government contractor.
6. Upon lawful execution, the Department of Corrections shall record the completion of the sentence and notify the appropriate judicial authorities.
7. A completed lawful execution shall constitute the permanent conclusion of the condemned individual's life and legal identity.

### §8.7.7 — Escape from Death Row

1. Escape from death row custody shall constitute a criminal offense.
2. Escape shall not invalidate or extinguish the underlying capital sentence.
3. A condemned individual who escapes shall remain subject to lawful apprehension and the continuing legal effect of any enforceable capital sentence.
4. The discovery of an escaped condemned individual shall not authorize summary execution.
5. An escaped individual who is lawfully returned to custody shall retain access to pending judicial proceedings and applicable appeal protections.
6. Any dispute concerning the validity, alteration, or termination of the underlying judicial record shall be resolved through lawful judicial proceedings.

## §8.8 — Eligibility for Parole

1. Parole shall constitute a conditional release from lawful correctional custody.
2. Individuals serving qualifying extended or life sentences may be considered for parole under applicable eligibility requirements.
3. Eligibility may depend upon the nature of the conviction, time served, institutional conduct, rehabilitation, and other legally relevant circumstances.
4. Eligibility to apply for parole shall not automatically guarantee release.
5. Parole applications shall be considered by an authorized judicial or parole authority.
6. An inmate shall not be permanently deprived of parole eligibility solely because a parole officer or judge is unavailable at the time of an intended hearing.
7. Life-sentenced inmates shall have an established opportunity to seek parole review according to the schedule adopted under applicable correctional legislation.
8. Precise eligibility periods for parole shall be established in a published sentencing and parole schedule.
9. Individuals sentenced to death shall be governed by the capital appeals and clemency procedures established under §8.7 unless their sentence is lawfully commuted.

## §8.9 — Good Conduct and Rehabilitation

1. The Department of Corrections may maintain approved rehabilitative and educational programs.
2. Recognized activities may include:
   - Correctional employment.
   - Educational instruction.
   - Vocational training.
   - Counseling and rehabilitative services.
   - Institutional service assignments.
   - Other programs authorized by correctional regulations.
3. An inmate's documented participation and conduct may be considered during parole proceedings or authorized sentence-reduction reviews.
4. Good conduct shall not automatically extinguish a life sentence or capital sentence.
5. Correctional officers may provide recommendations concerning rehabilitation and release but shall not independently pardon an inmate or overturn a judicial sentence.
6. An inmate shall not be penalized solely because approved rehabilitative programs are unavailable.
7. The Department of Corrections shall maintain appropriate records supporting recommendations made to parole or judicial authorities.

## §8.10 — Parole Hearings and Decisions

1. An eligible inmate may submit an application for parole through the recognized correctional or judicial process.
2. The reviewing authority may examine the inmate's conviction, sentence, institutional record, rehabilitation, and relevant public safety concerns.
3. The inmate shall be given an opportunity to provide a statement, evidence of rehabilitation, and supporting documentation.
4. The inmate may seek legal representation in accordance with applicable law.
5. The reviewing authority may approve, deny, or postpone parole within the limits of its lawful jurisdiction.
6. Parole decisions shall be recorded and communicated to the inmate.
7. Denial of parole shall not permanently prohibit future applications unless a specific lawful restriction applies.
8. Decisions concerning life-sentenced inmates shall be subject to the judicial authority and review procedures established by law.

## §8.11 — Conditions of Parole

1. A parole order may impose reasonable conditions related to lawful supervision, public safety, and rehabilitation.
2. Authorized conditions may include:
   - Compliance with criminal laws.
   - Reporting to an assigned supervising authority.
   - Restrictions concerning prohibited weapons.
   - Compliance with lawful protective orders.
   - Participation in specified rehabilitation programs.
   - Other conditions expressly authorized by the parole order.
3. Parole conditions shall be clearly communicated to the individual.
4. A parolee shall not be held responsible for violating a condition that was not lawfully imposed or adequately communicated.
5. Conditions shall not arbitrarily deprive an individual of protected civil rights.
6. Parole restrictions shall remain effective only for their lawful duration.

## §8.12 — Parole Violations

1. A parole violation occurs when an individual knowingly or culpably fails to comply with a lawful condition of parole, according to applicable law.
2. An alleged violation shall be supported by identifiable facts and appropriate evidence.
3. A parole officer or law enforcement officer may report suspected violations to the authorized reviewing authority.
4. Parole shall not be permanently revoked solely upon an unsupported allegation.
5. The individual shall have a reasonable opportunity to contest the alleged violation.
6. An authorized judicial or parole authority may impose lawful corrective conditions, revoke parole, or order recommitment where permitted.
7. Recommitment shall not exceed the lawful authority governing the underlying sentence and the applicable violation.
8. Individuals serving a life sentence who violate parole may be subject to recommitment under the continuing life sentence, where lawfully ordered.

## §8.13 — Bail and Conditional Pretrial Release

1. Bail is a lawful mechanism permitting the conditional release of an accused individual pending trial or another judicial proceeding.
2. Bail may include a monetary guarantee, reporting obligations, travel restrictions, or other lawful conditions.
3. Bail shall not be used as punishment before conviction.
4. An accused individual shall not be denied bail solely because of their financial circumstances without appropriate consideration of less restrictive alternatives.
5. Bail decisions shall consider the seriousness of the alleged offense, available evidence, risk of nonappearance, and identifiable threats to public safety.
6. Bail amounts and conditions shall be established by an authorized judicial authority.
7. Bail may be modified or revoked through lawful judicial review.
8. The accused shall remain presumed innocent unless and until convicted according to law.

## §8.14 — Pretrial Remand

1. Pretrial remand is the lawful detention of an accused individual pending trial or another specified proceeding.
2. Remand shall require a valid judicial order.
3. Remand may be authorized when sufficient evidence establishes a substantial risk that the accused will fail to appear, seriously endanger others, or unlawfully interfere with the proceedings, and less restrictive conditions are inadequate.
4. The seriousness of an alleged charge shall not automatically establish grounds for remand.
5. A remand order shall identify the legal grounds for detention and the applicable review requirements.
6. An individual held on remand shall retain the presumption of innocence.
7. Remanded individuals shall not be treated as convicted prisoners for sentencing purposes.
8. Remand shall remain subject to judicial review and applicable custody deadlines.
9. An accused individual shall not remain on remand indefinitely solely because a trial cannot immediately be scheduled.
10. Where continued remand is no longer lawfully justified, the accused shall be released under appropriate conditions or without conditions as ordered.

## §8.15 — Pretrial Custody and Correctional Treatment

1. The Department of Corrections may receive individuals held under valid judicial remand orders.
2. Remanded individuals shall be appropriately distinguished from sentenced prisoners in official correctional records.
3. Correctional officers may apply necessary institutional security requirements to remanded persons.
4. Remanded individuals shall retain reasonable access to legal counsel, medical treatment, and judicial proceedings.
5. A remanded individual shall not be compelled to participate in punitive correctional labor as though they had been convicted.
6. Time spent in lawful pretrial custody may be considered for sentence credit as established by the sentencing authority.
7. The Department of Corrections shall comply with judicial release or custody-modification orders.

## §8.16 — Correctional Work and Institutional Programs

1. The Department of Corrections may establish work, education, maintenance, and rehabilitative programs within correctional institutions.
2. Correctional work assignments shall be conducted under reasonable safety and institutional regulations.
3. Inmates may receive lawful compensation, privileges, or sentence-credit consideration where authorized.
4. Work assignments shall not be administered as unlawful forced labor or cruel punishment.
5. Inmates shall not be required to purchase access to basic necessities, medical treatment, or fundamental legal protections.
6. Correctional programs may be administered by authorized personnel, including approved contracted service providers where permitted by law.
7. Private contractors shall not acquire independent judicial or sentencing authority by operating correctional programs.

## §8.17 — Institutional Discipline

1. The Department of Corrections may establish reasonable disciplinary regulations necessary to maintain order and safety.
2. Disciplinary sanctions may include temporary restrictions on privileges, housing adjustments, supervised movement, or other lawful institutional measures.
3. Discipline shall not include torture, excessive force, or cruel treatment.
4. Correctional officers shall not impose additional criminal prison sentences through institutional disciplinary decisions.
5. Conduct constituting a criminal offense may be referred for independent investigation and prosecution.
6. An inmate accused of a serious institutional violation shall receive reasonable notice of the allegation and an opportunity to respond.
7. Institutional disciplinary findings may be considered in lawful parole proceedings.
8. Correctional discipline shall not prevent an inmate from seeking legal assistance or reporting unlawful treatment.

## §8.18 — Escape from Correctional Custody

1. Escape from lawful correctional custody shall constitute a criminal offense under the Penal Code.
2. An inmate who escapes shall remain subject to the legal consequences of the underlying sentence.
3. Escape shall not constitute a lawful discharge, pardon, or parole.
4. The Department of Corrections may issue appropriate notifications and coordinate with law enforcement to locate an escaped individual.
5. An escaped individual may be lawfully arrested and returned to correctional custody where a valid enforceable commitment or warrant exists.
6. Additional criminal charges arising from the escape shall require lawful investigation and criminal processing.
7. Escape from a fixed-term sentence shall not independently convert that sentence into life imprisonment or permit evasion of the statutory seven-day fixed-term maximum.
8. Disputes concerning the continuing legal effect of an expired, altered, or terminated commitment shall be resolved under applicable judicial procedures.
9. An escaped individual previously sentenced to life imprisonment who subsequently commits a new offense qualifying for life imprisonment may become eligible for capital sentencing under §8.7.
10. A new qualifying conviction shall be required before the court may consider imposing capital punishment on that basis.

## §8.19 — Judicial Record Integrity and Release Status

1. The State shall maintain recognized judicial records identifying an individual's criminal sentence, correctional commitment, and release status.
2. The Department of Corrections and recognized law enforcement agencies may rely upon authenticated judicial records in determining lawful custody status.
3. A record identifying a commitment as satisfied, discharged, or terminated shall be given effect unless independent lawful authority establishes continued custody.
4. Evidence suggesting unauthorized alteration or falsification of a judicial record may be referred for criminal investigation.
5. Suspected record manipulation shall not, by itself, permit arbitrary imprisonment.
6. Restoration or correction of a disputed commitment shall require the applicable judicial review and lawful determination.
7. Unauthorized manipulation of judicial records shall be subject to criminal penalties established by law.
8. No individual shall be presumed responsible for judicial record manipulation solely because their own record contains irregularities.
9. The provisions of this section shall also apply to disputed capital sentences, execution warrants, and death row commitments.

## §8.20 — Pardons and Clemency

1. A pardon is a lawful act of executive or other duly authorized clemency relieving an individual of specified criminal consequences.
2. Clemency may include a pardon, commutation, or other authorized reduction of punishment.
3. The authority to grant clemency shall belong only to the government office or judicial authority expressly empowered by law.
4. The Department of Corrections may provide records and recommendations concerning an inmate's conduct and rehabilitation.
5. A lawful pardon or commutation shall be recorded within the recognized judicial records system.
6. A pardon shall not automatically establish that the original conviction was factually incorrect unless the applicable order expressly makes such a determination.
7. Individuals serving life imprisonment may petition for clemency according to applicable law.
8. Individuals sentenced to death may petition for clemency under the procedures established in §8.7.
9. No private corporation or contracted judicial technology provider shall possess independent authority to grant pardons.

## §8.21 — Sentence Modification and Appeals

1. An individual may petition for judicial review of a sentence according to applicable law.
2. Grounds for review may include legal error, newly discovered material evidence, unlawful sentencing, or other recognized circumstances.
3. Authorized courts may affirm, modify, vacate, or remand a sentence where permitted by law.
4. A sentence shall not be increased solely because an individual exercised their lawful right to appeal.
5. Appeals shall not automatically suspend a valid sentence unless an authorized judicial order provides otherwise.
6. Capital sentences shall remain subject to the additional appeal and execution-stay protections established under §8.7.
7. An individual shall retain access to an appropriate judicial process for challenging unlawful continuing detention.
8. Automated judicial decisions shall remain subject to review in accordance with §3.14.

## §8.22 — Automated Judicial Review and Sentencing

1. An automated judicial service may assist with eligible sentencing, bail, remand, and correctional review functions where expressly authorized.
2. An automated system may examine official records, receive submissions, and issue determinations only within its lawfully delegated jurisdiction.
3. Automated sentencing decisions shall identify the applicable offense, legal authority, and imposed disposition.
4. An automated judicial service shall not independently impose life imprisonment or capital punishment under its present authority.
5. An automated service shall not invent criminal charges, disregard statutory sentence limits, or alter the legal standards established by this Code.
6. An individual affected by an automated determination may petition for human judicial review.
7. No automated decision shall be immune from lawful appeal or correction.
8. The private corporation responsible for maintaining the judicial service shall not independently exercise prosecutorial authority or determine the outcome of proceedings outside its authorized contractual duties.
9. Material errors or suspected interference affecting an automated judicial decision may be investigated by the Department of Justice.
10. No automated judicial service may independently issue a final execution warrant.

## §8.23 — Release from Correctional Custody

1. An individual shall be released from correctional custody upon lawful expiration, parole, pardon, sentence modification, court order, or another recognized legal discharge.
2. Standard fixed-term sentences shall expire according to their legally established duration without requiring the attendance of correctional personnel.
3. Extended fixed-term sentences shall likewise terminate upon lawful expiration unless an independently valid commitment remains in effect.
4. Individuals serving life sentences shall require a lawful release determination, including parole, pardon, commutation, or other authorized action.
5. Individuals sentenced to death shall remain subject to the capital punishment provisions of §8.7 unless the sentence is vacated, commuted, or otherwise lawfully terminated.
6. The Department of Corrections shall not intentionally delay or deny a lawful release.
7. The State shall record the basis and effective date of release.
8. Property lawfully held for safekeeping during imprisonment shall be eligible for return, except where it remains subject to lawful evidence retention, confiscation, or forfeiture.
9. An individual released from correctional custody shall not remain subject to institutional control except under valid parole, probation, or other lawful conditions.

## §8.24 — Wrongful Imprisonment and Correctional Misconduct

1. An individual subjected to imprisonment without lawful authority may petition the Department of Justice for review.
2. An individual may challenge the unlawful extension of a sentence, denial of release, or improper execution of a correctional commitment.
3. Correctional personnel shall not knowingly continue to imprison a person after the expiration or lawful termination of their commitment.
4. Knowingly falsifying sentence records, release documents, parole records, execution warrants, or correctional commitments may constitute criminal misconduct.
5. A court may order release, correction of records, or other appropriate remedies where a violation is established.
6. Wrongful imprisonment claims may give rise to lawful civil proceedings where authorized.
7. No government official shall retaliate against an individual for submitting a lawful complaint concerning correctional misconduct.
8. Unlawful or unauthorized execution shall be subject to criminal investigation and judicial review.

## §8.25 — Correctional Oversight and Accountability

1. The Department of Corrections shall administer correctional facilities in accordance with state law and applicable institutional regulations.
2. The Department of Justice shall retain authority to review the legality of correctional commitments, sentencing decisions, capital sentencing proceedings, and disputed release matters.
3. Correctional officers shall maintain appropriate records concerning custody, disciplinary actions, significant incidents, death row commitments, execution warrants, and release.
4. Government contractors operating correctional services shall remain subject to applicable laws and contractual oversight.
5. No contractor shall independently impose criminal sentences, revoke judicial release orders, order executions, or indefinitely detain individuals.
6. The State shall maintain lawful procedures for investigating allegations of correctional abuse, unlawful imprisonment, and institutional misconduct.
7. The protections established under Section 1 shall remain applicable throughout correctional custody.

# LEGISLATIVE CONTINUITY AND AUTHORITY

1. Section 8 shall be read in conjunction with Sections 1–7 of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code.
2. No criminal sentencing procedure shall override the fundamental civil rights established under Section 1.
3. Detention and arrest shall remain governed by Sections 2–3.
4. Searches and seizures shall remain governed by Section 4.
5. Law enforcement authority and lawful orders shall remain governed by Section 5.
6. All uses of force during criminal processing, transport, and correctional custody shall remain subject to Section 6.
7. Criminal processing and charging procedures shall remain governed by Section 7.
8. The statutory maximum for fixed-term imprisonment shall remain seven real-world days.
9. Life imprisonment and capital punishment shall constitute separate sentencing categories subject to their specific judicial requirements.
10. Capital punishment shall not be imposed automatically following escape, a prior life sentence, or the accumulation of ordinary criminal penalties.
11. Only an authorized human judicial authority may impose a sentence of death.
12. Departmental policies, institutional regulations, and private government contracts shall not supersede the protections established by this Code.
13. Judicial and correctional records shall be maintained according to the legal requirements established herein.
14. Subsequent legislation shall establish the offenses eligible for life imprisonment and capital punishment, specific criminal penalties, parole eligibility periods, procedural deadlines, and delegated automated judicial powers.

END OF SECTION 8
