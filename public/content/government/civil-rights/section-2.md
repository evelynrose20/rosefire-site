# SECTION 2 — DEFINITIONS AND LEGAL STANDARDS

## §2.1 — General Definitions

For the purposes of this Code, the following terms shall have the meanings assigned below.

**Individual:** Any person located within the territorial jurisdiction of the State of San Andreas.

**Law Enforcement Officer (LEO):** An authorized and sworn officer employed by a recognized law enforcement agency and empowered to enforce criminal laws.

**Law Enforcement Agency (LEA):** Any state, county, municipal, or other law enforcement organization formally recognized by the State of San Andreas.

**Department of Justice (DOJ):** The judicial and legal authority responsible for the administration of courts, judicial orders, licensed legal representation, and other duties assigned by law.

**Department of Corrections (DOC):** The state authority responsible for the lawful custody, supervision, care, and management of persons committed to state correctional facilities.

**Court Official:** A duly appointed judge, prosecutor, or other official expressly authorized to exercise a judicial or prosecutorial function.

**District Attorney (DA):** The prosecutorial official authorized to represent the State in criminal proceedings.

**Assistant District Attorney (ADA):** An authorized prosecutor serving under the authority of the District Attorney or the applicable prosecutorial office.

**Detention:** A temporary restriction upon an individual's freedom of movement for a lawful investigative purpose, without necessarily placing the individual under arrest.

**Arrest:** The taking of an individual into lawful custody based upon probable cause, a valid arrest warrant, or other lawful authority.

**Custody:** A condition in which an individual is under arrest or otherwise deprived of freedom of movement to a degree equivalent to formal arrest.

**Investigative Questioning:** Questions intended to obtain information concerning a suspected criminal offense.

**Custodial Interrogation:** Investigative questioning of an individual while in custody.

**Search:** An examination of an individual, vehicle, residence, business, container, electronic device, or other property for evidence, prohibited items, or persons.

**Seizure:** The lawful taking, detention, or restriction of property or evidence by government authorities.

**Warrant:** A written judicial order authorizing a specified arrest, search, seizure, or other lawful investigative action.

**Dwelling:** Any house, apartment, rented room, or other location lawfully occupied as a private residence.

**Private Business:** A legally owned or leased establishment operated for commercial purposes, including areas not normally accessible to the general public.

**Public Place:** A location ordinarily accessible to members of the public, including roads, sidewalks, parks, and publicly accessible areas of commercial establishments.

**Motor Vehicle:** Any self-propelled vehicle designed for transportation upon public roads or other lawful operating areas.

**Commercial Vehicle:** A motor vehicle or combination of vehicles operated for commercial transportation and subject to applicable commercial licensing requirements.

**Correctional Facility:** A prison, detention center, or other institution designated by the State for the lawful confinement of individuals.

**Remand:** The judicially authorized detention of an accused individual pending trial or another specified proceeding.

**Standard Correctional Custody:** A fixed-term sentence administered through ordinary correctional procedures and eligible for release upon completion without requiring the attendance of correctional personnel.

**Extended Correctional Custody:** A judicially authorized sentence involving longer-term correctional supervision, potential rehabilitative participation, and applicable judicial review.

**Life Imprisonment:** An indefinite sentence imposed following a qualifying conviction and judicial proceeding, subject to lawful release, parole, clemency, or judicial modification.

**Parole:** The conditional release of an individual from correctional custody under obligations established by an authorized judicial or correctional process.

**Reasonable Force:** Force that is objectively necessary and proportionate to accomplish a lawful purpose under the circumstances.

**Less-Lethal Force:** Force intended to control or incapacitate an individual without ordinarily causing death, including approved restraint devices and less-lethal equipment.

**Lethal Force:** Force capable of causing death or serious bodily injury.

**Exigent Circumstances:** Urgent conditions justifying immediate lawful action where obtaining a warrant would create an unreasonable risk of harm or another legally recognized emergency.

**Judicial Intelligence System:** A state-authorized automated technological service used in the administration of specified judicial proceedings and official records.

**Judicial Record:** An official record of a conviction, warrant, commitment, sentence, release order, or other legally recognized judicial determination.

## §2.2 — Reasonable Suspicion

1. Reasonable suspicion exists when a law enforcement officer can identify specific, articulable facts suggesting that an individual is involved in criminal activity.

2. Reasonable suspicion must be based upon observable conduct, credible information, or other circumstances supporting an investigative detention.

3. A mere hunch, personal dislike, unsupported assumption, or generalized suspicion is insufficient.

4. Reasonable suspicion permits temporary investigative detention but does not independently authorize arrest or a full evidentiary search.

5. Reasonable suspicion shall be evaluated according to the totality of circumstances known to the officer at the time.

6. The lawful exercise of protected civil rights shall not independently establish reasonable suspicion.

## §2.3 — Probable Cause

1. Probable cause exists when reasonably trustworthy facts and circumstances would cause a prudent person to believe that a particular offense has occurred, is occurring, or is about to occur, and that the relevant individual or property is connected to that offense.

2. Probable cause may be established through direct observation, reliable witness testimony, physical evidence, investigative findings, or other credible information.

3. Probable cause may justify an arrest where authorized by law or support an application for a search warrant.

4. Probable cause does not automatically authorize the search of unrelated persons, vehicles, residences, or properties.

5. Probable cause shall be assessed objectively based upon information available when the action was taken.

## §2.4 — Consent

1. Consent is the voluntary and informed permission given by an individual legally authorized to permit a specified search or action.

2. Consent shall not be valid when obtained through unlawful threats, physical coercion, or fraudulent assertions of legal authority.

3. An individual may limit the scope of a consensual search.

4. Consent may be withdrawn at any time before completion of the search, except where officers have independently established lawful authority to continue.

5. Consent to search one location or item shall not automatically extend to other locations or property.

6. Mere silence or failure to resist an officer shall not constitute consent.

## §2.5 — Exigent Circumstances

Exigent circumstances are urgent conditions requiring immediate lawful action where obtaining a warrant would create an unreasonable risk.

Such circumstances may include:

1. An immediate threat of death or serious bodily injury.

2. An active emergency requiring assistance to an endangered individual.

3. The imminent destruction of material evidence, supported by specific and articulable circumstances.

4. Qualifying hot pursuit of an individual for whom officers possess lawful grounds to arrest.

5. An immediate threat to public safety requiring entry or other limited emergency intervention.

Any action undertaken under exigent circumstances shall remain limited to what is reasonably necessary to address the emergency.

## §2.6 — Judicial Authority

1. Judicial authority shall be exercised only by persons or systems lawfully empowered by the State.

2. Judges shall possess the authority granted to their office under applicable law.

3. Prosecutors may initiate, pursue, and manage criminal proceedings within their designated authority but shall not exercise powers reserved exclusively to judges.

4. Correctional officers shall not exercise independent judicial sentencing authority.

5. Automated judicial systems may exercise only those powers expressly delegated under applicable law.

## §2.7 — Interpretation of Time

1. Where a legal provision expressly references real-world time, the period shall be measured by actual elapsed time.

2. Where a legal provision uses in-state calendar time, the applicable legislation or judicial order shall specify the governing period.

3. Fixed-term imprisonment shall be governed by actual elapsed time, subject to any lawful interruption expressly established by subsequent legislation.

4. No individual shall be required to remain continuously present within a correctional facility solely to advance an ordinary fixed-term sentence.

5. Specific detention deadlines, appeal periods, and parole eligibility requirements shall be established by the relevant provisions of law.
