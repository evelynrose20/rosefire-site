# ROSEFIRE CIVIL RIGHTS & JUSTICE CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**\
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.2 — Sections 7–8**\
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing the lawful procedures governing criminal processing, formal charges, prosecutorial authority, judicial proceedings, sentencing, pretrial detention, correctional imprisonment, rehabilitation, parole, and release within the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–6 remain enacted and in force. Sections 7–8 form a continuation of the same Code and shall be interpreted consistently with all previously established civil rights, law enforcement limitations, and correctional protections.

---

# SECTION 7 — POLICE PROCESSING & CRIMINAL CHARGES

## §7.1 — General Processing Authority

1. A law enforcement officer may process an individual following a lawful arrest conducted in accordance with Section 3 of this Code.
2. Criminal processing shall be conducted for the purpose of establishing an accurate record of the alleged offenses, identifying the accused, securing lawful evidence, and determining the appropriate disposition.
3. Processing shall not be used as a means of punishment, intimidation, retaliation, or unlawful coercion.
4. Officers shall not intentionally prolong processing solely to punish an individual or interfere with their protected civil rights.
5. All criminal processing shall remain subject to the protections established under Section 1.
6. An individual's refusal to confess, enter a guilty plea, or waive legal representation shall not justify an increased criminal charge or punishment.

## §7.2 — Arrest and Booking Procedures

1. Upon arrival at a recognized law enforcement processing facility, the arresting officer shall establish the identity of the accused and document the grounds for arrest.
2. The arresting agency shall maintain an official record containing, where applicable:

   a. The accused individual's legal name and identifying information.

   b. The date and time of arrest.

   c. The arresting officer and responsible agency.

   d. The offenses alleged.

   e. A summary of the facts establishing probable cause.

   f. Evidence and property seized during the investigation.

   g. Any injuries, medical concerns, or significant uses of force associated with the arrest.

   h. The disposition of the accused, including release, citation, transfer, or correctional commitment.
3. The accused shall be informed of the charges being considered or filed against them.
4. An accused individual shall not be required to confess or provide an incriminating statement as a condition of completing booking.
5. Booking procedures may include authorized identification photographs, fingerprint collection, and inventory of personal property.
6. Booking shall not independently authorize access to protected electronic communications or private digital records.

## §7.3 — Notification of Criminal Charges

1. Before imposing an authorized penalty or transferring an individual for imprisonment, the responsible officer shall inform the accused of each criminal charge being filed.
2. The notification shall include the recognized offense, its legal classification, and the potential penalties associated with the charge.
3. The accused shall have a reasonable opportunity to request clarification of the charges.
4. An individual shall not be sentenced for an offense that has not been formally identified and documented.
5. Charges may be amended when supported by additional evidence or prosecutorial review, provided the accused is informed of the amendment.
6. Knowingly filing false charges or deliberately misrepresenting material facts may constitute official misconduct and an offense under the applicable Penal Code.

## §7.4 — Right to Respond to Charges

1. Every accused individual shall be provided a reasonable opportunity to respond to the allegations before disposition.
2. The accused may admit, deny, or decline to make a statement concerning the alleged offense.
3. The accused shall not be compelled to provide evidence against themselves.
4. Statements made voluntarily may be considered during lawful criminal proceedings.
5. An officer shall not treat an individual's silence as a confession or automatic admission of guilt.
6. An accused individual who disputes the charges shall retain the right to seek judicial review and contest the allegations according to applicable court procedures.

## §7.5 — Legal Representation During Processing

1. An accused individual may request assistance from a licensed attorney during criminal processing.
2. Upon a valid request for counsel, custodial interrogation shall cease in accordance with §1.4.
3. An attorney may consult with the accused, review charges and available relevant information, and communicate with the responsible officer or prosecutor.
4. Attorneys may negotiate lawful dispositions or submit objections on behalf of their clients.
5. An attorney shall not interfere with lawful evidence preservation, necessary security procedures, or emergency medical treatment.
6. The absence of an available attorney shall not authorize indefinite detention or the denial of an otherwise lawful release.
7. When an attorney is unavailable, the accused may elect to proceed with an informed waiver of counsel or await an authorized judicial procedure.
8. An accused individual shall not be forced to waive representation merely to avoid unnecessary delay in processing.
9. Cases requiring judicial determination may be referred to an authorized automated judicial service where the service possesses the necessary legal authority.

## §7.6 — Pleas and Admissions

1. An individual formally charged with a criminal offense may enter a plea of Guilty or Not Guilty.
2. A Guilty plea must be voluntary, informed, and entered without unlawful coercion.
3. A Not Guilty plea shall preserve the accused individual's right to contest the charges through an authorized judicial proceeding.
4. Silence or refusal to enter a plea shall not automatically constitute a Guilty plea.
5. Where an accused declines to enter a plea, the case shall be treated as contested and directed to the appropriate judicial process.
6. A guilty plea entered through threats, unlawful pressure, or material deception may be challenged before the Department of Justice.
7. The State shall maintain an official record of the plea and resulting disposition.

## §7.7 — Citation and Fine Authority

1. An authorized law enforcement officer may issue citations and monetary penalties for offenses designated as eligible for administrative or officer-issued disposition.
2. All fines shall be established by the applicable Penal Code or other duly enacted legislation.
3. Officers shall not create new monetary penalties or arbitrarily increase established fines.
4. An individual shall be informed of the offense and amount associated with a citation.
5. An individual may contest a citation through the appropriate judicial process.
6. Refusal or inability to pay a citation shall not automatically authorize imprisonment unless a specific law and lawful judicial process permit such a consequence.
7. A disputed citation shall remain subject to the applicable collection and appeal procedures.
8. Officers shall not demand payment directly to themselves or any unauthorized personal or business account.

## §7.8 — Criminal Sentencing Authority

1. Criminal penalties may be imposed only by authorities expressly empowered under the laws of San Andreas.
2. Law enforcement officers may impose eligible routine penalties and fixed-term sentences only where an applicable law or authorized criminal disposition procedure grants that power.
3. An officer's authority to file a criminal charge shall not independently establish authority to adjudicate a contested charge.
4. Contested criminal charges requiring determination of guilt shall be referred to an authorized judicial proceeding.
5. Serious criminal offenses designated for mandatory judicial review shall not receive final sentencing solely at the discretion of the arresting officer.
6. Judges may impose lawful sentences within the limits established by the Penal Code and this Code.
7. Prosecutors may recommend penalties, negotiate lawful dispositions, and present sentencing arguments, but shall not independently exercise judicial powers reserved to judges.
8. Authorized automated judicial services may issue only those criminal dispositions expressly delegated by law.
9. Life imprisonment shall require an authorized human judicial proceeding unless subsequent legislation expressly provides otherwise.
10. No sentencing authority may impose a fixed term exceeding the seven-real-world-day maximum established under §3.12.

## §7.9 — Plea Agreements and Negotiated Dispositions

1. An authorized prosecutor or other official possessing lawful disposition authority may negotiate a plea agreement with an accused individual.
2. A plea agreement may concern eligible charges, monetary penalties, imprisonment, restitution, or other authorized conditions.
3. An agreement shall not include a penalty or condition prohibited by law.
4. The accused shall be informed of the material terms before accepting the agreement.
5. The accused may consult legal counsel before accepting or rejecting a proposed agreement.
6. Refusal to accept a plea agreement shall not independently constitute an aggravating offense.
7. Agreements requiring judicial approval shall not become final until accepted by the appropriate judicial authority.
8. An accepted and lawful plea agreement shall be recorded within the recognized judicial records system.
9. Agreements obtained through unlawful coercion, intentional material deception, or abuse of authority may be challenged before the Department of Justice.

## §7.10 — Prosecutorial Review

1. The District Attorney and duly authorized prosecutors may review criminal charges filed by recognized law enforcement agencies.
2. Prosecutorial review may include examination of probable cause, legal sufficiency, evidentiary support, and compliance with applicable rights.
3. An authorized prosecutor may recommend amendment, reduction, dismissal, or continuation of charges in accordance with applicable law.
4. A prosecutor shall not knowingly pursue charges unsupported by sufficient lawful evidence.
5. Prosecutors shall disclose material exculpatory evidence as required by law.
6. Allegations of serious official misconduct, wrongful imprisonment, or material judicial record manipulation may be referred for prosecutorial investigation.
7. A prosecutor shall not conceal evidence of unlawful conduct for the purpose of protecting a public official, private contractor, or government institution.

## §7.11 — Mandatory Judicial Referral

The following matters shall require review or determination by an authorized judicial authority:

1. Applications for pretrial remand.
2. Contested charges requiring formal adjudication.
3. Proposed life sentences.
4. Extraordinary sentencing orders.
5. Disputed correctional commitments or release orders.
6. Applications for substantial property forfeiture requiring judicial authorization.
7. Cases specifically reserved for judicial consideration under the Penal Code.
8. Proceedings involving a material dispute over the authenticity or validity of a judicial record.
9. Other matters expressly assigned to the courts by law.

An automated judicial service may act in a matter listed above only where the specific function has been lawfully delegated to it.

## §7.12 — Processing When a Judge Is Unavailable

1. The unavailability of a human judge shall not prevent an authorized law enforcement agency from performing lawful arrest, booking, citation, and ordinary processing duties.
2. Where a matter requires judicial authorization, the responsible agency may submit the matter to the authorized judicial intelligence service if that service possesses jurisdiction.
3. An automated judicial service shall not exercise authority beyond that granted under §§3.14–3.16 and applicable judicial legislation.
4. Where neither an authorized human judge nor an appropriately empowered automated judicial service is available, the State shall not impose an extraordinary sentence or judicial hold without lawful authorization.
5. An accused individual shall not be held indefinitely solely because an authorized judicial decision-maker is unavailable.
6. The matter may be scheduled for subsequent judicial proceedings, subject to applicable release, summons, bail, and remand provisions.
7. Emergency applications may be considered under established judicial procedures when a qualifying emergency exists.

## §7.13 — Evidence and Property During Processing

1. Items lawfully seized as criminal evidence shall be documented and transferred to appropriate evidence custody.
2. Illegal weapons, prohibited substances, and other unlawful property may be retained according to applicable law.
3. Lawfully owned personal property not required as evidence shall be secured and made eligible for return.
4. Personal items held during temporary custody shall not be permanently forfeited without lawful authority.
5. Officers shall maintain an appropriate chain of custody for criminal evidence.
6. Knowingly tampering with, destroying, falsifying, or concealing evidence may constitute an offense.
7. The accused may petition for the return of improperly retained property under §4.12.

## §7.14 — Transfer to Correctional Custody

1. An individual shall not be transferred to a state correctional facility without a lawful commitment, sentence, remand order, or other recognized custody authorization.
2. The transferring authority shall provide the Department of Corrections with the necessary commitment information.
3. Transfer documentation shall identify:

   a. The individual committed.

   b. The legal authority for confinement.

   c. The relevant charges or conviction.

   d. The authorized term or nature of custody.

   e. Any applicable judicial conditions.

   f. Known immediate medical or security concerns relevant to safe custody.
4. The Department of Corrections shall verify the existence of lawful commitment authority before accepting an individual into correctional custody.
5. Correctional officers may refuse or seek clarification of a defective commitment order without independently determining criminal guilt.
6. Emergency medical needs shall be addressed before or during transport as reasonably necessary.
7. The responsible authorities shall document the transfer of custody.

## §7.15 — Criminal Records and Judicial Entries

1. Criminal charges, convictions, judicial decisions, and lawful dispositions shall be recorded within the recognized state judicial records system.
2. Arrest records shall distinguish allegations from final criminal convictions.
3. A charge dismissed or resulting in acquittal shall not be represented as a conviction.
4. Sentence completion, parole, pardon, expungement, and other lawful dispositions shall be reflected in the individual's official record.
5. Records maintained through the judicial intelligence system shall remain subject to judicial oversight.
6. Unauthorized alteration, deletion, or falsification of criminal records shall be governed by §3.15 and applicable criminal law.
7. An individual may petition to challenge an inaccurate or unlawfully maintained judicial record.

## §7.16 — Misconduct During Criminal Processing

1. No law enforcement officer, prosecutor, correctional officer, or other government official shall knowingly fabricate charges, falsify statements, or alter material evidence.
2. No official shall demand unauthorized payment, personal favors, or private compensation in exchange for lawful processing, release, or charge dismissal.
3. No official shall deliberately deny an accused individual access to rights guaranteed under Section 1.
4. Alleged misconduct may be referred for departmental investigation, prosecutorial review, or judicial proceedings.
5. The legality of an arrest shall not exempt subsequent misconduct from independent investigation.
6. Officials shall not retaliate against individuals for making lawful complaints or seeking judicial review.
