# ROSEFIRE CIVIL RIGHTS & JUSTICE CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.4 — Sections 9–10**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act establishing the organization and jurisdiction of the Department of Justice; governing courts, attorneys, judicial proceedings, civil disputes, appeals, and official legal records; and regulating the suspension, seizure, revocation, and reinstatement of licenses and permits within the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–8 remain enacted and in force. Sections 9–10 constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code and shall be interpreted consistently with all previously established civil rights, criminal procedures, judicial safeguards, and correctional protections.

---

# SECTION 9 — DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE & COURT PROCEEDINGS

## §9.1 — Establishment of the Department of Justice

1. The Department of Justice shall serve as the recognized judicial and legal authority of the State of San Andreas.

2. The Department shall be responsible for the lawful administration of courts, criminal prosecution, civil proceedings, judicial orders, legal records, and other duties expressly assigned by law.

3. The Department shall administer justice in accordance with the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code, the Penal Code, and other duly enacted laws.

4. The Department shall exercise its powers independently of unlawful interference by law enforcement agencies, correctional institutions, private businesses, and government contractors.

5. No government official or private corporation shall possess authority to overturn a lawful judicial determination except through a recognized legal procedure.

6. The Department shall provide reasonable access to judicial proceedings and legal remedies to all individuals entitled to such access under state law.

## §9.2 — Organization of the Department of Justice

The Department of Justice shall recognize the following offices and professional functions:

1. **Attorney General:** The senior legal officer responsible for state legal affairs and those administrative, supervisory, and prosecutorial responsibilities assigned by law.

2. **Judiciary:** The duly appointed judges authorized to hear cases, issue judicial orders, determine disputes, and impose sentences within their lawful jurisdiction.

3. **District Attorney:** The prosecutorial authority responsible for representing the State in criminal proceedings.

4. **Assistant and Deputy District Attorneys:** Authorized prosecutors operating under the applicable prosecutorial office.

5. **Licensed Attorneys:** Individuals authorized to represent clients before courts and government authorities.

6. **Court Clerks and Administrative Personnel:** Authorized personnel responsible for filings, scheduling, records, and court administration.

7. **Judicial Intelligence Service:** An authorized automated service performing expressly delegated judicial and administrative functions under state oversight.

The creation of an office or title shall not independently confer powers beyond those established by law.

## §9.3 — Judicial Appointments and Court Composition

1. The State of San Andreas shall maintain no more than three active judicial appointments at one time.

2. Judges shall be appointed or otherwise seated according to the lawful judicial appointment process.

3. Each judge shall exercise only the jurisdiction and authority assigned to their office.

4. Judicial appointments shall be recorded by the Department of Justice.

5. A judge shall not preside over a matter in which they possess a material personal, financial, or professional conflict of interest.

6. A judge may be required to recuse themselves where impartiality could reasonably be questioned.

7. Temporary administrative duties may be assigned to an eligible judicial officer where permitted by law, provided the statutory limit on judicial appointments is not circumvented.

8. The Judicial Intelligence Service shall not occupy a human judicial appointment or increase the statutory number of human judges.

## §9.4 — Judicial Independence

1. Judges shall decide matters according to law and the evidence properly presented.

2. No judge shall accept money, property, personal favors, or other improper benefits in exchange for a judicial determination.

3. No public official, law enforcement officer, correctional employee, private corporation, or other person shall unlawfully threaten or coerce a judge concerning a pending proceeding.

4. Judges shall disclose relevant conflicts of interest and recuse themselves when required.

5. A judicial decision shall not be altered solely because an outside party disagrees with the outcome.

6. Judicial misconduct may be investigated through lawful disciplinary or removal procedures.

7. Judicial independence shall not exempt a judge from criminal law or accountability for unlawful conduct.

## §9.5 — Authority of the Attorney General

1. The Attorney General shall serve as the State's senior legal officer.

2. The Attorney General may supervise state legal affairs and coordinate authorized prosecutorial and legal functions.

3. The Attorney General may review allegations of public corruption, unlawful government conduct, and serious institutional misconduct within their lawful authority.

4. The Attorney General may initiate or direct appropriate legal proceedings on behalf of the State.

5. The Attorney General shall not exercise powers reserved exclusively to a sitting judge.

6. The Attorney General shall not independently impose criminal imprisonment or overturn a final judicial judgment.

7. The Attorney General may petition the courts concerning disputed judicial records, unlawful administrative actions, and matters affecting the public interest.

8. The Attorney General shall remain subject to the laws of San Andreas and applicable conflict-of-interest requirements.

## §9.6 — Prosecutorial Authority

1. The District Attorney and authorized prosecutors shall represent the State in criminal proceedings.

2. Prosecutors may file charges, present evidence, negotiate plea agreements, and make lawful sentencing recommendations.

3. A prosecutor shall not knowingly pursue a charge unsupported by sufficient lawful evidence.

4. Prosecutors shall disclose material exculpatory evidence in accordance with applicable law.

5. Prosecutors may seek dismissal, reduction, or amendment of charges where legally appropriate.

6. A prosecutor shall not threaten unlawful prosecution to secure money, property, personal favors, or an involuntary plea.

7. Prosecutorial decisions may be subject to appropriate judicial review.

8. Prosecutors shall remain bound by the civil rights and criminal processing protections established in Sections 1 and 7.

## §9.7 — Licensing and Regulation of Attorneys

1. Individuals wishing to practice law before the courts of San Andreas shall possess valid authorization issued or recognized by the Department of Justice.

2. Licensed attorneys may represent individuals, businesses, and other lawful entities in criminal, civil, and administrative proceedings.

3. Attorneys may establish or join lawful private legal practices and law firms.

4. Legal authorization shall not require employment directly by the State.

5. Attorneys shall maintain professional obligations of confidentiality, candor, reasonable competence, and avoidance of unlawful conflicts of interest.

6. The Department of Justice may establish reasonable admission, registration, and professional conduct requirements.

7. Suspension or revocation of legal practice authorization shall require lawful grounds and an opportunity for review.

8. No attorney shall be denied the ability to represent a client solely because the client is unpopular, accused of a serious offense, or previously convicted.

## §9.8 — Attorney-Client Privilege

1. Confidential communications between a client and licensed attorney for the purpose of obtaining or providing legal advice shall be protected by attorney-client privilege.

2. Law enforcement officers and government officials shall not knowingly compel disclosure of privileged communications without lawful judicial authority.

3. Privilege shall not ordinarily extend to communications made for the purpose of facilitating future criminal conduct.

4. The client may voluntarily waive applicable privilege.

5. Privileged communications shall not lose their protection solely because the client is detained or incarcerated.

6. Correctional institutions may apply reasonable security procedures to attorney visits but shall not intentionally monitor privileged legal discussions for investigative purposes without specific lawful authorization.

7. Disputes concerning privilege shall be determined through an authorized judicial proceeding.

## §9.9 — Access to Legal Representation

1. Every individual facing criminal proceedings shall retain the rights established under §1.4.

2. Attorneys may consult with detained and arrested individuals through reasonable procedures established by the responsible agency.

3. Law enforcement shall not unlawfully prevent legal representation merely because an attorney challenges the officer's interpretation of the law.

4. Attorneys attending secure law enforcement or correctional facilities shall comply with reasonable identification and security requirements.

5. Security procedures shall not be used as a pretext to deny protected legal representation.

6. An attorney shall not knowingly interfere with an active emergency or the lawful operation of a secure facility.

7. An individual may voluntarily waive counsel as permitted under Section 1.

## §9.10 — Jurisdiction of the Courts

1. The courts of San Andreas shall possess jurisdiction over criminal, civil, and administrative matters assigned by law.

2. Criminal jurisdiction shall include the adjudication of offenses established under the Penal Code.

3. Civil jurisdiction shall include lawful disputes involving contracts, property, business relationships, personal injury, and other recognized claims.

4. Administrative jurisdiction shall include review of licensing decisions, government actions, and other matters assigned by legislation.

5. Courts may hear petitions challenging unlawful detention, search, seizure, imprisonment, or denial of protected rights.

6. Courts shall not exercise authority over matters expressly excluded from their jurisdiction by applicable law.

7. Judicial proceedings may be conducted through authorized physical, written, or electronic procedures.

## §9.11 — Commencement of Criminal Proceedings

1. Criminal proceedings may commence through the filing of recognized criminal charges by an authorized law enforcement or prosecutorial authority.

2. The filing shall identify the accused, alleged offenses, and material factual basis supporting the charges.

3. The accused shall receive notice of the allegations and applicable judicial procedure.

4. An accused individual may enter an authorized plea or contest the charges in accordance with Section 7.

5. The court may require additional information or clarification where the initial filing is materially deficient.

6. Cases requiring mandatory judicial determination shall not be resolved solely by an officer's unilateral declaration of guilt.

## §9.12 — Arraignment Proceedings

1. An arraignment shall provide an accused individual the opportunity to appear before an authorized judicial authority concerning formally filed criminal charges.

2. The accused shall be informed of the charges and applicable rights.

3. The accused may enter a plea or confirm a previously entered plea.

4. The court may consider issues concerning legal representation, bail, remand, and scheduling.

5. A Not Guilty plea shall not establish guilt or independently justify harsher punishment.

6. Failure to appear may result in appropriate judicial action where lawful notice was provided.

7. Failure to request arraignment shall not automatically constitute an admission of guilt.

8. The Department of Justice shall establish published procedural deadlines for arraignment and related filings.

## §9.13 — Criminal Trials

1. An accused individual contesting criminal charges shall be entitled to a lawful judicial determination.

2. The prosecution shall bear the burden of proving each required element of the charged offense beyond a reasonable doubt.

3. The accused shall be presumed innocent unless guilt is established according to law.

4. The prosecution and defense may present admissible evidence, examine witnesses, and raise lawful objections.

5. The accused shall have a reasonable opportunity to challenge the evidence presented against them.

6. A judge shall determine disputed questions of law and procedure within their jurisdiction.

7. A criminal conviction shall not be entered solely because the accused refuses to testify.

8. A court shall issue an official verdict and record the resulting disposition.

9. Proceedings involving possible life imprisonment or capital punishment shall require the human judicial authority established under Section 8.

## §9.14 — Evidence and Testimony

1. Courts may receive relevant and admissible evidence concerning matters properly before them.

2. Evidence may include physical exhibits, documentary records, recordings, electronic records, expert analysis, and witness testimony.

3. Evidence shall be evaluated according to its relevance, reliability, authenticity, and applicable legal restrictions.

4. Knowingly presenting fabricated evidence or false sworn testimony may constitute a criminal offense.

5. Evidence obtained through unlawful government conduct may be challenged under §4.13.

6. The court may exclude evidence that is inadmissible, improperly authenticated, or materially prejudicial under applicable evidentiary standards.

7. Parties shall not intentionally destroy or conceal material evidence subject to lawful preservation obligations.

8. Judicial records generated by automated services shall not be presumed infallible merely because they originate from an official state system.

## §9.15 — Witnesses and Subpoenas

1. A court may issue a lawful subpoena requiring the attendance of a witness or production of designated evidence.

2. Subpoenas shall identify the issuing authority and the obligations imposed.

3. Individuals may challenge subpoenas that are unlawful, unduly burdensome, or improperly directed toward privileged material.

4. Knowingly refusing a valid judicial subpoena without lawful justification may constitute contempt or another applicable offense.

5. Witnesses shall not be threatened, bribed, or unlawfully coerced concerning their testimony.

6. Witnesses shall retain applicable protections against compelled self-incrimination.

7. A subpoena shall not independently authorize an unrestricted police search.

## §9.16 — Judicial Warrants and Orders

1. Authorized judges may issue arrest warrants, search warrants, subpoenas, remand orders, and other judicial directives within their jurisdiction.

2. Applications shall establish the legal requirements applicable to the requested order.

3. Warrants shall identify their scope, purpose, and relevant legal authority.

4. Officers executing judicial orders shall comply with the conditions established by the issuing authority.

5. An order issued through the Judicial Intelligence Service shall be valid only where the specific judicial function has been lawfully delegated.

6. Judicial orders may be challenged, modified, suspended, or vacated through authorized proceedings.

7. Knowingly forging or unlawfully altering a judicial order may constitute a criminal offense.

## §9.17 — Civil Proceedings

1. Individuals and legally recognized entities may bring civil claims before the appropriate court.

2. Civil proceedings may concern contracts, property, commercial obligations, personal injuries, unlawful conduct, and other legally recognized disputes.

3. The claimant shall identify the legal basis for the claim and the relief sought.

4. The responding party shall receive reasonable notice and an opportunity to answer.

5. Civil liability shall ordinarily be determined by a preponderance of the evidence, unless another standard is expressly established by law.

6. Courts may award monetary damages, restitution, declaratory relief, injunctions, or other lawful remedies.

7. The amount and nature of available relief shall remain subject to applicable law.

8. Civil judgments shall not independently authorize criminal imprisonment for conduct not established as a criminal offense.

## §9.18 — Contract Disputes

1. Individuals and businesses may enter lawful agreements concerning goods, services, employment, property, financing, and other commercial matters.

2. Courts may determine whether an agreement is valid and enforceable.

3. A party alleging breach of contract may seek appropriate civil relief.

4. Fraud, coercion, material misrepresentation, or other legal defects may affect enforceability.

5. Courts may consider written agreements, communications, payment records, and other relevant evidence.

6. A court may award lawful damages or other remedies supported by the agreement and applicable law.

7. Contract disputes shall not automatically constitute criminal offenses.

8. A criminal investigation may proceed separately where sufficient evidence of fraud or another criminal offense exists.

## §9.19 — Property, Ownership, and Real Estate Proceedings

1. Courts may hear disputes concerning lawful ownership, possession, leases, property transfers, and contractual interests in real estate.

2. Property owners and lawful occupants shall retain the protections established under Sections 1 and 4.

3. An individual shall not be permanently deprived of lawful property ownership without appropriate legal authority and process.

4. Disputes concerning mortgages, defaults, foreclosures, and contractual property obligations may be reviewed by an authorized court.

5. A foreclosure or property repossession shall not independently authorize unlawful eviction, physical intimidation, or seizure of unrelated property.

6. The court may issue appropriate orders concerning ownership, possession, disputed claims, and enforcement.

7. Property transactions administered through licensed real estate businesses shall remain subject to applicable contract and property law.

8. Financial disputes involving lawful credit arrangements shall not automatically constitute criminal wrongdoing.

## §9.20 — Judicial Review of Government Conduct

1. Individuals may petition the courts concerning alleged unlawful actions by government officials or agencies.

2. Reviewable matters may include unlawful detention, improper seizure, denial of licenses, official misconduct, and other legally recognized grievances.

3. Courts may examine whether the challenged conduct complied with applicable law.

4. A court may grant appropriate lawful relief, including correction of records, return of property, or orders restraining unlawful conduct.

5. Government employment shall not independently exempt an official from legal accountability.

6. Lawful judicial review shall not be obstructed solely because the challenged conduct concerns a government institution.

## §9.21 — Appeals

1. An individual or party entitled to appeal may seek review of a judicial determination through the recognized appellate process.

2. Appeals may concern legal error, procedural violations, improper admission or exclusion of evidence, unlawful sentencing, or other recognized grounds.

3. Appellate proceedings shall not automatically constitute a new trial unless authorized by the reviewing court.

4. A reviewing authority may affirm, modify, vacate, or return a matter for further proceedings.

5. An individual shall not receive an increased criminal sentence solely as punishment for exercising a lawful appeal.

6. Capital proceedings shall remain subject to the additional safeguards established under §8.7.

7. The Department of Justice shall establish published appeal procedures and filing deadlines.

8. An authorized judicial intelligence service may provide administrative assistance with appeals but shall not independently exercise appellate authority unless specifically authorized by law.

## §9.22 — Contempt of Court

1. A court may address intentional conduct that substantially disrupts judicial proceedings or knowingly defies a valid judicial order.

2. Contempt proceedings shall require identification of the conduct alleged.

3. An individual accused of contempt shall receive an appropriate opportunity to respond, except where immediate temporary action is necessary to maintain courtroom safety or order.

4. Contempt sanctions shall be limited to penalties expressly authorized by law.

5. A judge shall not employ contempt powers solely to punish lawful criticism or disagreement.

6. Contempt sanctions remain subject to applicable judicial review.

7. No contempt sanction shall circumvent the sentencing limitations established under Section 8.

## §9.23 — Judicial Intelligence Service

1. The State of San Andreas recognizes an authorized Judicial Intelligence Service for designated court administration and judicial functions.

2. The service may receive filings, examine records, schedule proceedings, process authorized applications, and assist with matters lawfully delegated to it.

3. The service may issue binding determinations only where specific authority has been granted by law.

4. Automated determinations shall identify the controlling legal provisions and preserve an official decision record.

5. Individuals shall retain the right to challenge automated decisions through the Department of Justice.

6. The Judicial Intelligence Service shall not independently impose life imprisonment or capital punishment under its existing authority.

7. The service shall not create criminal offenses, amend legislation, or exceed sentencing limitations.

8. Human judicial review shall remain available according to applicable legal procedures.

9. The Department of Justice shall remain responsible for ensuring that automated judicial operations comply with state law.

## §9.24 — Contracted Judicial Technology

1. The State may engage an independent private corporation to develop, maintain, and support judicial technology.

2. Holding a government technology contract shall not independently confer judicial, legislative, or prosecutorial powers.

3. The contractor shall operate within the terms of its agreement and applicable state law.

4. The Department of Justice shall retain legal oversight of judicial decisions and official judicial records.

5. Unauthorized alteration, deletion, or manipulation of judicial records shall constitute an offense where established by the Penal Code.

6. A contractor shall not use privileged access to alter convictions, issue unauthorized release orders, or interfere with judicial proceedings.

7. Contractors may be investigated for suspected misconduct, security failures, or unlawful interference.

8. A material dispute concerning the authenticity of a judicial record shall be resolved through appropriate judicial proceedings.

9. Private contractual provisions shall not eliminate statutory civil rights or lawful judicial review.

## §9.25 — Court Records and Public Access

1. The Department of Justice shall maintain official records of judicial proceedings, orders, filings, and dispositions.

2. Court records shall distinguish pending allegations from final judgments.

3. The State may provide public access to designated judicial records in accordance with applicable law.

4. Records may be restricted or sealed where necessary to protect legally privileged information, confidential investigations, protected persons, or other recognized interests.

5. Authorized corrections to judicial records shall be documented.

6. Individuals may petition for correction of materially inaccurate judicial records.

7. Access to judicial records shall not authorize unauthorized alteration or disclosure of protected information.

8. The Department of Justice shall maintain appropriate procedures for determining the authenticity of disputed records.

## §9.26 — Expungement and Sealing of Criminal Records

1. An eligible individual may petition for expungement or sealing of qualifying criminal records.

2. Expungement may remove or restrict the legal visibility of designated records as authorized by court order.

3. Sealing may restrict public access without necessarily destroying the underlying record.

4. Eligibility shall depend upon the nature of the offense, disposition, subsequent conduct, and applicable law.

5. Expungement shall not be granted through unauthorized alteration of government records.

6. A court may deny or limit expungement where continued retention is required by law.

7. A valid expungement or sealing order shall be reflected in the recognized judicial records system.

8. The exact offenses eligible for expungement shall be established by subsequent legislation.

## §9.27 — Official Registries

1. The Department of Justice may maintain official registries for legally recognized matters requiring formal documentation.

2. Registries may include:

   a. Wills and estates.

   b. Marriage and divorce records.

   c. Adoption orders.

   d. Business and contractual filings where authorized.

   e. Copyright and intellectual property registrations.

   f. Judicial liens and court-ordered property interests.

   g. Other records expressly authorized by law.

3. Registration requirements shall be established through applicable legislation.

4. Official registries shall not create rights or obligations beyond those recognized by law.

5. Disputes concerning registered records may be brought before the appropriate court.

6. Unauthorized modification or fraudulent registration may constitute criminal conduct.

## §9.28 — Wills and Estates

1. An individual may execute a lawful will directing the disposition of their estate upon death.

2. A will may be registered with the Department of Justice according to established procedures.

3. Courts may determine the validity of disputed wills.

4. Where no valid will exists, the estate shall be administered according to the applicable laws of succession.

5. Joint ownership, secured debts, contractual obligations, and other lawful property interests shall be considered during estate administration.

6. Fraudulent wills or deliberate misrepresentation of inheritance rights may be subject to criminal or civil proceedings.

7. The death of a property owner shall not automatically transfer their assets to the State where lawful heirs or other valid interests exist.

## §9.29 — Copyright and Intellectual Property

1. The State may recognize legal protection for qualifying creative works, commercial identities, and intellectual property.

2. Eligible protection may include literary, musical, artistic, audiovisual, and other qualifying works as established by law.

3. Individuals and businesses may register eligible works through authorized procedures.

4. Registration shall not validate fraudulent ownership claims.

5. Courts may hear disputes concerning infringement, unauthorized use, and contractual licensing.

6. Remedies may include appropriate civil damages, injunctions, and other lawful relief.

7. Criminal penalties shall apply only where an applicable criminal law expressly establishes an offense.

## §9.30 — Judicial Misconduct and Accountability

1. Judges, prosecutors, attorneys, court employees, and judicial technology contractors shall remain subject to applicable laws governing official misconduct.

2. Bribery, evidence fabrication, unlawful interference, intentional record manipulation, and other prohibited conduct may give rise to criminal investigation.

3. A complaint against a judicial official shall be reviewed through an appropriate independent process.

4. A judicial official shall not be removed or punished solely for rendering a lawful but unpopular decision.

5. Proven serious misconduct may result in disciplinary action, removal, license revocation, civil remedies, or criminal proceedings where authorized.

6. Judicial misconduct shall not automatically invalidate unrelated proceedings.

7. Affected parties may seek review of proceedings materially influenced by proven misconduct.
