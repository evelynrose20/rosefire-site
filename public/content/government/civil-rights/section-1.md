# SECTION 1 — FUNDAMENTAL CIVIL RIGHTS

## §1.1 — Equal Protection Under the Law

1. Every individual residing in, visiting, or otherwise present within the jurisdiction of the State of San Andreas shall be entitled to equal protection under the law.

2. No individual shall be denied legal protection, public services, or access to judicial proceedings on the basis of race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, sex, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, or lawful occupation.

3. No public official, law enforcement officer, correctional officer, or judicial authority may knowingly enforce or administer the law in a discriminatory manner.

4. All individuals remain subject to the same criminal laws regardless of wealth, employment, public office, or social standing, except where expressly established legal immunities or lawful official duties apply.

5. Claims of discriminatory enforcement may be brought before the Department of Justice for review.

## §1.2 — Right to Life and Personal Security

1. Every individual possesses the right to protection against unlawful violence, excessive force, arbitrary detention, and unlawful deprivation of liberty.

2. No individual shall be subjected to torture, cruel treatment, or punishment exceeding the lawful authority of the State.

3. Government authorities shall exercise only such force as is reasonably necessary and proportionate to the circumstances.

4. Individuals retain the right to lawful self-defense and defense of others in accordance with the applicable criminal laws of San Andreas.

5. The State shall not deprive an individual of life except pursuant to lawful authority expressly established by legislation and judicial process.

## §1.3 — Right to Remain Silent

1. No individual shall be compelled to provide statements that may incriminate themselves in a criminal investigation or proceeding.

2. An individual may refuse to answer investigative questions, except where law expressly requires identifying information or compliance with a specific lawful administrative requirement.

3. The refusal to answer voluntary investigative questions shall not independently establish reasonable suspicion, probable cause, or guilt.

4. No individual shall be threatened, physically coerced, or unlawfully punished for exercising the right to remain silent.

5. Voluntary statements made to law enforcement may be admitted as evidence in judicial proceedings, subject to applicable evidentiary rules.

6. The right to remain silent shall apply regardless of whether a law enforcement officer has formally announced or explained that right.

## §1.4 — Right to Legal Representation

1. Every individual accused of a criminal offense has the right to seek representation from a licensed attorney.

2. An individual subject to custodial interrogation may request legal representation at any time.

3. Upon a clear request for legal counsel, law enforcement shall suspend custodial interrogation until counsel is present or the individual knowingly and voluntarily withdraws the request.

4. Individuals may elect to represent themselves in judicial proceedings, provided the waiver of counsel is voluntary and informed.

5. No individual shall be denied reasonable communication with legal counsel while lawfully detained or arrested.

6. The temporary unavailability of an attorney shall not automatically invalidate an otherwise lawful arrest or prevent lawful booking, medical care, transport, or necessary security procedures.

7. The absence of an attorney shall not authorize continued custodial interrogation after a valid request for counsel.

8. Access to legal counsel may be subject to reasonable institutional security procedures, but such procedures shall not be used to deliberately obstruct representation.

## §1.5 — Right to Due Process

1. No individual shall be deprived of liberty or property by the State without lawful authority and appropriate legal process.

2. Every individual formally accused of a criminal offense shall be informed of the charges against them.

3. Every accused individual shall have a reasonable opportunity to respond to the allegations and contest the evidence presented.

4. No individual shall be presumed guilty solely because they have been arrested, detained, or formally charged.

5. Criminal guilt shall be determined according to the standards established under the laws and judicial procedures of San Andreas.

6. Individuals shall retain access to appeals, petitions, and judicial review as prescribed by law.

7. Government authorities shall not knowingly fabricate evidence, conceal material exculpatory evidence, or deliberately interfere with lawful defense proceedings.

## §1.6 — Protection Against Unreasonable Search and Seizure

1. Every individual has the right to security in their person, residence, private property, personal belongings, and lawful possessions.

2. Government authorities shall not search or seize private property without lawful justification.

3. Searches shall be conducted pursuant to a valid warrant, voluntary lawful consent, or an expressly recognized exception to the warrant requirement.

4. An individual's refusal to consent to a search shall not independently constitute reasonable suspicion, probable cause, obstruction, or an admission of guilt.

5. Searches shall remain limited to the lawful purpose and authorized scope of the search.

6. Any search or seizure exceeding its lawful authority may be challenged before the Department of Justice.

## §1.7 — Protection Against Double Jeopardy

1. No individual shall be prosecuted a second time for the same criminal offense following a final acquittal or conviction, except where a lawful retrial is authorized through judicial review.

2. Separate criminal acts arising from distinct incidents may be prosecuted independently.

3. Multiple legally distinct offenses arising from the same incident may be charged when each offense satisfies its required legal elements.

4. Civil proceedings and criminal proceedings concerning the same conduct may be pursued independently where permitted by law.

5. The State shall not repeatedly prosecute an individual for the purpose of harassment or circumventing a final judicial decision.

## §1.8 — Right to Petition and Judicial Review

1. Every individual may submit a lawful complaint, petition, or legal filing to the Department of Justice.

2. Individuals may challenge unlawful arrests, improper searches, seized property, criminal convictions, correctional detention, and official misconduct.

3. No government official shall retaliate against an individual solely for filing a lawful complaint or petition.

4. Judicial decisions and orders shall be binding upon all parties unless suspended, amended, or overturned by a court possessing appropriate authority.

5. Individuals shall have the right to seek review of an automated judicial determination as established under this Code.

## §1.9 — Automatic Protection of Civil Rights

1. All rights established under this Code apply automatically to every individual within the jurisdiction of the State of San Andreas.

2. No law enforcement officer, correctional officer, or government official is required to formally recite or read these rights for them to become effective.

3. Failure to announce, explain, or recite an individual's rights shall not suspend, diminish, or invalidate those rights.

4. An individual's silence, lack of legal knowledge, or failure to expressly invoke a protected right shall not constitute a waiver of that right.

5. Any waiver of a protected right must be voluntary, informed, and clearly expressed, except where otherwise expressly provided by law.

6. A law enforcement officer or government official who knowingly interferes with or violates an individual's protected rights may be subject to disciplinary proceedings, criminal charges where applicable, and judicial review.

7. Evidence obtained through a violation of protected rights may be challenged under §4.13.

8. Nothing in this section shall require law enforcement to deliver a scripted warning upon arrest. The legality of an officer's conduct shall be determined by the officer's actions and compliance with the law, not merely the presence or absence of a verbal warning.
