# ROSEFIRE CIVIL RIGHTS & JUSTICE CODE

**STATE OF SAN ANDREAS**  
**DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE**

**Version 1.6 — Sections 13–14**  
**Status: ENACTED — IN FORCE**

*An Act regulating controlled substances, licensed cannabis commerce, unlawful drug production and distribution, controlled substance enforcement, wildlife protection, hunting, fishing, conservation activities, and the lawful powers of designated wildlife enforcement authorities within the State of San Andreas.*

**Legislative Standing:** Sections 1–12 remain enacted and in force. Sections 13–14 shall constitute a continuation of the Rosefire Civil Rights & Justice Code and shall be interpreted consistently with the fundamental civil rights, enforcement standards, licensing provisions, criminal procedures, and judicial protections previously established.

---

# SECTION 13 — CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES

## §13.1 — Purpose and General Authority

1. The State of San Andreas shall regulate the possession, manufacture, cultivation, transportation, distribution, and commercial sale of designated controlled substances.

2. Controlled substance laws shall serve the lawful purposes of public safety, prevention of unlawful trafficking, protection of legitimate commerce, and regulation of substances presenting recognized risks to public health.

3. No substance shall constitute a controlled substance solely because a law enforcement officer considers it dangerous, suspicious, or undesirable.

4. Controlled substances and their classifications shall be identified in an officially adopted state substance schedule.

5. Criminal penalties shall be established under the Penal Code.

6. Authorized medical, scientific, industrial, and commercial uses of designated substances may be permitted under applicable law.

7. All controlled substance enforcement shall remain subject to the protections established under Sections 1–7.

## §13.2 — Classification of Controlled Substances

The State shall recognize the following regulatory classifications:

**Class I — Prohibited Controlled Substances**

1. Substances designated as prohibited for ordinary civilian possession, manufacture, or distribution.

2. Possession, manufacture, transportation, or distribution shall be unlawful except under a specific statutory authorization.

**Class II — Restricted Controlled Substances**

1. Substances permitted only for designated medical, scientific, governmental, or other authorized purposes.

2. Civilian possession and distribution shall require the legal authorization applicable to the substance.

**Class III — Regulated Commercial Substances**

1. Substances permitted for designated civilian or commercial activity under licensing and regulatory conditions.

2. Cannabis and designated cannabis products may be included within this class.

**Class IV — Unrestricted or Ordinary Lawful Substances**

1. Substances not subject to criminal controlled-substance restrictions under the official schedule.

2. Such substances may remain subject to ordinary commercial, product safety, or public health regulations.

The Department of Justice shall recognize the substance classifications adopted through lawful state procedures.

## §13.3 — Official Controlled Substance Schedule

1. The State shall maintain an official schedule identifying controlled substances by their recognized name and classification.

2. The schedule may establish specific restrictions concerning possession, cultivation, manufacture, transport, and distribution.

3. Amendments to the schedule shall require lawful governmental authority and publication.

4. Law enforcement officers shall not independently create new controlled-substance classifications.

5. An individual shall not be convicted of violating a controlled-substance restriction that was not legally effective at the time of the alleged conduct.

6. Disputes concerning the identity or classification of a substance may be addressed through appropriate judicial proceedings.

7. A substance's classification shall not be altered solely because it is sold under a different commercial name.

## §13.4 — Possession of Controlled Substances

1. Knowingly possessing a controlled substance contrary to applicable law may constitute a criminal offense.

2. Possession shall be evaluated according to the substance's classification and any applicable authorization.

3. The State shall distinguish between ordinary unlawful possession and possession with intent to distribute.

4. Possession of a substance shall not automatically establish intent to manufacture or distribute it.

5. Relevant evidence may include quantity, packaging, commercial documentation, statements, transaction records, and other lawfully obtained circumstances.

6. An individual shall not be presumed to possess a controlled substance solely because it is discovered near them.

7. Criminal responsibility shall require sufficient evidence connecting the individual to the prohibited possession.

8. Controlled substances lawfully prescribed, supplied, or held under an applicable authorization shall not be treated as unlawful solely because they are regulated.

## §13.5 — Possession With Intent to Distribute

1. Knowingly possessing a controlled substance with the intent to unlawfully sell, supply, or distribute it may constitute a separate offense.

2. Intent to distribute shall require evidence beyond mere unsupported suspicion.

3. Relevant evidence may include:

   a. Quantity and packaging.

   b. Commercial transaction records.

   c. Communications concerning distribution.

   d. Equipment or materials associated with distribution.

   e. Credible witness testimony.

   f. Other reliable evidence lawfully obtained during an investigation.

4. The mere possession of multiple containers shall not conclusively establish intent to distribute.

5. Officers shall document the facts supporting any distribution-related charge.

6. The State shall not apply distribution penalties solely because an individual refuses to admit personal possession.

## §13.6 — Unlawful Drug Distribution

1. The unauthorized sale, supply, delivery, or distribution of a controlled substance may constitute a criminal offense.

2. Distribution offenses may be classified according to the substance, circumstances, scale, and conduct established by the Penal Code.

3. Selling or supplying a substance under a valid commercial authorization shall not constitute unlawful distribution when the transaction complies with applicable law.

4. A license shall not authorize distribution of substances outside its recognized scope.

5. Knowingly supplying a prohibited substance under the false representation that it is lawful may constitute an additional offense where established by law.

6. Participation in a distribution operation shall require evidence of the individual's actual involvement.

7. Mere acquaintance with a suspected distributor shall not independently establish criminal responsibility.

## §13.7 — Unlawful Manufacturing and Processing

1. Knowingly manufacturing or processing a controlled substance contrary to law may constitute a criminal offense.

2. Manufacturing shall include unlawful production, refinement, conversion, or preparation of designated controlled substances for distribution or other prohibited purposes.

3. Authorized medical, scientific, and other permitted production shall remain subject to applicable licensing.

4. Ordinary possession of equipment capable of lawful use shall not independently establish unlawful manufacture.

5. Relevant evidence may include prohibited substances, production materials, records, equipment use, and other circumstances establishing the offense.

6. Hazardous manufacturing operations may be subject to emergency intervention where an immediate danger exists.

7. Searches of suspected manufacturing locations shall remain subject to Section 4.

8. Officers shall not enter a private residence solely because it is suspected to contain equipment that could be used for unlawful manufacture without appropriate legal authority.

## §13.8 — Unlawful Cultivation

1. Cultivation of designated controlled plants shall be regulated according to the official substance schedule.

2. Knowingly cultivating a prohibited plant without lawful authorization may constitute an offense.

3. Commercial cultivation of regulated plants may require specific agricultural or controlled-substance authorization.

4. Authorization to sell a regulated product shall not automatically authorize unrestricted cultivation of the plant from which it is derived.

5. Lawful cultivation shall remain subject to applicable land-use, licensing, and public safety requirements.

6. Officers investigating suspected unlawful cultivation shall comply with the applicable search and seizure standards.

7. Plants, equipment, and related property shall not be permanently forfeited without a lawful basis.

## §13.9 — Cannabis Regulation

1. The State of San Andreas may permit designated cannabis-related activities under lawful licensing and regulatory conditions.

2. Cannabis shall be regulated according to the official substance schedule.

3. Lawful cannabis possession shall remain subject to any applicable possession, age, location, or activity restrictions formally established by law.

4. Cannabis-related commercial activity may require a Weed Seller License or another specifically authorized permit.

5. A lawful cannabis product shall not become prohibited solely because an individual possesses it outside a commercial establishment.

6. Cannabis use shall not excuse impaired driving, reckless conduct, or other criminal offenses.

7. Individuals and businesses operating lawfully within the cannabis regulatory system shall retain the protections afforded under this Code.

8. Unauthorized commercial sale may constitute an offense even where personal possession of the same substance is otherwise lawful.

## §13.10 — Weed Seller License

1. A Weed Seller License shall authorize the holder to engage in designated cannabis-related commercial activity within the State of San Andreas.

2. The license shall apply only to the activities expressly authorized by its classification.

3. The State may regulate the locations, products, records, and lawful methods of licensed cannabis sales.

4. A Weed Seller License shall not independently authorize the sale of prohibited controlled substances.

5. Licensed operators shall not knowingly distribute products outside the scope of their authorization.

6. A license may be suspended or revoked under the procedures established by Section 10.

7. The licensing authority shall maintain a recognized record of the license and its current status.

8. Lawful cannabis commerce shall not independently constitute probable cause for a criminal investigation.

## §13.11 — Licensed Cannabis Businesses

1. A business engaged in regulated cannabis commerce shall possess the applicable business and substance-related licenses required by law.

2. A general business license shall not automatically substitute for a required Weed Seller License.

3. A Weed Seller License shall not automatically satisfy unrelated commercial licensing requirements.

4. Authorized businesses may acquire, store, display, and sell qualifying products in accordance with applicable law.

5. Licensed businesses shall maintain records expressly required by applicable regulation.

6. Commercial inspections shall remain limited to the authority granted by law.

7. Restricted storage areas, offices, financial records, and private communications shall remain protected against unreasonable search.

8. A suspected licensing violation shall not automatically authorize permanent closure or forfeiture of business property.

9. An accused business shall retain the right to contest enforcement and licensing actions through appropriate proceedings.

## §13.12 — Cannabis Cultivation and Supply

1. Commercial cultivation, processing, and supply of cannabis may be conducted where expressly authorized by state law.

2. Cultivation and processing may require authorization separate from retail sales.

3. Lawful operators shall comply with applicable product, transport, and commercial requirements.

4. Unauthorized diversion of regulated products into prohibited distribution channels may constitute an offense.

5. A licensed retailer shall not be criminally responsible solely because another independent supplier commits an offense without the retailer's knowledge or involvement.

6. Documentation required for regulated cannabis transport may be verified through lawful commercial inspection procedures.

7. Seizure of commercial inventory shall require appropriate legal authority.

## §13.13 — Controlled Substance Transportation

1. Transportation of controlled substances shall be regulated according to the classification of the substance and the purpose of transport.

2. Commercial operators transporting regulated substances may be required to possess appropriate transport documentation.

3. Knowingly transporting prohibited substances in violation of law may constitute a criminal offense.

4. Commercial vehicle operators shall remain subject to the licensing requirements established by Section 11.

5. A valid CDL shall not independently authorize the transportation of prohibited substances.

6. Lawful transportation documents shall not excuse knowing involvement in criminal trafficking.

7. Searches of vehicles, trailers, and cargo shall comply with Sections 4 and 11.

8. Cargo unrelated to criminal activity shall not automatically be subject to permanent forfeiture.

## §13.14 — Drug Trafficking

1. Drug trafficking shall mean the knowing participation in the unlawful commercial-scale production, transportation, sale, or distribution of designated controlled substances, as further defined by the Penal Code.

2. Trafficking offenses may involve coordinated activity, substantial quantities, or other statutorily designated circumstances.

3. A trafficking charge shall require evidence satisfying the applicable offense.

4. Officers shall not classify ordinary possession as trafficking solely to increase a potential sentence.

5. Criminal proceeds and property connected to qualifying trafficking offenses may be subject to lawful seizure or judicial forfeiture.

6. The mere ownership of a vehicle, residence, or business used by another person shall not automatically establish participation in trafficking.

7. Relevant innocent-owner protections shall remain applicable.

8. Serious trafficking offenses may carry enhanced penalties where established by the Penal Code.

## §13.15 — Distribution to Persons Not Legally Authorized to Receive a Substance

1. Supplying a regulated substance to a person legally prohibited from receiving it may constitute an offense.

2. Licensed businesses shall comply with any age, authorization, or other lawful eligibility requirements applicable to the product.

3. Knowingly falsifying eligibility records may constitute an additional offense.

4. An individual shall not be criminally punished under an eligibility requirement that has not been duly established by law.

5. Penalties shall be established according to the substance and conduct involved.

6. Businesses shall retain access to administrative and judicial review of alleged violations.

## §13.16 — Medical and Scientific Authorization

1. Designated controlled substances may be possessed, supplied, or administered for lawful medical or scientific purposes.

2. Such activities shall require the authorizations applicable to the substance and professional activity.

3. Qualified medical personnel shall not be presumed to engage in criminal distribution solely because they possess regulated medical supplies.

4. Unauthorized diversion of regulated medical substances for unlawful commercial distribution may constitute an offense.

5. Medical records and confidential patient information shall remain subject to applicable legal protections.

6. Searches of medical facilities or protected medical records shall require the appropriate lawful authority.

7. Emergency medical care shall not be denied solely because an individual is suspected of a controlled-substance offense.

## §13.17 — Public Consumption and Intoxication

1. The State may regulate public consumption of designated controlled substances where expressly provided by law.

2. A person shall not be arrested solely because they appear intoxicated unless facts establish an applicable offense or another lawful basis for intervention.

3. Public intoxication involving conduct that creates a substantial danger may be addressed under applicable public order or safety laws.

4. Drug-related impairment shall not excuse unlawful operation of a motor vehicle.

5. An officer may request necessary medical assistance when an individual appears to be suffering from a serious medical emergency.

6. Medical intervention shall not be used as a pretext for an otherwise unlawful search.

## §13.18 — Drug Impairment and Motor Vehicles

1. Operating a motor vehicle while unlawfully impaired by a controlled substance may constitute an offense.

2. Officers may investigate suspected impairment according to the standards established under §5.6.

3. Impairment shall be evaluated using applicable observations, evidence, and authorized testing procedures.

4. Mere possession of a lawful cannabis product shall not independently establish impaired driving.

5. A positive substance-related finding shall be evaluated according to the legal requirements applicable to the offense.

6. License suspension or revocation may occur only under the procedures established in Section 10.

7. Vehicles may be impounded only where lawful grounds exist under Section 11.

## §13.19 — Drug-Related Search and Seizure

1. Controlled-substance investigations shall remain subject to Section 4.

2. Officers may seize prohibited substances lawfully discovered during an authorized search.

3. The odor, appearance, or presence of a substance shall be evaluated according to its actual legal classification and surrounding circumstances.

4. The presence of a substance whose possession is lawful shall not independently establish probable cause of criminal activity.

5. A controlled-substance investigation shall not authorize unrestricted searching of unrelated persons or property.

6. Seized substances shall be documented and handled under established evidence procedures.

7. Officers shall not knowingly substitute, falsify, contaminate, or unlawfully dispose of controlled-substance evidence.

8. Evidence obtained through an unlawful search may be challenged before the Department of Justice.

## §13.20 — Controlled Substance Testing and Identification

1. When the identity of a suspected controlled substance is material to a criminal charge, the State shall establish an appropriate evidentiary basis for the identification.

2. Identification may be supported by authorized testing, qualified analysis, credible records, admissions, or other reliable evidence.

3. An officer's unsupported assertion shall not conclusively establish the chemical identity of a disputed substance.

4. Test results and analytical findings shall be documented when relied upon in judicial proceedings.

5. Defendants may contest the reliability or authenticity of testing evidence.

6. Knowingly falsifying a controlled-substance test result may constitute official misconduct or a criminal offense.

## §13.21 — Drug-Related Property Seizure

1. Property materially connected to qualifying controlled-substance offenses may be subject to lawful seizure.

2. Permanent forfeiture shall require the appropriate judicial or statutory process.

3. A business, vehicle, or residence shall not automatically be forfeited solely because controlled substances were discovered there.

4. The State shall consider lawful ownership, knowledge, involvement, and applicable third-party interests.

5. Seized property shall be documented.

6. Individuals may petition for the return of unlawfully seized or improperly retained property.

7. Vehicle forfeiture shall remain subject to the protections established under Section 11.

## §13.22 — Substance-Related Financial Crime

1. Knowingly concealing or disguising proceeds of qualifying controlled-substance crimes may constitute a separate offense where established by law.

2. Financial investigations shall comply with applicable legal standards governing records, searches, and judicial orders.

3. The use of a bank account or licensed financial service shall not independently establish unlawful activity.

4. Financial institutions shall comply with valid judicial orders within their lawful scope.

5. Accounts, assets, or records shall not be seized or disclosed without appropriate legal authority.

6. Financial crime allegations shall require evidence of the applicable criminal elements.

7. Lawful businesses shall not be presumed complicit in drug trafficking solely because they have conducted transactions with an individual later accused of a crime.

## §13.23 — Controlled Substance Licensing Violations

1. A licensed business or individual may be subject to administrative action for violations of applicable controlled-substance licensing requirements.

2. Licensing violations shall be distinguished from criminal offenses unless the conduct independently satisfies a criminal statute.

3. Temporary suspension may be authorized where an immediate and substantial danger exists.

4. Permanent revocation shall require the procedures established under Section 10.

5. A licensing authority shall not impose criminal imprisonment as an administrative sanction.

6. Affected individuals and businesses may seek judicial review.

7. Restoration of a suspended or revoked license shall comply with applicable reinstatement requirements.

## §13.24 — Treatment, Rehabilitation, and Judicial Alternatives

1. Courts may consider lawful treatment or rehabilitative alternatives for qualifying controlled-substance offenses.

2. A treatment-related disposition shall require appropriate legal authority.

3. Rehabilitation may be considered in sentencing, probation, parole, or other authorized judicial proceedings.

4. Treatment shall not automatically excuse trafficking, violence, or other independently established serious criminal conduct.

5. An individual shall not be compelled to confess to a criminal offense merely to obtain emergency medical care.

6. Medical and rehabilitative services shall be administered with reasonable regard for privacy and personal dignity.

7. The Department of Corrections may maintain appropriate substance-related rehabilitation programs.

## §13.25 — Controlled Substance Enforcement Accountability

1. Law enforcement officers shall not fabricate drug evidence or knowingly present false substance-related allegations.

2. No official shall personally appropriate confiscated controlled substances.

3. Officials shall not use controlled-substance investigations as a pretext for unlawful searches, extortion, or retaliation.

4. Alleged misconduct may be referred for departmental or judicial investigation.

5. The Department of Justice may review disputed evidence, licensing actions, seizures, and criminal proceedings.

6. A lawful controlled-substance investigation shall not exempt an officer from applicable civil rights or use-of-force requirements.
